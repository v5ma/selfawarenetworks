# Sparse Distributed Representation

**Sparse Distributed Representation** (SDR) is the [[self-aware-networks|Self Aware Networks]] (SAN) page for the encoding scheme — adopted into SAN from Pentti Kanerva's *Sparse [[distributed-memory|Distributed Memory]]* and [[jeff-hawkins|Jeff Hawkins]]' / [[jeff-hawkins|Numenta]] adaptation in *On Intelligence* — in which information is represented as a pattern over a population of units, where only a small fraction of units are active for any given stimulus and any one unit participates in many different patterns. Within SAN, SDR is the encoding scheme that the [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]] tomographic-rendering machinery operates on, refined by [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] to apply *at the synaptic level* and embedded in the wider [[hofstadter-strange-loop|strange-loop]] of [[memory-prediction-cycle|memory–prediction]] over [[cortical-column|cortical column]] arrays.

![Sparse Distributed Representation synaptic phase code diagram](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/sparse-distributed-representation/sparse-distributed-representation-synaptic-phase-code-20260627.png)

This SAN image shows SDR as a sparse population code whose meaning is distributed across the active subset, whose overlap preserves similarity between related inputs, and whose SAN-specific interpretation applies the sparse subset at synaptic scale before phase-selected tomographic readout.

## Post-SAN

Compared to the canonical [[jeff-hawkins|Hawkins]]/Numenta SDR formalism, the SAN reading takes one substantive step away and one substantive step further. The step away is the *substrate*: where Numenta's BaMI-SDR white paper treats SDRs as bit-vectors over abstract neural units, SAN argues the SDR lives at the *individual synapse* and is read out as the action-potential's potential-difference profile (`a0341z--md`, line 43, [on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0341z.md); `a0288zslow--md`, line 194, [on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0288zslow.md); `a0324z--md`, line 74, [on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0324z.md)). The step further is the *interpretation*: Numenta's framing is compatible with both holographic and tomographic pictures of how the population code reconstructs the world; SAN explicitly rejects the holographic reading and asserts the tomographic one (`b0153y--md`, line 29, [on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/b0153y.md); `b0153yWhisperTranscription--md`, line 15, [on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/b0153yWhisperTranscription.md)). External validation, where it exists, is on the substrate rather than the interpretation: the [[gh-gpt2023x|GPT2023X]] commentary at line 1460 ([on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/GPT2023X.md)) records that "Your idea that neurons transmit a 'sparse distributed representation' of their complex structure is in line with current understandings of neural coding," but the holographic-vs-tomographic call remains a SAN-side commitment with no `paper-external` confirmation in this packet's evidence base. Where SAN's framing makes a testable [[prediction]] the published SDR literature does not, it is in the [[memory-as-probability-distribution|memory-as-probability-distribution]] direction articulated in `a0191z--md`, line 61 ([on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0191z.md)): that *invariance* in stored memory is possible "if what is really stored is just a general probability distribution, and not a sparse distributed representation of an actual image in an actual location of the brain" — which reframes the SDR substrate as a sampling of a posterior rather than as the stored content per se.
