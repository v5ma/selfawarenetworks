# Neural Lace Podcast S2E3: Kyle E. Mathewson and Optical Imaging

Published May 12, 2020, this episode compares optical imaging and fNIRS with other brain-measurement
methods and records Micah's 2018 Noisebridge EEG-to-WebVR implementation.

- **Published:** 2020-05-12

- **Guest:** Kyle E. Mathewson, University of Alberta

## What the episode contributes

Mathewson explains optical measurement through scattering tissue, hemoglobin absorption and fNIRS,
including the gap between fast electrical activity and slower vascular signals. The conversation
also covers source localization, structural MRI plus EEG, motion artifacts, depth and spatial
resolution.

Micah describes a 2018 Noisebridge project that sent EEG through a local Python server into WebVR,
where spectral measures controlled a visual environment. That is a concrete BCI-XR integration,
while the discussion also makes clear why it is not a semantic percept decoder.

## Evidence boundary

fNIRS tracks hemodynamic changes, not individual spikes. EEG and fNIRS both require calibration,
inference and artifact control. A working WebVR feedback loop demonstrates integration, not decoded
thought or validated consciousness measurement.
