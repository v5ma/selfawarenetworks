At the root of sensation is distinction: any difference between one thing and another is the minimal unit of conscious experience. Consciousness emerges when a neural circuit detects the contrast between two distinct patterns.

A minimal conscious circuit requires three elements: two distinct patterns and an observer that registers the difference. This scales into distributed [[self-aware-network]] where neurons act as both cameras and LED pixels — detecting incoming [[phase-wave-differentials]] and re-transmitting transformed phase patterns downstream.

## Oscillatory substrate

Neural activity spans 0.02 to 600 Hz. Consciousness arises from synchronized firing across distant, unconnected cortical regions — [[neural-oscillations]] produce multi-layered representations comparable to overlapping transparent planes. Inhibitory signals propagate across [[cortical-columns]], amplifying pattern differences rather than raw magnitudes.

Perception consists of neurons detecting coincidences in frequency over time. The brain does not record the world; it constructs a prediction of it via distributed, synchronized oscillatory activity.

## Relation to NAPOT

This distinction-based view underpins [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography): arrays project oscillating phase patterns that higher arrays read as rendered scenes. The "camera and LED pixel" metaphor is a compact restatement of NAPOT's bidirectional array role.

## History

The framework was developed alongside psychedelic phenomenology — subjective reports of perception as layered virtual reality provided intuition pumps for the oscillatory multi-layer model, though the theoretical claims stand independently of those reports.
