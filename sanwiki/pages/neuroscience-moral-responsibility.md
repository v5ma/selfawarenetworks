If [[criterial-causation-quantum-retrocausality|free will]] is grounded in brain mechanics, then destructive behavior classified as "evil" may be better understood as **brain malfunction** — the result of tumors, parasites, chemical imbalance, or frontal lobe inhibition failure — rather than a failure of moral agency in the theological sense.

## Mechanisms of behavioral pathology

Specific brain mechanisms that can produce violent behavior without volitional [[choice]]:
- **Hippocampus compression** (tumor squeezing the hippocampus → disrupted memory/decision-making)
- **Frontal lobe inhibition failure** — malnourished frontal cortex failing to suppress violent impulses
- **Parasitic infection** — organisms such as *Toxoplasma gondii* alter brain chemistry and have been linked to psychosis, schizophrenia, and increased suicide risk without prior mental health history
- **Acquired brain injury** disrupting the default mode network and rational planning circuits

## The court-medicine convergence

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s 2012 position: when evil is recognized as a medically treatable condition, the court system and the medical system will merge. Sin (theology: "missing the mark") and crime (legal: culpable [[choice]]) both assume volitional agency. Neuroscience undermines that assumption — not by eliminating responsibility but by redirecting it from punishment toward diagnosis and treatment.

## Relation to SAN

SAN holds that behavior is the output of a deterministic (or stochastically determined) neural information-processing system. The question of moral responsibility then becomes a question about which neural configurations produce prosocial vs. antisocial behavior — and how those configurations can be modified. This is consistent with [[napot]]'s view of the brain as a projection system whose outputs depend on its current state.
