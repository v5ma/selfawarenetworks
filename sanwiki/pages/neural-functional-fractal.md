# Neural Functional Fractal

**Neural Functional Fractal** is SAN's source-authenticated 2022 proposal that some neural operations recur approximately across biological scales. The recurring operation is functional rather than anatomically identical: a state is received, transformed by the current receiver, selectively inhibited or recruited, and re-expressed through a larger connected population.

This is not the claim that every neural scale has the same anatomy, that one algorithm is repeated everywhere, or that a memory is copied unchanged from one synapse into the whole brain.

## Four meanings that must remain separate

1. **Anatomical fractal measurement:** a bounded structure can show statistical self-similarity over a measured spatial range. Fractal dimension is a descriptor, not a mechanism.
2. **Scale-free neural dynamics:** a signal can show power-law or long-range temporal statistics over a measured range. A 1/f-like spectrum is not proof that the anatomy is fractal.
3. **Functional isomorphism:** different scales can perform partly comparable operations even when their structures and state variables differ.
4. **The SAN FRACTAL hypothesis:** local learned state can be asymmetrically re-expressed through progressively larger, connected neural populations.

The first two are measurable scientific categories. The third is a model comparison. The fourth is the SAN-specific proposal developed in [[fractal-conscious-perception|Fractal Conscious Perception]].

## The source-recovered SAN operation

The 2022 notes and Whitepaper describe the following candidate sequence:

```text
synaptic and dendritic changes within a learned ensemble
-> cue-dependent cellular and local-circuit reactivation
-> recurrent excitation, inhibition, and route selection
-> transformed re-expression across connected populations
-> broader influence on perception, attention, memory, or action
```

The source called this scaling or magnification. The medically defensible translation is **increased participation and causal reach**, not physical enlargement or lossless copying. A synaptic change may contribute to a memory, but a complete declarative or perceptual memory is not ordinarily assigned to one synapse. Larger-scale activity is an asymmetric transformation because every receiving population has different connectivity, cell types, inhibition, neuromodulation, and current state.

The system itself performs the observation and action. No additional viewer watches the result. See [[screen-metaphor-no-inner-viewer]].

## What fractal measurements do and do not show

Two common measurements have different meanings:

```text
N(epsilon) = C * epsilon^(-D)     bounded spatial scaling
P(f) = C * f^(-beta)              bounded spectral scaling
```

A fractal-dimension estimate depends on the spatial range, segmentation, and method. Human cortical folding has shown approximately fractal geometry over a bounded MRI scale ([Kiselev et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1053-8119(03)00380-X)), while dendritic-arbor work also demonstrates why fractal interpretation requires caution and comparison with nonfractal morphology measures ([Ristanovic et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00422-002-0342-1)).

Long-range temporal correlations have been measured in human EEG and MEG oscillatory amplitude fluctuations ([Linkenkaer-Hansen et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.21-04-01370.2001)), and neuronal-avalanche distributions have shown scale-related statistics in bounded neocortical preparations ([Beggs and Plenz, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-35-11167.2003)). Neural spectra also contain separable periodic peaks and an aperiodic component, so a 1/f-like slope must not be treated as a named oscillation or a direct signature of one mechanism ([Donoghue et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-00744-x)).

An EEG electrode over a small region and a whole-brain recording do not produce the same signal. Similar scaling exponents across measurements would still require source, reference, volume-conduction, spatial-resolution, and model controls.

## Established components near the SAN proposal

- Intrinsic neural timescales differ systematically across primate cortical areas; sensory and prefrontal areas are not simply fast and slow copies of one another ([Murray et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3862)).
- A contextual fear memory in mice can involve functionally connected engram ensembles distributed across many regions; this does not mean that every detail exists everywhere or that one synapse expands into a global image ([Roy et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29384-4)).
- Hippocampal and cortical replay can be coordinated in defined states, and disrupting awake hippocampal sharp-wave ripples can impair a spatial-memory task. These findings support route-specific reactivation, not a universal whole-brain ripple broadcast.
- Recurrent excitation, inhibition, traveling activity, dendritic integration, and neuromodulation can change which populations participate in a computation. Their effects remain pathway-, state-, species-, and task-specific.

## Tonic, phasic, and inhibited roles

The historical fractal map names **tonic, phasic, and inhibited** as a repeating pattern. The source-faithful modern reading is:

- **tonic:** an ongoing or maintained receiver context;
- **phasic:** a consequential departure from that context;
- **inhibited:** reduced activity or transmission in a specified cell, synapse, or route.

These are candidate functional roles, not three universal frequency bands, magnitude classes, or anatomical states. Inhibition is not automatically memory encoding. A phase difference is not automatically a [[phase-wave-differential|PWD]]. See [[phase-language-firewall]].

## Claim ladder

1. **Established:** bounded neural structures and signals can exhibit fractal or scale-free statistics; neural systems also contain nested spatial and temporal organization.
2. **Evidence-supported SAN interpretation:** local plasticity, recurrent recruitment, distributed engrams, and cross-region replay provide plausible components for transformed re-expression across scales.
3. **Open SAN hypothesis:** a common receive-transform-project-and-recruit motif links local learned state to larger perception-action assemblies.
4. **Stronger FRACTAL hypothesis:** cross-scale re-expression preserves measurable representational relations while adding receiver-specific transformations that predict conscious access or action better than ordinary recurrent and engram models.

## Tests

Record a defined learned representation simultaneously at cellular, local-population, and interareal scales. Quantify what is preserved and what changes as the representation is re-expressed. Compare ordinary recurrent-state, distributed-engram, firing-rate, spectral, and SAN FRACTAL models on held-out prediction.

Then perturb one declared route, inhibitory population, or timing relation. The stronger hypothesis is weakened if:

- no reproducible cross-scale representational relation exists;
- larger-scale activity is explained without the proposed transformation variables;
- selective disruption produces no predicted change in recruitment, perception, memory, or action; or
- 1/f-like statistics are the only supporting result.
