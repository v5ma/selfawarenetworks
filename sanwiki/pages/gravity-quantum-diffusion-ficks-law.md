**Gravity as Quantum Particle Diffusion Gradient** is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s early [[gh-formulation|formulation]] proposing that gravitational attraction arises from the diffusion physics of particle concentrations near massive objects — specifically drawing an analogy to Fick's First Law, which describes how particles move from regions of high concentration to low concentration.

Near a massive object, particle density is higher, particle collision frequency is elevated, and — paradoxically — particles slow down (time dilates). This creates a diffusion gradient in which particles "downstream" of the mass have effectively more open space ahead of them in the direction of the mass, making movement toward the mass statistically more likely with each step.

## Formal intuition

Fick's First Law: particle flux is proportional to the negative concentration gradient (`J = -D ∇C`). Applied to spacetime: the increased density of particles (and time-frame density in [[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation]]) near mass creates a directional asymmetry. A particle at distance r has a higher probability of its random walk stepping toward the mass than away, because each step toward mass enters a region of higher activity — analogous to "a new area opening up with each iteration."

This is complementary to, but distinct from, the [[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation]] [[gh-formulation|formulation]] (which frames gravity as increased time-frame density) — the diffusion view emphasizes particle collision dynamics rather than temporal density.

## Time/particle diffusion matrix

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] introduces a "time/particle diffusion matrix or field": time intervals are shorter (slower) near mass (like time dilation), while particle frequency (collision rate) is higher. These two effects are inverses of each other — decreased time interval × increased particle frequency = a net acceleration gradient that produces effective gravitational pull.

GPS satellite clocks tick faster than Earth-surface clocks — consistent with this: lower particle density → faster effective time intervals at altitude.
