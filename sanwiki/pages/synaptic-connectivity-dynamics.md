A refinement to the classical **strong/weak synapse** model: rather than grading synaptic efficacy along a continuous strength axis, connectivity is better modeled as a binary switch — synapses **connect or disconnect** via a physical lengthening/shortening process.

The 2012 SAN notes distinguish between:

- **Old model**: synaptic weight varies continuously (strong ↔ weak). Memory is encoded in the magnitude of transmission.
- **New model**: synapses physically connect or disconnect. A synapse that shortens makes contact; one that lengthens loses it. The binary state IS the memory encoding, not the amplitude.

This has implications for [[synaptic-computation-ltp]]: if the fundamental unit is connection/disconnection, long-term memory becomes a topology question (which synapses exist) rather than an amplitude question (how strongly they fire). Structural plasticity — synaptogenesis, dendritic spine formation — is the persistent-memory layer; synaptic weights modulate the moment-to-moment dynamics on top of that topology.

**2022 update**: neurons themselves can and do move, which further complicates the static-topology assumption of the 2012 note.
