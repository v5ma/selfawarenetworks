# Action-Potential Magnitude

## Begin with the problem

A regenerative action potential is often summarized as one event. The next cell, however, does not
receive an abstract event marker. A terminal waveform opens calcium channels, release machinery
produces a distribution of fusion outcomes, transmitter reaches receptors, and the receiver
transforms that input again.

The problem is therefore not whether a spike happened. It is how to describe the **degree and form
of the change produced downstream** without collapsing several different measurements into one
number.

## Earn the term

The SAN sources use **action-potential magnitude** to point toward that missing degree of change.
For a scientific measurement, the term must name its variable. It may refer to peak membrane
voltage, waveform width or area, axonal current, terminal calcium, vesicle-fusion statistics,
postsynaptic current, or a downstream population effect. These are linked transformations, not one
conserved signal magnitude.

The strongest source-faithful sequence is:

**channel and terminal state -> compartment-specific spike waveform -> calcium-current timing and
amount -> probabilistic synaptic release -> receptor- and receiver-specific effect**

> **Scientific boundary:** A larger or broader recorded spike does not universally cause a fixed increase in transmitter, a larger spike in the next neuron, stronger synchronization, LTP, or conscious access. Each step is conditional and must be measured in the relevant compartment and circuit.

> **Image status:** The current replacement passed the bounded medical and source-operation review. It separates local waveform measures, terminal calcium, probabilistic release, receptor and dendritic effects, receiver-relative population change, and distinct downstream outcomes. The earlier deterministic APME plate and a preliminary label-error plate remain quarantined and unembedded.

The glutamatergic synapse is one example, not a universal transmitter identity. The figure's claim is that magnitude must be declared separately at each transformation and relative to a named receiver; it does not assert that every larger source waveform produces a larger or excitatory downstream effect.

## Separate the candidate variables

- **Peak voltage:** the local maximum at a declared recording compartment.
- **Duration or area:** defined waveform measurements such as half-width or integral.
- **Terminal calcium:** a local signal shaped by voltage, channel state, buffering, and geometry.
- **Synaptic output:** stochastic fusion and transmitter-dependent receptor activation.
- **Postsynaptic effect:** current or voltage in a receiver at a declared dendritic location.
- **Population consequence:** changed recruitment, timing, gain, phase, or behavior.

Calling all six "magnitude" hides the transformations that the theory needs to explain.

## Magnitude also needs direction, extent, and a receiver

A magnitude is nonnegative, but neural effects have direction and structure. Increased release at an
excitatory synapse can increase depolarizing drive; increased release at an inhibitory synapse can
decrease a target's firing or change its timing. Shunting, receptor reversal potential, dendritic
location, and the receiver's current state can change the sign or form of the consequence.

The source also used **spatial magnitude** for the extent of a later network effect. A medically
bounded translation is not "more field energy." It is a measured pattern such as:

- which receivers changed;
- how many receivers crossed a declared response criterion;
- the signed change in each receiver;
- the duration and timing of those changes; and
- the resulting change in recruitment, route selection, or behavior.

The same source event can therefore have different effective magnitudes for different receivers,
and two different source events can produce similar effects at one receiver.

## Waveform and synaptic output

A regenerative spike can be relatively stereotyped at one location while varying across cells,
compartments, bursts, and states. Potassium and other channels influence repolarization, but no
single potassium-control knob sets one universal AP magnitude.

The all-or-none principle remains useful for regenerative initiation, but it does not imply identical
waveforms or identical synaptic consequences. Recent conductance history can shape somatic
waveform ([de Polavieja et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0242-05.2005)).
Somatic voltage can alter axonal spike amplitude and duration and change average postsynaptic
response through a calcium-dependent mechanism
([Shu et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04720)). Kv1 channels can control
repolarization in cortical axon collaterals and boutons
([Foust et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2752-11.2011)).

At presynaptic terminals, waveform shape can influence calcium entry and release. Output also
depends on release-ready vesicles, active-zone structure, coupling distance, stochastic fusion,
receptors, and history
([Hoppa et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.09.038);
[Holler et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-03134-2)).
Schaffer collateral boutons typically favored single-vesicle release under low vesicular release
probability and shifted toward multivesicular release under high-calcium conditions
([Durst et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-33565-6)). This is a
conditional distribution, not a fixed magnitude alphabet.

Inhibition and neuromodulation usually change input integration, threshold, gain, timing, or channel
state. They do not simply scale the height of every fully generated spike. LTP also cannot be
inferred from spike magnitude alone; plasticity depends on synapse-specific activity, voltage,
calcium, molecular state, and protocol.

## Tonic and phasic boundary

An ongoing oscillatory or membrane state can change excitability and the effect of an arriving
event. It does not create one global floor and ceiling for spike magnitude. "Tonic canvas" and
"phasic ink" are receiver-relative functional terms: the receiver's current state supplies context,
and a new event changes that state. The relation must be defined at a scale and measurement.

The source-faithful operation is:

**declared ongoing receiver or population trajectory -> phasic source event and synaptic
transformation -> signed departure in one or more receivers -> amplification, suppression,
redistribution, or relaxation -> revised ongoing trajectory**

Oscillatory phase can organize periods of higher and lower excitability, and entrainment can affect
stimulus selection in bounded circuits
([Lakatos et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1154735)). That does not make
one frequency band a universal tonic canvas, make every phasic event high frequency, or prove that
greater synchrony always has greater magnitude. Phase alignment can increase coincidence at a
receiver, place inputs in a less excitable phase, or have little effect, depending on timing,
anatomy, synaptic sign, and state.

The older word **dissipation** can be made measurable as relaxation toward a baseline or attractor,
redistribution across receivers, inhibitory cancellation, adaptation, or energy loss. Which process
occurred must be tested rather than inferred from the metaphor.

## Bounded SAN interpretation

SAN can define **effective magnitude** as a measured downstream consequence rather than spike peak:
how strongly a source event changes an identified receiver after synaptic transformation. This makes
the hypothesis testable but not automatic.

For source intervention `s`, receiver `r`, time window `W`, and declared distance measure `d`:

**Effective-magnitude equation:** `M(s -> r; W) = d(receiver trajectory after intervention s,
matched control trajectory)` **[1]**

This scalar distance must be accompanied by the **direction**, **receiver identity**, and
**multivariate trajectory** if those carry the relevant distinction. A signed synaptic current,
change in firing probability, trajectory displacement, number of recruited cells, and behavioral
effect are different magnitude definitions.

The SAN-specific hypothesis is that a receiver-relative [[phase-wave-differential]] can have
measurable temporal, chemical, spatial, and population extent after the known synaptic
transformations are modeled. The stronger claim survives only if that multivariate departure adds
prediction or causal control beyond spike count, timing, full terminal waveform, calcium, bouton
state, transmitter, receptors, receiver baseline, common input, and movement.

## Coincidence, electrical valence, and action

Concurrent synaptic inputs can summate at a receiver, and appropriately timed convergent drive can
help a motor neuron or motor pool cross firing threshold. Phase alignment is one way a distributed
system can organize coincidence. It is not a separate reservoir of voltage or energy.

SAN's phrase **electrical valence** can therefore be operationalized as the signed, accumulated
effective drive at a named receiver or motor circuit during a declared window. Excitatory,
inhibitory, and shunting inputs, dendritic location, conductance state, and motor-pool recruitment
must all be measured. The bounded chain is:

**ongoing coordination -> phasic source events -> receiver-specific coincidence and summation ->
changed effective drive -> motor-pool threshold and recruitment -> muscle or vocal action**

This does not say that a beta, gamma, or other band directly moves a muscle. It predicts that timing
and population-state variables may improve explanation of the synaptic and spiking events that do.

## Discriminating tests

1. **Variable test:** declare whether magnitude means waveform, calcium, release, receiver current,
   trajectory displacement, recruited extent, or behavior.
2. **Transformation test:** record each intermediate stage rather than treating magnitude as
   conserved across the synapse.
3. **Direction test:** compare excitatory, inhibitory, and shunting routes and preserve signed
   receiver effects.
4. **Tonic-phasic test:** model the receiver's ongoing trajectory and ask whether event effect
   depends on phase or state beyond rate and stimulus energy.
5. **Spatial-extent test:** test whether the pattern and number of affected receivers carry
   reproducible information beyond the summed response.
6. **Action test:** preserve spike count while perturbing timing or waveform, then measure motor-pool
   recruitment and behavior.

## Recovered effective-magnitude hypothesis

The SAN sources use *magnitude* for several related ideas: waveform amplitude and duration, quantity of synaptic transmission, degree of departure from an oscillating group's prior state, and strength of the later network change. These should be represented as a causal chain, not one conserved scalar.

For a declared receiver, an operational effective magnitude can be defined as the change in a measured receiver trajectory caused by a source intervention relative to an appropriate control. The distance measure, time window, baseline state, receiver population, and intervention must all be stated. Different receivers may therefore assign different effective magnitudes to the same source event.

This formulation preserves the PWD intuition that departures have degree without equating degree with spike height. SAN's stronger test is whether a multivariate source event predicts the receiver-specific change beyond spike count, timing, full terminal waveform, calcium, bouton state, transmitter, receptor state, and common network variables. It must also distinguish changed response magnitude from changed content, valence, synchrony, plasticity, or conscious access.

> **SAN boundary:** Effective magnitude is not conserved energy, excess potassium, vesicle count, synchrony, or phenomenological intensity. It is a declared causal effect on a declared receiver under a declared measurement.
