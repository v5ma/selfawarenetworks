In [[qgtcd]] ([[dark-time-theory|Dark Time Theory]]), light deflection near massive objects arises from **time density gradients** rather than (or in addition to) spacetime curvature. As a photon enters a high time-density region, it acquires negative energy — slowing and bending toward the mass. Moving away, it acquires positive energy — a mechanism that parallels gravitational lensing but replaces the purely geometric GR account.

## Modified Geodesic Equation

The standard geodesic equation is extended by adding the time-density gradient as a forcing term:

```
d²xᵘ/dτ² + Γᵘₙλ (dxⁿ/dτ)(dxλ/dτ) = ∇ρₜ
```

where `ρₜ` is the local time density (high near mass, low far from mass) and `∇ρₜ` is its gradient. The Christoffel symbols `Γ` capture the usual curvature contribution; `∇ρₜ` adds the [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] contribution.

## Time Density Gradient

Time density varies with mass distribution:

```
∇ρₜ = f(M, r)
```

meaning it is a function of the nearby mass `M` and distance `r`. The precise functional form connects to the gravitational time dilation already confirmed by GR, positioning [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] as an extension rather than a replacement.

## Modified Schrödinger Equation

For quantum particles in a time-density field, the Schrödinger equation acquires an extra potential term:

```
iℏ (∂ψ/∂t) = (Ĥ + ρₜ) ψ
```

This means quantum systems near a large mass evolve more slowly — an energy-level effect of compressed temporal frames.

## Physical Interpretation

- Light approaching a mass: enters compressed time → negative energy acquired → path curves inward.
- Light leaving a mass: exits compressed time → positive energy acquired → wavelength redshifts (gravitational redshift reinterpreted).
- This unifies lensing and redshift under one mechanism: **time-density gradient**.

## Relationship to dark matter and dark energy

The same `∇ρₜ` term that explains light deflection also reframes [[dark-time-theory]]'s core claims: anomalous galaxy rotation curves and the Hubble tension both emerge from local time-density variation, not invisible matter or accelerating expansion.
