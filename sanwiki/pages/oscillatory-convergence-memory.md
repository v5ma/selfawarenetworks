Memory in the brain is not stored by synaptic configurations alone. Three intertwined
sensor modalities may simultaneously encode correlated time-series of wave patterns:
(1) [[synaptic-configuration]] rewiring, (2) acoustic and mechanical soliton waves
(with or without [[microtubule-memory]]), and (3) changes to a cell's magnetic dipole
affecting the local [[phase-field]].

## Core mechanism

The pre-synapse acts as a loudspeaker that emits charge (ions, [[atp]]); the post-synapse
acts as a microphone that accumulates charge until an [[action-potential|action potential]] fires. That
[[action-potential|action potential]] transmits a [[phase-pattern]] to downstream networks. Networks
*deconverge* the pattern — many slightly different representations are generated across
cortical layers and columns — then oscillatory re-convergence selects the most
consistent patterns via error correction. This is analogous to [[napot]] (Neural Array
Projection Oscillation Tomography): multiple 2D perspectives converge into a learned
3D model, as in the [[fourier-slice-theorem]] applied to neural columns.

## Fractal repetition across scales

The same oscillatory convergence process repeats fractally: within a synapse, within
a cortical column, across inter-neuronal horizontal connections, and across large
thalamo-cortical bridges. [[ltd]] (Long-Term Depression) coordinates group firing in
post-synaptic cells that share a common temporal oscillation, reinforcing dominant
patterns at each scale. The [[cortical-column]] is proposed to functionally mimic
[[grid-cells]] and [[place-cells]] everywhere in the cortex — a hypothesis consistent
with Jeff Hawkins' *A Thousand Brains* voting model.
