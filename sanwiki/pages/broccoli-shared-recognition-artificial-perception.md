# Broccoli, Shared Recognition, and Artificial Perception

The Broccoli example connects five questions that are often treated separately: how differently organized brains recognize the same public object, how one brain relates many sensory and action properties, how those relations remain distinguishable from nearby concepts, what it would mean to align representations across people, and what a brain-computer interface would have to do to create part of a perception artificially.

## The ordinary-language problem

Two people do not have neuron-for-neuron identical brains, yet both can recognize broccoli, discuss its shape and color, anticipate its texture and taste, distinguish it from kale, and act toward it. Their internal activity need not be identical. What must be preserved is enough relational structure for each person to identify the same public object, predict similar consequences, and coordinate language and action around it.

A BCI version of the problem is harder. What would have to be recorded, modeled, and stimulated for a person to experience one broccoli-related property without broccoli reaching the natural sensory organ? How would the induced event be placed into the person's ongoing scene, body state, expectations, and actions without producing conflict or an unwanted percept?

## Why broccoli does five jobs

1. **Shared reference:** common knowledge does not require a universal neuron address or identical wiring.
2. **Multimodal relation:** broccoli includes shape, color, texture, smell, taste, crunch, language, valence, location, and learned affordances.
3. **Category boundaries:** broccoli must remain distinguishable from kale while preserving properties that both share as vegetables or living things.
4. **Recognition and action:** knowing the object includes predictions about what can be done with it and what is likely to happen.
5. **Artificial perception:** writing even one property requires a causal, individually calibrated, state-conditioned intervention model, not raw signal transfer between brains.

### 4. Relational category geometry, 2024

[[gh-hex-7|Hex 7]], publicly Git-fixed on January 4, 2024, makes the broccoli-versus-kale operation explicit. Incoming patterns are described as relative both to an ongoing tonic state and to other learned patterns. A broccoli representation should preserve its own property relations, remain distinct from kale, and retain higher-order relations that the two concepts share.

The historical source calls this "electromagnetic tonotopy." In established neuroscience, **tonotopy** specifically refers to ordered representations of sound frequency in auditory pathways. It is not the accepted name for a whole-brain semantic map. The source-faithful scientific translation is **distributed representational geometry**: task-dependent population relations can preserve feature and category structure despite individual differences in cortical topography. Any additional claim that endogenous electromagnetic field organization is necessary for that geometry remains a separate SAN hypothesis requiring direct measurement.

## What established research already supports

### Object identity is relational and develops over time

Human MEG decoding has distinguished object identity across changes in size and position, with invariant information developing in stages rather than appearing as one fixed object token. This supports transformation-tolerant population representation, not a universal broccoli code. [Isik et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00394.2013)

### Shared geometry can coexist with different cortical topographies

Haxby and colleagues aligned high-dimensional ventral-temporal response spaces across people and found shared response-tuning dimensions alongside individual-specific cortical topographies. Huth and colleagues found distributed semantic gradients for many object and action categories. These results support the possibility of shared relational structure without identical neuronal addresses. They do not show that conscious content can be copied between people. [Haxby et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.08.026) [Huth et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.10.014)

### Multimodal object knowledge is distributed

Human imaging and lesion evidence implicates complementary contributions from posterior superior temporal and perirhinal regions during crossmodal object-feature binding. This is evidence for distributed and staged integration, not one universal binding center. [Taylor et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0509704103)

### Simple artificial tactile percepts are possible

In one person with long-term spinal cord injury, intracortical microstimulation in the hand region of primary somatosensory cortex evoked localized hand sensations that remained stable for months; stimulation amplitude graded reported intensity. This is an important proof of bounded sensory write-in. It does not establish thalamic object-state copying or full multimodal reconstruction. [Flesher et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aaf8083)

### Intervention effects depend on the receiver's state

Adaptation experiments using TMS over visual cortex showed that the effect of stimulation depended on the initial state of functionally selective populations. This supports the 2017 state-reconciliation question as a real experimental concern. It does not show arbitrary object insertion. [Silvanto and Muggleton, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2008.02.002)

## The wrist pathway, stated anatomically

For a discriminative tap at the wrist, the principal established route is:

**cutaneous mechanoreceptor -> primary afferent with cell body in a dorsal-root ganglion -> ipsilateral cuneate fasciculus -> cuneate nucleus in the caudal medulla -> decussation -> contralateral medial lemniscus -> ventral posterolateral thalamus (VPL) -> primary somatosensory cortex (S1) -> distributed recurrent processing**

This route is specific to fine touch, vibration, and related mechanosensory information from the upper limb. Pain and temperature use different ascending pathways. VPL is therefore a modality- and body-specific relay, not a universal semantic port, and S1 activation is not by itself a complete object experience. [NCBI Neuroscience: dorsal column-medial lemniscus system](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11142/)

## Source-faithful translation of the difficult phrases

- **"The pattern of broccoli"** becomes a task-dependent family of distributed population relations, not one fixed waveform or neuron address.
- **"Intercept it at the thalamus"** becomes the search for a measurable and causally useful relay within a specific pathway, not a claim that every object property converges into one thalamic code.
- **"Copy it into another brain"** becomes cross-person representational alignment followed by an individual causal forward model. A decoder from one person is not automatically a stimulation code for another.
- **"Where does the artificial glass go?"** becomes state-conditioned percept integration: position, body relation, attention, expectation, and current sensory evidence constrain the result of stimulation.
- **"Electromagnetic tonotopy"** becomes a two-part claim: distributed representational geometry is an evidence-supported comparator; a necessary electromagnetic field implementation is a stronger SAN hypothesis.

## The BCI read/write ladder

A serious Neural Lace or NerveGear program must keep these achievements separate:

1. record neural and bodily signals;
2. decode a task-relevant state within one person;
3. align representational structure across sessions or people;
4. identify a causal intervention target in a specified pathway;
5. estimate the receiver's current state;
6. stimulate with calibrated spatial and temporal patterns;
7. measure perception, report, behavior, and unwanted effects;
8. adapt the intervention through closed-loop feedback; and
9. test transfer, specificity, reversibility, and safety.

Success at one step does not imply success at the next. Category decoding is not neural writing, and evoking one tactile quality is not full multimodal object reconstruction.

## What SAN specifically proposes

The later SAN account proposes that object knowledge is a distributed, recurrent relationship among learned sensory, motor, contextual, emotional, linguistic, and predictive patterns. A receiver evaluates an incoming event relative to its ongoing tonic state and other active patterns. Phasic departures can change local timing, recruitment, inhibition, and downstream transmission. Recurrent pathways then update multiple participating systems; no separate inner viewer watches an internal broccoli picture.

[[phase-wave-differentials|PWD]] adds the hypothesis that receiver-relative phase, timing, frequency, waveform, and transmitted-quantity differences help identify and route the update. [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] adds the many-to-one-to-many reconstruction proposal across neurons and populations. The broccoli example adds three constraints:

1. cross-person alignment should preserve relational geometry rather than absolute location;
2. stimulation should be conditioned on the receiving person's ongoing state; and
3. a successful write-in should be evaluated as a bounded property update before claiming a complete object percept.

## A decisive experimental sequence

1. Build a controlled object family containing broccoli, kale, and related items with measured visual, lexical, tactile, olfactory, gustatory, affective, and action properties.
2. Estimate each participant's within-person representational geometry across views, tasks, and modalities using complementary temporal and spatial measurements.
3. Test whether relational alignment predicts held-out object identity across people better than anatomical alignment alone.
4. Test whether temporal alignment, receiver-relative phase, or a fuller PWD model adds held-out predictive value beyond firing rate, spectral power, and representational geometry alone.
5. Restrict causal write-in initially to a simple, localizable property such as tap location or pressure; calibrate within the individual and measure state before every perturbation.
6. Compare state-conditioned stimulation with the same physical stimulation delivered under mismatched contexts.
7. Treat specificity, reversibility, adverse percepts, and behavioral consequences as primary outcomes.

The SAN extension is weakened if receiver-relative timing variables add no reproducible predictive value, if relational alignment does not generalize across people, or if state-conditioned interventions do not outperform matched state-agnostic stimulation. A failure at one stage must not be hidden by success at another.

## Boundaries that protect the argument

- There is no established universal broccoli address in the brain.
- A successful decoder does not prove that the decoded variables constitute consciousness.
- A thalamic relay is not one universal semantic port for every property of an object.
- Similar task geometry across brains does not imply identical biological implementation.
- Tonotopy should not be generalized from auditory frequency maps into a settled whole-brain semantic anatomy.
- Artificially evoking a simple sensation does not establish complete NerveGear-style perception.
- Evidence for distributed representation does not by itself prove SAN's PWD, field, rendering, or consciousness mechanisms.
