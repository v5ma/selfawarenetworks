Within [[dark-time-theory]] and [[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation]] ([[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]), constructive wave interference is proposed as the mechanism of *attraction* (gravity, binding), while destructive wave interference is proposed as the mechanism of *repulsion* (electromagnetic repulsion, nuclear forces).

## Core claim

Constructive wave interference locally increases the density of time — more time frames per unit volume — which manifests as an enhanced gravitational effect. Destructive wave interference reduces time density and thereby reduces gravitational attraction, producing a net repulsive force. At the quantum level, this dual-action mechanism binds atoms together or pushes them apart depending on relative phase.

## Scale applications

- **Nuclear binding**: atoms are held together where constructive interference is dominant.
- **Electromagnetic repulsion** (like charges): destructive interference between aligned same-polarity fields increases the effective time-area around the combined charge, creating a repulsive trajectory.
- **Gravity vs. electromagnetism ratio**: the vast difference in force magnitudes is explained as the degree of alignment of charges within a mass — higher density aligns charges → stronger effective gravitational force.
- **Quantum entanglement**: entanglement requires oscillatory binding; at high temperatures, waves move too fast for constructive binding to stabilize, so entanglement breaks down.

## Virtual particles as repulsive force

Below the energy threshold needed to create a real particle from a virtual configuration, virtual particles dissipate rather than materialize. This dissipation is the mechanism behind the weak nuclear force and the repulsive component of electromagnetism — the energy required to combine two electrons (given non-complementary phase) exceeds their available kinetic energy, and the resulting dissipation pushes them apart.
