# Quantum Microtubule Hypothesis

The **quantum microtubule hypothesis** is a family of proposals in which quantum states in neuronal microtubules perform a function relevant to neural computation or consciousness. [[orch-or|Orch OR]] is the best-known version. The phrase covers several distinct claims, and evidence for an early step does not establish the later ones.

## The causal ladder

A complete quantum-microtubule account must establish, in order:

1. a defined physical state exists in tubulin or microtubules;
2. the state persists under specified biological conditions;
3. living neurons generate or control it;
4. a biological receiver reads it;
5. changing it selectively alters a declared neural computation;
6. the change alters a declared conscious content or capacity;
7. the result requires the proposed quantum mechanism rather than a matched classical cytoskeletal explanation.

The first two steps concern physical feasibility. The final steps concern biological use and consciousness. They require different experiments.

## What has been measured

Established microtubule biology includes neuronal architecture, polarity, cargo transport, organelle positioning, and dynamic interaction with actin-rich spines. These classical functions can affect neural computation without a long-lived quantum state.

In an in-vitro preparation using purified tubulin and stabilized microtubules, Kalra and colleagues used ultraviolet excitation and fluorescence quenching to estimate electronic-energy migration over about 6.6 nanometers. Isoflurane and etomidate reduced the measured diffusion ([ACS Central Science, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114)). The experiment measured photoexcitation energy transfer in prepared protein structures. It did not measure an intact neuron, endogenous neural signaling, a quantum computation, conscious content, or objective reduction.

Babcock and colleagues reported collective ultraviolet-emission behavior in large tryptophan networks assembled in biological architectures, including microtubules ([Journal of Physical Chemistry B, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c07936)). That result is also a prepared-system photophysical measurement, not a demonstration that neurons use such behavior for cognition.

Cantero and colleagues measured spontaneous ionic-current oscillations in voltage-clamped rat-brain microtubule bundles, including a prominent fundamental near 39 Hz, and related activity in membrane-permeabilized cultured hippocampal neurites ([Scientific Reports, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-30453-2)). This is a classical, preparation-specific electrical result. Its frequency overlap with gamma is not evidence by itself that microtubules generate intact-brain gamma synchrony or EEG, and it does not establish a quantum mechanism.

A 2025 multiscale electrokinetic paper modeled ionic propagation and oscillation along microtubules using coupled nonlinear transmission lines ([Scientific Reports, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24920-w)). It extends the same research program as a mechanistic model rather than serving as independent in-vivo confirmation.

## Anesthesia-associated evidence

A rat study reported that a single dose of the microtubule stabilizer epothilone B delayed isoflurane loss of righting reflex by an average of 69 seconds in eight male rats ([Khan et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024)). A mouse study using prolonged drug administration found that epothilone D and vinblastine increased isoflurane sensitivity, while paclitaxel produced a smaller shift toward resistance ([Li et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-025-02956-9)). A later single-dose mouse study reported a 29-second within-subject delay after epothilone B ([Huang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2026.110834)).

These studies support further investigation of microtubules as contributors to anesthetic response. They do not isolate a quantum mechanism. Loss of righting reflex is a behavioral arousal endpoint, and microtubule-modulating drugs can affect transport, morphology, signaling, physiology, and multiple downstream systems.

## Decoherence remains model-specific

Tegmark calculated very short decoherence times for the neuronal and microtubule polarization models he analyzed ([Physical Review E, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)). Replies disputed the selected state variables and parameters. The correct conclusion is not that every possible quantum-neural mechanism is impossible or that later optical results prove one. Each proposal must name its physical state, preparation, lifetime, coupling, receiver, and required neural timescale.

Hameroff, Bandyopadhyay, and Lauretta's 2026 "fractal time crystals" article proposes nested microtubule oscillatory subsystems across multiple frequency scales and connects them to consciousness ([Journal of Consciousness Studies, 2026](https://doi.org/10.53765/20512201.33.1.211)). It belongs on the proposal level of the ladder. Independent experiments would need to establish the time-crystal criterion, the claimed subsystem assignments, intact-cell coupling, biological readout, and selective cognitive consequence.

## Relation to SAN

SAN does not require quantum microtubule consciousness. A classical cytoskeletal contribution can matter by changing cellular readiness, transport, receptor availability, excitability, morphology, or plasticity. A verified quantum contribution could be added at the molecular scale without moving the observer into the microtubule.

The distributed SAN observer remains the field-cell-network operation that relates sensory differences, memory, body state, and action. A microtubule mechanism would be one causal term that changes how cells participate in that process.

Micah's 2013 source discussion did not claim that Orch OR was proven. It asked whether scale and decoherence objections were sufficient to exclude quantum contributions and considered the possibility that field-scale effects might matter. That question should be preserved as theory provenance, not converted into empirical support.
