# Auditory Cortex

The **auditory cortex** comprises several cortical fields in the temporal lobe that participate in hearing. It receives already transformed auditory information through thalamocortical pathways and interacts with brainstem, midbrain, thalamic, motor, frontal, multisensory, memory, and action systems. It is not the terminal point of a one-way hearing cable and does not contain a complete acoustic scene heard by an inner listener.

> **Image status:** The earlier thalamocortical-rendering plate is preserved in the failed-medical-review archive and is not approved for publication. It omitted most of the ascending pathway, universalized one layered cortical-column circuit, and depicted phase coherence as the step that binds sound into a conscious NAPOT scene. A replacement must distinguish canonical lemniscal input, higher-order and feedback routes, cortical-field heterogeneity, motor influence, descending projections, developmental rewiring, and the distributed auditory system.

## From cochlea to cortex

Sound pressure is transformed by the outer and middle ear, cochlear mechanics, hair-cell transduction, and auditory-nerve population activity before cortex is involved. Cochlear position, firing rate, spike timing, fiber identity, adaptation, and recent history preserve different aspects of the stimulus.

Ascending auditory pathways pass through multiple brainstem and midbrain nuclei before reaching divisions of the medial geniculate body. In a canonical lemniscal route, ventral medial-geniculate projections strongly innervate middle cortical layers of primary auditory cortex, especially layer 4 and adjacent layer 3. Other thalamic divisions, cortical fields, cell types, and feedback pathways have different laminar targets. Describing all thalamic auditory input as arriving in layer 1 is inaccurate.

Auditory cortex contains multiple fields with distinct response properties, connection patterns, and degrees of tonotopic organization. Tonotopy is an important constraint, not a complete account of voices, objects, locations, language, or music.

## Auditory grouping is distributed

An auditory object is a changing grouping that supports prediction and action, not an acoustic picture. Frequency relations, onset and offset timing, harmonic structure, spatial cues, temporal coherence, attention, learning, and recurrent activity can all affect whether components are treated as one source.

Elhilali and colleagues showed that temporal coherence influenced human auditory grouping and ferret auditory-cortical responses beyond frequency separation alone. This supports a population and time-dependent grouping process. It does not establish one universal cortical decoder or prove SAN/NAPOT.

Auditory cortex can contain information that an external model uses to classify or reconstruct speech. Successful decoding establishes recoverable information under the recording and model conditions. It does not show that speech is stored as a waveform, that the decoder is the brain's own receiver, or that decoded audio is a literal internal sound.

## Action and descending control

Hearing occurs in a moving and sound-producing body. Schneider, Nelson, and Mooney identified a secondary-motor-to-auditory-cortex projection that helped produce movement-related changes in mouse auditory cortex. This is a defined route in a defined preparation, not proof that all top-down auditory influence comes from one frontal source.

Auditory cortex also sends descending projections. Bajo and colleagues showed that selectively eliminating corticocollicular neurons impaired training-dependent recalibration of sound localization in adult ferrets while sparing ordinary localization under normal hearing. Descending activity can therefore alter learning and lower-stage use without being one global prediction or cancellation signal.

Expected self-generated sound is not simply erased. Different routes can attenuate, preserve, compare, or emphasize sensory consequences according to task and error.

## Historical SAN source recovery

The earlier automated page contained several anatomical overclaims, but it was also trying to preserve a dated SAN development that the first correction compressed.

### Modality-weighted contributions, not separate inner displays

A [June 10, 2022 snapshot](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/9e07121ed3da44c51b77ab556497d082516c82e0/a0036z.md) used a VR-game-engine analogy: visual content was associated with visual cortex while music was associated with auditory cortex. A related [June 11 source](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/e2f2885356339f6547bead9a7b95ef662c1a86d5/a0126z.md) proposed that auditory, visual, and somatosensory systems carry different partial relations for an attended thing.

The medically bounded operation is **modality-weighted partial representation inside a distributed percept**. Sound is not played inside auditory cortex, each thing is not assigned to one universal column, and the modalities are not fused on a final screen.

### Developmental cross-modal rewiring

Two SAN sources from [June 10](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/b80f8193ec0817b968d168df4710595ed983806f/a0038z.md) and [June 12, 2022](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/398ca74dec6db78014bb5cd7fef1bbd0ff30e58d/a0411z.md) used the compressed phrase that eyes could be "plugged into" auditory cortex and the cortex would learn vision. The underlying ferret experiments were more specific: retinal projections were redirected neonatally into the auditory thalamic pathway. Rewired auditory cortex developed visual response properties and orientation modules, and the redirected pathway could support visual behavior.

Those results show that early afferent activity can strongly shape cortical organization and perceptual use. They do not show that adult sensory cortices are interchangeable, that auditory cortex becomes anatomically identical to V1, or that every cortical column executes one general-purpose algorithm.

### The source-authored electrochemical fallback

The [June 11, 2022 source](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/24fa34944c35c7fd92b2176eed5d93f32e5c7608/a0286z.md) explored mechanical, electrical, and magnetic soliton transmission and suggested that auditory sequences could encode musical structure. A [June 22 revision](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/dc00722c82fe2a05e40e8ce709dcceec756cf00d/a0286z.md) explicitly supplied a fallback: even if the soliton proposal fails, ordinary electrochemical events distributed over multiple neurons can still carry temporospatial patterns.

That fallback is the source-faithful, medically usable operation. Auditory features are represented across populations and time; one spike or generic neuron does not contain a complete musical note. Mechanical and electromagnetic consequences of neural activity are measurable, but independent content-bearing soliton channels have not been established.

### Decoding, widespread state, and vocal action

A [June 12, 2022 source](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/1088ce784ef295edd3e6248cde0e5c8d44e7c306/a0371z.md) preserved the speech-reconstruction research direction. Human auditory-cortical activity has supported closed-set speech reconstruction by an external trained decoder. This demonstrates recoverable stimulus information, not a stored waveform or the brain's own decoding rule.

An [August 19, 2022 note](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/a3ad84338cd6529b2448e4452fdb3d82c76163bd/a0111z.md) connected fronto-auditory oscillatory dynamics to bat vocalization and explicitly retained the study's correlational caution. Frontal-to-auditory information flow predominated before vocalization, while the later experiment found auditory-to-frontal flow after an echolocation pulse returned. This is a bounded sensory-to-action-to-feedback route, not proof that a frontal oscillation directly commands the vocal cords.

A [November 28, 2022 source](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/4e0cf1ee0975c01dc57182368497d3f0cd864030/a0325zNAPOT6.md) preserved a preprint claim that task-specific information could be detected widely and that extra-auditory information changed with sedation. The final 2024 study reported that auditory-cortical stimulus information persisted across sedation levels while stimulus-related information outside auditory cortex degraded. This supports a distinction between local sensory encoding and wider perception-related state. It does not establish that widespread decodability is sufficient for consciousness.

## SAN interpretation

SAN interprets hearing as distributed receive-transform-re-express-use activity:

1. mechanical receptors and peripheral circuits capture and transform pressure relations;
2. ascending populations preserve multiple place, rate, timing, and connection-specific variables;
3. binaural, subcortical, thalamic, cortical, motor, and body-state systems form and revise source estimates;
4. axons and synapses re-express changed states into downstream receivers;
5. orienting, vocal correction, attention, report, and later sampling expose what the organism used.

The older microphone and speaker analogy names the directions of capture and re-expression. A neuron is not literally a microphone or speaker, and auditory cortex is not an internal concert hall.

In SAN terms, tonic activity is the maintained receiver context against which a phasic departure becomes consequential. These roles are relative to the named population, timescale, and task. They are not fixed frequency bands. The distributed system itself performs observer-action; no separate listener is added.

## Scale boundary

- **Micro:** hair-cell transduction, auditory-nerve spikes, synapses, dendrites, and identified neurons.
- **Meso:** cochlear-nucleus, superior-olive, inferior-colliculus, thalamic, and local cortical populations.
- **Macro:** interactions among auditory cortical fields, motor and frontal systems, multisensory networks, memory, and descending pathways.
- **Organismal:** the ears, head, vocal tract, body movement, acoustic environment, behavior, and learning history.

Evidence at one scale does not automatically establish the same code or operation at another.

## What would test the SAN extension

A distinctive SAN test must identify the stimulus relation, receiver population, ongoing context, phasic update, route, downstream receiver, and behavioral consequence. It should compare the added SAN variable against sound geometry, binaural cues, rate, spike history, recurrent state, predictive processing, and established auditory-control models. A selective perturbation should cause a preregistered feature-specific error and a compensating intervention should rescue it.

A broad reduction in hearing, arousal, movement, or performance would not identify a SAN mechanism. Neither would oscillatory power, phase locking, decoding accuracy, or a traveling wave without demonstrated biological use.

## Evidence boundary

- Established: cochlear and neural pathways preserve multiple place, rate, timing, and connection-specific variables.
- Established: auditory cortex is heterogeneous, recurrent, movement-sensitive, and connected through ascending and descending routes.
- Established: cortical activity contributes to grouping, learning, and action without constituting the whole auditory percept.
- Established: neonatal redirection of retinal input through the auditory pathway can reorganize ferret auditory cortex and support visually guided behavior.
- Established: trained external models can reconstruct constrained speech stimuli from recorded auditory-cortical population activity.
- Proposed by SAN: receiver-relative tonic context and phasic updates participate in a distributed auditory observer-action.
- Not established: universal oscillatory-tomographic columns, mechanical soliton decoding, fixed band meanings, or a complete acoustic render located in auditory cortex.
