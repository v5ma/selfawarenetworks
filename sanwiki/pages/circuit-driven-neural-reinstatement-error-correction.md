# Circuit-Driven Neural Reinstatement and Error Correction

Can the surrounding network train a newly introduced neuron so that it acquires locally appropriate
inputs, outputs, tuning, and participation in learned activity?

[[gh-a0428z|a0428z]], Git-fixed in June 2022, proposes that a replacement neuron could be
`re-encoded` by the circuit around it, analogizing distributed network constraints to an
error-correcting code. The source says `Hammond`; the standard coding-theory term is **Hamming**.

## Scientific neighborhood

Research shows that grafted neural progenitors can form functional synapses and express
activity-dependent plasticity. Transplanted cortical neurons can receive host-area-appropriate
inputs and develop stimulus tuning resembling host neurons. Transplanted cortical organoids can
also mature, receive host sensory inputs, and influence behavior.

These findings support host-shaped integration. They do not prove that a missing neuron's private
memory is reconstructed or that oscillations alone perform error correction.

## SAN hypothesis

Repeated host input, plasticity, neuromodulation, and action feedback may reduce mismatch until a
new unit occupies a useful local role. Oscillations may organize training windows, but their effect
must be separated from connectivity, sensory experience, and molecular maturation.

The critical experiment follows grafted cells longitudinally, perturbs the host activity pattern
during integration, and tests a rescue with the predicted pattern. A phase-resolved SAN model must
add predictive and causal value beyond standard activity-dependent plasticity.

## Manuscript route

This mechanism belongs in the existing **Telescoping Network Reinstatement in Self-Aware Networks**
working paper. It is not a separate new paper.
