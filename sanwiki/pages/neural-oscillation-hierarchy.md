> **Image status (2026-07-18):** The retained plate is conceptual and is not medically certified. It presents spikes, local rhythms, and brainwaves as a fixed hierarchy whose macroscopic electromagnetic field feeds back strongly enough to stabilize individual neurons for NAPOT. That causal chain is not established, and the original page greatly overstated the neuron count of the suprachiasmatic nucleus.

Neural activity is multiscale, but **scale** and **frequency** are not the same hierarchy. Single-cell events contribute to local population signals; local circuits interact with distant populations; and slow and fast dynamics can modulate one another. There is no universal ladder in which every fast event flows upward and one global slow field controls the levels below.

## Bottom-up aggregation

Extracellular and macroscopic signals reflect weighted population activity, geometry, synchrony, and the measurement apparatus. They are not simple sums of identical neuronal oscillators. A scalp rhythm cannot be assigned back to every contributing cell, and an action potential's decay constant does not directly set one group frequency.

The suprachiasmatic nucleus is a specialized circadian network containing on the order of tens of thousands of neurons, not millions. Its cellular clocks and coupling illustrate population coordination but do not provide a template for all cortical oscillations.

## Top-down modulation

Large-scale network state can alter local excitability through ordinary synaptic, neuromodulatory, thalamic, corticocortical, and body-state pathways. Endogenous electric fields can also influence nearby neurons under some conditions, but evidence for local ephaptic effects does not establish a strong brain-wide electromagnetic controller. Anastassiou et al. demonstrated slow endogenous field effects in cortical tissue and explicitly bounded the phenomenon experimentally ([Nature Neuroscience, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2727)).

## SAN interpretation

The useful SAN claim is bidirectional constraint across declared scales. A local event can change population state; a population state can alter the context in which later local events are received. NAPOT and GCS should identify the physical routes and receivers rather than use **field feedback** as a catch-all explanation.

## Tests

Separate synaptic, neuromodulatory, sensory, vascular, movement, and ephaptic routes. Measure field strength at the relevant membrane, predict the direction and latency of a cellular effect, and show that blocking the proposed route removes it while preserving alternatives.
