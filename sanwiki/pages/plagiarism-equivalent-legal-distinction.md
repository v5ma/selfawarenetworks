# Plagiarism Equivalent Legal Distinction

**Plagiarism Equivalent Legal Distinction** is the cross-referenced term for the legal/normative distinction between (a) reusing another author's specific *expression* — verbatim or near-verbatim, the prototypical academic-plagiarism case — and (b) independently arriving at the same *idea* through one's own reasoning trajectory. Within [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] this distinction is invoked when [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s priority claims are compared against later mainstream literature: the relevant legal/normative line is not "did anyone else say this later" but "did the later writer copy [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s expression, or did they arrive at the same idea independently." The SAN-internal corollary is the chronological-priority reading, which establishes *when* an idea first appeared in [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s writing rather than asserting that any later writer copied it.
