# SAN: A Lifelong Research Journey

**Childhood questions in the 1980s. A reading quest in 1999. A career focus in 2005.
Three lifetime vows in 2012. A growing body of research through 2026.**

Micah Blumberg traces his fascination with consciousness and machine consciousness to **childhood
in the 1980s**, especially *Short Circuit*. Stories about Number Five, Data in *Star Trek: The Next
Generation*, and later *The Matrix* formed a continuing imaginative background: could a machine
have a mind, and what makes an experienced world feel real? These influences preceded the later
technical research program.

At **age sixteen**, an experience with LSD in high school intensified his preoccupation with
the mystery of consciousness. This is part of his autobiographical account, not an experimental
finding about how consciousness works.

In **1999**, reading Douglas R. Hofstadter's *Godel, Escher, Bach* launched a quest for the most
conceptually enlightening books he could find. That was a new phase of an existing fascination,
not the first time he had wondered about minds. [Publisher record](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/douglas-r-hofstadter/godel-escher-bach/9780465026562/).

In **2005**, Jeff Hawkins and Sandra Blakeslee's *On Intelligence* changed the direction of his
life: neuroscience and artificial intelligence became his career focus. The reading quest had
become a focused effort to understand living brains and intelligent machines. In **2012**, that
direction sharpened into the three lifetime vows described below.
[On Intelligence publisher record](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780805074567/onintelligence/).

These stages follow Blumberg's recollections, clarified for this history in October 2026 and
read alongside the current Book 2 preface. Childhood interest, an adolescent experience, a reading
quest, a career focus, and a lifetime commitment are different milestones. The earliest preserved
public precursor in the current SAN genealogy remains the Facebook post dated **November 18, 2010**.
The personal history is longer than the recovered public record; neither backdates the complete
mature SAN theory to childhood or to 1999.

## The three lifetime vows in 2012

The current Book 2 preface, *A Man Named Android*, describes three problems to which Blumberg
committed his life:

1. **NerveGear:** solve the full-dive, two-way brain-computer interface.
2. **An External Cortex or Extended Brain:** extend a person's useful thought within an interval of time, with the ambition of making lived experience richer and subjectively longer.
3. **Artificial brains:** build brains with conscious awareness comparable to a mammal or a person.

The preface says that in 2012 he understood these as a connected research path. Communicating
meaningfully with a living brain would require understanding its read-write operations; extending
that brain would require a learned, co-adaptive relationship; constructing an artificial brain
would draw on the resulting understanding of learning, internal modeling, and feedback.

This was the direction of his commitment, not a claim that the three technologies had already
been achieved or that every research program must follow that order. The older
[three-goal note](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/f734ca8814bfc59f6d01b1aeb30b00e35ed9dfdb/a0231z.md#L5-L16)
preserves the same ambitions, and the
[Extended Brain note](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/009c908e6c4fba12166c6eb3b9df6623d2e5edbb/a0087z.md#L105-L118)
explicitly recalls the 2012 dedication. Their later public preservation is not mistaken for a
contemporaneous 2012 publication date.

## Unexpected details worth knowing

**The brain-as-hard-drive discussion has an identifiable place in the story.** The Android Jones
episode was recorded June 7, 2017 and published June 11. Its question was not simply whether a
brain stores data: it connected present-state measurement, a desired change, and a returned
sensory difference. The [[neural-lace-podcast-s1e7-android-jones-2017|episode source page]] preserves
that bridge; the [original article](https://medium.com/silicon-valley-global-news/the-brain-as-a-special-kind-of-hard-drive-4bd3a99dba4c)
gives it its memorable title. It was an engineering analogy, not a demonstration of arbitrary memory upload.

**Broccoli became a serious interface question.** The May 2017 Andre Watson conversation asks how
differently wired brains can recognize the same object, and what would have to be reproduced to
create an artificial percept. An ordinary food example links shared reference, learned features,
multisensory binding, and neural stimulation. See
[[broccoli-shared-recognition-artificial-perception|Broccoli and Shared Recognition]].

**The podcast numbers are not a chronological clock.** In the recovered collection, Season 2
Episode 4 is dated 2019, while Episode 3 is dated 2020. The transcript collection's older
"2017-2019" label therefore misses its final dated conversation. The
[[neural-lace-podcast-source-genealogy|episode ledger]] preserves the actual source identities.

**The current Book's visual split is exactly even.** Its 336 figure placements divide into
168 non-equation figures and 168 equation cards. The equality is a count of this edition,
not an assumed design rule. The additional outside-reconstruction/world-encounter figure in
Cycle 4 explains why the older 335-placement audit is no longer the current total.

**The Guide has four supporting arguments per Cycle and two details per supporting argument.**
That yields 164 supports and 328 explanations across 41 Cycles. Main arguments, scope passages,
and inter-Cycle connections bring the mapped total to 615. The current prose version carries
those units in 250 paragraphs. Including 42 headings gives 292 narration-aligned blocks:
paragraph count, narration-block count, and argument count are three different measurements.

**Thirty early screenshots do not mean thirty early works.** They preserve 26 dated documents:
24 SAN-relevant stages and two adjacent physics stages. Multiple captures sometimes preserve
different parts of the same post. The [[early-san-facebook-genealogy-2010-2013|Facebook ledger]]
keeps the actual documents and their contexts intact.

**The bibliography reaches much further back than the project.** Its dated material runs from
1861 to 2026, a 165-year span. Within the core scholarly and technical section, the range is
1948-2026; 73 entries carry dates from 2023 through 2026. Older foundations and recent work
are both present, rather than the synthesis being confined to one scientific moment.
See [[san-research-depth-and-breadth|Research Depth and Breadth]].

## Images, biology, and mathematics: exact counting boundaries

The **168 non-equation Book figures** include infographics, biological diagrams, source plates,
historical reconstructions, conceptual scenes, and worked examples. These categories overlap.
The current manifests do not supply an exclusive infographic taxonomy, so there is no additional
"infographic total" to add to the figure count.

A reproducible caption-and-alt-text screen identifies **133 non-equation Book figures** and
**160 Wiki image placements** containing biology-related vocabulary. These are **retrieval
candidates**, not a completed visual classification or a count of medically certified images.
The underlying files, labels, matches, and hashes are recorded for review. No image is accepted,
rejected, or medically re-certified merely because it was counted here.

For a concrete biological route, the Book includes dedicated hearing, touch, and umami mechanism
plates linking potassium-channel questions, action-potential duration, and downstream consequences
in their stated preparations. The [[action-potential-duration|APD article]] and
[[phase-wave-differentials|PWD article]] connect these examples without treating different sensory
receptors as identical hardware.

The Wiki source census also finds **107 paired display-math passages**: 67 double-dollar-delimited
passages and 40 backslash-bracket-delimited passages, excluding code blocks. These are markup
occurrences, not 107 unique mathematical discoveries. Inline notation, prose equations, archived
duplicates, and mathematics printed inside images need different accounting. Book equation-card
identities remain the cleaner formal total: **168**, with TeX, MathML, and raster versions counted once.

## A concise description of the achievement

> Rooted in a childhood fascination with consciousness and intelligent machines, Micah Blumberg's
> journey developed through a reading quest in 1999, a neuroscience and AI career focus in 2005,
> and three lifetime vows in 2012. Public precursors preserved from 2010 connect that personal
> history to the continuing Self Aware Networks research program. Its current companion book
> organizes 41 Cycles, 168 illustrated figures, 168 explained
> equation cards, and a bibliography with more than 800 distinct external-literature DOI
> identifiers. Historical notes, practical neurofeedback, interviews, computational work, and
> explicit experimental proposals connect the theory's development to questions readers can inspect.

The strength of this history is not a large number in isolation. It is the ability to move from
a present argument to its earlier formulation, its scientific sources, a concrete illustration,
and the test that would make the next step informative. Bibliography size and project scale do
not replace evidence for a particular mechanism; they make the scope of the sustained inquiry visible.

The [dated statistics and paper inventory](/wiki/assets/research-depth/san-history-scale-20261002.json)
provide counting definitions, source fingerprints, and the selected paper titles and PDF page
counts. This is a local edition snapshot, not a claim that every hosted surface has already been updated.
