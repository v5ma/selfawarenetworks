# Computational Neuroscience

![Computational Neuroscience expanded biological primitives](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/computational-neuroscience/computational-neuroscience-expanded-biological-primitives-20260706-v1.png)

This plate shows the SAN critique of spike-only computational neuroscience as an expansion of primitives: point-neuron spike trains remain useful abstractions, but dendritic compartments, multi-vesicular release, phase-locked oscillations, and cortical-column arrays carry the biological signal structure SAN treats as missing from the standard model.

**Computational Neuroscience** is, in [[self-aware-networks]], the established field that the SAN corpus engages with critically — sympathetically borrowing its methods while pointing out the assumptions SAN believes it must replace, particularly the spike-centric modelling of [[neural-arrays]]. SAN treats it as a productive but mistaken paradigm: useful, but operating with primitives ([[neural-spikes]] alone, point-neuron summation, fixed labelled-line coding) that the SAN tomographic models — [[biological-oscillating-tomography]], [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography]], and [[cellular-oscillatory-tomography]] — argue are insufficient.

The SAN sources state the critique bluntly. [[gh-a0088z|a0088z]] says "computational neuroscience makes unreasonable assumptions about how the brain works, and that is it's strength … but it is most definitely unreasonable. In fact computational neuroscience is flat out wrong, it's a wonder that it actually does interesting things" ([[gh-a0088z|a0088z]]--md, line 82). [[gh-a0185z|a0185z]] localizes the issue at the spike-centric modelling assumption: "the idea of the spiking model, is that neurons communicate with spikes, and you see that idea all over neuroscience publications and all over computational neuroscience publications" ([[gh-a0185z|a0185z]]--md, line 73). The SAN reading replaces — or at least supplements — pure spike communication with phase-locked oscillatory communication, which is why [[brain-rhythms]], [[brainwave]], [[gamma-oscillations]], [[theta-oscillation]], the [[action-potential]] and [[action-potential-waveform]] together (rather than spikes alone) carry the representational load.

[[gh-a0221z|a0221z]] extends the critique into a specific empirical front: "[[synaptic-unreliability|Synaptic unreliability]], a foundational concept, found in deep learning, and in computational neuroscience, has been undermined by a math proof that shows us that MVR ([[multi-vesicular-release|multi-vesicular release]]) is wide spread" ([[gh-a0221z|a0221z]]--md, line 7). The SAN claim is that what computational neuroscience modelled as random failure is actually [[multi-vesicular-release|multi-vesicular release]] — a structured, [[synaptic-vesicle-release]]-level event whose statistics revise [[hebbian-learning]] and [[long-term-potentiation]] expectations and which feeds directly into the [[bridging-molecular-mechanisms]] story via the [[nmda-receptor]], [[gaba]]-driven [[inhibitory-circuits]], [[acetylcholine]] / [[dopamine]] modulation, and [[camkii]] cascades. [[gh-a0263z|a0263z]] places the field educationally too: "the Coursera Computational Neuroscience course … does not even rise to the level of a freshman level" treatment in the SAN author's view ([[gh-a0263z|a0263z]]--md, line 10), arguing that mainstream pedagogy underrepresents the [[oscillatory-dynamics]] and [[oscillatory-tomography]] picture SAN considers central.

At the same time, SAN positions computational neuroscience inside a wider intellectual coalition. [[gh-a0209z|a0209z]] notes that "Mankind via neuroscience, computational biology, computational neuroscience and deep learning neural networks has been and is now awakening to the overwhelming evidence that we are all mechanical robots" ([[gh-a0209z|a0209z]]--md, line 9), placing it alongside [[deep-learning-neural-networks]], [[convolutional-neural-networks]], [[neural-rendering]], [[predictive-coding]], and [[3d-semantic-segmentation]] as part of a programme that converges on a mechanistic view of mind. The SAN response is not to abandon computational neuroscience but to widen its primitives to include phase, [[apical-dendrites]], [[dendritic-spines]], [[cortical-columns]], [[micro-columns]], [[microtubules]], and the engineering-side machinery — [[neuralink]], [[braingate]], [[neurofeedback]], [[fmri]], [[eeg]] — required to read and write at that level. Read alongside [[engrams]], [[criterial-causation]], the [[agentic-viewpoint]], the [[attention-schema]], and the [[hard-problem-of-consciousness]], computational neuroscience in SAN is the field whose tools the project uses, and whose foundations the project asks to be redrawn.
