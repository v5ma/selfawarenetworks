# Human Magnetoreception as an Active-Sensing and Training Hypothesis

## The question in plain language

Some animals use Earth's magnetic field. Humans contain magnetic material, and controlled studies have reported neural or behavioral responses under particular magnetic-field conditions. That does not yet establish a reliable, consciously accessible, everyday human magnetic sense.

The sharper SAN question from [[gh-a0170z|a0170z]] is:

> Could an existing weak response become behaviorally useful if a person actively moves, compares the result with body position, receives feedback, and trains over time?

## Source genealogy

The first public Git state on June 8, 2022 preserved two separate `a0170z` files: one splay-state research lead and one magnetoreception note. They were merged on June 11. The magnetoreception branch proposed that a perception-action cycle might convert a weak response into a learned discrimination and suggested air-traffic controllers or other sky watchers as a candidate population. The occupational example is an untested prediction, not evidence.

## Evidence classes that must not be merged

### Magnetic material in human brain tissue

Gilder et al. mapped remanent magnetization in 822 specimens from seven postmortem brains and found regional differences ([Gilder et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-29766-z)). This establishes magnetic material in tissue. It does not identify a sensory receptor, transduction pathway, behavior, or percept. Some brain magnetic particles may also have environmental rather than sensory origins.

### Human cryptochrome tested in a fly system

Human CRY2 restored light-dependent magnetic behavior in cryptochrome-deficient fruit flies ([Foley et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1364)). This shows that the human protein can participate in a magnetosensitive fly assay. It does not demonstrate that human retinal cells use CRY2 to detect direction or that people consciously see magnetic fields.

Avian CRY4 is a comparative mechanism, not a human result. Purified European-robin CRY4 showed magnetically sensitive photochemistry in vitro ([Xu et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9)). The experiment did not demonstrate a human CRY4 compass or a literal visible overlay of field lines.

### Human neural response

Wang et al. enrolled 36 volunteers; condition-specific analyses used overlapping subsets, including 29 people for matched inclination active-versus-sham tests and 26 for declination tests. Selected Earth-strength field rotations produced alpha-band event-related desynchronization, and several strong responders reproduced their individual response weeks or months later ([Wang et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019)). Participants did not report conscious field perception. An EEG response supports neural transduction under the tested conditions, not a usable sense.

### Human behavioral reports

Chae et al. studied 34 men in a rotary-chair, two-alternative forced-choice protocol and reported light-, field-, and radiofrequency-dependent orientation effects under restricted fasting and illumination conditions ([Chae et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-12460-6)). The authors inferred a radical-pair mechanism. That differs from the ferromagnetic interpretation proposed by Wang et al.

Oh et al. later reported magnetic-condition effects on probabilistic stone selection in men and women; most participants reported no conscious field sensation ([Oh et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2025.1497021)). The study shares investigators and assumptions with the Chae program and is not an independent replication.

A recent physiological review treats EEG results as substantial evidence for a possible subconscious response while emphasizing that independent replication is required before firm conclusions ([Nordmann et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00032.2025)).

## Established learning principle versus SAN hypothesis

Perceptual learning can improve performance when practice supplies repeatable task information, often with stimulus and task specificity ([Sasaki, Nanez, and Watanabe, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2737)). This established principle does not show that training can create a receptor or make any arbitrary physical field consciously accessible.

SAN's proposed operation is narrower:

```text
existing weak transduction
+ active head and body sampling
+ reliable trial feedback
+ repeated sensorimotor comparison
-> improved held-out discrimination, if the signal is usable
```

Failure to improve under blinded, artifact-controlled training would count against the SAN extension. No evidence recovered here shows that air-traffic controllers already possess enhanced geomagnetic perception.

## The experiment SAN should propose

Use silent, blinded three-axis field rotations and indistinguishable sham trials. Measure head direction, eye state, movement, vestibular and auditory artifacts, temperature, vibration, expectation, EEG, confidence, and forced-choice behavior.

Separate five questions:

1. Does a neural response differ between active field rotations and sham?
2. Does behavior exceed chance on preregistered held-out orientations?
3. Does training improve behavior across sessions and days?
4. Does improvement transfer to new field directions or body orientations?
5. Can an independent laboratory reproduce the effect?

The decisive behavioral quantity is:

```text
performance under real field conditions
minus
performance under indistinguishable sham conditions
```

An EEG difference without above-chance behavior would support neural response, not a usable sense. Above-chance performance without transfer could reflect task-specific learning. Conscious magnetoreception would additionally require reproducible reportable experience linked to real-versus-sham conditions.
