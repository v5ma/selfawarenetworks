An oscillating group of neurons does not represent **only** the current sensory moment — it carries a **temporal window spanning the recent past**, from the last ~10 seconds up to several minutes of experience. This temporal persistence emerges from the group's oscillatory dynamics: outlier neurons that stray in phase are pulled back to alignment by the group, preserving the accumulated pattern rather than erasing it with each new input.

## Why the temporal window matters

Stable representations in the brain are held by approximately **2% of actively firing neurons** at any moment. The other activity is background oscillation. When incoming sensory data arrives, it does not overwrite the current rendering — it modifies **small parts** of the ongoing multimodal rendering, shifting what is already being computed.

This has a practical consequence for neural decoding: looking for a direct neural correlate of "what someone sees right now" is looking for the wrong thing. What you observe is a representation of the current percept *plus* the trailing temporal context — the whole recent episode, not a snapshot.
