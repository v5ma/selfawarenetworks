A **belief**, in SAN vocabulary, is a sequence of [[spatiotemporal neural firing patterns]] that encodes a memorized causal relationship and can be projected forward in time as anticipation or thought.

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s 2013 [[gh-formulation|formulation]]: *"Fear is a story like any belief, a sequence of tempo-spatial patterns that represents a remembered casual relationship now projected into the future as thought."* This is among the earliest recorded articulations of the spatiotemporal-sequence view of mental states in the SAN lineage.

## Core claims

- Emotions and beliefs are not static representations but **temporal sequences** enacted in the neural substrate.
- Fear specifically is a sequence that maps a remembered cause (threat) to a projected future state (danger), activating the [[motor-cortex]] and [[amygdala]] in concert.
- Stories are chains of beliefs: *"A sequence of neural expectations is a belief; a sequence of beliefs is a story."*

## Neural democratic grounding

The mechanism by which beliefs gain or lose force is **neural democratic evidence accumulation**: neurons collect evidence systematically and "surge forth with energy" when a direction becomes clear. This connects to [[napot]]'s threshold dynamics and to [[pattern-neurons]] as the atomic units registering each spatiotemporal coincidence.

See also: Michael Shadlen's [[criterial-causation|criterial causation]] work on how neural populations accumulate evidence to reach decisions (referenced in source).

## Relationship to SAN core theory

This framing anticipates the [[napot]] framework: if a belief is a spatiotemporal sequence, then understanding, learning, and emotional change all reduce to editing, reinforcing, or extinguishing such sequences. Change is not imposed from outside — it is structural re-sequencing within an already-changing neural substrate.
