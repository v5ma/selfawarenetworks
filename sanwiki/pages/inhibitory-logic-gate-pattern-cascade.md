An inhibitory neuron functions as a **logic gate**: it creates a new [[fractal-pattern]] distinct from the baseline firing state of the network. A single inhibition transforms one firing pattern into two — before and after — and that bifurcation ripples through the entire oscillatory network.

## Core Hypothesis

Human brains exhibit a sparsity of sodium channels relative to animal brains. Because sodium influx drives excitatory depolarization, fewer sodium channels proportionally increases the inhibitory-to-excitatory ratio. This heightened inhibition is proposed as a structural basis for the greater complexity of human cognition.

Contrast: the heart is a near-pure excitatory network. It beats in a regular oscillation and never generates new patterns on its own. Introduce even one inhibitory neuron and the surface of possible firing patterns doubles. More inhibitory logic gates → exponential growth in the pattern space accessible to the network.

## Relation to Pattern Cascade

Each inhibition is a branch point. In [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography) terms, each inhibitory event generates a new [[phase-differential-rendering|phase wave differential]] that propagates outward, forcing downstream arrays to compute a new rendering. The resulting cascade drives the richer [[pattern-neurons|pattern firing]] observed in higher cortical networks.

## Thalamic Context

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] revisits the older *thalamus-as-projector-screen* hypothesis here: the thalamus modulates and retransmits signals, acting as an oscillating relay. The entorhinal–thalamic–hypothalamic loop may serve as a convergence site where these cascading inhibitory patterns are assembled into the rendered scene of perception. See [[tonic-phasic-canvas]] for the tonic/phasic role distinction.

## Integration Hubs

The hippocampus, temporoparietal junction (TPJ), and prefrontal cortex (PFC) act as **network endpoints** where many cortical columns converge. They form schemas — linked pattern renderings — rather than performing primary sensory computation. The TPJ is implicated in Graziano's [[attention-schema|Attention Schema]] Theory: conscious [[attention]] correlates with right-TPJ activation; inattention leaves it dark.
