**Cortical column as display** is a historical rendering metaphor. A cortical column or local
laminar circuit can contribute selective, recurrent population activity, but it is not a biological
pixel bank, frame buffer, or screen watched by another system.

## Anatomical and scale boundary

Columnar organization varies by cortical area, species, development, method, and definition.
Columns and minicolumns are not one universal repeated processor. Layer structure, cell types,
inputs, outputs, inhibition, and computations differ across areas. A canonical laminar diagram must
be labeled illustrative rather than universal.

At meso scale, a named local population may:

1. receive thalamic, cortical, local, and modulatory input;
2. transform it through dendritic and recurrent dynamics;
3. maintain selective and context-sensitive population state;
4. influence downstream targets through projection neurons;
5. change with feedback, movement, and learning.

That population state can contribute to a working model without containing a complete local patch
of experience.

## Display metaphor translation

| Historical phrase | Bounded translation |
|---|---|
| Addressable tile | A population with identifiable inputs, outputs, and state variables |
| Pixel bank | Distributed selective activity, not a fixed raster |
| Playback | Reactivation under current context, not replay of a stored picture |
| Composite scene | Coordination across many recurrent systems and body-action loops |
