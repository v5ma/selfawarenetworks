Brain-to-brain EEG frequency synchronization is the phenomenon where two people engaged in conversation show coupled oscillatory activity in the EEG spectrum — their [[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]] frequencies converge, suggesting that communication itself is a form of neural entrainment between separate brains.

Medical imaging studies show that conversational partners shift toward a shared brainwave frequency. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] interprets this as evidence that human minds partially integrate during dialogue, consistent with the [[fractal-modules]] model where the brain is a fractal of interacting modules — regions containing columns containing circuits. Just as distributed octopus limb-brains function as one organism, two humans in communication may transiently couple as an extended distributed cognition system.

The synchronization is not metaphorical: EEG data shows measurable frequency alignment between speakers. The person "on your wavelength" in conversation is literally synchronizing their oscillatory state with yours, enabling the information-sharing that constitutes understanding. This relates to [[oscillatory-synchronization]] as the fundamental mechanism and to [[napot]] as the underlying projection model.

## Proposed Experiment (Micah)

Pre-entrain subjects into contrasting brainwave states (alpha vs. delta) before pairing them for conversation. Measure whether:
- The delta brain pulls the alpha brain down
- The alpha brain pulls the delta brain up
- They equilibrate to a median frequency (theta/beta)
- No stable synchrony emerges

Crossing all pairings across six subjects allows detection of directional dominance vs. averaging dynamics in inter-brain coupling.
