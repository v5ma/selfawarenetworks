In SAN theory, every biological cell — not just neurons — operates as a **chemical neural network**. Cell-surface receptors function as threshold detectors; they activate only when specific coincident signals arrive, implementing [[hebbian-learning]] at the molecular level.

Receptors are [[three-dimensional-protein-structures]] whose geometry encodes learned patterns. Evolution engineers them to detect particular signal combinations via coincident detection — the same Hebbian principle ("cells that fire together wire together") that governs synaptic plasticity, operating one scale down.

## Cell as Clock

Each cell embeds a clock: gene A triggers protein B, which triggers RNA C, which feeds back to modulate gene A. These feedback loops run on cycles ranging from minutes to 24 hours. Chemical gradients — quantities of signaling molecules like retinoic acid — layer additional timing and positional information on top: a developing neuron navigating to its cortical address reads gradient concentration as a *measuring tool*, not a binary switch.

The clock mechanism integrates with [[cellular-oscillating-tomography]]: cells that synchronize their cycles become phase-locked oscillators, which SAN argues is the physical basis for neural-band brain rhythms (1–4 Hz delta, 7–10 Hz alpha, etc.).

## Unified Sensor Array

When cells oscillate in phase across a tissue, the collective becomes a unified [[sensory-input-array]]. A touch signal on one skin point propagates through interconnected cells so the whole body registers the event — not just the brain. This distributed sensing is foundational to [[neo-mind-cycle]]'s BCI vision: link two such arrays and sensory experience becomes shared.
