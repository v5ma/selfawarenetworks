# Conscious-State Perturbation Tomography

## The idea in plain language

A single brain scan is one view of a moving system. A stronger experiment would measure the same
person many times: awake, asleep, performing tasks, recovering, and under separately approved
perturbations such as anesthesia, medication, or stimulation. Each condition reveals different
parts of the system's organization.

The aim is to build a person-specific map of neural state and behavior. The word *tomography* is
being used for reconstruction from multiple views. It does not mean that EEG is a direct CT-like
image of consciousness.

## Why this matters to SAN

One 2020 experiment found that general anesthesia disrupted communication between distal apical
dendrites and the cell bodies of Layer 5 pyramidal neurons. A 2022 study found something that
prevents an easy slogan: anesthesia also increased widespread synchrony in Layer 5 cells.

Together, these results show why "more coherence" or "less coherence" is too simple. We need to
know which cells and compartments are coordinated, in what direction, for how long, and whether
their activity can still influence memory, report, or action.

## Anesthesia And Spatiotemporal Isolation

[Luppi et al. (2026), *Comprehensive profiling of brain dynamics during anesthesia across phylogeny*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02460-4), analyzed over 6,000 time-series features across humans, macaques, marmosets, mice, zebrafish and nematodes. Mammalian recordings used fMRI; zebrafish and nematode recordings used calcium imaging.

Three measurements clarify the reported isolation:

- **Persistence:** local intrinsic timescales shortened, measured from the area under the autocorrelation curve up to its first zero crossing.
- **Coordination:** regional signals showed weaker synchronous cofluctuation, measured by the magnitude of zero-lag correlation.
- **Shared dynamics:** regional feature profiles became less similar, and profile similarity became less coupled to synchrony.

Centromedian thalamic stimulation in anesthetized macaques reversed this profile alongside restored responsiveness; an alternative thalamic stimulation site served as a control. Models changing inhibitory or excitatory synaptic decay times reproduced much of the observed dynamical signature (Figures 4-7).

### Connection To The Original SAN Argument

In [[gh-b0232y|the original SAN anesthesia note]], Micah describes conscious patterns as repeatedly sustained across time and space through feedback. He connects this to the oscillatory component of NAPOT and to a temporally active rendered pattern. The later cellular dialogue also explores how patterned input could change potassium-dependent AP duration and downstream output. Preserving this context matters: the source links cellular transformation, recurrent maintenance and distributed perception.

The new results support the persistence-and-coordination part of that argument. In SAN terms, the relevant question is whether a differentiated pattern can remain available to receiving populations and participate in subsequent updates. [[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|PWD]] retains both excitation and inhibition relative to the tonic reference state. The model's treatment of both synaptic processes offers a useful comparison for that account.

### What These Measurements Resolve

Here, isolation means reduced statistical persistence and coordination at the recorded scales. Directly testing spatial propagation requires perturbing a source and measuring its downstream effects. The paper's synchrony measure is not an oscillatory phase-locking assay, and a synaptic decay constant is distinct from AP duration. The connection to SAN's potassium/AP-duration pathway therefore calls for a joined cellular and network experiment. Behavioral responsiveness is the recovery endpoint measured here; the experiment does not directly read out subjective experience.

## What a strong experiment must separate

- wakefulness from conscious content;
- reportability from experience;
- movement from neural state;
- memory failure from loss of experience;
- drug effects from consciousness-specific effects;
- global synchrony from pathway-specific coupling;
- condition classification from causal explanation.

## Relationship to machine consciousness

The method could help design controls for NP73's machine-consciousness measurement program, but a
human anesthesia signature is not automatically a
universal test for an artificial system.
