# Brain Networking Protocol: A Source-Recovery Question

The protocol question has a longer source history than the term itself. In September 2012,
[[neuron-shape-information-storage|Neuron Shape as Information-Bearing State]] proposed that a
neuron's learned structure acts as a program and that its output expresses a compressed consequence
of that structure. In the first [[gh-nlp1|Neural Lace Podcast]], Micah Blumberg then asked how a
brain interface could discover a biological route's "transmission protocol" and send the difference
between a present pattern and a desired pattern. The 2022 note [[gh-a0027z|a0027z]] returns to the
exact phrase **Brain networking protocol** while exploring attention, tonic and phasic activity,
spatial representation, and a possible brain "filing system."

The durable SAN question is practical and testable:

> What state-dependent signaling rules must a device measure or respect in a named biological route in order to decode, perturb, or restore a defined function?

It is not evidence that neurons run TCP or UDP, that each frequency band is an address channel, that
bursts are universally formatted symbol packets, or that attention supplies a literal routing table.

## The source-recovered operation

The early Neural Lace program combines three ideas:

1. A BCI must record from or stimulate a declared cell population, pathway, or circuit rather than
   "the brain" in general.
2. The intervention must account for the target's current state, input history, timing, and
   physiological response.
3. Success must be measured by a declared receiver effect, behavioral output, sensory consequence,
   or closed-loop correction.

```text
named biological route
-> measure its state, input, and output constraints
-> fit and test a route-specific read/write model
-> perturb or restore a declared function
-> measure receiver, behavior, and safety consequences
```

This is an engineering research program, not a completed neural communications standard.

This sequence protects two facts at once: the early program and difference-update ideas are genuine
SAN ancestors, and the later names must retain their actual dates.

## What the computer-network analogy contributes

The 2017 TCP/UDP comparison asks useful questions: does a message require a receiver's prior state?
Does a second confirming signal matter? Is feedback necessary? Is the signal directed or broad? Is
there correction after an unsuccessful change?

Those questions can organize experiments without treating biological signaling as a literal Internet
stack. The companion page [[brain-tcp-udp-analogy|Brain TCP/UDP Analogy]] and the later
[[alternating-transmission-protocol-cellular-handshakes|Alternating Transmission Protocol]] preserve
the historical analogy and its 2022 cellular extension.

## Relation to later SAN concepts

Later SAN work offers candidate descriptions, not retroactive proof of the 2017 question:

- [[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|Tonic context and PWD]] describe a candidate
  receiver-relative difference against ongoing state.
- [[napot-revision-genealogy|NAPOT]] describes a proposed receive-transform-project architecture
  across declared scales.
- [[cellular-oscillatory-tomography|COT]] asks whether related receive-transform-retain-transmit
  operations apply to non-neural cells.

The historical sequence matters. The 2012 source supplies the morphology-dependent
transform-and-re-express ancestor. The 2017 podcast poses the BCI and differential protocol problem.
The later terms provide candidate mechanisms and experimental variables; they are not silently
backdated into either source.

## A scientifically useful BCI test

For a named target, compare a conventional decoder or stimulation rule with a state-aware model.

```text
baseline: anatomy + task + measured activity + fixed intervention rule
candidate: baseline + current route state + input history + timing + receiver response + closed-loop update
```

The candidate gains support only if it improves held-out decoding, restoration, discrimination,
action, or safety in the declared preparation. A successful decoder does not by itself reveal a
native neural language, reconstruct a complete experience, or establish consciousness.

## Source boundary

`a0027z` also contains speculative proposals about skull focusing, field organization, fixed
tonic/phasic assignments, and large-scale representation. They are retained as historical source
material, but they are not adopted here as established anatomy, physiology, or BCI mechanism.

The April 2017 date belongs to the podcast episode and its public media history. The detailed current
`nlp1` transcript was first Git-fixed in June 2025. An older file with the same filename described a
different guest episode, so filename continuity alone is not evidence continuity. See
[[neural-lace-podcast-source-genealogy|Neural Lace Podcast Source Genealogy]].
