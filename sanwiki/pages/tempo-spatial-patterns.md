## Position

In the [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] / [[oca-overview|OCA]] picture, perceptual content is not a feature vector but a *trajectory* across [[cortical-column|cortical columns]] / [[neural-array|neural arrays]] under the [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|dominant phase-wave differential]] rhythm. A *tempo-spatial pattern* is the SAN unit for that trajectory: the set of phase-and-amplitude states across a population of arrays at successive moments. The transcript at `b0165y--md` line 113 makes the *volumetric* dimension explicit: a single percept (broccoli, an apple, a face) is "things from certain angles, things for certain angles over time" — i.e., it has spatial extent, angular extent (what the [[3d-semantic-segmentation|3D semantic segmentation]] page calls *view-conditioned* representation), and temporal extent. The implication for [[engram|engrams]]: an [[engram]] is not a static set of synaptic weights but a *replayable tempo-spatial pattern* — which, given [[memories-as-attractors|memories-as-attractors]] and [[ltp-tempo-spatial-rhythmic-memory|tempo-spatial rhythmic memory]], is exactly the SAN identification.

## Post-SAN — bridges to mainstream literature

The phrase "spatiotemporal pattern" is well-established in mainstream [[computational-neuroscience|computational neuroscience]]; SAN's specific gloss adds two things on top. (1) The pattern is *phase-structured*: not just a set of firing-rate intensities at successive moments but a [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] structure under the [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|DPWDR]] of the moment, so two patterns with identical firing rates but different phase relations are *different content*. (2) The pattern is *volumetric and angle-conditioned*: per `b0165y--md`, the tempo-spatial pattern carries view-conditioned content, which puts it adjacent to [[3d-semantic-segmentation|3D semantic segmentation]] but at a different substrate level (column-population dynamics, not pixel-level segmentation). Where the connectionist literature usually represents temporal patterns with RNN hidden states or transformer keys, SAN claims that a *physical* tempo-spatial pattern is the substrate, and that the connectionist abstractions are coarse-grained projections of that substrate.
