**[[quantum-super-time-position|Quantum SuperTimePosition]]** (QSTP) is a deterministic reinterpretation of quantum mechanics proposed by [[gh-resnote04|Micah Blumberg]] in which apparent quantum randomness arises from *temporal undersampling* - our observational frame (a slow "gear") sampling a much faster deterministic particle cycle (a fast "gear") only at discrete intervals.

## Core claim

Quantum particles do not genuinely "roll dice." Each particle follows a rapid, deterministic [[phase-attractor]] cycle evolving faster than any human-scale instrument can resolve. What we record as a probabilistic outcome is simply a snapshot of this hidden cycle at the moment our measurement gear meshes with the particle's internal gear.

## Entanglement as phase-lock

[[quantum-entanglement]] is reinterpreted as *phase-lock synchronization* between two particles' deterministic cycles. Once A and B are entangled, their internal clocks run in a fixed phase relationship. Measuring A reveals which phase of its cycle aligns with our time frame; because B's cycle is phase-locked to A's, every subsequent measurement of B will yield the correlated (e.g. opposite spin) result — regardless of the elapsed time between measurements.

This removes the need for "spooky action at a distance": no signal travels between A and B. The correlation was encoded in their shared phase-lock from the start.

## Clock-gear analogy

[[gh-resnote04|Micah Blumberg]]'s central metaphor: our observational frame is a **large gear**, the particle's internal phase cycle is a **small gear**. The small gear completes many revolutions for each tooth of the large gear. At each mesh point (measurement event), the large gear reads a deterministic output of the small gear. Apparent randomness is a property of the sampling relationship, not of the small gear itself.

Different particles have different phase relationships to our time frame, explaining why probability theory is useful for predicting individual outcomes even within a deterministic framework.

## Relation to Einstein's intuition

QSTP vindicates Einstein's remark that "God does not play dice" by locating the source of quantum randomness not in fundamental indeterminism but in our limited observational bandwidth. [[bell-inequality]] violations are preserved because we aggregate snapshots from a hidden deterministic cycle — the statistics still match quantum predictions.
