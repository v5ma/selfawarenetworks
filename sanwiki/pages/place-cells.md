# Place Cells

> **Evidence boundary:** Place cells are neurons in the hippocampal formation whose activity can relate to an animal's location, context, task, goals, and behavior. The broader SAN proposal is not that literal hippocampal place cells occur in every cortical column. It is that **event-relative neural coding** may be a reusable functional motif: a neuron or population can become selective for where, when, or under what task-defined conditions a consequential event occurs.

## Established physiology

A **place cell** fires more strongly when an animal occupies one or more regions of an environment, called place fields. A field is a measured relation between neural activity and sampled position. It is not a picture stored inside one neuron, and a cell need not have exactly one permanent field.

Place-cell responses can depend on sensory cues, self-motion, environmental geometry, context, task demands, goals, experience, and network state. Cells can remap when an environment or task changes. In sufficiently large environments, one cell can express multiple fields.

Common measurements include:

- **Rate map:** firing rate as a function of sampled position.
- **Place field:** a region in which firing exceeds a declared criterion.
- **Remapping:** a change in which cells fire or in their firing rates across environments or contexts.
- **Phase precession:** in some tasks and cells, spikes occur at progressively earlier phases of a measured theta rhythm as the animal crosses a field.
- **Replay or sequence activity:** population patterns can recur during behavior, rest, sleep, or planning.

These are related but distinct observations. A place field is not a traveling image, phase precession is not by itself a phase-wave differential, and replay is not generated by one place cell.

## Plasticity can create and move fields

In rodent hippocampal CA1, dendritic calcium plateau potentials can rapidly create new place fields. Behavioral-timescale synaptic plasticity (BTSP) can also move an existing field by potentiating some inputs and depressing others according to their timing and prior synaptic weight.

This is important for SAN because it supplies an experimentally measured chain:

**distributed input history -> dendritic plateau event -> bidirectional synaptic change -> altered future population representation**

The evidence does **not** establish that every place field begins with one ordinary somatic spike, that every later encounter must produce a burst, or that BTSP is a universal learning rule throughout cortex. Plateau potentials, complex spikes, ordinary action potentials, bursts, and hippocampal sharp-wave ripples are different events at different scales.

## Beyond literal physical location

The hippocampal-entorhinal system also represents variables that are not reducible to the animal's current position:

- During a task in which rats manipulated sound along a continuous frequency axis, hippocampal and entorhinal neurons formed firing fields at particular sound frequencies. The participating populations overlapped with known spatial cell types.
- Hippocampal time cells can become active at successive moments in a remembered interval and can change their temporal fields when the interval changes.
- Hippocampal ensembles can encode order in non-spatial event sequences, including theta-structured reactivation and phase progression.
- Some CA1 populations distinguish transferable event units, such as lap number, separately from spatial position.

These results support a bounded generalization: hippocampal circuits can organize task-relevant dimensions, times, and events using population mechanisms that resemble spatial mapping. They do not prove that all cortex uses place-cell anatomy or that every non-spatial representation is a cognitive map.

## What the SAN sources were trying to preserve

The dated SAN record develops a more specific idea than the earlier Wiki rewrite retained:

1. **June 2022 - a question about generality.** Micah asked whether grid-cell and place-cell functions express a more general reference-frame dynamic and whether a single cell could combine parts of those operations.
2. **June 2022 - functional isomorphism.** SAN proposed that cortical populations might perform an operation functionally similar to hippocampal-entorhinal coordination: a relatively persistent context constrains faster-changing, task-relevant samples.
3. **September 2022 - plastic event update.** SAN connected dendritic plateau events and bidirectional BTSP to rapid revision of an existing representation.
4. **September 2022 - local event cells.** Micah proposed the term **local spacetime event cell**, or **local event cell**, for a broader functional motif that detects the recurrence of a consequential relation and helps reinstate a learned population response.
5. **September 2022 - memory-prediction-rendering.** The proposal joined event recurrence, dendritic integration, learned readiness, population projection, and eventual action into a receive-update-project cycle.

The source sometimes expressed that hypothesis as "place cells outside the hippocampus." Read literally, that would assign a hippocampal cell identity to unrelated cortical neurons. The source-faithful recovery is functional rather than anatomical:

> **SAN local-event hypothesis:** Some neurons and populations outside the hippocampus may acquire firing fields over task-defined event spaces. Their responses may depend on a learned conjunction of context, input, relative timing, and recurrent state, and their output may help reinstate a distributed memory-prediction that changes later perception or action.

## Anatomy recovered without losing the operation

The original source also used "post-somatic" language that sometimes treated the apical dendrite like an output or exit terminal. A medically accurate version preserves the proposed comparison while assigning each operation to the correct structure:

- Basal and apical dendrites are both **input and integration compartments** with different connectivity and biophysics; neither is an axonal exit terminal.
- A somatic action potential can backpropagate into dendrites, but that does not turn the apical dendrite into an axon.
- Apical dendritic calcium events can interact with other dendritic and somatic inputs to alter burst probability and plasticity in particular pyramidal-cell types and circuits.
- The axon and its presynaptic terminals carry the neuron's principal projected output to downstream receivers.
- A sharp-wave ripple is a coordinated hippocampal population event, not the inevitable product of one cortical neuron's burst.

The recoverable SAN operation is therefore:

**segregated dendritic evidence and context -> nonlinear cellular integration -> spike or burst output -> distributed receiver update**

Whether this operation implements the proposed local-event code outside the hippocampus remains an experimental question.

## Micro, meso, and macro interpretations

### Micro: candidate event-sensitive cell

A single neuron can integrate many synaptic inputs, undergo dendritic nonlinearities, change synaptic weights, and alter its spike output. A candidate local-event cell would be defined by a reproducible firing relation to a declared event variable, not by visual resemblance to a hippocampal place cell.

### Meso: event-relative population

A local circuit can distribute an event across many partially selective cells. Inhibition, recurrence, oscillatory timing, and plasticity can alter which assembly is recruited. No single bright point contains the event.

### Macro: nested context and action

Hippocampal, entorhinal, cortical, thalamic, subcortical, and bodily systems can jointly supply spatial, temporal, sensory, motivational, and action-related context. SAN proposes that these nested relations contribute to a distributed observer-action cycle. The hippocampus may supply important relational context without being a master viewer or the sole reference frame for the brain.

## Relation to phase-wave differentials

Phase precession establishes that spike timing relative to a measured oscillation can vary systematically across a place field. SAN uses it as a biological precedent for receiver-relative temporal structure, not as proof of PWD.

PWD makes a stronger claim: a receiver interprets a multidimensional departure from its expected tonic state, potentially involving timing, phase, frequency, amplitude, duration, excitation, inhibition, and transmitted quantity. To connect place-cell timing to PWD, an experiment must show that the fuller departure predicts a receiver's subsequent state or behavior better than position, firing rate, phase, or prediction error alone.

## Discriminating tests

The local-event hypothesis would gain support if:

1. Identified non-hippocampal populations form reproducible firing fields over a well-defined event space after controlling for sensory input, movement, reward, arousal, and elapsed time.
2. The same cells preserve a relational code across changed surface features or transfer learned event structure to a new setting.
3. Dendritic plateaus or other declared nonlinear events causally create, move, or erase those fields.
4. Receiver-relative spike timing adds predictive information beyond firing rate and task covariates.
5. Perturbing the candidate population changes downstream reinstatement, choice, or motor preparation in the predicted direction.
6. The effect appears in some declared circuits without requiring one universal cortical-column algorithm.

The proposal would be weakened if apparent event fields disappear after controlling for ordinary sensory, motor, reward, and time variables; if timing adds no receiver-relevant information; or if dendritic perturbation does not alter the learned relation.
