This SAN plate maps neural oscillation synchrony deviation as a conscious-observation mechanism: a tonic 20 Hz neural array is perturbed by a local stimulus, a tiny millisecond-scale phase shift appears at 19.997 Hz, and the larger oscillator network detects that synchrony break as an event.

**Neural oscillation synchrony deviation** is the SAN framework's mechanism for conscious observation: when individual neurons deviate from their group oscillation by a few milliseconds, the entire oscillator network registers this deviation as meaningful activity — what we experience as a feeling or perception.

## The television cube analogy

The brain functions as a multi-layered three-dimensional array of "pixels" (neurons) that learn firing patterns and build associations across spatial and temporal dimensions. This maps directly to [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-theory]] (NAPOT): the brain is not a sequential processor but a volumetric array where patterns of activation project across the whole.

## Synchrony and deviation

Large groups of neurons oscillate together at a baseline frequency (e.g., 20 Hz). When a significant stimulus occurs — "a red t-shirt" — some neurons desynchronize from the group, producing a minute frequency shift (e.g., 20 Hz → 19.997 Hz). The collective network detects this shift as an event. The detection of the deviation *is* the conscious observation.

This model predicts:
- Consciousness is not in any single neuron but in the network-level detection of synchrony breaks.
- Millisecond-scale timing is fundamental — not an implementation detail.
- [[gamma-oscillations]] and [[theta-oscillations]] at different scales serve as the carrier frequencies that make deviation detectable.

## Implications for SAN

This mechanism is the core of [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-theory|NAPOT]]: the array "observes itself" by detecting when part of it falls out of phase. [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-awareness emerges from recursive oscillation monitoring — the network watching its own frequency landscape.
