# Dendritic Architecture

**Dendritic architecture** is the geometry and compartment organization of a neuron's dendritic arbor. It includes branch number and order, length, diameter, taper, orientation, spines, synapse placement, and the spatial distribution of receptors and ion channels. Architecture constrains which signals can interact, how they are filtered, and where local regenerative events can occur.

## Structure shapes integration

A dendritic tree is neither a passive wiring diagram nor a fixed collection of logic gates. Cable properties cause voltage to attenuate with distance and time. Branch points, membrane conductances, inhibition, and synapse placement can make integration sublinear, approximately linear, or supralinear under different conditions.

In rat neocortical pyramidal neurons, [Polsky, Mel, and Schiller (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1253) found that nearby inputs on the same thin basal or oblique branch summed sigmoidally, whereas more widely separated inputs or inputs on different branches summed approximately linearly. This supports branch-level computational subunits in that preparation, not a universal Boolean rule for all dendrites.

## Plasticity and memory

Experience can alter synaptic strength, receptor trafficking, spine morphology, inhibition, intrinsic excitability, and sometimes branch structure. These changes are candidate components of memory storage and retrieval. The safe claim is that dendritic architecture participates in the physical substrate of learned function.

The stronger statement that the branching pattern **is** a neuron's complete long-term memory is not established. Memories depend on distributed changes across cells and circuits, and the same morphology can support different activity states. A dendritic subtree should not be labeled a semantic "representation token" without a task, recording, and causal manipulation that demonstrate that mapping.

[Matsuzaki et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02617) showed input-specific enlargement and potentiation at stimulated hippocampal CA1 spines after glutamate uncaging. The result links local structural change with synaptic plasticity; it does not show that arbor geometry alone stores a complete memory.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can describe architecture as a learned, high-dimensional constraint on cellular reception and transformation. This is compatible with [[criterial-causation]] if "criterion" means a measurable sensitivity produced by synapses, channels, inhibition, and morphology together. It should not be reduced to one anatomical feature.

At the NAPOT scale, dendritic architecture supplies many local receiving and transforming compartments. Tomographic gathering and redistribution, however, are population and circuit operations. One selected subtree does not itself become a cortical rendering or a NAPOT readout.
