Pattern competition is the mechanism by which one neural representation wins access to conscious awareness over others competing simultaneously. In SAN theory, multiple oscillatory patterns across cortical columns and neural arrays are in constant competition; the pattern that achieves the broadest synchronization and highest [[neural-magnification]] dominates the foreground of experience.

This contrasts with modular theories (e.g., Graziano's [[attention-schema|Attention Schema]] Theory) that assign consciousness to a dedicated region like the temporoparietal junction (TPJ). SAN treats the TPJ not as a consciousness "switch" but as a convergence hub — a peak in the cortical hierarchy where competing observers and attentional signals collide, with the strongest synchronized coalition winning.

## Distributed vs. Centralized Control

The competition is distributed: each cortical column and neural array acts as a local oscillator and local observer. There is no central arbiter. Consciousness emerges from the winning coalition of synchronized oscillators, implementing what SAN terms the [[observer-mechanism]] at the collective level.

## Attention at Every Level

Critically, SAN proposes that [[attention]] is not exclusive to higher cortical hierarchies — it operates at every neural level. Each oscillating unit allocates its phase relationship toward the strongest incoming drive. Pattern competition is thus the aggregate result of [[attention]] happening simultaneously at micro (synaptic), meso (columnar), and macro (whole-brain) scales — see [[multi-scale-neural-processing]].

## Anesthesia as Competition Disruption

Anesthesia disrupts consciousness primarily by impairing [[pyramidal-cell|pyramidal cell]] function, severing the long-range inter-regional connections that allow pattern coalitions to form. Without these connections, local patterns cannot coordinate into a dominant coalition, and the competition produces no winner — no conscious foreground.

## Self-Representation as Learned Pattern

A consequence of the competition model: the [[gh-a0206z|self]]-concept is itself a learned pattern that enters and wins competition like any other. [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-representation is acquired through [[attention]] and curiosity (parallel to visual or linguistic learning), not hard-wired. Evidence: patients regaining eyesight must relearn visual interpretation through sustained [[attention]], indicating perceptual patterns are built rather than innate.
