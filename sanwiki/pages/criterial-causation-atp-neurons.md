Neurons recognize information as **coincidences in the timed firing** of other neurons — this is [[criterial-causation]]. The "criteria" a neuron uses to fire is not fixed: [[neurotransmitter]] transmission resets the coincidence-detection threshold of downstream neurons, while [[dendritic-spines]] (rapidly changing structures) further modulate the firing criteria in real time.

Crucially, the criteria are metabolically grounded. Dendritic spines connect to [[mitochondria]] inside neurons; mitochondria produce [[atp]] (adenosine triphosphate) through aerobic metabolism and even import [[atp]] from neighboring [[glial-cells]]. [[atp]] availability directly shapes the energetic substrate of criterial evaluation — less [[atp]] means degraded criteria sensitivity, which manifests as reduced executive function and lower willingness to initiate action.

## Key claims (from 2013 note)

- Willpower and executive function are downstream of [[atp]] production, not purely of will or habit.
- Dietary inputs (grains, processed sugars) that impair mitochondrial metabolism reduce [[atp]] and thus degrade the criteria available for deliberation.
- Having more [[atp]] changes *some* decision criteria (e.g., threshold for initiating physical activity) but does not determine behavior — other criteria still apply.

## SAN connection

This early note anticipates the SAN position that [[self-aware-networks]] operate through nested criterial processes: low-level metabolic criteria ([[atp]] thresholds) feed into higher-level coincidence-detection criteria in neural assemblies. [[napot]] theory later formalizes how neural arrays fire in timed patterns whose coincidence is the substrate of perception and action selection.
