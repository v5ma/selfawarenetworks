A human being in a social network is a functional fractal of a neuron in a neural network: both resonate with, transmit, receive, and store patterns.

## Core claim

The same functional operations that define a neuron — pattern reception, pattern transmission, pattern storage, pattern replay — are exhibited by a person in their social relationships. This isomorphism holds across scales: [[synapse-as-quantum-event]] → neuron → [[cortical-column]] → [[neuro-oscillator]] → whole brain → person in social context.

Just as synaptic connections grow or weaken based on activity, social connections strengthen or break. High-level configuration structures (dipole patterns visible in EEG across alpha/theta/delta/gamma bands) exert top-down influence on lower-level assemblies — and the reverse: small-scale perturbations can tip an entire assembly via [[tonic-oscillation-bridging]].

## Fractal hierarchy

- Synapse → neuron: chemical signaling, [[gap-junction]] → [[chemical-synapse]] transition
- Neuron → cortical column: inhibitory sculpting of [[sensory-pattern-distinction]]
- Cortical column → neuro-oscillator: excitatory cascades for coordinated motor function via [[long-term-depression]]
- Person → social network: oscillator coupling, with breaks and unions as transformational state changes

## Key distinction: sensory vs motor function

Inhibitory neurons subdivide patterns for sensory distinction; excitatory oscillatory networks amplify small events into coordinated motor output. This asymmetry appears at every fractal level.
