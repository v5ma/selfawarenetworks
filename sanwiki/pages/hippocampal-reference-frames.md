# Hippocampal Reference Frames

> **Evidence boundary:** Hippocampal-entorhinal circuits support spatial, contextual, episodic, and relational processing. The hippocampus proper is three-layer allocortex, not a six-layer neocortical column. Older SAN language calling it "cortical column #1" is therefore a functional and hierarchical analogy, not an anatomical identity claim. The analogy can be tested operation by operation without discarding the source idea.

The hippocampal-entorhinal system combines self-motion, landmarks, sensory context, goals, memory, and internal state. Its representations are produced by interacting populations rather than by one cell class or one central observer.

## What the cell classes contribute

- **Place cells** can fire in one or more locations and remap across contexts, tasks, and experience.
- **Grid cells** can express periodic spatial firing fields with module-dependent scale and orientation.
- **Head-direction cells** relate firing to facing direction.
- **Boundary and object-vector cells** relate firing to environmental borders or objects.
- **Mixed and conjunctive responses** can combine position, direction, speed, goals, objects, and task state.

These signals interact through reciprocal hippocampal, entorhinal, subicular, septal, thalamic, retrosplenial, parietal, prefrontal, and other cortical pathways. Place cells are not simply fixed anchors, grid cells are not simply a complete metric, and no single cell class has been shown to contain the whole reference frame.

## Reference frames and relational memory

Spatial representations can be egocentric, allocentric, object-centered, route-centered, landmark-centered, or task-defined. Experiments now show bidirectional hippocampal-parietal coordination during transformations among map-like, route-centered, and body-centered variables. Hippocampal-entorhinal activity also supports relationships that are not literally spatial, including learned graph structure and associations among events.

This evidence supports a distributed relational system. It does not prove that every abstract concept is encoded by a grid cell, that one universal coordinate system governs the brain, or that "where" and "when" are supplied by one cell type.

## A functional cortical-column analogy

The phrase **"cortical column #1"** should be read at the level of operations:

- convergent input is transformed by recurrent excitation and inhibition;
- current input is compared with stored or expected relations;
- sparse and conjunctive activity can separate, complete, retrieve, or update patterns;
- transformed output changes other cortical and subcortical receivers;
- oscillatory timing can coordinate selected parts of this exchange.

Comparative neuroanatomy identifies recurrent excitation, feedforward and lateral inhibition, and pyramidal-cell integration as members of a broader cortical microcircuit family shared across three-layer and six-layer cortices. That supports a bounded functional comparison. It does not make hippocampal CA fields a neocortical minicolumn, erase their different lamination, or establish one universal cortical algorithm.

The historical genealogy also needs precision. Hawkins and Blakeslee's *On Intelligence* described the hippocampus near the apex of a cortical processing hierarchy. Micah identifies his reading of a 2005 edition as the source encounter behind the later analogy. **"Cortical column #1" is Micah's own later formulation**, publicly fixed in 2022; it should not be attributed verbatim to Hawkins. Micah's 2022 notes also cite Buzsaki's 2006 work and later Hawkins reference-frame theories as additional influences.

## Recovering the intended SAN operations

Earlier corrections preserved anatomical caution but removed five source-level questions or proposals:

1. **Single-cell composition question:** A June 2022 note asked whether one cell could combine functions analogous to the relations supplied by grid and place populations. This was a research question, not evidence that one generic neuron directly performs all grid, place, boundary, direction, object, and memory functions. A defensible version asks whether identified neurons show conjunctive, receiver-relevant coding sufficient for a bounded transformation.
2. **Relational scaffold plus distributed content:** An August 2022 note proposed that hippocampal-entorhinal activity contributes volumetric or relational organization while distributed cortical populations contribute object and feature contents. Related models independently separate transferable structure from sensory content. SAN adds recurrent rendering and receiver-relative timing; those additions remain hypotheses.
3. **Nested reference frames:** September 2022 notes proposed local event relations inside cortical populations and a larger hippocampal frame with which those local states interact. The medically defensible claim is a hierarchy of interacting population reference frames, not a miniature coordinate grid inside every neuron.
4. **Phase-coordination prediction:** June 2022 notes proposed that increased attention to an object would change coordination among the relevant cortical population, medial-temporal system, and parietal-temporal systems. Hippocampal-neocortical theta coordination and cross-frequency coupling are measurable, but generic synchrony does not prove awareness or SAN. The SAN test must identify the represented variable, sender, receiver, direction, phase relation, and behavioral consequence.
5. **From reference to action:** The recovered proposal is not a passive map. Relational states are repeatedly converted into memory retrieval, prediction, choice, movement, and sensory updating. The coordinated network performs observer-action; nothing inside the hippocampus watches a rendered scene.

Together these propositions make the hippocampal-entorhinal system a meso- and macro-scale example of the SAN receive-transform-project-update motif. They do not localize consciousness there or reduce NAPOT to one structure.

## Tonic context and phasic change

SAN proposes that relatively persistent population context can act as a receiver-relative tonic reference while faster departures supply differentiated updates. Applied here, the claim is not that theta is always the canvas or gamma is always the ink. It is that identified receivers may use an ongoing state as context for event-specific departures, with the relevant frequency, phase, amplitude, cell class, and direction determined empirically.

The stronger hypothesis is that phase-wave differentials help select which relational states alter downstream cortical and motor processing. Existing oscillatory findings provide measurable components, not proof of the complete SAN interpretation.

## Correction record

This page retains the operations behind earlier compressed wording:

- **"Place cells are anchors and grid cells are the metric"** is an oversimplified division of labor. The retained idea is complementary and conjunctive population coding.
- **"Can one cell do both?"** is preserved as a bounded conjunctive-coding question, not converted into a claim that one generic neuron is a complete map.
- **"The hippocampus is cortical column #1"** is preserved as Micah's functional and hierarchical analogy. It is not literal six-layer anatomy and is not attributed as a Hawkins quotation.
- **"Each cortical column maintains a reference frame"** remains a theory-level extension associated with Hawkins and SAN, not established universal circuitry.
- **"Frames are phase-locked"** becomes a receiver-specific coordination hypothesis. Shared frequency or coherence alone is not enough; information, direction, task dependence, and causal effect must be measured.
- **"Base rendering of consciousness"** is retained as a historical localization question but rejected as a hidden-viewer model. SAN's mature account is distributed across recurrent perception, memory, bodily, and action systems.

## Discriminating tests

A source-faithful experiment should separate four models:

1. firing-rate geometry without a privileged phase relation;
2. relational structure separated from item or sensory content;
3. network-level coordination among hippocampal, parietal, entorhinal, and cortical populations;
4. SAN's stronger receiver-relative phase-wave differential account.

Record identified populations while an animal or person learns the same relational structure with different sensory contents, or the same contents under different reference frames. Test whether hippocampal-entorhinal structure generalizes, whether cortical content can be reinstated from partial cues, and whether direction-specific perturbation disrupts transformation into memory-guided action. The SAN account gains support only if a specified phase relation or multidimensional differential predicts the receiver's next state and behavior beyond firing rate, power, connectivity, and task variables alone.
