# SAOv9: Historical Self-Aware Networks Overview

SAOv9 is a large planning and source-discovery artifact for the Self-Aware Networks book program.
Its repository Markdown and 67-page PDF were publicly Git-fixed on January 4, 2024. The reconciled
outline contains fourteen cycles, five prompt families per cycle, and ten prompts per family: 700
planning prompts in total.

SAOv9 is historically important because it shows the intended breadth of the program. It is not the
current book manuscript, a list of 700 accepted claims, or independent evidence for SAN.

## Three historical parts

| Part | Cycles | Intended scope |
|---|---:|---|
| I. Neo Mind Cycle and brain-computer interface evolution | C1-C3 | Neurofeedback and BCI origins, Neural Lace and community history, read-write interfaces, neural rendering questions, fractal and cross-scale organization, and the early network-protocol route toward NAPOT. |
| II. Neurophysics | C4-C11 | Cellular recognition, memory, sensory systems, oscillatory coordination, attention, perception-consideration-action, emotion, language, and the proposal that distributed neural activity constructs an actionable world model. |
| III. Artificial Neurology | C12-C14 | Richer artificial neural units, machine perception and agency, choice and social systems, artificial consciousness questions, safety, and long-range applications. |

## What SAOv9 preserves

SAOv9 connects several lines that later became clearer Encyclopedia routes:

```text
BCI read-write problem
-> brain network protocol question
-> cellular and dendritic pattern recognition
-> tonic context and phasic differences
-> recurrent neural reconstruction and memory
-> attention, consideration, inhibition, and action
-> artificial-neurology translation and tests
```

The outline also preserves exploratory branches involving holography, fractals, quantum models,
cosmology, and future technology. Their presence records the research program's scope. It does not
make those branches established neuroscience or necessary premises of NAPOT.

## Scientific and authorship boundary

SAOv9 mixes Micah Blumberg's outline language and source questions with explicitly labeled AI-made
summaries and planning prompts. Each prompt therefore requires source recovery before it is treated
as an author claim, a scientific claim, or a current Book 2 destination.

Several compressed summaries also require correction by context:

- SAN does not replace firing rate with phase. It proposes a richer receiver-relative state that can include rate, timing, phase, duration, waveform, transmitted quantity, and network context.
- Historical hologram language is an analogy and research question. [[napot-tomography-not-holography|NAPOT is not a claim that established neuroscience has found a literal brain hologram]].
- Three-dimensional semantic segmentation is an engineering comparison for object and scene organization, not evidence that cortex runs a standard computer-vision segmentation algorithm.
- QGTCD belongs to a later physics branch and is not required to explain the bounded neuroscience mechanism.
- A proposed artificial implementation or successful simulation would not by itself prove phenomenal consciousness.

## Current relationship to the book

The current 2026 research-edition architecture has thirty-one chapters in six parts. It retains
SAOv9's questions while separating sensory systems, cellular mechanisms, memory, reconstruction,
action, artificial neurology, and evidence more carefully. The reorganization is a later editorial
development and must not be backdated into the 2024 artifact.
