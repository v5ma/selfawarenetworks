Near-threshold saddle-point dynamics is the condition in which neural populations hover in metastable configurations — poised at bifurcation points between [[attractor]] states — so that small information inputs (not brute energy) can decisively shift activity patterns.

## The core observation

Neurons do not simply fire when input exceeds a fixed threshold. Many cortical cells are maintained in a "near-threshold hold" where they sit at a saddle point: a dynamical configuration where the system can fall into one of several stable attractors depending on tiny perturbations. The brain maintains large populations in this state simultaneously, giving the entire network extreme sensitivity to patterned input.

## Why this matters for SAN

This is the physical substrate that allows [[criterial-causation]] to work. [[non-physical-information|Non-physical information]] (coincidence patterns with no mass or energy) can flip a near-threshold neuron into firing precisely because the cell is already at the saddle point. Without criticality, triggering a cell would require energetically significant inputs — information patterns would be insufficient. With criticality, pattern recognition IS sufficient.

The principle generalizes: neural criticality is the mechanism by which [[oscillating-functional-fractal-isomorphism|fractal-scale]] oscillatory patterns propagate decisions through cortical hierarchy without brute force amplification at each stage.

## Neural noise and the saddle

Brain noise (variable tonic frequency) is often treated as interference. The SAN interpretation inverts this: noise fluctuations keep populations near the saddle by providing constant low-level perturbation that prevents premature commitment to one [[attractor]]. Only meaningful coincidence patterns (information) produce decisive [[attractor]] transitions. The vowel-analogy from the source: meaning emerges from *changes in the noise pattern*, not from the average signal.

## Connection to dendritic integration

[[dendritic-leaky-membrane-coincidence-filter]] implements saddle-point sensitivity at the single-neuron level. Dendritic spines are arranged so that spatially and temporally coincident inputs arrive simultaneously — only if enough "water" accumulates before leakage can the dendritic spike initiate. This is the biophysical version of "waiting at the saddle until the right pattern arrives."
