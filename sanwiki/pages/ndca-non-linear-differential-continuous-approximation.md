**Non-linear Differential Continuous Approximation (NDCA)** is SAN's proposed continuity framework
for asking how changing neural populations could maintain action-relevant distinctions across
nonlinear, noisy, and delayed sensory and body-state signals. It is not an established neuroscience
term, a demonstrated semantic decoder, or a completed mathematical operator. See [[ndca]] for the
canonical scientific boundary.

## Scientific and scale boundary

Established findings include nonlinear dendritic integration, recurrent circuit dynamics, and
population trajectories that can be represented in state spaces. SAN interprets those components as
possible contributors to NDCA. It does not follow that cortical columns compute literal attractor
cross-sections, that a manifold is itself a percept, or that one mechanism runs identically at cell,
column, whole-brain, and body scales.

Micro, meso, and macro claims must name different biological denominators. A testable NDCA account
must define its state variables, transform, measurement, receiver, cross-scale mapping, alternatives,
and failure conditions.

## Core Mechanism

Within SAN, NDCA names a candidate sequence:

1. Partial signals alter cells and local populations.
2. Nonlinear recurrent dynamics transform the current state.
3. The trajectory preserves or loses distinctions over time.
4. Downstream receivers use the altered state in prediction and action.

State-space manifolds are useful descriptions of population data, but the claim that the brain
"reads" meaning directly from a manifold is interpretive unless a biological readout and causal test
are specified. Coincidence detection and predictive coding should remain distinct until that mapping
is demonstrated.

## Relation to NAPOT

SAN assigns NAPOT to the proposed receive-integrate-redistribute cycle and NDCA to the continuity
problem within and across updates. The relationship is architectural, not proof that every traveling
wave, tonic state, or phase difference implements either construct. Each diagram and experiment must
declare whether it addresses a cell, local circuit, or distributed brain-body network.

## Relation to QGTCD

QGTCD is a separate SAN/SIT physics proposal. No biological NDCA claim is established merely by
placing it above QGTCD or by using the phrase "oscillatory physics." Any bridge between the programs
requires its own variables, derivation, and evidence.
