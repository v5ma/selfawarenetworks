# Agentic Brain

The **Agentic Brain** is a SAN systems model in which many scale-specific biological
components contribute conditionally to perception, learning, choice, and action. It
does not require a single executive observer, but it also does not make every protein,
cell, column, or rhythm into a miniature person.

> **Scientific boundary:** On this page, *agent* is a declared modeling role. It means that a bounded system has a state, receives inputs, undergoes conditional transitions, produces outputs, and can sometimes alter its later response. The word alone does not establish deliberation, learning, a private viewpoint, consciousness, or organism-level goals.

## The recovered SAN operation

The 2022 sources do not begin with the later phrase "autonomous agents at every scale."
They begin with a more specific operation:

**local state and history plus current input -> scale-specific threshold, competition, or
state transition -> emitted, maintained, or suppressed output -> receiver and circuit
consequences -> changed local, network, bodily, or environmental state -> feedback**

This operation can recur at several scales, but the variables and mechanisms change at
each scale. Functional similarity does not establish identical anatomy, one universal
algorithm, or one shared physical signal grammar.

## What agency means at each scale

- **Molecular and subcellular:** Receptors, ion channels, kinases, cytoskeletal systems,
  and gene-regulatory networks undergo state-dependent transitions. Feedback,
  bistability, hysteresis, and recent stimulation can change later responses. A protein
  conformation is not by itself a decision, memory, or point of view.
- **Cellular:** A neuron integrates compartment-specific synaptic and intrinsic state
  into dendritic events, somatic or axon-initial-segment output, plasticity, and
  transmitter release. Glia and immune cells contribute through their own cell types,
  receptors, signaling pathways, and timescales. They do not all exchange one
  frequency-phase code.
- **Circuit and population:** Candidate representations or actions can coexist,
  compete, recruit inhibition, and be biased by sensory evidence, memory, value,
  context, basal-ganglia-thalamic loops, and other control systems. A cortical column
  is not a universally defined autonomous executive.
- **Organism and environment:** Only the embodied system produces an organism-level
  choice or action. Descending motor and autonomic pathways, muscles, biomechanics,
  the environment, and sensory return complete the loop.

The same component can be modeled differently depending on the question. Calling an
ion channel an agent may help analyze a molecular state transition; it does not give
the channel ownership of the organism's goal.

## Distributed choice without a hidden executive

A June 2022 SAN source proposes two interacting directions:

1. bottom-up receptor, cellular, and bodily thresholds constrain which transitions are
   available;
2. top-down learned context and ongoing population state bias which candidate routes
   are recruited.

The source equated receptor configuration too directly with LTP. The recoverable claim
is narrower: receptor composition, synaptic efficacy, dendritic structure, intrinsic
conductances, inhibition, and recent activity can all alter a cell's later input-output
relation. Organism-level choice emerges from interactions among many such stateful
components and named control circuits; it is not literally decided by every cell.

Later 2022 sources add array-to-array reception, learned receiver tuning, and competition
among task-relevant cell assemblies. Together they support a distributed criteria
model: many local conditions influence which larger coalition becomes effective, while
no local condition contains the complete meaning or choice.

## Oscillatory coordination is conditional

Oscillatory phase and excitability can alter effective connectivity in named circuits.
For example, phase-targeted stimulation has changed measured interhemispheric motor
inhibition under specific conditions. But coherence is not a universal admission gate.
It can also arise as a consequence of anatomical connectivity, sender power, and shared
postsynaptic input. A region that is out of phase is not necessarily unfinished,
incorrect, unconscious, or excluded from control.

SAN's testable extension is that receiver-relative PWD and structured
coherence-dispersion patterns help bias recruitment among competing routes. The
required test must compare phase with firing rate, power, connectivity, common input,
arousal, and task state, then perturb the proposed relation in a named circuit.

## Relation to COT, NAPOT, and BOT

- **[[cot-cellular-oscillating-tomography|COT]]** asks how cellular state and
  oscillatory input are transformed into conditional cellular output.
- **[[napot|NAPOT]]** asks how many receiver-specific transformations participate in
  recurrent many-to-one and one-to-many neural rendering.
- **[[bot-biological-oscillatory-tomography|BOT]]** asks how coordination and
  differentiation scale across larger biological networks.

These are proposed SAN interpretations. They do not establish that every biological
signal is a soliton, that electrical, chemical, magnetic, mechanical, and spin
variables are interchangeable, or that one mechanism repeats unchanged from molecule
to mind.

## Established research, SAN hypothesis, and stronger claim

- **Established components:** molecular signaling networks can show history-dependent
  and switch-like dynamics; neurons and circuits perform conditional transformations;
  competing action representations can influence evidence accumulation; distributed
  cortical-basal-ganglia-thalamic systems contribute to selection and control.
- **Supported interpretation:** agent language can describe bounded conditional
  input-state-output behavior when the scale, state variables, and outcome are named.
- **SAN-specific hypothesis:** nested receiver-transformer-projector systems combine
  local criteria, learned state, competition, and feedback into a distributed
  observer-action architecture.
- **Stronger unproven claim:** one phase-matching rule admits agents into executive
  control or makes the resulting coalition conscious.

## AI implications

The biology suggests engineering questions rather than a proven design. Multi-agent AI
can be tested for scale-declared local state, uncertainty, arbitration, inhibition,
feedback, error containment, and recovery. Synchronization may be one coordination
tool, but a reliable system also needs explicit ownership, verification, and failure
isolation. See [[agent-phase-sync-feedback-loop]].
