# Three Source Fragments: Distributed Sensing, Consciousness Detection, and Labor Ethics (a0445z)

## Source boundary

The short a0445z note was first publicly Git-fixed on **June 8, 2022**. It contains
three adjacent fragments, not one continuous scientific argument:

1. a "single sensor" proposal about coupled sensors;
2. a speculative future-observer and Jupiter-storm thought experiment; and
3. a separate "galactic workers union" labor-policy proposal.

[The immutable first public note](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/73b1e6e5b8f135937aee39c6612fe52279f03fc7/a0445z%20ctpr.txt)
preserves that historical mixture. The newer large-v3-turbo retranscriptions recover
the first two source families more clearly, but they do not justify merging the labor
fragment into SAN neuroscience.

## Fragment 1: many sensors acting as one sensor

Two early-2022 recordings make the intended operation clearer. The proposal was not
that many biological receptors become one anatomical receptor. It was that signals
from many locally sensitive units can be exchanged and combined so that the larger
system produces one coordinated estimate or response:

```text
many local sensor states
-> recurrent exchange and timing relationships
-> propagation of local departures
-> population-level estimate or coordinated response
-> action and returned consequence
```

The February 24 recording describes signal dissipation across an array as allowing
many nodes to act as a "single sensor." The January 26 recording connects the same
idea to near-sequential Hebbian activity and to the analogy of coupled fireflies.

Established neuroscience supports multisensory convergence in particular circuits,
reliability-weighted cue combination, and timing-dependent changes in effective
interaction. It does **not** establish that every synchronized group is conscious.
Firefly synchrony demonstrates coupled timing, not collective consciousness.

## Fragment 2: a consciousness-detection ethics problem

The Jupiter passage is best read as an ethics thought experiment. A December 26, 2021
Artificial Neurology recording asks how an investigator could recognize consciousness
in an unfamiliar system before perturbing or constructing it. A hypothetical storm is
used to make the risk vivid: if an unfamiliar dissipative system supported consciousness,
an apparently minor intervention might have morally important consequences.

The source does not establish that a storm, planet, hive, or artificial network is
conscious. It proposes a research obligation:

```text
candidate system
-> controlled perturbation
-> measured propagation, differentiation, integration, recovery, and behavior
-> comparison with nonconscious alternatives
-> bounded conclusion with explicit uncertainty
```

Modern perturbational-complexity work is relevant because it tests distributed,
differentiated responses rather than treating raw synchrony as sufficient
([Casali et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)).
It does not validate SAN's substrate-independent consciousness criterion by itself.

## Fragment 3: labor ethics is separate

The final fragment proposes a future labor institution that could resist forced labor,
human trafficking, serfdom, and compensation far below the value of work. No exact
large-v3-turbo source-family match has yet been established for this fragment. It is
preserved as part of the historical note but should not be used as neuroscience evidence.

## Scale boundary

- **Cellular:** receptors, synapses, dendrites, and neurons remain distinct biological units.
- **Circuit:** recurrent populations can integrate selected signals and coordinate responses.
- **Organism:** brain, body, action, and sensory return can function as one sensorimotor system.
- **Beyond one organism:** collective sensing is a useful systems analogy, but consciousness
  requires independent evidence beyond synchronization or aggregate behavior.
