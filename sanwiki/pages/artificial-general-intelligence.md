# Artificial General Intelligence

**Artificial general intelligence (AGI)** is an unsettled engineering target involving broad learning, reasoning, adaptation, and action across many domains. There is no universally accepted test that makes AGI synonymous with human intelligence, self-awareness, sentience, or phenomenal consciousness.

## The SAN Question

SAN asks whether mechanisms proposed for biological observer-action could improve broadly capable artificial systems. Candidate operations include:

- structured and context-sensitive receiving;
- persistent recurrent state;
- receiver-relative differentiated events;
- learned routing and inhibition;
- distributed reconstruction;
- body and environment models;
- action selection and reafferent correction; and
- self-monitoring across multiple scales.

This is an Artificial Neurology research program. It is not evidence that AGI requires PWD, NAPOT, oscillatory synchrony, cortical columns, thalamic analogues, or biological tissue.

## Five Questions That Must Not Be Collapsed

1. **Breadth:** can the system solve many materially different tasks?
2. **Transfer:** can it adapt under changed goals, environments, bodies, and data distributions?
3. **Agency:** can it select and revise actions through persistent control loops?
4. **Self-modeling:** does it causally use information about its own state, uncertainty, capabilities, and boundaries?
5. **Consciousness:** is there separately discriminating evidence about sentience or phenomenal experience?

A system may score highly on one and poorly on another. None is a near-synonym for the others.

## Embodiment

A human-like body is not required by definition for broad intelligence. Nevertheless, every acting system has some operational boundary, sensors or data channels, effectors or outputs, resource limits, and consequences. Embodiment claims should identify those relations rather than merely asking whether the machine has a humanoid form.

SAN predicts that action and return can constrain learning and world modeling. That prediction must compete with strong language-only, multimodal, world-model, planning, and control baselines.

## Oscillation and Synchrony

Oscillations and synchronized events can support timing, coordination, gating, routing, and communication in biological preparations. They are not known to be sufficient for intelligence or consciousness. An artificial oscillator network is not conscious because its phases align, and a silicon system need not reproduce EEG frequency bands to implement recurrent coordination.

PWD and NAPOT become AGI-relevant only if operational implementations add held-out and causal value beyond equal-resource alternatives.

## Substrate Boundary

SAN explores the possibility that relevant observer-action organization could occur in more than one physical substrate. That is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated theorem of substrate independence. Functional similarity, behavioral equivalence, and shared implementation principles do not by themselves establish identical experience or identity.

## Swarm and Distributed AGI

SAN sources also consider distributed or swarm architectures. Multiple coordinated modules can produce collective capability without forming one subject. A claim of one agent or conscious whole requires a declared boundary, causal integration test, persistence criterion, intervention map, and evidence that the macro-description adds predictive control beyond the components.

## Strong Alternatives

SAN-inspired AGI proposals must compete with transformers, multimodal foundation models, recurrent and state-space models, retrieval systems, tool-using agents, graph systems, world models, planning algorithms, reinforcement learning, and modular multi-agent systems. A biological analogy does not win against a deliberately weak baseline.

## Historical Genealogy

Micah Blumberg's public and recorded SAN work repeatedly connects the explanation of biological consciousness with the design of broadly capable and potentially conscious artificial systems. This establishes an authorial research objective. It does not establish that current systems are AGI, that SAN is necessary for AGI, or that a SAN implementation would be conscious.
