# Receptive Field

A **receptive field** specifies the conditions under which a measured neuron or population changes
its response. In sensory neuroscience this often means a region of sensory space and the stimulus
features that influence activity. The definition must name the cell or population, stimulus domain,
response measure, behavioral state, and mapping procedure.

## Receptive fields are measured relations

A receptive field is not a physical picture stored inside a neuron. It is an experimentally
estimated relationship among input conditions, current neural state, and measured response.
Classical visual receptive fields can include an excitatory center, inhibitory surround,
orientation, motion, color, disparity, and contextual influences, depending on the area and method.

Receptive fields are not always fixed. Attention, adaptation, context, eye position, task, and
network feedback can change measured response profiles. In macaque area MT, spatial attention has
been shown to shift and reshape center-surround receptive-field structure:

- [Anton-Erxleben, Stephan, and Treue (2009), attention reshapes MT receptive fields](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhp002)

That result supports dynamic context dependence. It does not establish SAN's phase-wave,
tomography, or consciousness claims.

## Scale declaration

| Scale | Receptive field can mean |
|---|---|
| Micro | Inputs and local conditions that alter one cell or dendritic compartment |
| Meso | Stimulus or network conditions that alter a population or local circuit response |
| Macro | A modeled sensitivity profile of a distributed system under a specified task |

The term should not be extended from one scale to another without naming the new denominator.

## SAN interpretation

SAN emphasizes that a receiver is already in an ongoing state. Incoming events matter relative to
that state, and learned morphology, synaptic history, inhibition, neuromodulation, and recurrence
shape the response. This makes receptive fields compatible with SAN's tonic-context / phasic-change
language while preserving the standard experimental definition.

Historical SAN notes also use receptive-field language for experienced spatial structure, schema
components, or place-related information. Those are proposed representational extensions, not
synonyms for a mapped sensory receptive field. A place-cell code, an experienced spatial field, and
a visual neuron's receptive field must remain separate unless an experiment relates them.

## Relation to the inceptive field

[[inceptive-field|Inceptive field]] is SAN's proposed output-side complement: the set of downstream
targets and measurable effects influenced by a declared neuron or population. A neuron receives
contributions from many upstream output footprints and contributes through its axon to many
downstream receptive fields.

## Test rule

A claim that phase, stimulation, or attention changed a receptive field should preregister the
mapping method, baseline, response metric, spatial and feature model, behavioral state, sham or
control condition, and alternative changes in gain, eye movement, arousal, or adaptation.
