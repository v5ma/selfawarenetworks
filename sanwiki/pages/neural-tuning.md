# Neural Tuning

Neural Tuning is SAN's account of how learned cellular selectivity and current network state determine which inputs matter, which cells can influence one another, and which temporary larger assemblies form. It must be separated into established tuning, state-dependent routing, and a stronger SAN phase-routing hypothesis.

## The ordinary-language problem

A neuron or circuit does not respond to every input equally. Dendritic structure, synaptic history, inhibition, neuromodulation, recent activity, feedback, and task context change which combinations can drive it. Learning changes those conditions over longer timescales; ongoing activity changes them from moment to moment.

The SAN question is therefore not only, "What feature is this neuron tuned to?" It is, "How does a learned pattern recognition event change the routes available to the rest of the network?"

## Three meanings that must remain separate

### 1. Established feature or sensory tuning

Neurons and populations can show measured selectivity for stimulus, movement, task, location, or other variables. This is the conventional empirical starting point.

### 2. State-dependent routing

Gain, selectivity, effective connectivity, dendritic integration, inhibition, feedback, and neuromodulation change with behavioral and network state. A structurally connected network therefore has many possible functional subnetworks.

### 3. The stronger SAN hypothesis

SAN proposes that receiver-relative phase, frequency, magnitude, burst duration, and inhibitory organization help select and retune those routes across neural arrays. Evidence for the first two meanings does not validate this third meaning merely because all three are called tuning.

## Receive, transform, and project

In the SAN model, dendritic and synaptic configuration stores learned constraints on what counts as a consequential input. When a matching combination arrives, local branch events, inhibition, somatic spikes or bursts, release dynamics, and feedback alter what the cell transmits. The output then changes the state and future selectivity of connected cells.

Relevant variables may include:

- branch-specific coincidence and local nonlinear events;
- somatic spikes, bursts, and subthreshold changes;
- inhibition and disinhibition;
- action-potential waveform, duration, release history, and postsynaptic effect;
- thalamocortical and corticocortical feedback;
- ongoing population phase and traveling waves; and
- plasticity that changes later recognition and routing.

This is a multivariable state transition, not one permanent frequency assigned to one cell.

## Layer 6 and thalamic boundary

The early SAN notes use layer 6 pyramidal neurons, apical dendrites, and thalamocortical loops to develop the tuning account. A modern treatment must be cell-type, projection, cortical-area, and task specific. Layer 6 is heterogeneous. No one feedback rule should be generalized across all cortex without direct evidence.

The same caution applies to basal and apical language. Dendritic compartments can receive different input classes and support nonlinear integration, but the simple camera/screen or input/output metaphor is a conceptual aid, not a universal anatomical rule.

## Three-stage Layer 6 attention chronology

The relationship to the newer Layer 6b attention theory is specific enough to record, but the
stages should not be collapsed.

1. **Kasevich and LaBerge, 2017:** proposed neuroelectric tuning through layer 5/layer 6 apical
   dendrites and corticothalamic loop circuits. This is a predecessor hypothesis, not a consensus
   cortical law.
2. **Micah Blumberg, 2022:** integrated that precursor into the SAN Neural Tuning account and added
   phase-change signaling between loops, frequency-sensitive gating, cooperative column
   recruitment, inhibitory selection, tonic/phasic state, and rendered perception. These are the
   SAN extensions recorded in `a0007z.md`, not results established by citing the 2017 paper.
3. **Zolnik, Eickholt, Molnar, and Larkum, 2026:** proposed the Layer 6b Attention Theory, locating
   L6b at the intersection of top-down cortical drive and convergent neuromodulation and proposing
   control over layer-5/higher-order-thalamic attention loops. The perspective discusses
   synaptic facilitation, working memory, perceptual binding, and related thalamocortical functions.

- [Kasevich and LaBerge, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2017.00037)
- [Micah's public 2022 Neural Tuning source](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/2a428afcab608c434af5c16dac3927c3e2f9ab83/a0007z.md)
- [Zolnik and colleagues, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.11.024)

The 2026 article is a circuit-based theory/perspective. Its overlap makes it an important later
comparator and source of testable anatomy, but it does not experimentally validate LAT, SAN's
phase-routing extension, NAPOT, or a consciousness mechanism. It is also not an Earl Miller paper.

## Relationship to Attention Schema Theory

Attention Schema Theory proposes that a system constructs a simplified control model of its own
attention. Neural Tuning and the Layer 6b Attention Theory instead concern candidate circuitry for
selective control and routing. A future synthesis can ask whether corticothalamic tuning supplies
signals used by an attention schema, but one theory should not be presented as the established
implementation of the other.

## Experiments that distinguish the stronger hypothesis

- Perturb relative phase while controlling mean firing and stimulus information.
- Compare phase-aware and rate-only models on held-out prediction and causal intervention.
- Test whether dendritic state predicts route changes beyond somatic output alone.
- Test whether corticothalamic or inhibitory perturbation changes the predicted receiver-specific route rather than only global gain.
- Specify the spatial scale, frequency range, coupling route, and magnitude variable before interpreting a result.
