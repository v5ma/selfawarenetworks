The neuroscience of volition examines how [[self-aware-network|self-aware networks]] in the brain generate the experience of [[choice]] without requiring libertarian free will. The brain builds internal maps of everything it senses — including its own body and mental states — and decision-making emerges from this [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modeling process.

## Core SAN Position

Determinism and [[choice]] are not mutually exclusive. Neural decisions operate as probability distributions: most choices cluster around habitual patterns (the center of a bell curve), while novel or effortful choices occupy the low-probability tails. This is consistent with the [[napot|NAPOT]] framework, where neural activity phase-oscillates around [[attractor]] states that represent learned behavioral tendencies.

## Neuroplasticity and Self-Regulation

Recognizing that the brain is [[neuroplasticity|plastic]] — capable of rewiring its own probability distributions — is psychologically and practically significant. Agents can shift their own bell curves through deliberate practice, expanding the space of reachable choices over time. This is how [[self-modeling]] feeds back into [[volition]]: knowing the brain can change motivates behavioral change.

## Responsibility Without Libertarian Free Will

Even under a probabilistic-deterministic model, organisms remain appropriate targets of praise and coordination. [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-regulation and [[choice]] are evolved mechanisms for survival and social coordination — their value is functional, not metaphysical. Neurological health and psychological state shape how freely [[choice]] is experienced, making the quality of inner [[self-modeling]] central to agency.
