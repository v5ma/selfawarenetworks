A 1/F (inverse frequency) relationship governs brainwave amplitude across frequency bands: low-frequency oscillations (delta) have high amplitude; high-frequency oscillations (gamma) have low amplitude. At the population level, this is a universal fractal scaling principle.

The paradox: individual action potentials are all-or-nothing in amplitude. So where does the graded amplitude-frequency tradeoff at the macroscale come from?

## Resolution via magnitude

SAN's resolution invokes [[action-potential-magnitude-via-duration]]: the graded variable is not amplitude but **duration** (amplitude × duration = magnitude). Longer action potentials open potassium channels for longer, which regulates calcium channel duration and thus neurotransmitter release timing. Low-frequency bursts = longer duration spikes; high-frequency activity = shorter duration spikes. The 1/F relationship emerges from this duration-frequency tradeoff scaling through [[fractal-neural-scale-hierarchy]].

## Cross-scale universality

The 1/F principle appears beyond the brain: in quantum particle interactions, in galactic structure, in climate systems. SAN treats this as evidence of a universal fractal organization — the same oscillatory convergence-divergence structure operating across scale levels. The brain instantiates the same physics as cosmological structures, which grounds the speculative cosmological claims in [[qgtcd-dark-time-theory]].

## Neural network implication

Convergence (many → few neurons) and divergence (few → many) are the neural correlates of scaling: signals converge up the hierarchy (increasing frequency, decreasing amplitude) and diverge down (increasing amplitude, decreasing frequency). This defines the bandwidth-magnitude tradeoff in any [[self-aware-network]].
