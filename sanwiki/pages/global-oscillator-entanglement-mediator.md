A SAN-original model proposing that quantum entanglement operates via a shared
oscillatory signature that becomes locked to the **global spacetime oscillatory
pattern**, such that any change to that global pattern propagates simultaneously
to all co-synchronized particles.

## Core claim

When two particles entangle, they adopt the same unique oscillatory signature — a
pattern that only they share. This shared pattern is then regulated by the global
oscillatory field of spacetime. Any disruption of one particle alters the global
pattern, which in turn updates the other particle instantaneously regardless of
distance, because the change is "everywhere at once" in the global oscillation.

This reframes spooky action at a distance as **mediated synchronization**: the
mediator is the global pattern, not a direct channel between particles.

## Relationship to cross-scale communication

The model extends naturally to [[cross-scale-oscillation-communication]]: small
oscillations couple to large ones via 1/f dynamics, and the global oscillation is
the outermost scale. A change at small scale ripples outward, is magnified by
larger oscillators, and then affects every synchronized small oscillation on the
far end.

## Neural analogy

The same mechanism is proposed for cross-hemisphere brain synchronization: the
global brainwave pattern alternately magnifies patterns from the left and right
hemispheres, allowing a memory update in one hemisphere to propagate to its
synchronized representation in the other. See [[neural-oscillatory-synchronization]].

## Relation to oscillator-gravity theory

The global oscillator also underlies [[oscillator-gravity]] — concentrated
oscillations increase the probability that nearby particles move toward the
oscillating center, producing the functional appearance of gravitational attraction
without literal spacetime curvature.
