Agent-al Morality is [[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]]'s account (May 2024) of how organisms and artificial agents develop a sense of morality rooted in the structure of their internal goal representation — without requiring universal panpsychism or objective moral facts external to the agent.

## Core Claim

An **Agent-al Material** (Michael Levin's term) is a living or artificial entity with a coherent goal capable of pursuing multiple paths to achieve it. Unlike an ordinary material (e.g., a rock), an Agent-al Material builds an internal map of the environment in terms of *consequences for itself*. Morality emerges from this map: outcomes that push the agent toward extreme negative states (extreme heat, death, structural collapse) are represented as "bad"; outcomes that promote goal achievement are "good."

The "feels good / feels bad" dimension is implemented as a measurable but abstract neural activity manifold — a low-dimensional surface in brain state space that maps qualia valence to detected environmental parameters. This is a concrete, information-theoretic definition of subjective morality grounded in [[napot-theory]]'s rendering-loop account of consciousness.

## Why This Avoids Panpsychism

A rock does not qualify as an Agent-al Material: it has no coherent internal goal beyond structural integrity at the atomic scale. Morality does not apply to it. The Agent-al Material concept neatly restricts morality to entities that generate goal-oriented internal representations — precisely the entities whose brains (or artificial analogs) run the oscillatory rendering loops described in [[theory-of-redness]] and [[phenomenological-consciousness]].

## Shared Morality

Two or more Agent-al Materials can converge on *common* moral experiences when they share consequence sensitivities — e.g., all conscious organisms experience extreme heat as bad. This convergence produces moral experiences that *appear* objective (cross-agent agreement, communication, measurable parameters) while remaining grounded in subjective internal representations. Cooperation to avoid shared extreme consequences is the basis of collectively perceived moral good.
