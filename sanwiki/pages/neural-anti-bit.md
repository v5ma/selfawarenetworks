A **neural anti-bit** is the polar opposite of a [[coincidence-detection-neural-bit|coincidence]] in
the brain's phase-pattern language. Where a coincidence of phase patterns constitutes a
positive unit of neural information (a "bit"), the **splay state** — maximal phase
dispersion, the opposite of synchrony — constitutes the anti-bit: a deliberate
non-coincidence that signals the *absence* of a feature or the suppression of a pattern.

The term was coined in notes on SAN computational biology to describe the
complementary logic of oscillatory codes: the brain does not simply silence neurons to
negate — it actively encodes negation via maximal temporal spread (the splay state).

## Splay state mechanics

In a splay state, a group of neurons fires at maximally spaced intervals — equal
angular separation around the oscillation cycle. No two neurons coincide. This
produces:

- Zero net correlation in the readout population
- Maximum entropy discharge — the group carries no conjunctive information
- Effective *inhibition by timing* without requiring additional inhibitory interneurons

## Information-theoretic framing

If [[coincidence-detection-neural-bit|coincidence]] is `1` and splay is `0`, then phase
patterns can implement a signed binary code. Reference frames and [[grid-cells]] can
represent positive feature conjunctions (1) and their logical complements (0) within
the same oscillatory substrate.

This framing aligns with [[napot]]: the neural array computes perceptual content via
the ratio of coincidence to splay across columns at each oscillation cycle.

## Relation to attention and prediction

When a [[dendritic-compartmentalization|dendritic compartment]] suppresses incoming
coincidences, it is enforcing a splay state downstream — a mechanism for selective
[[attention]] by anti-bit injection. The prediction error signal in SAN can be encoded as
the difference between expected coincidence and observed splay.
