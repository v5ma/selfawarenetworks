## Stabilization mechanism

The brain maintains a persistent sense of spatial reality by continuously feeding new sensory data into its oscillatory rendering process. The key claim: *sensory input stabilizes the rendering*. When that input is present:
- Spatial and temporal coordinates remain consistent across moments.
- [[tonic-oscillation|Tonic brainwave oscillations]] provide a carrier that new data modulates predictably.
- The rendered scene matches what sensors report â€” perceived reality feels solid.

When input drops (sleep, sensory deprivation, dissociation):
- The rendering becomes [[gh-a0206z|self]]-referential; it updates from internal predictions only.
- Dream scenes mutate, teleport, and shift unpredictably because prediction errors go uncorrected.

## Implications for AI

AI language models (LLMs) face an analogous instability: without real-time sensory or world-state grounding, their outputs drift (hallucinate). The fix proposed in the source parallels biological stabilization â€” connecting models to live external information streams acts as the sensory anchor. See [[ai-sensory-grounding]].

## Relation to NAPOT and SAN

The rendering hypothesis is the phenomenological counterpart of [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography]] (NAPOT): NAPOT describes the *mechanism* (oscillatory phase arrays projecting spatial representations), while the volumetric rendering framing describes the *experiential result*. The [[phase-wave-differential]] between tonic and phasic activity is what makes the rendering update moment to moment.

Language itself is formed from sequences of oscillations (neural phase arrays), not from static symbolic tokens â€” this extends the rendering model to the domain of linguistic thought.
