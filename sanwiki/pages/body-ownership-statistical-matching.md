# Body Ownership as Multisensory Causal Inference

> **Scientific boundary:** Body ownership is influenced by visual, tactile, proprioceptive, spatial, temporal, anatomical, and motor evidence. No established result shows that ownership is created by one phase gate, a near-zero phase difference, or a single cortical matching center.

Body ownership is the experienced relation that a body or body part is mine. It is related to body schema, limb position, agency, and self-location, but those variables are not interchangeable.

The phrase **statistical matching** entered the 2018 source through Sarit Shashkes's explanation of the rubber-hand illusion, not as a term coined by Micah Blumberg or SAN. It is useful here when translated into a testable inference problem: do several signals support the hypothesis that a seen object, felt contact, and predicted bodily consequence have a common cause?

## What the rubber-hand experiment establishes

Botvinick and Cohen hid a participant's biological hand while synchronously stroking it and a visible rubber hand. Coordinated seen and felt contact could produce reports that the rubber hand felt like part of the participant's body and refer felt touch toward it ([1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/35784)).

This result does not move peripheral receptors into the rubber hand. It shows that the nervous system can revise which visible object and felt event are treated as belonging together.

Later work supplies important boundaries:

- ownership reports and proprioceptive drift can dissociate ([Rohde et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0021659));
- Bayesian causal-inference models can explain important parts of the illusion without invoking a unique binding rhythm ([Samad et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0117178)); and
- whole-body visual-somatosensory conflict can alter bodily identification and self-location without proving that the self is a free-floating image ([Lenggenhager et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1143439));
- the temporal window for combining events differs across individuals, so physical synchrony should not be treated as one universal threshold ([Costantini et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2016.08.010)); and
- susceptibility and phenomenology vary across people, reinforcing the need to measure individual differences rather than assume one response ([Haans et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2012.07.016)).

## Variables that must remain separate

| Variable | Operational question | Not established by |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Is this body or part experienced as mine? | Proprioceptive drift alone |
| Limb position | Where is the limb estimated to be? | Ownership report alone |
| Agency | Did my action cause this event? | Synchronous stroking alone |
| Body schema | What action-ready configuration is being used? | One illusion questionnaire |
| Self-location | Where is the embodied point of view estimated? | Hand ownership alone |
| Tactile discrimination | What contact occurred at the skin? | Ownership of the contacted object |

## Established mechanism class

The defensible established account is distributed multisensory estimation: cutaneous receptors register contact; proprioceptive and visual evidence constrain limb position; temporal, spatial, anatomical, and motor relations affect common-cause assignment; distributed participating systems contribute according to task; and the resulting estimate can change report, localization, protection, and action.

This is not one universal circuit. Different ownership paradigms recruit different evidence and operations.

## What the 2018 source actually contributes

[[gh-a0476z|a0476z]], internally dated April 14, 2018 and first Git-fixed in June 2022, is a dialogue rather than a single-author theory statement ([immutable source](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/617eb5583ae0b0dca83cd68fda1ad4f47ee4d19b/a0476z.md)). Its claims must therefore remain attributed:

- **Shashkes's donor proposition:** sight and proprioception can support a statistical match, as illustrated by the rubber-hand illusion; avatar-body similarity and context may matter.
- **Blumberg's source-authored extension:** intelligent systems distinguish representations hierarchically and contextually; a temporary ownership illusion does not by itself establish durable dysmorphia; and the emotional weight of a self or body representation may vary with person, culture, developmental stage, bodily condition, and time.
- **Shared open question:** mirror, virtual-reality, and other controlled illusions might have therapeutic or harmful consequences depending on context. That is a research question, not a clinical conclusion supplied by this dialogue.

The medically recoverable SAN idea is therefore not a hidden phase gate. It is that a body representation is a context-sensitive, history-dependent control estimate whose meaning and persistence depend on the receiver, the competing evidence, and the consequences for perception and action. The 2018 source anticipates person-specific and time-varying body-model effects; it does not establish their neural implementation.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats ownership as one output of a distributed body-world update, not as a picture delivered to an inner observer.

- **Tonic canvas:** the receiver's maintained body, task, and environmental context.
- **Phasic ink:** a new touch, visual event, prediction error, movement, or timing relation that changes that context.
- **Observer-action:** the participating network and body revise localization, ownership judgment, protection, sampling, or movement.

SAN may propose that receiver-relative timing, recent state, and phase structure contribute to this update. That proposal must name the source, physical route, receiving population, reference signal, measured relation, and behavioral consequence. Synchronous stimulation alone does not prove phase locking, NAPOT, GCS, consciousness, or a near-zero phase-difference criterion.

## Scale map

- **Micro:** mechanotransduction, peripheral afferents, synapses, dendrites, and cellular responses.
- **Meso:** spinal, brainstem, cerebellar, thalamic, somatosensory, parietal, and premotor circuits.
- **Macro:** distributed estimation linking body configuration, ownership, self-location, agency, and action.

## Decisive SAN test

A stronger SAN experiment would manipulate visual-tactile timing, limb geometry, motor prediction, and sensory reliability independently; measure ownership, limb position, agency, tactile discrimination, self-location, and action separately; compare equal-capacity causal-inference, predictive, recurrent-history, and SAN timing models; and require endpoint-specific loss plus relation-specific rescue after selective perturbation.
