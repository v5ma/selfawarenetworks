## Post-SAN

Mainstream cortical-interneuron neuroscience already accepts that inhibition is functionally heterogeneous (PV, SST, VIP subtypes), that it shapes gain and timing rather than only suppressing activity, and that gamma rhythms depend on PV-driven inhibition. SAN sharpens this into a *rendering-pipeline* commitment with two testable consequences. First, the SAN reading predicts that the *quality* of perceptual experience — its sharpness, its coherence, its absence of intrusive hallucinatory content — should track the *integrity* of [[dendrite]]-targeting inhibition specifically (the SST-style population), not just total inhibitory tone. Disrupting [[dendrite]]-targeting inhibition without changing somatic inhibition should produce *characteristic* perceptual distortions, predictable from the SAN rendering reading. Second, by binding the inhibitory pass to [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differentials]] and to [[cortical-traveling-waves|cortical traveling waves]], SAN predicts that the inhibitory contribution should be measurable as a *phase-relationship* between fast PV-rhythm activity and slower SST/VIP modulation — a phase-relationship the published literature treats descriptively but does not yet treat as the mechanistic *purpose* of the inhibitory pass. Where SAN converges with the published work is in the [[inhibitory-interneurons-plasticity-gating|plasticity-gating]] story, in interneuron-driven gain-control accounts, and in the broader rendering/correction-loop reading already implicit in predictive-processing models of [[cortex]].
