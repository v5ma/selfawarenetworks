# Inceptive Field

**Inceptive field** is SAN's proposed output-side complement to a receptive field. It names the
downstream target set and measurable effects that a declared neuron or population can initiate over
a stated time window.

It is a functional term, not a standard anatomical structure.

## Anatomical correction

A neuron's ordinary output route is:

1. synaptic input and dendritic state;
2. nonlinear dendritic and somatic integration;
3. action-potential initiation, normally at the axon initial segment;
4. propagation through the axon and its branches;
5. probabilistic release at presynaptic boutons;
6. receptor- and state-dependent effects in downstream cells.

Active apical and basal dendrites can strongly shape cellular output, but they are not normally the
presynaptic exit arbor. The inceptive field should therefore be mapped through axonal branches,
terminals, target cell types, and downstream effects. It should not be drawn as a reversed apical
dendrite broadcasting directly into the network.

## Operational definition

A rigorous inceptive-field claim must state:

- the source neuron, cell class, population, or circuit;
- the axonal target set;
- the intervention or naturally occurring source event;
- the response variables measured in each target;
- the time window and behavioral state;
- direct, indirect, excitatory, inhibitory, and modulatory effects;
- uncertainty and unobserved targets.

The inceptive field is therefore closer to a causal output footprint than to a display screen.

## Micro, meso, and macro use

| Scale | Bounded use |
|---|---|
| Micro | Axonal boutons and postsynaptic effects of one neuron or defined cell class |
| Meso | Downstream populations influenced by a local circuit or projection-defined ensemble |
| Macro | Distributed systems altered by a named pathway or coordinated population update |

One source can affect many targets, and every target combines that effect with many other inputs.
The output footprint does not deposit a complete picture or sparse distributed representation into
the recipient. The recipient transforms the arriving event according to its receptors, voltage,
inhibition, neuromodulation, and history.

## Historical SAN metaphor

Older SAN notes describe the inceptive field as a screen, Etch A Sketch, speaker, or array on which a
cell renders a prediction. The useful intuition is divergent re-expression: one transformed cellular
or population state can alter many downstream receivers. The medically bounded translation is
axon -> terminal -> release -> receptor -> downstream integration. Screen language does not create
a display surface or a separate viewer.

## Relation to NAPOT

NAPOT's many-to-one-to-many cycle can use receptive and inceptive terminology when scale is explicit:
many upstream output footprints contribute to a receiver; the receiver transforms them; its axonal
or population output influences many downstream receptive fields. Tomography remains a meso- or
macro-scale reconstruction hypothesis unless a cellular reconstruction operator is specified.
