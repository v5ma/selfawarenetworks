# Olfactory Branching and Recurrent Circuits

> **Scientific boundary:** Olfactory processing cannot be reduced to exactly three simultaneous pathways with a fixed arrival order. Bulb output reaches several forebrain targets through branching and recurrent circuits whose contribution depends on species, stimulus, sampling, task, learning, and state.

The earlier title **tripartite circuit** is retained in the page slug for continuity, but it should be read as a historical simplification rather than a canonical anatomical count.

## Receptor convergence and bulb transformation

Olfactory sensory neurons expressing a specified receptor converge on a small number of glomerular targets in mouse olfactory bulb ([Mombaerts et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674%2800%2981387-2)). This is receptor-linked projection geometry, not a one-glomerulus-one-odor map. Mitral and tufted cells distribute transformed bulb output to several olfactory forebrain targets.

## Distributed cortical and limbic relations

Piriform cortex is a principal component of primary olfactory cortex. Mouse imaging showed sparse, spatially dispersed piriform odor ensembles rather than a smooth copy of bulb organization ([Stettler and Axel, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.09.005)).

Olfactory information also participates in amygdalar, entorhinal, hippocampal, thalamic, and orbitofrontal relations. These labels do not define isolated identity, emotion, memory, routing, and value modules. Human single-neuron recordings found odor-modulated populations in piriform cortex, amygdala, entorhinal cortex, and hippocampus, with regional differences and cross-modal relations ([Kehl et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08016-5)).

An indirect piriform-mediodorsal-thalamus-orbitofrontal route changes with attention ([Plailly et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5607-07.2008)). This establishes thalamic participation without turning the whole system into one mandatory serial chain.

## Timing follows sampling and circuit state

Each sniff supplies a repeatable reference event. Experimentally shifting activation within the sniff cycle changed discrimination behavior in mice ([Smear et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10521)). That result supports timing relative to active sampling; it does not establish a universal sequence in which a direct temporal route carries coarse identity first and slower thalamic or hippocampal routes add context later.

A medically accurate diagram should therefore show:

1. receptor-population sampling shaped by airflow and adaptation;
2. convergence and local transformation in the bulb;
3. branching projections to several forebrain populations;
4. recurrent interactions among cortical, limbic, thalamic, and orbitofrontal populations;
5. behavioral and autonomic consequences that alter the next sniff and network state.

## SAN interpretation

This architecture is useful to SAN because no single route or region receives a finished smell. Different populations maintain different tonic contexts and receive phasic updates tied to receptor state, respiration, memory, value, and action. SAN proposes that their recurrent, receiver-relative coordination contributes to the aggregate observer response.

That proposal remains distinct from the established component findings. The cited work does not prove a three-channel consciousness circuit, fixed frequency hierarchy, NAPOT reconstruction, or one olfactory meaning center.
