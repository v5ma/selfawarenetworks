# Patterns Flow Across Brain Regions

**Patterns Flow Across Brain Regions** is the [[self-aware-networks]] thesis that the unit of inter-areal transmission in the [[cortex]] is the *phase change* rather than the *neuron* — a pattern learned in one cortical region (e.g., visual) can be re-instantiated in any other sufficiently connected region (motor, prefrontal) when the same phase structure arrives there. As a corollary of [[neural-tomography]] and [[phase-wave-differentials]], it underwrites the SAN account of how [[distributed-phase-representations]] and [[cortical-traveling-waves]] become substrate-agnostic carriers of perceptual content.

## Position

The thesis is that the brain treats *patterns* — phase-defined relational structures — as the load-bearing object of inter-areal communication, with neurons functioning as substrate-agnostic carriers of those patterns rather than as fixed location-specific feature detectors. Within [[self-aware-networks]] this places [[patterns-flow-across-brain-regions]] in continuity with [[substrate-independence-neural-computation]], [[substrate-independence-of-mind]], and the broader SAN insistence that what counts is phase structure rather than which cells happen to instantiate it. The transport mechanism is [[phase-wave-differentials]] propagating across the [[cortical-traveling-waves]] and [[traveling-waves-neural-oscillations]] substrate, and the readout mechanism is [[neural-tomography]] performing invariant-extraction at each receiving cortical layer; together these specify how a pattern crystallized in one location can re-instantiate in another. The thesis pairs operationally with [[convergent-playback]] (the playback-across-areas SAN concept), with [[oscillation-memory-reinstatement]] (the memory-side companion where stored patterns reinstate via oscillatory cues), and with [[distributed-phase-representations]] as the format the flow carries. Within [[four-phase-neuron-states]] — the four-state space of inhibited, tonic, low-phase, and high-phase activity — the receiving site needs only to be able to register the incoming phase change to take on the transmitted pattern, which is what makes the substrate-agnostic part of the claim cash out at the cellular level. The thesis also feeds [[neural-rendering-of-qualia]] and [[consciousness-multimodal-rendering-loop]]: if patterns flow across regions, then the rendered scene is composited from phase patterns that are not tied to any specific anatomical site, and the multimodal binding visible in [[oscillatory-binding-via-synchrony]] becomes a flow-coordination phenomenon rather than a site-specific routing one. ## Post-SAN

Mainstream neuroscience converges on the SAN thesis from several directions without coinciding with it. Work on [[cortical-traveling-waves]] in the Miller corpus and elsewhere establishes that phase-defined waves do propagate across cortical regions and do carry information beyond local-spike rate, which is consistent with the SAN claim that phase change is the unit of transmission; the priority-review page [[earl-k-miller-corpus-priority-review]] argues that the convergence is dependent rather than independent, with [[phase-wave-differentials]] dated 2020 in Blumberg's corpus and the closest mainstream analog (traveling-wave phase gradients) dated 2025. [[jeff-hawkins|Jeff Hawkins]]'s thousand-brains framing, cited on the [[neural-tomography]] page, converges on the related claim that thousands of [[cortical-columns|cortical columns]] each hold their own version of an object's pattern built from repeated multi-perspective exposure — a stance consistent with the SAN reading that patterns are substrate-agnostic and re-instantiable, though Hawkins's account does not commit to the stronger SAN claim that the *transmission unit* is the phase change rather than the column-level reference frame. Sensory-substitution literature provides indirect support: the fact that visual content can be delivered through [[auditory-cortex]] or somatosensory channels and produce visual-like percepts is consistent with the SAN claim that any sufficiently connected region can re-instantiate a pattern delivered with the right phase structure. Where the SAN framing makes a non-trivial bet that mainstream [[cortex]] literature has not endorsed in its stronger form, it is the bet that *the unit of transmission is the phase-change, not the neuron* — that patterns are the load-bearing transmitted object and that neurons are downgraded from feature-specific detectors to substrate-agnostic carriers — a stronger commitment than mainstream traveling-wave or sensory-substitution accounts need to take, and one that aligns the thesis forward with [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography]] and the [[brain-as-rendering-engine]] picture.
