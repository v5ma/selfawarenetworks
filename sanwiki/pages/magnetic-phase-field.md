The magnetic phase field is the magnetic pattern produced by the electrical movement of an [[action-potential]] along an axon. As ionic current flows from soma to terminals, it generates a rippling magnetic field aligned with an imaginary vector running through the axon's center.

## Core idea

Each firing neuron produces a vector line of electrical charge that dissipates through the nodes of Ranvier and divides at axon terminals. This creates a row-level **magnetic phase pattern** — a spatial signature of which neurons fired and in what temporal order.

In SAN theory this phase pattern is one layer in a multi-modal memory store. The same event is simultaneously encoded as:

- **Ionic gradient** — electrochemical state at the synapse
- **Acoustic / mechanical wave** — cell-membrane expansion as heat traveling directionally
- **Chemical wave** — neurotransmitter concentration gradients
- **Electrical brainwave** — the aggregate EEG-scale charge

Each encoding is a different projection of the same underlying [[fractal-pattern]]. Together they give the brain redundancy and multi-resolution access to the same representational content.

## Relation to cortical columns

[[Jeff Hawkins]] argued that a [[thousand-brains-theory|Thousand Brains]] arrangement means similar patterns occur independently in many cortical columns — not a hologram, not a single engram, but *variations* of the same template distributed across columns. The magnetic phase field is what each column emits as it processes its local portion of the pattern.

Successive cortical layers decode, re-encode, and magnify parts of the pattern: each layer resolves the feature at a different scale, passing a refined phase signal forward.
