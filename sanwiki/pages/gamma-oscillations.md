This plural page is the scale and measurement bridge to [[gamma-oscillation|Gamma Oscillation]]. Gamma phenomena include transient bursts, sustained narrowband peaks, spike-field locking, interareal coherence, traveling phase patterns, and broadband high-frequency activity. They must not be merged because they can have different biophysical origins.

## Measurement checklist

For every gamma claim, report:

1. recording type and reference;
2. anatomical area, layer, and species;
3. frequency definition and spectral method;
4. whether the signal is narrowband, burst-like, evoked, or broadband;
5. spike, muscle, line-noise, and volume-conduction controls;
6. source and receiver populations; and
7. the predicted behavioral or computational consequence.

## Common overclaims

Gamma is not automatically:

- a perceptual binding code;
- a working-memory slot;
- a prediction carrier;
- a consciousness marker;
- a feedforward signal in every pathway; or
- evidence for NAPOT, GCS, or a phase-wave differential.

Empirical gamma effects can still constrain SAN. For example, attention-related interareal gamma synchronization and working-memory gamma bursts show that fast rhythmic coordination can be selective and transient. The relevant question is which receiving circuits are changed and whether timing adds causal information beyond rate, power, and common input.

## SAN boundary

SAN may use gamma as a candidate phasic timing mode inside a slower or more persistent context. The term **phasic** is functional and receiver-relative; it is not a synonym for gamma. Likewise, tonic context is not assigned to one slower band.

## Primary anchors

- Ray and Maunsell separated narrowband gamma from broadband high-frequency activity ([PLOS Biology, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1000610)).
- Bosman et al. measured selective attention-related synchronization between visual areas ([Neuron, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.06.037)).
- Lundqvist et al. measured brief gamma and beta bursts during working memory ([Neuron, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.02.028)).
