# Choice

> **Canonical mechanism article:** [[neural-path-selection-interneurons-choice|Neural Path Selection, Interneurons, and Choice Across Scales]]

In SAN, **choice** is not an event performed by a hidden executive inside the brain. It is the
embodied transition by which several possible next states are constrained by learned structure,
present evidence, predicted consequences, valuation, excitation, inhibition, and recurrent
context until one effective route recruits action. The action changes the body or world, and the
returned consequence changes later readiness.

```text
possible futures + learned criteria + present context
-> competing distributed activity and effective routes
-> context-sensitive recruitment, inhibition, and disinhibition
-> commitment to one physical transition
-> action
-> returned consequence
-> updated criteria for later choice
```

The person-level choice is produced by the whole closed loop. A single neuron may undergo a
criterion-sensitive state transition, but it does not privately deliberate or contain the
organism's options.

## Source-recovered genealogy

SAN's Choice account developed in stages:

- [[gh-a0153z|a0153z]], internally dated May 15, 2012 and first publicly Git-fixed in June 2022,
  describes learned pathway strengths and competing neural patterns. Its enduring operation is
  distributed competition, not the literal image of every neighboring neuron suppressing every
  other neuron.
- [[gh-b0160y|b0160y]], recorded October 4, 2021 and first publicly Git-fixed June 8, 2022,
  connects learned experience, present context, several possible futures, short- and long-horizon
  consequences, and one selected behavior. A later retranscription recovered obvious
  speech-to-text errors without changing the source date.
- [[gh-a0149z|a0149z]], first publicly Git-fixed July 7, 2022, names **Choice as a Neuropath** and
  joins distributed decision activity to temporary effective routes and inhibitory circuitry.
- [[gh-a0081z|a0081z]], first publicly Git-fixed June 10, 2022, describes choice refinement:
  deterministic agents can change later choices when new evidence changes their physically
  implemented criteria.

Peter Tse's criterial-causation framework is an acknowledged predecessor. Gyorgy Buzsaki's work
on inhibition and brain rhythms, and Steven Strogatz's work on oscillator synchronization, are
historical donors to SAN's later synthesis. They do not by themselves establish SAN's complete
Choice mechanism.

## What neuroscience establishes

Several established results support parts of this architecture without proving the full SAN
interpretation:

- Macaque dorsal premotor populations can represent more than one reach option before the selected
  option strengthens and the rejected option is suppressed
  ([Cisek and Kalaska, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.01.027)).
- Context and sensory evidence can be selected and integrated through recurrent population
  dynamics rather than by one executive neuron
  ([Mante et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12742)).
- Experimental manipulation of direct and indirect basal-ganglia pathways can bidirectionally alter
  movement initiation in mice, although the classical two-pathway diagram is not a universal
  account of all action selection
  ([Kravitz et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09159)).
- Hippocampal theta sequences can extend ahead of an animal in a goal-dependent manner, providing
  one measured route by which current goals relate to possible future trajectories
  ([Wikenheiser and Redish, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3909)).

These findings support distributed candidate representation, context-dependent selection,
goal-sensitive future structure, and circuit-level movement control. They do not show that every
choice uses one frequency band, one cortical layer, one brain region, or one universal inhibitory
motif.

## The SAN interpretation

The source-faithful SAN synthesis separates three scales:

### Cellular scale

**Established substrate:** dendritic integration, membrane state, synaptic plasticity, excitation,
and inhibition.

**SAN interpretation:** learned cellular structure contributes criteria that make some incoming
patterns more consequential than others.

### Circuit scale

**Established substrate:** recurrent dynamics, competition, disinhibition, effective connectivity,
and population trajectories.

**SAN interpretation:** a temporary active route emerges from the larger structural network and
carries one candidate toward action.

### Organism scale

**Established substrate:** prediction, valuation, motor preparation, execution, sensory
consequence, and learning.

**SAN interpretation:** the embodied observer-action loop is the chooser; there is no additional
inner agent inspecting the result.

The stronger SAN hypothesis is that receiver-relative tonic state and phasic
[[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differentials]] help organize which assemblies become
effective across these scales. That claim is experimentally open. It should be tested against
rate, timing, known circuit state, and ordinary population-dynamics models rather than assumed
from oscillatory correlation alone. If receiver-relative PWD features fail to improve out-of-sample
prediction of route commitment after firing rate, spike timing, task variables, and measured circuit
state are included, the PWD-specific extension loses support even if the broader distributed-choice
account remains viable.

[[electrical-valence-receiver-relative-drive|Electrical Valence]] names the proposed receiver-
relative effective drive in that route-to-action bridge. It does not rename affective valence,
field power, or muscular energy.
