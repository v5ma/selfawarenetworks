Super Dark Time (SDT) is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s theory that gravity emerges from local variations in *time density* — the number of discrete time increments per unit coordinate time — rather than from spacetime curvature alone. It is part of the [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] (Quantum Gradient Time Crystal Dilation) framework.

## Core claim: the time density field

SDT introduces a local time-density field ρₜ(xᵘ) ≡ dN/dt: the count of discrete "time frames" per unit coordinate time. Mass locally compresses time — more ticks per coordinate second near massive objects. Gravitational attraction, time dilation, and lensing emerge as cumulative effects of local phase updates biased by elevated ρₜ, not from geometric curvature per se.

## Super Time Position (STP)

The related [[super-time-position]] concept holds that quantum particles have ultrafast, deterministic internal phase cycles ("time gears") that measurement devices cannot fully resolve. Apparent quantum randomness is an undersampling artifact. Wave-function collapse is reinterpreted as phase synchronization between the particle's fast cycle and a slower measuring clock, itself modulated by local ρₜ.

## Micah's New Law of Thermodynamics (MNLOT)

[[micahs-new-law-of-thermodynamics]] (MNLOT) frames entropy as iterative wave-phase difference dissipation. Each local interaction reduces phase mismatches deterministically. SDT integrates MNLOT so that gravitational and quantum phenomena both emerge from local "computations" driven by ρₜ — connecting thermodynamics, gravity, and quantum mechanics in one framework.
