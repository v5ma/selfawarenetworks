# Multimodal Sensory Geometry and Active Sampling

Different sensory systems do not deliver interchangeable copies of the world. Each receptor population transforms a local physical or chemical condition through a modality-specific anatomy, and the organism's own movements help determine what is measured.

The earlier plate compressed these facts into a single "4D phase-field reconstruction" and included obsolete claims about fixed vesicle counts and ATP transfer. Those elements are not established physiology. This page separates established sensory organization from the proposed SAN interpretation.

## Established sensory organization

- **Vision:** the retina is a curved neural sheet whose photoreceptors and downstream circuits transform local light. Retinal position preserves spatial relations, but the signal is already selective and changes as the eyes move.
- **Hearing:** the cochlea performs a mechanical frequency-dependent transformation before hair-cell transduction. Tonotopic organization is real, but it is not a visual map and it has developmental and preparation-specific limits ([Echteler, Arjmand, and Dallos, 1989](https://doi.org/10.1038/341147a0)).
- **Touch and proprioception:** receptor type, body location, tissue mechanics, posture, and self-generated movement jointly shape the available signal. In awake mice, whisker forces predicted primary-afferent firing during active exploration ([Campagner et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.10696)), and identified touch events produced precise layer-4 responses ([Hires et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.06619)).
- **Olfaction:** a large receptor family supplies combinatorial chemical sensitivity ([Buck and Axel, 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2891%2990418-X)). Receptor-linked axons converge in organized olfactory-bulb glomeruli ([Mombaerts et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674%2800%2981387-2)), while piriform-cortex odor responses are distributed rather than arranged as a simple spatial picture ([Stettler and Axel, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.09.005)).

An **array** here means a declared set of receptors or neurons whose identities, connections, locations, timing, and state constrain a later receiver. It need not be flat, rectangular, topographic, or shared across modalities.

## Active sampling changes the evidence

The body is part of the measurement loop. Fixational eye movements can improve discrimination of fine spatial detail in tested conditions ([Rucci et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05866)). Sniffing recruits activity separable from odorant content in human imaging ([Sobel et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/32654)), and changing activation time within a sniff can change odor discrimination in mice ([Smear et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10521)).

Different signals also have different delays and local integration windows. Human audiovisual simultaneity judgments can recalibrate after repeated lag exposure ([Fujisaki et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1268)). Behavioral model fits further show that cue combination depends on an estimate of whether signals share a cause, not temporal proximity alone ([Kording et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000943)). These results do not establish one universal neural clock or show that tissue literally computes a particular Bayesian equation.

## Multisensory interaction is not one fusion room

Incongruent visual speech can change a reported syllable ([McGurk and MacDonald, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1038/264746a0)). Coordinated visual and tactile stimulation can alter reported hand ownership and position ([Botvinick and Cohen, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/35784)). Cat superior-colliculus neurons can integrate visual, auditory, and somatosensory input ([Meredith and Stein, 1986](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1986.56.3.640)).

These are bounded interaction results. They do not identify one global binding center, a completed volumetric reconstruction, a universal phase code, or a localized observer.

## SAN interpretation

SAN proposes that heterogeneous receptor and neural populations may participate in a repeating receive-transform-re-express loop. A relatively maintained **tonic action field** supplies the current receiver and action context; **phasic departures** alter that context and its downstream consequences. Across many recurrent populations, those updates may support a usable body-world working model.

This is a proposed operational role, not a decorative metaphor, but the cited component studies do not by themselves validate it. The network itself is the observer-action process: there is no second viewer waiting to inspect an internal screen.

A distinctive SAN mechanism would require more than decodability. It should improve held-out prediction over matched modality-specific and recurrent models, identify the biological receiver, survive controls for movement, rate, history, and connectivity, and produce a selective perturbation error with rescue.

## Cellular boundary

Ordinary chemical transmission proceeds through terminal depolarization, presynaptic calcium entry, probabilistic vesicle fusion, cleft concentration, receptor activation, and postsynaptic consequence. Vesicle count is not a universal fixed 0-to-3 alphabet, and ATP is not passed into a receiving neuron as the sensory message.
