# Tonic Context, Rare Deviations, and Tomographic Language

## The idea in plain language

Five repetitions of "hmm" establish an expectation. A following "hey" is informative partly
because it is unusual relative to that context. SAN uses this example to connect a maintained
tonic background, a phasic departure, and the later idea of a phase-wave differential.

```text
maintained receiver context + common events -> expected event model
  -> new, missing, or delayed event -> receiver-specific response
  -> recurrent transformation -> percept, prediction, or action
  -> updated context
```

In Shannon's formal sense, a less probable event has greater surprisal under a declared model:

```text
I(e | c) = -log2 p(e | c)
```

That does not mean that every rare event is meaningful. Shannon surprisal describes probability
under a model; it does not by itself establish biological salience, semantic meaning, or conscious
content. The tonic context is also not empty or useless; it is what makes the deviation measurable.
Meaning and consequence depend on the receiver's learned state, adaptation, task, and available
routes.

## Why "tomographic" appears here

Imagine describing a cube one side at a time. Each description is partial, but a receiver can
retain relations across the views and build a more stable model than any one description
contains. The October 2021 recording asks whether neural processing poses a similar problem:
different populations receive partial activity, transform it according to their anatomy and
learned state, and re-express it for other populations.

The June 2022 public revision sharpens the distinction. It rejects the idea that one complete
image is duplicated everywhere. The proposed percept is constructed over time from topologically
distributed and differently transformed contributions. SAN calls this reconstruction
tomography-like.

Distributed representations, recurrent processing, multisensory convergence, and transformation
tolerance are established operations. Li and DiCarlo showed that temporally contiguous visual
experience can reshape tolerant object representations. Meredith and Stein demonstrated
multisensory convergence in single superior-colliculus neurons, while Foxe and colleagues found
auditory-somatosensory convergence in human auditory cortex. NAPOT's stronger claim that the
relations among many transformed neural contributions implement an identifiable, action-usable
reconstruction remains a hypothesis.

## Multisensory and recurrent support

The source proposes that coincident sensory evidence can recruit a broader working model and that
the resulting population state can alter what lower-level input becomes effective next.
Experimental work supports bounded parts of this operation:

- multisensory inputs can converge and either enhance or suppress responses;
- synchronization can selectively change which input population communicates effectively with a
  target;
- pulvinar state can regulate transmission among visual cortical areas under attention;
- feedforward and feedback influences can use different frequency channels in a specific primate
  visual hierarchy;
- computational circuit models show how changing phase relations can reconfigure effective
  directionality without changing structural connections.

The comparison stops before treating the temporoparietal junction as one universal coincidence
detector, assuming that larger-scale activity always dominates smaller-scale activity, or
identifying synchronization alone with consciousness. SAN's extension is a recurrent scale
ladder: local events recruit population states, and population states constrain later local
excitability, routing, and response probability through measurable pathways.

## Keep the scales separate

| Scale | What can be measured | What this page does not claim |
|---|---|---|
| Cellular | membrane state, adaptation, spike timing, firing rate, and response selectivity | that one neuron contains or views a complete percept |
| Local circuit | inhibition, disinhibition, recurrent gain, coincidence, and population state | that one canonical microcircuit is universal across cortex |
| Interareal network | directed coupling, phase relations, thalamocortical routing, and distributed representational geometry | that one frequency band has a fixed meaning in every pathway |
| Organism and task | expectation, relevance, report, movement, and learning | that surprisal alone establishes meaning or awareness |

These levels can constrain one another without being interchangeable. In particular, an fMRI
semantic map is population-scale evidence and cannot establish single-neuron tomography, while a
cellular deviance response does not by itself establish a distributed percept.

## Coupling without merger

The source also asks how assemblies can interact while retaining boundaries. Two populations can
coordinate enough to exchange influence without becoming one population. They may synchronize
selectively, remain phase-locked at a nonzero offset, form separate coherent clusters, or couple
only during a task.

Established synchronization and effective-connectivity research supplies those component
operations. SAN adds the hypothesis that coherent subassemblies and structured dispersion jointly
organize rendered content and routing: compatible relations support communication, while phase
separation, inhibition, or competition preserve distinctions. This must be tested at a declared
cellular, circuit, field, or behavioral scale.

## A break in rhythm can be the message

[[gh-a0383z|a0383z]], publicly fixed on June 8, 2022, adds a useful extension: the informative event
may be a new coincidence, a missing expected event, a delay, or another break in the regular timing
relation. Ulanovsky and colleagues found stronger cat auditory-cortex responses to rare than common
instances of the same sound, while the thalamic neurons sampled in that study did not show the same
frequency-deviant probability sensitivity. Parras and colleagues later separated hierarchical
auditory prediction-error signals from adaptation. In mouse V1, Furutachi and colleagues causally
identified a cooperative pulvinar-input and VIP-interneuron circuit that selectively amplifies
unexpected visual features. These are pathway-specific mechanisms, not one universal novelty
detector. SAN asks whether a typed PWD explains additional receiver-relative consequences beyond
event probability, adaptation, firing rate, power, and conventional prediction-error models.

## How to test it

Present the same event as common or rare while independently varying meaning, task relevance, and
adaptation. Compare probability, firing-rate, power, recurrent-state, and typed-PWD models. Then
perturb relative phase while controlling the other variables and test for a receiver-specific
downstream effect.

For the NAPOT branch, present transformed views across position, size, modality, and time. Decode
the latent object or scene from each population and from their joined recurrent activity. Compare
feedforward, recurrent, predictive, representational-geometry, and NAPOT models. Perturb one
projection route and test whether the joined model fails in the specific relation that route was
predicted to supply. The stronger SAN account earns support only if it adds predictive and causal
value beyond the conventional models.
