A collaborative arXiv paper-in-progress combining [[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]]'s [[self-aware-networks]] (SAN) theory with Michael Miller's *Building Minds with Patterns* theory. The central thesis: AI systems should autonomously reconfigure their architectures in response to novel problems, mirroring biological brain adaptability.

## Four functional groupings

The paper organizes intelligence into four roles:

1. **Observation** — interaction with the environment via sensory and motor channels
2. **Coordination** — bridging desired states with current states (analogous to [[predictive-coding]])
3. **Reflection** — motivation, daydreaming, exploration, and play
4. **Consolidation** — memory compression and skill automaticity

## Memory and neural propositions

Memory is conceived as an active blackboard where stigmergic agents interact. The unit of memory is the *neural proposition* (or *scheme*) — a multipurpose structure that can represent both episodic and procedural knowledge.

This maps onto [[nonlinear-differential-continuous-approximation]] (NDCA) as the cross-scale communication mechanism, and onto [[cellular-oscillating-tomography]] (COT) for information encoding via oscillatory dynamics.

## Bio-mechanistic interpretability

The paper frames its goal as "Bio Mechanistic Interpretability" — connecting low-level neural engineering (dendritic [[vector-embeddings]], oscillatory phase differentials) with high-level AI architecture decisions. Cortical columns are treated as independent agents (following Jeff Hawkins' Thousand Brains theory), coordinated via [[brainwave-entrainment|brainwave entrainment]].
