# Five New SAN Research Directions: Papers 109-113

The September 23, 2026 releases extend the selected research portfolio from **108 to 113 paper
identities**. These five papers develop connected SAN questions through theory, explicit
mathematics, source-data checks, and bounded computational applications. Their shared interest
is how a history-shaped receiver turns present evidence into useful relations, action, and later learning.

They are **version 1.0 preprints, not peer-reviewed journal acceptances**. The summaries below are
based on their released manuscripts. The local census verifies the selected PDF files against
the current manifest; it does not independently replicate their experiments or recheck every
online repository. The 113-paper total includes the wider SAN/SIT research portfolio, not SAN alone.

## Paper 109: A connectome constrains the construction

**Self-Aware Networks in a Connectome-Constrained Fly Brain** asks how anatomy can constrain a
SAN construction without being mistaken for its complete dynamics. It connects receiving cells,
tonic organization, PWD, multimodal relations, action-return, and learning, while keeping fly
physiology distinct from mammalian cortical assignments.

The contribution includes version-bound anatomical and physiological comparisons, source-data
checks, and receiving-to-action diagnostics. One visual-memory projection has **79 inputs and
147 receiving cells** in the inspected export; the paper preserves the difference from the
source article's stated 74-input population. It distinguishes a change made before fixed mixing
from a gain applied afterward, and tests what actual returned observations add to action.

The result is an inspectable construction and identification program, not a completed biological
SAN fly or a consciousness demonstration. Conventional observers and unresolved data limitations
remain part of its record. [Read Paper 109](https://zenodo.org/records/22924326).

## Paper 110: A rendering has composition as well as detail

**Neural Rendering Composition and Granularity Tuning** separates four questions: which contents
participate, how their relations are bound, which distinctions survive, and how far an episode
extends through time. A rendering can include remembered, bodily, conceptual, emotional, and
prospective relations; it is not merely a visual picture with a resolution setting.

The paper joins conditional mathematical results, trained recurrent-network applications,
active-query experiments, and a proposed joint-measurement test. Its **thirteen retained figures**
belong to this paper, not automatically to the Book or Wiki image inventory. The reported temporal
task failure remains visible alongside the later active-acquisition results; an alternative
application is not presented as a retrospective repair of that earlier test.

This extends [[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]] toward operational questions about assembly,
access, and use. It does not establish a unique neural algorithm or completed biological mechanism.
[Read Paper 110](https://zenodo.org/records/22924401).

## Paper 111: Resolve an encounter before deciding to rewrite memory

**Selective Dissipation for Continual Learning** distinguishes temporary diagnosis from durable
learning. A history-shaped receiver first processes an encounter with retained weights fixed;
a separate controller can then propose, check, and accept a bounded change using verified consequences.

The current application brings a trained recurrent receiver, learned gates, protected update
transactions, and later-task evaluation together. **Eight frozen methods** encounter matched
220-event streams in four polynomial and four shifted-rule cases. The work asks whether the
course of a residual helps allocate useful retained changes beyond a controller with access
to the same relevant history.

The reported results do not demonstrate a general trajectory-control advantage. Stronger controls,
order interventions, retained-state checks, and later learning are kept in view. This is an
engineering investigation of selective change, not a claim that dissipation destroys knowledge
or creates information. [Read Paper 111](https://zenodo.org/records/22924457).

## Paper 112: Separate plasticity from the appearance of learning

**Selective Dissipation and Gated Plasticity in a History-Shaped Neural Canvas** gives the
biological question a three-part structure: identify a measured differential time course,
test its contribution under the relevant learning gates, and determine whether retained change
alters later reception.

BTSP, inhibition, eligibility, receiving competence, and dendritic state constrain the proposed
join. The paper includes exact counterexamples, a synthetic calibration, and a **one-cell
field-feasibility check** of a pinned public BTSP raw-data pair. The check establishes which
columns are available, not all the measurements required by the SAN hypothesis.

Its central practical distinction is between changing a stored relation and changing the
expression of an already stored repertoire. An apparently improved response alone does not
identify which occurred. This is a testable theory and analysis protocol, not empirical
confirmation of the complete mechanism. [Read Paper 112](https://zenodo.org/records/22924465).

## Paper 113: Keep continuity without losing order or ownership

**Temporal Ensembles in Self-Aware Networks** asks how history-dependent receiving components
could contribute in a coordinated sequence while earlier contributions remain usable. Activation,
recovery, retained content, coupling, downstream use, and the further experience hypothesis are
treated as separate questions.

Under declared hard-window assumptions, the paper derives the conditional requirements
`n >= k` and `nM >= (k - 1)R` for its regular-schedule example. These are model conditions,
not a universal biological law. Five bounded applications examine schedules, competing branches,
content retention, and alternative implementations. The local-state construction retains the
orders and ownership assignments in its fixed noiseless grid, while a nonoscillatory comparator
produces the same downstream outputs under the stated rules.

That comparison sharpens the experiment: the next test must identify a timing contribution
beyond generic state dependence. The paper also recovers graded 2011-2012 SAN antecedents
without backdating the later three-branch proposal. [Read Paper 113](https://zenodo.org/records/22924518).

## What these additions bring to SAN

Together the five papers extend the program in complementary directions: a species-constrained
construction, a vocabulary for multimodal assembly, a learning-control application, a causal
plasticity protocol, and a compartment-to-network temporal hypothesis. Their value is not that
five titles constitute five confirmations. It is that the broader proposal acquires more explicit
variables, implementations, controls, source checks, and discriminating next experiments.

[[phase-wave-differentials|PWD]] remains core throughout: a typed, receiver-relative departure
from a declared tonic pattern, including excitation, inhibition, delay, missing expected events,
and their interactions. An ordinary residual without that oscillatory reference is not silently
renamed a complete PWD implementation.
