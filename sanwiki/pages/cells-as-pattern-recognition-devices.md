This diagram makes the page's cell-scale SAN claim explicit: a platelet can be read as a biological detector, selector, and dispatcher. A local wound cue pattern binds receptor sets at the membrane, receptor coincidence selects a stored RNA or vesicle response, and the selected molecular output recruits nearby cells into a distributed response.

In SAN theory, the pattern-recognition logic attributed to neurons also applies at the
single-cell level. A platelet cell, for instance, must detect damage-site chemistry,
recognize patterns in surrounding signals, and dispatch responses — making it a
primitive neural network. It is *preloaded* with a vocabulary of RNA molecules (from
the parent cell), each addressing a specific environmental problem, and fires the
appropriate molecule when the corresponding pattern is detected.

## Extending SAN to Non-Neural Cells

The [[self-aware-network]] framework argues that pattern recognition, coincidence
detection, and message dispatch are not exclusive to neurons but are properties of
biological cells more generally. A single platelet is:

1. **A pattern recognizer** — detects specific chemical or mechanical patterns.
2. **A vocabulary holder** — carries pre-encoded RNA responses (like a coincidence
   gate loaded with firing criteria; cf. [[coincidence-detection-neural-bit]]).
3. **A dispatcher** — on pattern match, releases RNA molecules that recruit other
   cells (white blood cells, other platelets), forming a *distributed neural network*
   of non-neurons.

This is a direct application of the [[fractal-feedback-loop]] principle: the same
computational logic (sense → recognize → dispatch) appears at every biological scale.

## Group Oscillations and Electromagnetic Force

The note also describes how large groups of neurons oscillating together at a tonic
frequency (e.g., the ~20M neurons of the suprachiasmatic nucleus) generate a
coherent electromagnetic wave larger than any single neuron could produce. This shared
EM field modulates smaller-scale oscillations and vice versa — top-down and bottom-up
regulation in the oscillatory hierarchy.

The electromagnetic output of synchronized neuron groups is what allows neural
activity to drive muscle movement: groups coordinate protein contraction via the
amplified EM force of collective oscillation. See [[tonic-phasic-oscillation]].
