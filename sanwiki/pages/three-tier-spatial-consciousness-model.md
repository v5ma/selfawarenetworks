**Three-Tier Spatial Consciousness Model** proposes that conscious experience is always composed of three simultaneously active spatial levels: (1) macro-scale spatial layout — which "room" or region of space one occupies; (2) meso-scale [[gh-a0206z|self]]-position — one's orientation and location within that region; and (3) micro-scale content — the detailed objects and stimuli immediately surrounding the [[gh-a0206z|self]] from that position.

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] argues that these three tiers are always co-present in moment-to-moment experience: "There's always a place… there's where I am relative to the space I'm in… and the content is like okay what's all the stuff around me."

## Why three tiers

The model offers an evolutionary explanation: mobile organisms require all three levels to survive. Without macro spatial layout (which room) they cannot navigate; without meso position (where in this space) they cannot orient; without micro content (what is nearby) they cannot act on immediate threats and opportunities. Losing any one tier degrades adaptive behavior.

The brain achieves this via [[multiscale-fractal-neural-oscillators]] — small patterns (micro content) repeat at larger scales (meso, macro), creating [[multi-scale-causality]] between representational layers. Information shared across scales allows real-time updates of the high-level room map when micro-level details change.

## Relation to SAN concepts

Each tier corresponds to a different temporal frequency band: macro spatial layout is stable across long intervals and maintained by slow oscillations; meso position updates with movement and is maintained by intermediate frequencies; micro content updates rapidly (ms–s) via fast [[neural-oscillations]]. This maps directly onto the [[tonic-phasic-neural-oscillation]] distinction — tonic firing holds room context, phasic bursts encode momentary content.

The three-tier model connects to [[napot-theory]] in that each level represents a distinct population of [[phase-temporal-spatial-patterns]] (the fundamental building blocks of representation in [[self-aware-networks]]). It also grounds the MemPalace construct: [[v5ma:mempalace]] uses rooms as navigable macro nodes, closely mirroring this spatial hierarchy.

## Topological isomorphisms across scales

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] further proposes that the brain transfers knowledge between tiers via **topological isomorphisms** — the same relational pattern (e.g., an ant colony ↔ human society) can be encoded at micro, meso, and macro scales simultaneously, enabling data updated at one scale to propagate to others. This is the mechanism behind [[functional-fractal-isomorphism]].
