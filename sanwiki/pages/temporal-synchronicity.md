# Temporal Synchronicity

**Temporal Synchronicity** is the simultaneous co-occurrence in time of two or more neural firing patterns whose joint detection binds them into a single experiential event within a [[self-aware-network]]. It is the relating factor that allows distributed [[spatiotemporal-neural-firing-patterns]] — the sound of a dog and the visual memory of a dog — to be experienced as one unified percept rather than as two unrelated channels, and in [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks it is what a [[dendritic-coincidence-detection|dendritic coincidence detector]] is built to register at the heart of the [[oscillatory-binding-problem]].

## Post-SAN

The closest published cognate in mainstream [[neuroscience]] is *temporal binding by synchronous oscillations* — the broader [[oscillatory-binding-problem|binding problem]] literature on [[gamma-oscillation|gamma-band]] coherence and population synchrony. Where that literature treats synchrony as a population-level statistical property of [[brainwave-vortex-attractor|cortical oscillations]], SAN's "temporal synchronicity" is more concrete and more local: it is a per-event detection performed by individual [[dendritic-coincidence-detection|dendritic coincidence detectors]] on [[apical-dendrites|apical compartments]], and the binding it produces is a single discrete [[coincidence-pattern-neural-bit|coincidence-pattern bit]] rather than a continuous correlation across populations. The testable difference is local rather than global: SAN predicts that disrupting the [[apical-dendrites]] of a [[cortical-column-oscillatory-collaboration|cortical column]] should impair cross-modal binding even when global [[gamma-oscillation|gamma]]-band synchrony is preserved, while pure oscillatory-binding accounts predict the opposite ordering. This is one of the cleanest places where SAN's framing makes a falsifiable [[prediction]] the published binding literature does not.
