# Feedback Loops, Strange Loops, and the Self-Model

## The problem in ordinary language

Living systems act, encounter the consequences of their actions, and use those consequences to
change what they do next. That circular organization is fundamental to nervous systems, but it is
not yet an explanation of a self.

A thermostat has feedback. A recurrent neural network has signals that return to earlier units.
A person can think about a personal trait. A brain can estimate where its body is, whether it
caused an event, and what it is likely to do next. Douglas Hofstadter's strange loop describes a
symbolic hierarchy that turns back on itself and produces the symbol called "I." These are related
ideas, but they are not interchangeable.

The central SAN question is more specific:

> How can a distributed brain use sensation, memory, prediction, action, and returning
> consequences to maintain an action-usable model of its own body, perspective, agency, and
> continuity without placing a second observer inside the brain?

This page owns the functional and historical route to that question. The stronger physical
identity proposal now has its own root page:
[[field-cell-self-bioelectric-information-entity|The Field-Cell Self]]. That branch asks whether
the continuing self is the reciprocal bioelectric process through which cells generate, detect,
remember, and alter a shared field-organized state.

SAN's answer developed in stages. The earliest source describes a learning loop that learns
itself. Neo Mind Cycle then made that loop an engineered neurofeedback practice. Later sources
move from the whole organism to neurons, dendrites, recurrent arrays, oscillatory timing, and
distributed rendering. The mature proposal is valuable only if those stages remain dated and if
ordinary recurrence is not renamed consciousness by definition.

## One word, several mechanisms

### Feedback

Feedback means that an output or action changes a later input or state. It can stabilize a system,
amplify a deviation, support control, or permit learning. Feedback exists far beyond conscious
brains.

### Recurrent or reentrant neural processing

Recurrent processing means that neural activity returns through horizontal or feedback
connections instead of moving only once from sensory input to output. Reentry can supply context,
figure-ground organization, prediction, attention, or later perceptual processing. It does not
automatically represent the organism itself.

### Sensorimotor closure

Sensorimotor closure is a loop in which action changes the body or environment, those changes
alter sensory input, and the nervous system uses the returning signal. A mirror, a microphone,
neurofeedback display, reaching movement, and ordinary vision can all participate in such loops.

### Self-related content

Self-related content is information about one's traits, body, memories, perspective, goals, or
social identity. A self-reference task can isolate one kind of self-related judgment without
capturing the whole self.

### Self-reference

A process is self-referential in the operational sense used here when a representation of the
system's own state, body, action, rule, or history is used in a later computation. Merely writing
`next_state = F(current_state, input)` is recurrence, not yet meaningful self-reference.

### Self-model

A self-model is a usable estimate of variables such as body ownership, self-location, viewpoint,
agency, capability, autobiographical continuity, and likely action. These variables can be
distributed and partly dissociable. They do not require one inner screen or one anatomical
"self center."

### Strange loop

Hofstadter's strange loop is a special abstract feedback loop in which representation crosses
levels and the system develops the high-level symbol "I." It is a conceptual predecessor to SAN.
It is not evidence that SAN's dendritic, phase, or NAPOT mechanisms are biologically correct.

### Rule-changing or metaplastic loop

A rule-changing loop alters parameters or update rules, not just the current state. Synaptic
plasticity, metaplasticity, and adaptive control can do this under specific mechanisms. They must
not be inferred merely because the present state is an argument to the next-state function.

### Conscious access and experience

Conscious access, report, and phenomenal experience are empirical targets. The presence of
feedback, recurrence, self-related content, or a self-model can support hypotheses about them, but
none should be declared sufficient by vocabulary alone.

## The 2011 starting point: a loop that learns itself

`a0051z.md` is internally dated September 21, 2011. `a0072z.md` says it contains material from a
conversation before that date. Both preserve the same central passage, so they are two records of
one source atom rather than independent confirmations.

The example begins with a video camera pointed at its own screen. It then adds a learning system
modeled on the neocortex, statistical memory, prediction, brain activity, muscles, senses, and the
returning consequences of action. The resulting formulation is a **learning loop that learns
itself**.

The durable contribution is the joined causal chain:

```text
experience -> learned memory -> prediction -> neural and bodily action
-> changed sensory input -> updated learned model
```

The self enters when the loop learns regularities about its own body, actions, predictions, and
returning signals. This is more specific than saying "everything is a loop," but it remains an
early functional account. It does not contain the later terms PWD, NAPOT, or Neural Rendering.

## The 2012 bridge: the brain receives information about itself

Three 2012 records turn the general loop into a practical and public program.

`a0017z.md`, internally dated July 30, 2012, explains that Neo Mind Cycle is not merely feedback
but a feedback **cycle**: EEG-derived light and sound become new sensory input to the same brain

`a0326z.md` gives the clearest short description: the Neurofeedback Salon creates a loop that gives

does not become the observer; it changes the information available to the participant's own
nervous system.

`a0339z.md`, internally dated September 7, 2012, describes the observer as a thought pattern
forming another thought pattern about thought patterns through incoming senses and outgoing
actions. It also introduces a dissipative cycle and a prediction that incorporates its own
development.

Together these sources add three important constraints:

1. The loop is embodied: senses and actions matter.
2. The loop can be experimentally altered by changing the feedback channel.
3. The represented self is a changing prediction or pattern, not a separate internal witness.

## The 2022 render-detect extension

Several 2022 sources transform the earlier feedback account into the mature SAN hypothesis.

- `a0049z.md` describes a learning system that processes, models, predicts, reviews its
  predictions, selects, inhibits, and acts. Its anti-panpsychism argument is that generic physical
  responsiveness is not the same as an information-learning feedback architecture.
- `a0310z.md` describes phasic changes altering a tonic baseline as the brain detecting and
  rendering its own information, with feedback loops making renderings available to the larger
  brain.
- `a0319z.md` rejects a central canvas, describes distributed neural pathways and feedback loops,
  and states that the brain's elements alternately render and detect renderings. It joins the
  loop to prediction and motor action.

This is where SAN becomes more than a generic strange-loop comparison. Its stronger claim is that
relative timing, duration, phase, waveform, and spatial distribution help carry the represented
content through recurrent neural arrays.

That stronger claim remains a hypothesis. The archive establishes that the proposal exists and
how it developed. It does not substitute intellectual chronology for causal neuroscience.

## What recurrence research supports

Visual neuroscience supplies direct evidence that feedforward and recurrent processing can have
different functions and mechanisms.

- [Fahrenfort, Scholte, and Lamme, 2007](https://www.fahrenfort.com/downloads/2007%20Fahrenfort%20JoCN.pdf)
  found preserved early feedforward visual activity but disrupted later reentrant activity when
  masking reduced figure detection to chance in their task.
- [Self et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3390882/) pharmacologically
  separated parts of feedforward-driven activity from recurrent figure-ground modulation in
  macaque V1, with different contributions from AMPA and NMDA receptors.
- [Bastos et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.018) reported theta/gamma-
  dominated feedforward and beta-dominated feedback influences across the tested macaque visual
  areas and task.
- [Michalareas et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4871751/) reported
  gamma-dominated feedforward and alpha-beta-dominated feedback influences among selected human
  visual areas measured with MEG.

These results support recurrence as a real, structured, and frequency-sensitive part of visual
processing. They do not establish:

- one universal frequency code for every brain region, species, state, or task;
- that recurrence by itself is sufficient for consciousness;
- that the SAN tonic/phasic vocabulary is identical to these preparation-specific findings; or
- that phase-wave differentials or neural tomography have been measured as SAN defines them.

## What self-related research supports

The empirical self is not one indivisible variable.

- [Kelley et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1162/08989290260138672) used an fMRI trait-judgment
  task to distinguish self-referential processing from judgments about another person or lexical
  features.
- [Botvinick and Cohen, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/35784) showed that coordinated vision,
  touch, and proprioception can alter felt ownership of a limb.
- [Blanke et al., 2002](https://www.nature.com/articles/419269a) repeatedly induced illusory
  whole-body displacement and transformations in one patient through focal electrical
  stimulation, implicating multisensory body integration.
- [Park et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27511016/) found that neural responses to
  heartbeats covaried with experimentally altered bodily self-identification in a full-body
  illusion.

These studies make self-related traits, ownership, self-location, and interoceptive body mapping
measurable. They do not identify one complete self-model, one consciousness center, or one neural
mechanism sufficient for first-person experience.

## Hebb belongs before SAN's stronger claim

Hebb's cell assemblies and phase sequences are important predecessors because they describe how
coactive cells can form organized ensembles and how one assembly can activate another in a
sequence. That provides a neural route from local learning to recurrent organization.

SAN's added questions concern:

- whether phase, duration, and receiver-relative timing carry information beyond rate and weight;
- how distributed sensory and motor models become one action-usable self-world state;
- how neural arrays alternately receive, transform, and project learned patterns; and
- whether NAPOT's tomographic geometry predicts data better than recurrent-network,
  attractor, predictive-processing, or workspace alternatives.

Hebb is therefore a necessary predecessor and comparison, not evidence that SAN's added mechanism
has already been demonstrated.

## A corrected SAN model

Let:

- `x_t` be the distributed neural and bodily state at time `t`;
- `u_t` be incoming sensory and interoceptive evidence;
- `a_t` be action;
- `m_t` be an explicit or implicit self-model containing body, perspective, agency, capability,
  and continuity variables;
- `theta_t` be the parameters that determine how the system updates.

A minimal closed loop is:

```text
x_(t+1) = F(x_t, u_t, a_t; theta_t)
a_t     = pi(x_t, m_t)
u_(t+1) = E(world_t, body_t, a_t)
m_(t+1) = G(m_t, x_(t+1), u_(t+1), a_t)
```

Plastic or rule-changing dynamics require a separate equation:

```text
theta_(t+1) = H(theta_t, x_t, u_t, a_t)
```

This separation prevents three common errors:

1. `x_t` influencing `x_(t+1)` is recurrence, not automatically self-reference.
2. A model becomes self-related only when variables about the system, body, action, or history
   are represented and used.
3. Parameters changing through learning is not the same as the system literally rewriting an
   arbitrary update rule.

The bounded SAN synthesis is:

1. Dendritic and circuit configurations select spatiotemporal input patterns.
2. Recurrent pathways make prior activity and higher-level context available again.
3. Prediction and action change what evidence returns to the system.
4. Distributed self-model variables help choose and evaluate action without a homunculus.
5. Learning changes the substrate and future routing.
6. SAN further hypothesizes that receiver-relative phase, duration, magnitude, and spatial
   pattern contribute to the rendered self-world state.
7. NAPOT is the stronger proposal that repeated receive-transform-project operations bind those
   partial states into a distributed volumetric representation.

The first five steps can be compared with established control, recurrent-processing, predictive,
active-inference, engram, and self-model literature. Steps six and seven require SAN-specific
measurements and model comparison.

## Discriminating experiments

- Compare feedforward-only, recurrent non-self, explicit self-model, and metaplastic models on the
  same embodied task.
- Break the action-to-sensation loop while preserving much of the incoming stimulus and test what
  happens to agency, prediction, and adaptive control.
- Decode body ownership, self-location, viewpoint, agency, autobiographical identity, and external
  object content separately.
- Perturb recurrent timing while approximately preserving feedforward drive and mean firing rate.
- Test whether phase, action-potential duration, waveform, and receiver-relative timing improve
  held-out prediction beyond rate, power, connectivity, movement, and arousal.
- Compare visual, auditory, somatosensory, interoceptive, and motor feedback rather than assuming
  one visual-cortex mechanism generalizes everywhere.
- Require every proposed self variable to have an identifiable downstream consumer in action,
  prediction, report, or learning.
- Compare SAN against recurrent processing, predictive processing, active inference, global
  workspace, attention schema, and embodied self-model baselines.
- Test conscious access and report separately from the mere presence of feedback.
- Preregister the perturbations, model-selection criteria, and failure conditions.

## Feedback makes a correlate available to the self-model

[[gh-a0563z|a0563z]], [[gh-a0569z|a0569z]], and [[gh-a0580z|a0580z]] connect physical events,
learned meaning, electrophysiological change, and returned feedback. A neurofeedback display does
not reveal the complete self. It makes one selected correlate available to a recurrent
brain-body-person loop, allowing the person to learn whether that signal covaries with an
experience or action.

The self-model is therefore neither an inner spectator nor one electrode value. It is the changing
distributed system that experiences, measures through its instruments, learns the association,
acts, and receives the consequence.
