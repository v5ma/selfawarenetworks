# Affective Computing

![Affective Computing oscillatory state inference diagram](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/affective-computing/affective-computing-oscillatory-state-inference-20260627-v2.png)

This SAN image shows affective computing as a bounded oscillatory state-inference pipeline: XR, BCI, eye, body, heart, breath, EEG, and fNIRS signals are time-aligned, phase-compared, and used to estimate a hidden affective state. The boundary is important: the system estimates spatiotemporal state patterns; it does not directly read private mental content.

**Affective Computing** is a field of [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence]] and human-computer interaction that uses sensors, machine learning models, and physiological monitoring to detect, interpret, and respond to human emotional states. In the SAN corpus it appears as a near-term application layer that sits downstream of [[brain-computer-interfaces|brain-computer interfaces]] and [[neural-rendering|neural rendering]] technology — a practical realization of [[self-aware-networks-theory|SAN theory]]'s claim that internal states such as [[emotions-as-spatiotemporal-patterns|emotions]] are computable spatiotemporal patterns accessible to external systems ([[gh-a0121z|a0121z]]--md, line 107).

The SAN notes reference TechCrunch's observation that "sensors and other devices are also getting very good at observing and interpreting facial features, body posture, gestures" as evidence that affective computing is already tractable ([[gh-a0121z|a0121z]]--md, line 107). More ambitiously, extended reality hardware with full-body tracking enables a system to use "sensors to track the entire body, the eyes, the heart rate, your breathing, to do affective computing, prediction your emotion, intention, and even diagnose you for the diseases" ([[gh-a0214z|a0214z]]--md, line 169). This positions [[augmented-reality|augmented reality]] headsets and XR platforms like [[webxr-bci-integration|WebXR BCI integration]] as distributed biosensor arrays rather than mere display devices — the same hardware that renders a [[brainwave|brainwave]]-driven experience can simultaneously infer the affective state generating that experience.

The integration pathway described in the SAN corpus is multi-modal: "WebXR technology such as Cryptovoxels with BCI and Eye tracking the computer could do what is called affective computing — predicting your emotions, intentions" ([[gh-a0214z|a0214z]]--md, line 190). Eye tracking feeds into [[four-dimensional-phase-tracking|four-dimensional phase tracking]] of visual [[attention]]; heart-rate and EEG data from [[eeg-xr-neurofeedback-bci|EEG-XR neurofeedback BCI]] layers provide physiological correlates of arousal and valence; and [[fnirs-functional-near-infrared-spectroscopy|fNIRS]] and other [[neuroimaging-next-generation|next-generation neuroimaging]] channels supply hemodynamic context. When these streams are combined with "deep neural networks and affective computing combined with biosensors and new medical imaging technologies," the resulting system can "take the research of studying" affect beyond behavioral observation into the neural substrate itself ([[gh-a0220z|a0220z]]--md, line 337). The expected output — emotion, intention, and disease diagnosis — maps onto the SAN hierarchy in which [[conscious-awareness|conscious awareness]] is a high-level inference built from lower-level [[pattern-neurons|pattern]] activations and [[senses|sensory]] streams. In this framing affective computing is not reading emotions "off the face" but decoding the oscillatory [[emotions-as-spatiotemporal-patterns|spatiotemporal pattern]] that constitutes the emotion in the nervous system.
