The brain is modeled as a **dynamic spectral graph** in which consciousness unfolds as a sequence of
temporal frames. Concepts appear as nodes at spatially distributed locations; when the graph learns
how two features relate, it bridges them through [[temporal-synchronicity]] rather than through
fixed anatomical wiring.

## Mechanism

[[Cortical-columns]] each capture a unique "slice" of a unified pattern. The invariant, oscillatory
frames shared across regions — not the oscillations themselves — form the substrate of
[[consciousness-as-frames]]. A pattern arising in one cortical column (e.g., sawdust) can transfer
to another (e.g., rain) because the invariant structure is column-agnostic; the columns differ only
in their bias toward particular textures, materials, or concept traits.

The connecting factor across all columns is **time** — temporal synchronicity is the mechanism by
which disparate sensory inputs are integrated into coherent perception.

## Learned Spatial Perception

A core implication is that spatial perception is **not innate**. The graph has no built-in metric
for space; space-time is learned content, just as a computer has no inherent sense of space. This
resolves an apparent paradox: a contentless observer — one stripped of learned constructs — has
no sense of spatial separation. Experiences with Salvia divinorum and DMT that dissolve spatial
boundaries are cited as first-person evidence: when the learned construct of space collapses, the
separation between objects collapses with it.

## Contentless Observer

The observer-[[gh-a0206z|self]] is inherently **contentless** — space-time is content *inside* the mind, not the
mind itself. Analogously, a blind person does not experience darkness; they simply have no
sense of light or dark. The observer stripped of learned content has no sense of space either.
This frames the mind's spatial representation as entirely dependent on [[oscillatory-frames]] and
temporal synchronization across the graph, rather than on any intrinsic spatial faculty.

## Relation to SAN

This spectral-graph framing is a precursor to [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation
Theory), which elaborates how oscillatory projections across neural arrays produce the specific
phase patterns that constitute perception. It also grounds [[self-aware-network]] in graph theory:
a network that models itself does so by bridging [[gh-a0206z|self]]-referential nodes through the same
temporal-synchronicity mechanism described here.
