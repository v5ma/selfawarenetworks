# Minimum Observer Circuit

The **minimum observer circuit** is an open empirical question, not a known three-neuron threshold.
SAN historically used a three-node comparator as a logical motif: two distinguishable inputs and a
third element whose state depends on their relation. That motif can implement comparison, but
comparison alone does not establish self-awareness or consciousness.

## Candidate comparator motif

A minimal experimental circuit might include:

1. source state A;
2. source state B;
3. a receiver whose response differs when the A-B relation differs;
4. an output route that changes later processing;
5. feedback that changes the circuit's next state.

The fourth and fifth requirements matter. A detector that responds selectively but has no
consequential output is not yet an observer-action loop. A circuit that changes behavior may still
implement unconscious control.

## Scale firewall

- A dendritic branch can compare inputs without representing a whole object.
- A three-neuron loop can detect a relation without possessing a self-model.
- A local recurrent circuit can regulate a task without becoming a conscious subject.
- A cortical column is not a standard minimum unit of consciousness.
- Scaling the number of phase patterns does not automatically scale phenomenal richness.

The same receive-transform-re-express motif can recur across scales, but the evidence burden changes
at each scale.

## What a minimum observer would require

At minimum, SAN needs operational criteria for:

- differentiated input;
- receiver-specific transformation;
- memory or state dependence;
- recurrent accessibility;
- self/world or body/action consequence;
- persistence across a declared interval;
- selective perturbation and rescue;
- separation from unconscious reflex, homeostasis, and generic feedback control.

Until those criteria are met, the three-node circuit is a candidate comparator and engineering
analogy, not an atomic unit of consciousness.

## Relation to NAPOT

[[napot-overview|NAPOT]] may use cellular and local comparison motifs as contributors to a larger
reconstruction and update cycle. A microcircuit does not perform tomography merely because it
detects phase or coincidence. Tomography requires multiple partial projections, an identifiable
target relation, reconstruction, and perturbation evidence.
