# Attention Schema Reinterpretation

**[[attention-schema|Attention Schema]] Reinterpretation** names the [[self-aware-networks-theory|Self-Aware Networks]] framework's specific critique and revision of Michael Graziano's [[attention-schema-theory|Attention Schema Theory (AST)]]. Where Graziano's original account treats the brain's model of its own [[attention]] as a deliberate simplification — an inaccurate sketch that the brain constructs for control purposes, and whose distorted character is precisely what generates the subjective experience of [[Consciousness]] — the SAN reinterpretation argues that the brain's [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model of [[attention]] is not a distortion but a structurally accurate phase-coded representation, and that [[Consciousness]] is not an illusion produced by inaccuracy but a real emergent property of the underlying [[neural-oscillations]] dynamics themselves.

The deflationary move in Graziano's original AST is that consciousness is "just" a simplified model, which makes phenomenal experience a kind of controlled confabulation. The SAN reinterpretation rejects this deflationary framing on empirical grounds drawn from [[napot-overview|NAPOT]]: the distributed holographic encoding of [[Cortical-columns]] (see [[attention-schema-holographic]]) cannot be meaningfully described as "simplified," because holographic encoding is maximally redundant rather than compressed. Each [[micro-columns|micro-column]] contributes a phase sample to the global interference pattern, and the fidelity of the aggregate pattern scales with the number of contributing units rather than degrading through abstraction. The [[Brainwaves]] carrying this pattern — gamma for local encoding, theta and alpha for cross-region synchrony — are not abstractions of [[attention]] but its physical implementation. The schema therefore is the [[attention]], not a model of it from the outside, and this identity dissolves the explanatory gap that the original AST attempted to bridge by appealing to inaccuracy.

The reinterpretation also addresses what the SAN framework calls the "control problem" of the original AST: if the schema is inaccurate, how can it reliably guide the very process it misrepresents? In the SAN account, the [[attention-schema|attention schema]] is coherent enough to allow metacognition and voluntary [[intention]] precisely because it is encoded at full resolution in phase relationships across [[Cortical-columns]], with [[inhibitory-circuits]] sculpting the active assembly and [[thalamus]] synchronizing inter-areal coherence. The [[prefrontal-cortex]] gains genuine executive leverage because it is reading an accurate distributed representation, not a caricature. This makes [[working-memory]] maintenance a fundamentally different operation than in the original AST: rather than holding a simplified sketch of attentional state, working memory holds a phase-locked subspace of the full holographic pattern, and degradation of that coherence is what phenomenally registers as [[attention]] lapsing or [[attention-awareness-consciousness-triad|awareness]] dimming.

The implications for [[neuroscience]] and [[artificial-intelligence]] diverge sharply between the original and reinterpreted theories. Under AST proper, building a conscious machine would require engineering a system that generates a simplified, inaccurate [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model of its [[attention]], which is a counterintuitive design target. Under the SAN reinterpretation, the requirement is instead to instantiate genuinely holographic phase-coherent representations that are [[gh-a0206z|self]]-referential at every scale — a more principled engineering goal, though technically demanding. [[deep-learning-neural-networks]] that employ transformer [[attention]] heads implement nothing like holographic phase coding; they produce learned weighting functions without global phase coherence, and the SAN framework treats this as the reason why such architectures, however capable, do not exhibit anything analogous to phenomenal [[Consciousness]]. The [[oscillatory-binding-problem|binding problem]], the [[global-workspace-theory]] broadcast requirement, and the [[integrated-information-theory]] integration measure all look different under the reinterpretation: each maps onto a specific feature of the distributed phase pattern rather than onto a centralized control or complexity metric. [[brain-computer-interfaces]] attempting to read intentional state must therefore target the coherent phase pattern rather than localized [[action-potential]] firing rates, and [[predictive-coding]] models must be extended to include phase as a first-class representational variable before they can capture the full structure of the reinterpreted [[attention-schema|attention schema]].
