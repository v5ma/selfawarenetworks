# Gamma Wave Consideration Sandwich

The **Gamma Consideration Sandwich (GCS)** is SAN's functional model for coordinating sensory evidence, thought, proprioception, and action. Alpha-associated primary-sensory-side input and beta-associated prefrontal thought/top-down input form the **Layer-4/input-side top bun**. Layer-2/3 gamma performs **consideration and proprioceptive coordination** in the middle. Layer-5 theta/gamma carries **action-side output and returned body input** in the **bottom bun**.

> **Medical boundary:** These are the roles assigned by GCS, not exclusive frequency-to-function laws. Alpha, beta, gamma, and theta occur in multiple regions and functions; laminar organization differs across cortical areas; and prefrontal cortex is not uniformly granular. Returned body information also travels through established peripheral, spinal, brainstem, cerebellar, thalamic, and cortical pathways rather than through Layer 5 alone. The GCS must therefore be tested circuit by circuit. See [[phase-language-firewall]], [[body-model-as-control-surface]], and [[san-scale-ladder-and-claim-boundaries]].

## The original SAN metaphor

The SAN vocabulary groups three functional levels and a recurrent return:

| SAN component | Intended role | Medical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Layer-4/input-side top bun: alpha-associated primary-sensory-side input | Carries current sensory evidence and sensory context toward consideration | Alpha has many circuit- and task-dependent roles; GCS emphasizes this sensory-side role without making it exclusive. |
| Layer-4/input-side top bun: beta-associated prefrontal thought/top-down input | Carries thought, goals, expectations, and top-down context toward consideration | Prefrontal beta has empirical links to working memory and top-down control, but beta is neither confined to prefrontal cortex nor synonymous with thought. Layer 4 here is a functional input-side label; prefrontal anatomy is not uniformly granular. |
| Middle: Layer-2/3 gamma | Performs consideration and proprioceptive coordination across the incoming relations | Gamma can participate in local processing, communication, and sensorimotor integration; the complete GCS operation remains a SAN hypothesis. |
| Bottom bun: Layer-5 theta/gamma | Carries action-side output and returned body input in the functional GCS role | Layer 5 provides major cortical output routes, but returned body input reaches and engages cortical circuits through distributed peripheral, spinal, brainstem, cerebellar, thalamic, and cortical pathways rather than through Layer 5 alone. |

The word *sandwich* names this arrangement: two distinct top-bun inputs converge on a gamma middle; the result engages a theta/gamma action-and-body bottom bun; bodily and environmental consequences then supply the next round of input.

## A medically bounded interpretation

1. Alpha-associated primary-sensory-side activity supplies the sensory part of the top bun.
2. Beta-associated prefrontal activity supplies thought, expectation, goal, and top-down input as the other part of the top bun.
3. Layer-2/3 gamma activity performs the proposed consideration and proprioceptive-coordination operation.
4. Layer-5 theta/gamma activity carries the action-side output and returned body input in the functional bottom-bun role; anatomically, those directions use distinct distributed routes.
5. Distributed motor and bodily pathways produce movement, vocal, autonomic, and environmental consequences that update the next cycle.

This is a distributed loop. GCS gives each band/layer a defined functional emphasis, while the biological implementation remains distributed and nonexclusive.

## The cortical traffic beneath the sandwich

GCS becomes more concrete when it is placed over Micah's earlier and later information-flow work.
The March 2021 [[flow-of-information-in-the-brain|Flow of Information in the Brain]] recording asks
how sensory signals are transformed through thalamus, cortex, dendrites, cellular output, recurrent
feedback, and artificial re-expression. The January 24, 2024
[Cortical Dynamics & Neurocircuitry](https://www.svgn.io/p/cortical-dynamics-and-neurocircuitry)
article adds a proposed laminar, corticothalamic, and long-range traffic map. The current synthesis
joins those source stages to action and reafferent sensory return without backdating that full loop.

The three pages divide the explanatory work:

- [[flow-of-information-in-the-brain|The Flow of Information in the Brain]] supplies the recovered sensory-reconstruction, transformation, recurrence, and artificial-re-expression ancestor; this page's joined current synthesis extends that traffic toward action and reafferent return.
- [[neural-tuning|Neural Tuning]] proposes how learned state, deep-layer recurrence, inhibition, and neuromodulation change which routes and receivers are effective.
- **GCS** proposes how receiver-relative oscillatory timing may coordinate sensory evidence, maintained context, consideration, action, and returned consequence across that traffic graph.

```text
TOP BUN
Layer-4/input-side alpha primary-sensory-side input
  + beta prefrontal thought/top-down input
  -> Layer-2/3 gamma consideration and proprioceptive coordination
  -> Layer-5 theta/gamma action-side output
  <-> returned body input
  -> distributed motor, vocal, autonomic, and environmental consequence
```

This traffic model also needs a conscious-membership boundary. Phase compatibility may help a
candidate interact with an active assembly, but gamma power or coherence alone does not admit content
to consciousness. The current SAN synthesis requires recurrent recruitment, reciprocal influence,
relative causal dominance, and a new capacity for flexible consequence. Inhibition, detuning,
competition, adaptation, or loss of reciprocal influence can let content leave while learned memory
constraints remain. See [[latent-pattern-conscious-rendering-transition|From Latent Pattern to
Conscious Rendering]].

The 2024 article's vertical/horizontal and front/back language is a teaching geometry, not a claim
that all cortical traffic follows one anatomical axis or one six-layer sequence. The relevant
laminar origins, termination patterns, thalamic relays, and oscillatory relationships must be
specified for each circuit and preparation.

The top bun's **Layer-4** label is therefore source-faithful functional geometry, not permission to
draw one canonical granular circuit everywhere. A biological test may place sensory alpha in a named
thalamorecipient Layer-4 population, but it must trace the chosen prefrontal beta pathway through its
actual source and target layers.

## Tonic canvas and phasic ink

GCS becomes clearer when the SAN **tonic canvas / phasic ink** distinction is kept receiver-relative. Tonic activity is the receiving population's current operating state. Phasic activity is a change that matters relative to that state. Either may contain several frequencies, and a signal can be tonic for one receiving timescale while phasic for another. Within GCS specifically, gamma is the proposed consideration-and-coordination middle; this does not make every gamma event a PWD or every PWD a gamma event.

## A direct modern comparator: spatial oscillatory control

Miller Lab studies provide a close but bounded comparison with GCS. In macaque prefrontal cortex, spatial alpha/beta patterns changed with task context and were inversely related to where content-related spiking was expressed, leading the authors to describe the oscillations as inhibitory spatial "stencils" ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.11.072)). In frontal eye field, working-memory readout varied with theta phase and target location, consistent with a spatially organized sampling process ([Han et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.09.031)). These results support preparation-specific oscillatory gating of cortical space and readout. They do not establish GCS's historical sensory-thought-action assignment, a universal layer/band map, tonic canvas and phasic ink as fixed frequency bands, or the complete SAN observer-action mechanism.

## What would test the proposal

A useful GCS experiment must name the circuit, layers, task, behavioral variable, frequency range, and direction of influence. It should test whether phase- or band-specific coupling predicts and causally changes sensorimotor updating after controlling for firing rate, broadband power, movement, common input, arousal, and connectivity. A finding that gamma, beta, or alpha changes with a task is not enough by itself.
