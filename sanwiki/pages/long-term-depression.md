# Long Term Depression

**Long Term Depression** (LTD) is a persistent, activity-dependent decrease in synaptic efficacy. Multiple LTD families exist, with induction and expression mechanisms that vary by receptor, pathway, synapse, age, species, and protocol; low-frequency stimulation is one experimental protocol rather than the definition of LTD. [Self-Aware Networks](self-aware-networks.md) interprets some weakening as **oscillatory decoupling**, but phase offset, forgetting, inhibitory untangling, and cosmological isomorphism are SAN proposals rather than established LTD biology.

Both can reduce AMPAR-mediated transmission, but they are not interchangeable or universal.

> **Medical-status note:** LTD is not synonymous with low-frequency input, phase mismatch, forgetting, route pruning, or cosmic void formation. The historical SAN source language below is theory provenance.

## Medical evidence boundary

- **Established phenomenon:** LTD is a persistent activity-dependent decrease in synaptic efficacy. Low-frequency stimulation, spike-pairing protocols, receptor agonists, and other manipulations can induce different LTD forms in bounded preparations.
- **NMDAR-LTD example:** In common hippocampal CA1 preparations, postsynaptic NMDAR Ca2+ can recruit calcineurin and PP1-family phosphatase signaling, reduce AMPAR phosphorylation or retention, and promote AMPAR endocytosis.
- **mGluR-LTD example:** Group-I mGluR signaling can recruit rapid local translation, including Arc/Arg3.1 in specific hippocampal preparations, and persistently increase AMPAR endocytosis. Protein-synthesis requirements vary by protocol and preparation.
- **Other forms:** Presynaptic expression, endocannabinoid signaling, cerebellar LTD, and receptor-specific depression use additional mechanisms. A single calcium-threshold diagram cannot represent all LTD.
- **SAN interpretation:** Oscillatory decoupling is testable only if relative phase predicts later weakening beyond firing rate, spike count, spectral power, timing covariates, and common input.
