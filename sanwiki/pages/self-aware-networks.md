# Self-Aware Networks

Self-Aware Networks (SAN) asks how distributed living neural tissue can collectively construct a continuously updated model of body and world, use that model to consider possible consequences, and act. SAN does not posit a little localized viewer standing outside neural activity. It identifies a sparse, distributed observer function performed by the network itself: cells and circuits act as internal receivers and transmitters, while their coordinated tonic action field registers phasic, synaptic differences across the network. It is a multiscale research program about biological sensing, learned pattern recognition, recurrent coordination, embodied rendering, action, and feedback.

## The problem in ordinary language

A brain has no separate, centralized homunculus standing apart from its activity. In SAN, the system itself perceives: sensory receptors change cellular signals; dendrites and local circuits respond selectively; phasic changes alter the ongoing tonic action field; activity circulates through recurrent networks; the body acts; and new sensory consequences return. The viewer function is therefore sparse and distributed across the network rather than absent. Neurons can play roles analogous to internal capture and transmission devices, while the coordinated tonic field differentiates incoming phasic "ink" and contributes to an aggregate observer-action. This analogy names roles performed by the system; it does not move perception into a second hidden agent.

The proposed explanatory ladder is:

1. receptors and cells register differences;
2. learned cellular structure makes some combinations consequential;
3. local recognition changes what a cell or circuit transmits;
4. inhibition, recurrence, and ongoing state select temporary routes;
5. distributed sensory, memory, body, and action relations form a usable working model;
6. predictions and possible actions are evaluated against that model;
7. action changes the body or environment; and
8. feedback updates the next cycle.

## Terms earned by the problem

SAN uses specialized terms only after the operation they name is clear:

- A [[neural-array|neural array]] is a cooperating population considered as a receive, transform, and project unit.
- A [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] (PWD) is the proposed receiver-relative difference among ongoing and changing timing, frequency, magnitude, and inhibitory patterns. It is not one universal frequency label.
- [[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]] is the repeated construction and revision of a behaviorally usable model across sensory, memory, body, and motor systems. SAN assigns both rendering and observing roles to distributed network dynamics rather than to a separate homunculus.
- [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] organizes the receive-transform-project process across cells and arrays and asks whether coordinated oscillatory relations contribute to reconstruction and action.
- [[neural-tuning|Neural Tuning]] asks how learned selectivity and current state reconfigure which routes are effective.
- [[cellular-oscillating-tomography|COT]] asks which SAN operations extend from neurons to other living cells and leads toward the separate Super Evolution Theory program.

## What is established and what remains proposed

Established neuroscience already supports active dendritic computation, diverse inhibitory circuits, recurrent processing, oscillations and traveling waves, plasticity, predictive and sensorimotor loops, and distributed sensory representations. SAN combines those findings into a stronger architectural account.

The stronger claims remain hypotheses: that receiver-relative phase organization supplies a general routing code; that Neural Rendering and tonic-field differentiation explain phenomenal consciousness; that NAPOT is a literal or mathematically identifiable tomographic process; and that the COT mechanism generalizes across ordinary cells in the proposed form. These claims need variables, alternatives, and causal tests rather than confirmation by vocabulary.

## Historical span

The SAN program grew from hands-on neurofeedback and BCI work, Neo Mind Cycle and NerveGear questions, the Neural Lace Podcast, the 2021 information-flow discussions, the 2022 GitHub theory revisions, and later books and papers. Historical evidence establishes development and provenance. It does not substitute for biological evidence.
