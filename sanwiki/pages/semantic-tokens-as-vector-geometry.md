Semantic tokens are not simple symbolic labels for meaning — they are **maps of vectors between nodes** that cross activation thresholds in a distributed network. Meaning emerges from spatial, geometric patterns rather than from sequential bit processing.

This idea, raised by [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] in a 2012 discussion, challenges the reductionist "block diagram" view of AI and neural architecture. Instead of meaning being stored discretely, it is the *shape* of excitation across a high-dimensional vector space that constitutes a semantic unit.

## Core claim

The brain does not feed information one bit at a time into a hierarchical model. It receives a "jumbled data stream — words, pictures, bits — all at once, and lets the structure sort it out." Semantic tokens are the emergent result of that geometric sorting: patterns above threshold that recur across different contexts and scales.

This is consistent with [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography), where meaning is the shape of a wave pattern projected across arrays — not a discrete token at a fixed address.

## Relationship to model-free intelligence

Monica Anderson's parallel argument — that true intelligence is the ability to reduce experience to models without pre-programmed structure — aligns with this: if meaning is geometric emergence, then semantic tokens cannot be hand-coded. They [[gh-a0206z|self]]-organize from the network's exposure to data.

See also [[self-aware-network]], [[neomindcycle]], [[fractal-consciousness]].

## Implications

- Category theory and vector mathematics, not block diagrams, are the right formalism for neural semantics.
- [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-similar, swappable neural regions support this: the same geometry algorithm applies across cortical areas.
- Qualia (color, sound, etc.) may emerge from the same connectome geometry — subjective experience as a high-complexity geometric [[attractor]].

## History

This concept was discussed informally in October 2012 on Facebook between [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]], Monica Anderson, Juan Carlos Kuri Pinto, and Pawel Pachniewski — predating the formal SAN whitepaper. It anticipates the [[napot]] formalism developed later.
