# Cellular Competence, Development, and Super Evolution Theory

> **Scientific boundary:** Cells integrate signals, retain state, regulate development, and generate phenotypic responses. Those within-lifetime changes become evolutionary evidence only when a declared effect passes a heritability test and changes differential survival or reproduction across generations.

> **Naming boundary:** The bounded 2022 sources call this a **new theory of evolution** developed from [[cellular-oscillating-tomography|Cellular Oscillating Tomography (COT)]]. They do not contain the exact title **Super Evolution Theory**. This page uses SET as the current Encyclopedia label for that biological extension; it does not backdate the later title into the 2022 record.

> **Image status:** No medically accepted image is assigned to this page. A bounded future figure is specified as `IMG-SAN-COT-TO-SUPER-EVOLUTION-001` in the SAN medically accurate image-prompt ledger.

Within the SAN Encyclopedia, **Super Evolution Theory (SET)** asks how stateful cellular and multicellular computation can help generate biological form and behavior while natural selection remains the gate for heritable evolutionary change. It is the evolutionary extension of COT, not a physics theory inferred from the word "Super," and not a replacement for genetics, development, inheritance, or selection.

## The operation before the label

```text
inherited cellular machinery and existing variation
+ environmental, intercellular, and organism-level signals
-> receptor-, pathway-, and state-dependent cellular transformation
-> physiological, developmental, morphological, or behavioral output
-> phenotypic variation or plasticity within an organism
-> explicit heritability gate
-> differential survival or reproduction
-> population-level evolutionary change across generations
```

The central distinction is between **producing a phenotype** and **evolving a population**. A cell can change its state or contribute to a different tissue form without changing inherited variation. A useful acquired response can affect evolution through genetic accommodation, genetic assimilation, selection on existing variation, or another demonstrated inheritance route, but that bridge must be measured rather than assumed.

## Recovering the intended 2022 proposal

Micah's phrase **compute future biological structure** is best read as a causal developmental claim, not conscious foresight by a cell. Receptors, signaling pathways, gene-regulatory states, cytoskeletal processes, and interactions among cells constrain which later structures and functions can develop from present conditions.

The 2022 source used three stages:

1. mutation or inherited variation;
2. COT-mediated pattern-sensitive cellular and multicellular development; and
3. natural selection.

Its distinctive proposal was that the middle stage is not a passive translation from genes to body. Cells and cell collectives use current state and temporally structured signals to generate constrained phenotypic possibilities. Selection then acts on outcomes that are heritable and affect fitness.

Several historical terms need careful translation:

| Source phrase | Source-faithful scientific interpretation | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Cell memory | A persistent, history-dependent cellular state that changes later response. | Episodic memory, neuronal LTP, or consciousness in every cell. |
| Hebbian-like receptor learning | Repeated or coincident inputs may produce a history-dependent change in receptor, pathway, transcriptional, or structural state. | One universal Hebbian rule operating at every receptor. |
| Computed structure | Present molecular and cellular state helps cause a later developmental or morphological outcome. | A cell consciously predicts or designs its future body. |
| Natural selection at multiple scales | Selection questions can be posed for genes, cells, organisms, and collectives when replication, inheritance, variation, and differential success are specified. | Proteins, cells, and organisms are interchangeable units of selection. |

## Established biological footholds

Established findings make the bridge scientifically discussable without proving SET:

- Human mammary epithelial cells combined multiple inputs through a context-dependent EGFR-ERK circuit with feedback and amplification ([Joslin et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1039/C003921G)).
- A defined differentiation system exhibited bistability, cellular memory, and reversible plasticity controlled by intracellular state ([Wang et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0806137106)).
- Waddington's selection experiments showed that an environmentally induced phenotype could become expressed under normal conditions after selection on genetic background, the classical genetic-assimilation result ([Waddington, 1952](https://doi.org/10.1038/169625b0)).
- Selection on a heat-responsive developmental reaction norm in *Manduca sexta* produced an evolved color polyphenism and changed hormonal regulation, an experimental example of genetic accommodation ([Suzuki and Nijhout, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1118888)).

These findings support context-sensitive cellular computation, state-dependent development, cellular memory in declared systems, and routes by which developmental plasticity can interact with selection. They do not prove COT's proposed tomography operator or show that every adaptive cellular response is inherited.

## What SET adds as a SAN hypothesis

SET proposes a joined operator:

```text
partial signals distributed across cells
-> history-sensitive integration and retained cellular state
-> constrained generation of tissue form or organismal response
-> exposure of structured phenotypic variation to selection
```

The added claim is not simply that development matters to evolution. It is that COT can become an explicit predictive model of how temporally ordered, partial signals are integrated across cellular scales to generate candidate forms and behaviors. The model earns explanatory value only if it predicts held-out developmental and evolutionary outcomes better than matched gene-regulatory, signaling-pathway, reaction-norm, and population-genetic baselines.

## Scale and inheritance gates

| Scale | State or output that can be measured | Additional gate before calling it evolution |
|---|---|---|
| Molecular and subcellular | Receptor occupancy, second messengers, ion flux, chromatin accessibility, transcription, cytoskeletal state. | Persistence and causal influence on a phenotype. |
| Cell | Differentiation, secretion, migration, proliferation, polarity, morphology, stress response. | Contribution to organismal or lineage-level variation. |
| Tissue and organism | Pattern formation, regeneration, physiology, behavior, developmental plasticity. | Transmission through genes, germline or other demonstrated inheritance, or persistent ecological inheritance. |
| Population | Trait distribution, fitness, survival, reproduction, lineage frequency. | Reproducible change across generations attributable to selection or another declared evolutionary process. |

This prevents a reversible response during one lifetime from being relabeled as evolution. It also prevents the opposite error: treating development as irrelevant merely because natural selection remains necessary.

## A discriminating experiment

Use a model organism or organoid system with a measurable developmental choice. Hold genotype as constant as possible while varying the temporal order, combination, and recurrence of declared extracellular signals.

Measure:

1. receptor and pathway dynamics;
2. transcriptional and chromatin state;
3. cell-state transitions and tissue morphology;
4. persistence after signal removal;
5. transfer to partial or novel cue combinations; and
6. transmission and fitness effects across generations where biologically applicable.

Compare four preregistered model classes:

1. a memoryless dose-response model;
2. a state-aware signaling or gene-regulatory model;
3. a COT model that reconstructs relations among temporally distributed inputs; and
4. a population model joining the measured developmental output to inheritance and selection.

COT gains support if its declared latent state improves held-out prediction and survives causal perturbation. The SET bridge gains support only if the resulting developmental variation passes the inheritance and selection gates. It is weakened if conventional state-aware models explain the same results, if the proposed temporal relations do not generalize, or if within-lifetime plasticity is mistaken for population evolution.

## Claim boundary

| Layer | Defensible statement |
|---|---|
| Established biology | Cells integrate signals, can retain history-dependent state, regulate development, and generate plastic phenotypes; developmental plasticity can interact with inherited variation and selection. |
| Source-supported SAN interpretation | COT supplies the proposed middle operation between inherited variation and selection: stateful cellular and multicellular pattern processing helps generate constrained biological form. |
| Open SET hypothesis | A formal COT reconstruction model will predict developmental variation and its evolutionary consequences better than matched signaling, development, and population baselines. |
