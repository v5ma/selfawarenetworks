Four essential functions of neural coding, as identified in Nature Neuroscience literature and discussed in SAN context: **stimulus representation**, **interpretation**, **transformation**, and **signal transmission**.

Each function describes a distinct operation the [[neural-code]] performs across brain regions:

- **Stimulus representation** — encoding sensory inputs into neural activity patterns.
- **Interpretation** — decoding meaning from those patterns downstream.
- **Transformation** — converting representations across modalities or abstraction levels.
- **Signal transmission** — propagating encoded information between brain areas. Notably, transmission has received less experimental focus than the other three, despite being the communication backbone of any [[self-aware-network]].

The four-function framework connects to [[napot]] by grounding the theory of mind in concrete information-processing roles rather than abstract computation. Transmission in particular is central to SAN's argument that [[tonic-oscillation]] and [[phasic-bursts]] are distinct transmission modes.

## Connection to mind-uploading

In [[gh-b0144y|b0144y]], [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] applies this framework to [[mind-uploading]]: rather than physically copying a brain, one could query its data structure via network-style protocols — effectively treating the brain as a server responding to HTTP/FTP requests. The neural code functions map onto query semantics: representation = stored state, transmission = response pathway.

[[transcranial-magnetic-stimulation]] is proposed as a practical tool for capturing neuron activation thresholds — sampling the threshold landscape that underlies the representation function.

## History

The four-function taxonomy originates in classical computational neuroscience; SAN adopts it as scaffolding for the [[napot]] framework rather than as a novel contribution.
