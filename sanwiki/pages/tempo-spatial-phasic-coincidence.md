## Position

In a [[self-aware-network]], a [[neural-array]] cell does not fire on co-firing alone. The SAN-distinguishing claim is that the [[coincidence-detection|coincidence detection]] performed by the [[apical-dendrite|apical dendrite]] / basal dendrites against the [[chemical-synapse|synaptic]] inputs that arrive at any given moment is *phase-conditional*: the same set of synaptic inputs, arriving at the same scalar rate but at a different phase relative to the [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|DPWDR]] of the moment, will fail to drive the cell. *Tempo-spatial phasic coincidence* names that joint criterion. The "tempo" piece is the time-series shape — the cell tunes to a *trajectory* of inputs, not a single snapshot, in the sense established by [[tempo-spatial-patterns]]. The "spatial" piece is the *which [[dendrite]], which spine* spatial distribution that defines the cell's tuning, in the sense established by [[dendritic-encoding|dendritic encoding]] and [[dendritic-morphology|dendritic morphology]]. The "phasic" piece is the *phase-relative-to-[[dpwdr]]* condition, in the sense of [[phasic-tonic-relationship|phasic-tonic relationship]] and [[tonic-phasic-canvas|tonic-phasic canvas]]. The cell's response is the conjunction of all three.

## Position — relation to standard coincidence detection

Standard dendritic [[coincidence-detection|coincidence detection]] in mainstream [[neuroscience]] is usually reported as *spike-time coincidence*: two or more EPSPs arriving within a narrow temporal window summate above threshold, drive a [[dendritic-spike|dendritic spike]], and propagate to soma. SAN does not deny this — it claims it is the temporal-only marginal of a richer joint criterion. The richer criterion is what the term *tempo-spatial phasic coincidence* names: (i) the inputs must arrive in the right temporal *sequence* (not just in a window — a trajectory), (ii) at the right *positions* on the dendritic tree (not just any branches — the ones the cell has grown to encode this pattern), and (iii) at the right *phase* relative to the column's [[gamma-oscillation|gamma]]/[[theta-oscillations-background-scripts|theta]] envelope. Failing any one of the three drops the cell back to baseline. The SAN [[prediction]] is therefore that experimentally controlled patch-clamp or two-photon experiments that vary phase while holding rate constant should reveal a *phase axis* in dendritic responsiveness that the rate-only literature does not see by construction.

## Position — what the conjunction buys

Why combine the three? The SAN argument runs as follows. (a) Tempo alone is too liberal: many distinct percepts produce overlapping rate-time-series. (b) Spatial alone is too liberal: many distinct percepts route through overlapping dendritic positions. (c) Phasic alone is too liberal: many percepts overlap in phase. But (a)∧(b)∧(c) — the conjunction — is an [[information-rarity-principle|information-rarity]] match: only the right percept satisfies all three at once. The cell's selectivity comes from the conjunction, not from any one axis. This is why a [[neural-array]] cell can be *highly* selective without needing a separate "grandmother" cell per percept: the same dendritic tree, conditioned on different [[dpwdr]] rhythms, codes for many different percepts because the *phase* axis multiplies the effective tuning space.

The cell's threshold for [[dendritic-spike|dendritic spike]] generation is then read out by [[apical-dendrite|apical-dendrite]] integration to soma.
