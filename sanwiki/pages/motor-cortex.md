# Motor Cortex

> **Boundary note (2026-07-18):** Motor cortex is one node in distributed action control, not the whole action-commit organ. Movement depends on premotor and motor cortical areas, parietal and sensory cortex, basal-ganglia loops, cerebellum, brainstem, spinal circuits, peripheral mechanics, feedback, and task context. Distal-tuft events, oscillations, engram findings, and BCI readouts are preparation-specific evidence, not general proof of NAPOT motor rendering. See [[body-model-as-control-surface]], [[san-scale-ladder-and-claim-boundaries]], and [[phase-language-firewall]].

The **motor cortex** includes primary motor cortex (M1) and adjacent motor-related cortical areas that participate in movement preparation, execution, and feedback control. It is neither a muscle map that acts alone nor a final executive that simply converts thought into movement.

## Established physiology

M1 contains corticospinal and corticobulbar neurons as well as dense local and interareal circuitry. Population activity can display structured dynamics related to movement, but individual neurons often have mixed, time-varying relationships to kinematics, force, context, and task variables. Premotor cortex and supplementary motor area can show dynamics that differ from M1 even during the same reach.

Movement is continuously shaped by sensory return. Rapid, goal-dependent responses to mechanical perturbation involve M1 and other feedback pathways before a slow, fully deliberative correction would be possible. Cerebellar, basal-ganglia, brainstem, spinal, and peripheral circuits remain essential parts of the behavior.

## What the evidence does not show

- A decoded movement variable is not necessarily the variable the cortex explicitly represents.
- Successful BCI decoding does not establish a complete motor code, an inner readout, or NAPOT.
- A distal apical-tuft event in one cell type and preparation is not a universal action-commit mechanism.
- A change in low-frequency power or coherence does not by itself prove that the rhythm causes behavioral updating.
- Motor learning is not the storage of a complete reach inside one engram cell.

These findings can identify candidate mechanisms. Their role must be tested against firing rate, connectivity, sensory feedback, task state, and alternative population-dynamics accounts.

## The SAN interpretation

SAN originally described motor cortex as the **action-commit side** of a memory-prediction cycle. The medically bounded revision keeps the insight but distributes the commit. An action becomes committed when interacting cortical, subcortical, spinal, and body states cross a point at which one trajectory is expressed and constrains what can happen next.

Under this interpretation:

1. tonic activity carries the current control state and readiness of participating receivers;
2. phasic sensory, dendritic, reward, or contextual changes alter that state;
3. population dynamics select and shape a trajectory;
4. descending pathways and spinal-musculoskeletal dynamics realize it;
5. sensory consequences update the next cycle.

Motor cortex is therefore a contributor to observer-action, not the location of the observer or the sole source of action.

## Dendrites, rhythms, and local criteria

Apical and basal dendritic compartments can integrate different input streams, and dendritic events can affect somatic output. Oscillatory state can change excitability and coordination. SAN may treat these as candidate local criteria within a larger action cycle, but it must not infer semantic prediction, conscious intention, or tomography from the cellular event alone.

## Testable SAN question

A discriminating experiment would ask whether a specified phase relation or dendritic event in a named motor circuit causally improves prediction of action selection or online correction after controlling for spike rate, population state, movement, sensory feedback, arousal, and connectivity. The claim fails if ordinary population dynamics or feedback control explains the effect equally well.
