## What the episode contributes

Micah describes Neurohackers as an effort to combine WebXR, VR and AR with EEG, ECG, EMG, eye
tracking, machine learning and 3D data processing. Jean compares MRI, CT, ultrasound, EEG and
electrical impedance tomography, explaining why open biomedical-imaging hardware matters.

OpenEIT supplies a practical tomography route, while the broader discussion exposes a central
constraint: different instruments observe different variables at different scales. No inexpensive
modality in the episode provides a complete neural state or direct percept decoder.

## SAN routes

- **BCI history:** practical NeurotechSF and WebXR feedback work.
- **Instrument atlas:** modality tradeoffs and reconstruction limits.
- **Tomography:** engineering context for later SAN terminology, not direct measurement of NAPOT.
- **Closed-loop systems:** biosignals changing a spatial interface in real time.

## Evidence boundary

The episode documents projects and reasoning. EIT performance, brain-depth claims, temporal and
spatial resolution, and neural decoding capability require independent evidence. OpenEIT is not by
itself validation of SAN neurobiology.
