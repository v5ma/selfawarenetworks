# Spike Frequency Coding

**Spike frequency coding** is the family of [[discrete-neural-coding|neural-coding]] schemes in which the *rate or temporal frequency of [[action-potential|action potentials]]* — rather than their precise timing or their dendritic site of arrival — is treated as the carrier of information. Within [[self-aware-networks]] this label sits alongside spatial and phasic coding as one of three independent axes the [[cortex|cortical]] machinery samples; the SAN reading insists that frequency-coding alone undercounts what a [[cortical-column|cortical column]] is doing, because [[ndca|NDCA]] requires all three axes to reconstruct [[consciousness|conscious]] content.

## Position

In [[self-aware-networks]], spike frequency coding is read as the [[brain-rhythms|brain-rhythm]] axis of a triplet that also includes the spatial axis (set by [[multi-vesicle-release|multi-vesicle release]] across [[dendritic-branches|dendrites]]) and the phasic axis (set by [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|phase-wave differentials]] across [[neural-array-projection|neural arrays]]). The same axis appears in [[gamma-oscillations|gamma]] and [[tonic-oscillation|tonic]] firing modes — fast frequency-coded transients riding on slower [[brainwave|brainwaves]] visible in [[eeg|EEG]] — and feeds the [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]] reconstruction as one of three orthogonal inputs. Frequency-coded spike rate also sets the [[membrane-potential|membrane-potential]] regime under which [[coincidence-detection|coincidence-detecting]] downstream cells fire, so it acts as the gain knob that admits or suppresses the spatial and phasic channels.
