# Self-Similar Cortical Structure

**Self-similar cortical structure** names SAN's proposal that a recurring receive-transform-project organization appears across cortical areas and across cellular, circuit, and network scales. The medically accurate form of the claim is **repetition with specialization**. Neocortex contains recurring laminar, cellular, and connectional motifs, but cortical areas are not anatomically or functionally identical, and current evidence does not establish one universal algorithm.

## What is established

Mountcastle's recordings in cat somatosensory cortex helped establish vertical functional organization: neurons encountered along penetrations perpendicular to the cortical surface often shared modality and receptive-field properties ([Mountcastle, 1957](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1957.20.4.408)). This supports columnar organization in the studied system. It does not prove that every cortical computation is performed by one sharply bounded canonical column.

Across neocortical areas, major cell classes, layered organization, and many connection patterns recur. They also vary by area, species, layer, cell subtype, developmental history, and long-range target. Harris and Shepherd therefore describe neocortex as variations on a basic circuit theme rather than copies of one identical module ([Harris and Shepherd, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3917)).

Plasticity and sensory substitution show that nervous systems can learn new mappings and recruit existing pathways for changed tasks. For example, participants learned sound-category discrimination from patterned wrist vibration ([Perrotta, Asgeirsdottir, and Eagleman, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2021.01.008)). That result supports adaptive use of transformed input. It does not show that one cortical region can replace any other region or that all cortex is functionally interchangeable.

## Computational comparators

Numenta's Thousand Brains framework proposes that cortical columns learn object-centered reference-frame models and exchange feature-location information ([Hawkins et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2018.00121)). This is a computational and neurobiological theory, not direct evidence that grid-like location codes exist in every cortical column.

Dileep George and colleagues derive a detailed thalamic and cortical microcircuit for predictive visual inference from a generative model ([George et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adr6698)). The model maps feedforward, feedback, lateral, laminar, and thalamic pathways to explicit computational roles and generates testable predictions. It remains a theory-derived circuit model; it should not be presented as a universal measured cortical circuit.

These programs are useful comparators because they make different commitments about what a repeated cortical motif computes. Similarity among their diagrams is not empirical confirmation that the mechanisms are identical.

## The SAN proposal

Micah's source notes use self-similarity for three related ideas:

1. cortical organization reuses motifs rather than assigning all intelligence to one executive location;
2. prediction and action arise from distributed recurrent processes rather than a separate inner chooser; and
3. a receive-transform-project relation may recur from dendritic compartments to neurons, local circuits, columns, and distributed networks.

SAN adds a proposed oscillatory implementation. Ongoing tonic population state serves as receiver context, while phasic departures carry differentiated updates that are integrated and re-expressed through learned routes. This is the SAN contribution to test. Repeated anatomy, plasticity, HTM, Thousand Brains, or predictive coding does not establish that phase-wave differentials implement the shared operation.

The statement that there is no privileged inner chooser is compatible with distributed decision mechanisms. It does not show that all areas do the same thing, eliminate regional specialization, or settle philosophical questions about choice.

## Scale boundary

- **Cellular:** dendritic compartments integrate different input streams and influence somatic or axonal output.
- **Local circuit:** recurrent excitatory and inhibitory populations transform input under local state.
- **Mesoscale:** columns and neighboring circuits participate in laminar and horizontal interactions.
- **Distributed network:** specialized cortical, thalamic, hippocampal, basal-ganglia, cerebellar, and bodily systems coordinate perception and action.

The same abstract arrow pattern may describe more than one scale, but anatomical equivalence cannot be inferred from diagrammatic resemblance. Each scale needs its own measured variables, routes, delays, perturbations, and failure conditions.

## What would test the SAN addition

- Identify the proposed repeated operation without assuming one universal cortical column.
- Show which variables are invariant across areas and which are area-specific.
- Compare SAN phase variables against capacity-matched predictive, recurrent, and reference-frame models.
- Perturb the proposed receiver context and phasic update separately.
- Require transfer across tasks or areas before claiming a shared computation.
- Reject universal-interchangeability language when a result only demonstrates bounded plasticity.
