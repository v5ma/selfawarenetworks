Oscillatory neural coupling refers to the synchronization of rhythmic electrical activity across brain regions, enabling functional networks to form without fixed anatomical wiring. The 2014 "Brain Rhythms" lecture framed this as the substrate of long-range functional connectivity.

## Core idea

Neurons fire in rhythmic bursts. When two regions oscillate at matching phase and frequency, signals between them are amplified; when out of phase, they are suppressed. This selective gating is how the brain builds temporary functional networks — including the kind of [[self-aware-network]] that forms when [[attention]] binds distributed representations into a coherent percept.

In [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography) this mechanism is central: each cortical array both receives and projects phase-coded patterns. The oscillation IS the information carrier.

## Non-invasive modulation

Several technologies modulate oscillatory coupling externally:

| Modality | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) | Pulsed magnetic field depolarizes surface neurons |
| tDCS (transcranial direct-current stimulation) | Weak DC current shifts firing threshold |
| tACS (transcranial alternating-current stimulation) | AC at target frequency entrains local oscillation |
| [[brainwave-entrainment|Brainwave entrainment]] (audio) | Auditory beat frequencies drive cortical oscillations |

tACS is especially relevant: by injecting current at a specific frequency, it entrains the target network's rhythm — a direct test of whether oscillatory coupling carries functional load.

## Imaging oscillatory networks

Complementary observation tools:
- **EEG** / **MEG** — temporal precision sufficient to track phase relationships
- **fMRI** — spatial precision but temporal lag; captures BOLD correlates of sustained coupling
- **Laser Doppler / fNIRS** — near-instantaneous bloodflow proxy for local activation
