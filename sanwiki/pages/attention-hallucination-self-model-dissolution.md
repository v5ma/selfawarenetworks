# Attentional Selection, Hallucination, and Self-Model Dissolution

## The problem in ordinary language

A person can look at the same room while attending to different objects. A person can also
experience a sound, image, bodily sensation, or presence that is not sufficiently constrained by
the current environment. In some altered states, the usual boundary between self and world can
weaken even while vivid experience continues.

These are related stress tests for a theory of consciousness, but they are not one phenomenon:

1. **Attention** concerns which available information is selected, amplified, routed, or used.
2. **Hallucination** concerns the content that is experienced and how strongly current sensory
   evidence constrains it.
3. **Self-model stability** concerns ownership, perspective, body boundary, agency, and the
   distinction between self and world.

The old SAOv9 heading called this family "Locus of Focus, Hallucination, Dissolving Egop." The typo
is preserved in the source inventory, but the governed concept is renamed because the original
heading also invites a deeper mistake: a focus of attention need not be one fixed anatomical point.

## No fixed center is required

The useful meaning of **locus of focus** is a transient state in which one representation gains
greater access to downstream discrimination, memory, report, or action. That state can recruit
anatomical hubs without turning any one hub into an inner observer.

The SAN archive contains both sides of this development. `a0306z.md` and `a0611z.md` tentatively
place a central or primary locus in a hippocampal-entorhinal-parietal-thalamic loop. The same
`a0611z.md` passage also calls the locus virtual and says different cells can instantiate it at
different times. The later `b0145ywhisper.md` account is more explicit: there is no central
observer; attention and observation are distributed operations, while the temporoparietal junction
may be a crossroads or indicator rather than the seat of experience.

The governed SAN formulation therefore treats focus as a distributed, time-varying selection
relation. Hippocampus, thalamus, salience and frontoparietal systems, sensory cortex, TPJ, and other
regions can participate depending on task and state. Their participation is an empirical question,
not evidence for a single consciousness center.

## What counts as a hallucination

For this Wiki, a hallucination is **percept-like content that is insufficiently constrained by the
current external input relevant to that content**. This definition is intentionally narrower than
"perception differs from reality" and broader than one diagnosis.

It requires at least four qualifiers:

- **modality:** auditory, visual, tactile, somatic, olfactory, or multimodal;
- **context:** sleep transition, sensory deprivation, neurological illness, psychiatric illness,
  medication, intoxication, or another condition;
- **insight:** whether the person recognizes the content as internally generated or anomalous; and
- **functional consequence:** distress, impairment, neutral experience, creativity, or adaptive
  behavior.

The definition does not imply that every hallucination has the same cause. It also does not imply
that top-down priors explain all hallucinations. Powers, Mathys, and Corlett used a Pavlovian task
to induce conditioned auditory hallucinations and found stronger prior weighting in voice-hearers.
That result establishes one experimentally tractable route, not a universal theory of every
hallucinatory state:

- [Powers, Mathys, and Corlett, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28798131/)

## What ego dissolution should mean here

The word **ego** is too broad to function as one biological variable. A stronger account separates:

- a minimal embodied self: this body and its immediate sensorimotor control;
- perspectival self-location: the point from which a scene is experienced;
- ownership and agency: whether sensations, thoughts, and actions feel like mine;
- narrative or autobiographical self: the temporally extended story of who I am; and
- social identity: the self represented in relation to other people.

**Ego dissolution** is retained as a phenomenological label for a reported weakening or
reorganization of one or more of these dimensions, especially self-world boundary and integrated
identity. It is not automatically unconsciousness, psychosis, depersonalization, derealization,
memory erasure, or literal destruction of a metaphysical entity.

Small human imaging studies show why a single-center account is inadequate. In fifteen healthy
participants, psilocybin-related ego-dissolution ratings were associated with medial-temporal to
cortical decoupling, reduced salience-network integrity, and reduced interhemispheric
communication. In another fifteen-participant within-subject study, LSD increased global and
between-network connectivity, and the increase correlated with ego-dissolution ratings. Different
drugs, measures, analyses, and network effects should not be flattened into one pathway:

- [Lebedev et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26010878/)
- [Tagliazucchi et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27085214/)

## The corrected SAN proposal

The source history supports a layered account:

1. Learned and recurrent activity can generate internally organized perceptual content.
2. Attention changes which content is amplified, stabilized, or made available for use.
3. The self is itself represented through body, perspective, memory, agency, and social
   relationships.
4. Hallucination changes the relation between generated content and current sensory constraint.
5. Ego dissolution changes the organization or boundary of the self-model.
6. Either can occur without the other, and neither proves a particular metaphysics.

SAN adds a stronger hypothesis: relative timing, inhibition, waveform, magnitude, persistence, and
cross-scale coupling may help determine which content becomes dominant and how self-model
components remain bound. `04san.md` expresses this as resource dominance by a phase-wave
differential, while `13san.md` joins shared phase patterns, recursive self-modeling, and temporal
persistence.

Those are testable additions, not conclusions licensed by attention studies or psychedelic fMRI.
They must explain held-out behavior or causal effects beyond firing-rate, recurrent-network,
predictive-processing, workspace, and state-space alternatives.

## What the external evidence permits

Selective visual attention can alter effective communication without requiring one anatomical
focus. In two macaques, Bosman and colleagues found that the behaviorally relevant V1 site
selectively gamma-synchronized with V4 during a change-detection task:

- [Bosman et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3457649/)

This supports dynamic selection by interareal coordination. It does not prove that gamma is the
only attentional mechanism, that coherence is represented content, or that SAN's dominant
phase-wave-differential claim is correct.

The psychedelic studies show correlations between reported self-boundary changes and distributed
network reorganization. They do not identify an ego center, prove that ego dissolution is one
unitary state, or establish that a specific phase pattern causes the experience.

## Discriminating experiments

- Vary attentional priority and sensory evidence independently, then test whether hallucination-like
  reports track prior weighting, sensory precision, selection, or their interaction.
- Measure self-location, ownership, agency, autobiographical continuity, and self-world boundary
  separately instead of using one undifferentiated ego score.
- Compare hallucination without ego dissolution, ego dissolution without prominent hallucination,
  and matched control states.
- Test whether SAN phase-relational features predict report, confidence, behavior, or perturbation
  response after controlling power, firing rate, arousal, eye movements, and network state.
- Perturb relative timing or coupling while approximately preserving mean activity, then measure
  selection, perceptual content, and self-model reports independently.
- Compare distributed-selection models with any fixed-locus model using cross-task and cross-state
  generalization.

## Clinical and ethical boundary

This page is not medical advice and does not recommend drug use. Autobiographical reports in
`b0081ydone.md`, `b0195ywhisper.md`, and the multi-speaker `b0324y.md` preserve SAN's
phenomenological and intellectual history. They are not substitutes for clinical assessment,
controlled research, or evidence that another person's experience has the same mechanism.

## Book route

This concept belongs in Chapter 21 as a stress test, not as a tour of unusual experiences. The
chapter should first explain ordinary attention, sensory constraint, body ownership, and
self-modeling. Only then should it introduce hallucination, displaced self-location, and ego
dissolution as ways those operations can separate.

## Inhibitory selection among candidate self-models

[[gh-a0511z|a0511z]] contains a 2017 source layer and an explicit June 2022 update. Its constructive
question is how learned self, other, imagined, and counterfactual models can be partially available
without an inner observer choosing among them. Attention, inhibition, sensory evidence, learned
value, and current network state change which candidate controls report or action.

The active distributed network is the selector. Historical speculation about alternate beings or
transfer through an unspecified field is not required for the operation. Later SAN work makes the
selection variables and consequences more precise without erasing this early identity-selection
problem.
