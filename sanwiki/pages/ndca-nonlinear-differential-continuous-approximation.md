# NDCA: Non-linear Differential Continuous Approximation

> **Image status:** No medically and mathematically accepted image is assigned to this page. A bounded future figure is specified as `IMG-SAN-NDCA-CONTINUITY-APPROXIMATION-001` in the SAN medically accurate image-prompt ledger.

Within SAN, **NDCA (Non-linear Differential Continuous Approximation)** is a proposed account of how time-varying neural populations could preserve usable distinctions while sensory and body-state signals remain nonlinear, noisy, delayed, and continuously changing. It is not an established neuroscience term or a completed mathematical operator. See [[ndca]] for the canonical historical boundary.

## Scientific and scale boundary

Established neuroscience supports nonlinear dendritic and circuit dynamics, recurrent population activity, and the use of state-space trajectories or manifolds to analyze neural data. SAN adds the hypothesis that related operations participate in a multiscale continuity process supporting an action-ready working model. Evidence for the established components does not by itself validate NDCA, NAPOT, phase-wave differentials, semantic readout, or consciousness.

- **Micro:** Synaptic and dendritic integration are possible component mechanisms, not proof that each cell performs NDCA.
- **Meso:** Population trajectories and attractor-like dynamics are measurable, but a claimed NDCA transform must be defined for a named circuit and task.
- **Macro:** Continuity of a distributed body-world model is the SAN interpretation to be tested, not a property already demonstrated by the word "manifold."

A scientific NDCA model still needs an explicit state variable, update or differential rule, measurement mapping, cross-scale relationship, alternatives, and failure conditions. Its relation to QGTCD is a separate speculative extension, not part of the neuroscience evidence.

## Core mechanism

The bounded SAN proposal is a candidate cycle rather than an established cortical algorithm:

1. Receptors, cells, and populations sample partial, time-varying state.
2. Nonlinear cellular and recurrent dynamics transform that state.
3. Population activity follows a trajectory that may preserve task-relevant distinctions over time.
4. Downstream receivers use the changed state to update prediction, selection, or action.

A manifold is ordinarily an analytical description of population activity. The stronger claim that cortical columns literally compute attractor cross-sections, extract semantics, or render a percept requires circuit-specific measurements and a defined readout.

## Relation to predictive coding

NDCA can be compared with predictive-processing and dynamical-systems models, but it should not be identified with them by definition. Coincidence detection is an established family of cellular and circuit phenomena; prediction error is a model-level quantity. Equating the two is a SAN hypothesis that needs an explicit mapping and tests against firing-rate, recurrent-state, and control accounts.

## Relation to the SAN mathematics family

NDCA names the continuity and approximation problem, not a license to treat every neighboring mathematical analogy as the same operation. In the shared biological running example, [[phase-wave-differential-calculus|PWD calculus]] describes a candidate receiver-relative update, [[neural-phase-trajectories-taylor-series-proposal|Taylor and Volterra comparisons]] ask how a changing trajectory might be approximated, and [[category-theory-neuroscience|the Category Theory-Petri Net bridge]] types states and tests whether local transitions compose across receivers and scales.

```text
measured changing state
-> receiver-relative event
-> nonlinear state update
-> continuity or trajectory approximation
-> typed downstream consequence
```

These are complementary modeling questions. None establishes the others by definition.
