Neuronal Global Resonation Theory proposes that brain activity does not stay local — neural firing patterns **ripple as wave fronts throughout the entire neural network**, propagating via resonance rather than discrete signal relay. Sensor arrays distributed across the brain could in principle map these propagating wave patterns and their dissipation routes.

## Key claims

- **Wave propagation, not relay**: Information in the brain spreads the way a stone's impact ripples across a pond — the disturbance propagates outward from the local firing site through the resonant properties of the whole network.
- **Dissipation mapping**: The routes by which a wave front dissipates carry information about network topology; mapping dissipation gives a functional anatomy of connectivity.
- **Universal oscillatory substrate**: The same wave-propagation physics that describes neural resonation applies (speculatively) at cosmic scales — particles as vortices, space as multi-scale wave patterns, all phenomena as fractal harmonic oscillations at different densities and scales.
- **Gravity as diffusion**: The source speculatively reframes gravity as a "time/particle diffusion matrix" — denser regions slow local time, creating a gradient that resembles diffusion pressure. This remains highly speculative and is not a formal physics claim.
