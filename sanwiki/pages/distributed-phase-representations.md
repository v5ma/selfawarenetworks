# Distributed Phase Representations in SAN: Relations, Receivers, and Physical Carriers

> **Image status:** The former multicarrier ensemble plate failed medical review and is preserved
> in the `archive-do-not-use-medical-review/br032-distributed-phase-representations-20260809`
> folder. It must not appear in a reader or public projection. A medically bounded replacement
> description is maintained in the SAN image prompt ledger; image generation remains paused.

A **distributed phase representation** is not one waveform spread uniformly across the brain. It
is a proposed description of content encoded in the changing states and timing relations of many
pathway-specific neural elements. Different cells and populations can contribute different
features, references, predictions, actions, and receiver consequences. Their joint organization can
be distributed without being identical, globally synchronous, or reducible to one scalar sum.

Self Aware Networks adds a stronger hypothesis: expected tonic relations provide an active context,
while uncommon excitation- or inhibition-driven phase-wave differentials alter that context in ways
that tuned receivers can detect, transform, and redistribute. SAN then asks whether the resulting
distributed field-cell differences are not merely a code used by the system, but part of the
physical form of experience itself.

That identity claim remains unresolved. The lower levels of the mechanism can be tested separately.

## Claim levels

| Level | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Established neuroscience | Neural representations can be distributed and overlapping; spike timing relative to a declared population reference can carry information; interareal coordination can alter effective communication. | Supported in bounded systems and tasks. |
| Established biophysics | Action potentials, transmitter release, ionic state, extracellular fields, and small mechanical changes are physically coupled aspects of neural activity. | Supported, with different mechanisms, scales, and evidential strength. |
| SAN mechanistic interpretation | Receiver-relative tonic context and multidimensional PWD events organize distributed representation, recurrent rendering, routing, memory, and action. | Open, testable synthesis. |
| SAN carrier-coupling hypothesis | Multiple physical observables may carry complementary information about one evolving distributed state and may sometimes contribute causally to its propagation. | Open; identical cross-medium messages are not established. |
| SAN identity hypothesis | Organized distributed field-cell differences constitute qualitative experience rather than being viewed by another internal agent. | Strong unresolved hypothesis. |

No finding about gamma power, one local field potential, one cortical region, one potassium channel,
or one stimulation site establishes all five levels at once.

## What distributed means

Distributed representation has at least three distinct meanings:

1. **Anatomical distribution:** information relevant to an object or state is present across more
   than one cell, population, pathway, or region.
2. **Temporal distribution:** the representation depends on a sequence, recurrence, relative phase,
   or history rather than one instantaneous sample.
3. **Receiver distribution:** different downstream receivers extract different consequences from
   partially shared activity.

Human fMRI work found widely distributed and overlapping category-sensitive patterns in ventral
temporal cortex, including discriminative information outside each category's maximally responsive
region ([Haxby et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11577229/)). Color has likewise been
decoded from distributed activity across multiple visual areas rather than one cortical color pixel
([Brouwer and Heeger, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19890009/)). These results support
distributed population representation. They do not establish a global phase field, NAPOT, or a
physical identity for consciousness.

Distributed also does not mean that every region contributes equally or that no region is causally
important. A measured area can be an indicator, a necessary relay, a control node, a receiver, or
several of these depending on the task and intervention. Temporoparietal and frontoparietal systems
must not be dismissed as mere "LEDs" without causal evidence.

## Phase is a relation, not a substance label

Phase is meaningful only relative to a declared oscillatory process or reference. For populations
`i` and `j`:

```text
Delta_phi_ij(t) = wrap(phi_i(t) - phi_j(t))                    [1]
```

The relation may be approximately stable, drifting, intermittently coupled, cross-frequency, or
structured into a splay pattern. Conduction delay, waveform shape, reference choice, filtering, and
volume conduction all affect its interpretation.

A conventional weighted order parameter can summarize alignment:

```text
R(t) exp(i psi(t)) = [1 / sum_k w_k] sum_k w_k exp(i phi_k(t)) [2]
```

`R` near one indicates alignment under the chosen measurement model. It does not identify what is
represented, prove communication, or create a conscious percept. Many distinct spatial patterns can
share the same `R`, while informative traveling or splay states can have a lower `R`.

The representation therefore requires a richer state:

```text
S(t) = {x_k(t), Delta_phi_ij(t), A_ij(t), C(t), H(t)}          [3]
```

`x_k` contains declared local variables; `A_ij` is anatomical or effective routing; `C` is current
context; and `H` is recent history. Equation [3] is a modeling grammar, not a claim that the brain
stores a literal mathematical set.

Primary recordings show why this relational framing matters. In macaque V1, spike phase relative
to low-frequency local-field activity conveyed visual information beyond spike count
([Montemurro et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18328702/)). In a visual-attention task,
the behaviorally relevant V1 site selectively gamma-synchronized with V4, altering effective
interareal influence without requiring global synchrony
([Bosman et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22958827/)). These are bounded examples of
phase-sensitive coding and routing, not evidence that one phase ensemble is consciousness.

## The SAN Shannon-PWD increment

SAN's useful distinction is not synchronized versus unsynchronized in isolation. It is expected
relation versus a structured, receiver-usable departure.

### Why gamma synchrony did not settle consciousness

A substantial late-twentieth- and early-twenty-first-century research program treated gamma-band
synchrony as a candidate mechanism for perceptual binding or a correlate of conscious percept
formation. For example, transient gamma synchrony accompanied reported perceptual switches during
binocular rivalry ([Doesburg et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16012336/)), and a later
model proposed transient theta-organized gamma assemblies as moments of perceptual experience
([Doesburg et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19582165/)). That program established an
important coordination problem, but it did not show that gamma frequency, gamma power, or maximal
synchrony is sufficient for consciousness. Intracranial recordings have even found greater gamma
power and wider beta/gamma synchrony during post-seizure unconsciousness in some participants
([Pockett and Holmes, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19775914/)), while controlled visual
experiments have separated induced gamma from awareness and associated it instead with
task-dependent post-perceptual processing
([Pitts et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25063731/)).

It would also be historically inaccurate to say that neuroscience never applied information
theory to oscillations or consciousness. Investigators have calculated mutual information in
phase codes ([McLelland and Paulsen, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19103680/)) and used
entropy or signal-diversity measures to distinguish states of consciousness
([Thul et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26480834/)). SAN's narrower proposed increment
is to evaluate a phase event against a **receiver-specific tonic expectation** and then require a
measurable receiver consequence. A perfectly repetitive phase-locked pattern may be highly
predictable and add little new self-information within that model. A rarer excitation-driven
advance, inhibition-driven delay, omission, synchronization onset, or structured splay relation can
be more informative. But frequency, rarity, and desynchronization do not become meaningful by
themselves: the event must be discriminable to a named receiver and alter what that receiver does.

For receiver `r`, event `x`, and context `C`:

```text
I_r(x | C) = -log2 p_0,r(x | C)                               [4]
```

A repeated expected relation adds little new conditional surprisal. A rare synchronization onset,
phase advance, burst, inhibition-driven delay, expected-event omission, or structured dispersion
can add more. Rarity alone is not meaning: matched random disruption can be surprising while
remaining unusable.

A candidate PWD is therefore typed and receiver-relative:

```text
PWD_r(t) = X_r(t) - E_0,r[X_r(t) | C]                         [5]
```

`X` may include circular phase, timing, frequency, amplitude, duration, waveform, transmitted
quantity, spatial relation, and local network state. Incompatible units are not literally
subtracted; each component requires its own measurement and normalization.

The receiver test closes the mechanism:

```text
Y_r(t + Delta_t) = F_r(S_r(t), PWD_r(t), A_r(t), H_r(t))      [6]
```

The event becomes a candidate neural symbol when a named receiver changes reproducibly and a
perceptual, mnemonic, affective, autonomic, routing, or motor consequence follows. See
[[phase-wave-differentials|Phase-Wave Differentials]] and
[[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|Tonic Baseline, Phasic Update, and PWD]].

## Coupled observables are not four copies of one message

The November 2021 *Waves Neurons Pattern* recording proposed electric, magnetic, chemical, and
mechanical phase fields and explicitly called the cross-medium assignment "weird speculation."
Its durable insight is that an evolving neural event has several physically detectable aspects and
that later arrays may detect highly configured changes. The current Wiki must preserve both the
proposal and its original uncertainty.

| Observable or process | Established boundary | What is not established |
|---|---|---|
| Axonal action potential | Regenerative membrane event propagating along a particular axon. | A free-standing phase packet broadcast identically to every nearby cell. |
| Chemical synaptic transmission | Presynaptic voltage and calcium influence probabilistic, bouton-specific transmitter release and postsynaptic effects. | A molecular copy of the axonal waveform or one universal amplitude code. |
| Extracellular electric field | Transmembrane currents generate local fields; weak fields can affect nearby neural excitability in bounded preparations. | A complete semantic scene or a replacement for synaptic pathways. |
| Magnetic field | Neural currents generate measurable magnetic fields, including signals detected by MEG. | An endogenous magnetic channel independently carrying the same content to distant cortical regions. |
| Mechanical displacement | Sub-nanometer motion has been measured alongside repeated action potentials in single mammalian neurons. | A cortex-wide acoustic or soliton consciousness channel carrying a duplicate message. |
| Ionic, metabolic, and glial state | These variables modulate excitability, transmission, homeostasis, and plasticity over characteristic scales. | One shared phase or bandwidth across all chemical and electrical processes. |

Weak electric fields entrained neocortical activity in vitro at bounded strengths
([Frohlich and McCormick, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20624597/)), and ephaptic effects
altered cortical-neuron membrane potential and spike timing in slices
([Anastassiou et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21240273/)). Optical measurements also
found small mechanical motion accompanying action potentials in single mammalian neurons
([Yang et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29570267/)). These findings justify measuring
multiple observables. They do not show that the observables are identical carriers or that each
contains an independently complete representation.

A medically bounded carrier model is:

```text
x_m(t) = G_m(S_local(t), anatomy, history) + epsilon_m(t)     [7]
```

Each measured modality `m` is a different transformation of local state with its own geometry,
latency, units, and noise. A shared latent cause can make signals correlated without making them
copies. SAN's stronger multicarrier hypothesis survives only if one modality contributes
independent predictive or causal information after the others and common causes are controlled.

## Potassium channels do not control a universal brightness dial

The rejected image translated potassium-channel effects into a simple phase amplifier. The valid
physiological bridge is narrower. At rat hippocampal mossy-fiber boutons, repeated high-frequency
activity inactivated fast potassium channels, broadened the presynaptic action potential, increased
calcium entry, and potentiated evoked postsynaptic currents
([Geiger and Jonas, 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11163277/)).

That causal chain depends on channel subtype, compartment, firing history, terminal geometry,
calcium-channel coupling, vesicle state, synapse type, and receiver. Potassium-channel opening can
also shorten or suppress excitability. "Brighter" should therefore be translated as **greater
effective drive at a named receiver under declared conditions**, not visual brightness, greater
consciousness, or a uniform increase in a global phase sum.

See [[action-potential-waveform|Action Potential Waveform]],
[[action-potential-duration|Action Potential Duration]], and
[[multi-vesicular-release|Multivesicular Release]].

## Micro, meso, and macro representations

| Scale | Medically bounded operation | SAN interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Receptors, synapses, dendritic compartments, soma, axon initial segment, axon, and boutons transform local state. | A cellular receiver can classify and re-express part of a larger pattern; it does not contain the whole percept. |
| Meso | Local excitatory and inhibitory populations coordinate, compete, disperse, recur, and change effective connectivity. | Tonic relations provide context while structured PWDs alter coalition membership and local rendering. |
| Macro | Specialized cortical, thalamic, hippocampal, basal-ganglia, cerebellar, brainstem, body, and sensory-return systems interact over task-dependent routes. | Partial representations are recurrently combined and redistributed as an embodied observer-action process. |

There is no universal cortical-column circuit. Cortical areas differ in cytoarchitecture, layer
prominence, cell types, inputs, outputs, and function. Thalamic nuclei likewise have distinct relay,
regulatory, and higher-order relationships. A correct diagram must show selected pathways rather
than giving every named region the same six-layer cylinder and a generic thalamic input.

Distributed representation also does not require all-to-all connectivity. Sparse anatomical paths,
conduction delays, oscillatory windows, inhibition, neuromodulation, and current task state limit
which receivers can use a signal. See [[functional-connectivity|Functional Connectivity]],
[[patterns-flow-across-brain-regions|Patterns Flow Across Brain Regions]], and
[[traveling-waves-phase-arrays|Traveling Waves and Phase Arrays]].

## No inner viewer and no single aggregate output

SAN does not propose that a central controller reads a completed phase sum. Cells and populations
receive, transform, project, inhibit, recur, and change one another. The organism reports,
remembers, or acts because this distributed process changes its own state and its body-world loop.

The representation can be unified enough to support one action while remaining sparse,
heterogeneous, and partly segregated. Coherent coalitions and structured phase dispersion can both
matter: one helps establish temporary compatibility, while the other preserves distinctions,
boundaries, competition, and transitions. See
[[coherence-dispersion-sculpting-hypothesis|Coherence-Dispersion Sculpting Hypothesis]] and
[[oscillatory-binding-problem|Oscillatory Binding Problem]].

The strongest SAN claim is that the organized differences across the active field-cell network are
the experience, not a picture delivered to another observer. That is a candidate physical identity,
not an established conclusion. See [[observer-mechanism|Observer Mechanism]],
[[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]], and [[qualia|Qualia in SAN]].

## Discriminating experiments

1. Declare the anatomical sources, receivers, reference signals, frequency ranges, delays, task,
   behavioral outputs, and artifact controls before estimating phase.
2. Compare firing-rate, spectral-power, local-phase, pairwise-phase, effective-connectivity,
   recurrent-state, and full receiver-relative PWD models on held-out content and behavior.
3. Match event rarity while scrambling source identity, spatial organization, or timing. A structured
   PWD should outperform equally surprising random disruption.
4. Perturb a selected interareal timing relation while matching local firing rate and power. Predict
   a specific loss of routing or content, then test restoration or rescue.
5. Record electrical, chemical, ionic, field, and mechanical observables where technically feasible.
   Test whether any modality adds independent prediction after common neural causes are modeled.
6. Manipulate a named potassium-channel subtype in a named bouton and receiver circuit. Measure
   waveform, calcium, release, postsynaptic effect, representation, and behavior separately.
7. Compare coherent, lagged, traveling, and structured-splay states rather than treating maximal
   synchrony as the success condition.
8. Test whether the declared receiver changes state and whether that change is necessary for the
   predicted perceptual, mnemonic, autonomic, or motor consequence.
9. For the phenomenal-identity hypothesis, require content-specific perturbation and report effects,
   not only correlation with wakefulness, attention, arousal, or task difficulty.

The carrier hypothesis would be weakened if apparent cross-modal agreement disappears after common
sources and artifacts are controlled. The PWD account would be weakened if it adds no held-out
prediction beyond rate, power, conventional phase, and recurrent-state models. The identity claim
would remain unproved even if the coding and routing predictions succeed.

## Why the earlier image failed medical review

The quarantined plate remains useful as a record of the intended question, but not as medical art.
It:

1. gave occipital, parietal/TPJ, prefrontal, temporal, and cingulate cortex the same canonical
   six-layer cylinder and generic thalamic input;
2. drew arbitrary all-to-all cortical routes without declaring pathways, direction, or evidence;
3. treated action potentials, transmitter release, electric fields, magnetic fields, and mechanical
   waves as parallel copies of one sinusoidal phase message;
4. depicted potassium-channel inhibition as a universal action-potential and release amplifier;
5. added unlike signals into one "coherent NAPOT output" with no named receiver;
6. implied that phase alignment itself specifies content and that every region contributes one
   interchangeable sample;
7. omitted conduction delay, reference choice, inhibitory structure, receiver-specific
   transformation, anatomical heterogeneity, body-world consequence, and competing controls.

The replacement prompt is `IMG-SAN-DISTRIBUTED-PHASE-RELATIONAL-MULTISCALE-001`. It requires
declared pathways, relational phase measurements, coupled-but-nonidentical physical observables,
micro-meso-macro separation, receiver consequences, and no inner viewer or global phase sum.
