# The Brain as a Three-Dimensional Neural Network

> **Evidence boundary:** The brain is a spatially embedded biological network. That fact does not establish a universal Cartesian wiring lattice, a literal three-dimensional screen, or a point-for-point match between the position of tissue and the position of experienced objects.

**The brain as a three-dimensional neural network** is a historical SAN proposal about how physical
location, directed connectivity, changing population state, learned structure, and body-world
representation may interact. The phrase means more than "the brain occupies volume," but each kind
of three-dimensionality must be declared separately.

## Six meanings that must not be collapsed

1. **Physical embedding:** neurons, glia, vessels, extracellular space, nuclei, layers, tracts, and
   body connections occupy physical volume.
2. **Structural and effective graphs:** cells and populations form directed, weighted, delayed,
   plastic connections. The routes that are anatomically possible are not identical to the routes
   that are functionally effective in one moment.
3. **Population-state geometry:** simultaneous activity across many cells can trace a
   high-dimensional manifold. A three-dimensional plot of that manifold is an analytical
   projection, not necessarily the network's native dimensionality or a picture in the brain.
4. **Represented geometry:** neural activity can preserve location, direction, distance, topology,
   body configuration, object relations, or other task variables.
5. **Experienced three-dimensionality:** perception organizes depth, surfaces, objects, body
   position, and possible action into a coherent lived environment.
6. **SAN's multimodal volumetric hypothesis:** electrical, magnetic, chemical, mechanical, metabolic,
   vascular, pressure, and thermal state changes may jointly contribute to a distributed rendering
   process when an identified biological receiver can detect and use them.

Physical volume, graph topology, population geometry, represented space, and experienced space need
not use the same coordinates. Evidence for one does not automatically prove the others.

## Recovered SAN proposition

The June 2022 source `a0645z.md` describes neural arrays and local transformations as parts of a
three-dimensional graph. It asks how learned local patterns can be recognized by downstream
populations, recruited across larger assemblies, and combined with distributed electrical,
magnetic, mechanical, metabolic, vascular, pressure, and thermal state changes. Its "flow like water" language
is an analogy for changing state through a physical volume. The source's stronger scientific
proposal is that multiple kinds of local physical change may be detectable and consequential. It is
not evidence that signals literally ricochet through the whole brain like water.

The related June 2022 source `a0126z.md` adds a distributed graph of partial internal
representations. Different sensory and association systems can maintain differently transformed
parts of an object, body, or situation; more persistent context can coexist with faster-changing
features; and candidate future states can be evaluated through action. The 2022 Whitepaper then
organizes this into a receive-transform-project architecture in which local learned readiness can
recruit larger oscillating groups.

The source-faithful operation is:

```text
partial receptor and synaptic evidence
        -> receiver-specific local transformation
        -> axonal and synaptic projection
        -> convergence with other partial states
        -> recurrent redistribution across selected populations
        -> revised body-world and action state
        -> movement and returned sensory consequence
```

Here **scaling** or **magnification** means that a small learned cellular or local-circuit pattern may
help recruit a larger distributed state. It does not mean that one dendrite contains a lossless
miniature scene that is physically enlarged into the brain.

## What current neuroscience establishes

Three-dimensional connectomics can register anatomy in a shared physical coordinate space. The
Allen Mouse Brain Connectivity Atlas reconstructed mesoscale projections in a common 3D reference
space. The MICrONS program later co-registered functional responses with electron-microscopy
reconstructions in a cubic-millimetre-scale volume of one mouse visual cortex, showing that physical
proximity, dendritic and axonal opportunity, synaptic connectivity, and functional similarity are
related but not interchangeable.

Population geometry is also real but distinct from anatomy. Visual-cortical population responses
can occupy high-dimensional representational geometries. In medial entorhinal cortex, the activity
of a recorded grid-cell module formed a toroidal population manifold whose coordinates tracked the
animal's location. The torus describes a relation among population states; it is not a torus-shaped
piece of tissue or a universal template for all perception.

Local field effects provide a bounded bridge to SAN's physical-field branch. Experiments have shown
that weak electric fields can modulate or entrain active cortical networks under specified
conditions, and that extracellular fields can influence nearby neurons through ephaptic coupling.
Those findings do not establish strong long-range wireless signaling, a unified electromagnetic
consciousness field, or the complete SAN rendering mechanism. Mechanical, metabolic, vascular,
pressure, thermal, and magnetic branches require separate measurements and receiver mechanisms.

## Distributed observer-action

SAN does not move perception into an internal spectator. Each participating receiver is changed by
what reaches it, transforms that evidence according to its current state and learned structure, and
produces consequences for other receivers. The sparse, distributed network-and-body process that
maintains, tests, and acts from the evolving model is the observer-action process.

Under the tonic-canvas/phasic-ink interpretation, relatively maintained population conditions
supply receiver-specific comparison context, while consequential departures supply changing
detail. This is a proposed operational relation among measurable neural states, not a claim that a
literal screen, pixel grid, or universal rhythm exists inside the head.

## Discriminating tests

1. **Anatomy versus effective routing:** Compare a spatially embedded structural graph with
   task-dependent effective-connectivity models. Test whether location, direction, delay, and
   plastic edge state improve held-out predictions of population transitions and behavior.
2. **Anatomy versus represented geometry:** Decode a declared spatial or relational variable, then
   test whether its neural geometry persists when the physical locations of recorded cells are
   shuffled or controlled. A useful representation need not mirror tissue coordinates.
3. **Local-to-distributed recruitment:** Identify a learned local pattern, perturb it selectively,
   and measure whether a predicted larger population state, perceptual report, or action is
   recruited. Compare against nonspecific arousal, movement, and common-input explanations.
4. **Field-coupling branch:** After accounting for synaptic transmission and volume conduction,
   perturb a measured local extracellular field and test for receiver-specific changes in timing,
   recruitment, or behavior.
5. **Multimodal volumetric branch:** Co-register electrical, hemodynamic, metabolic, mechanical,
   thermal, body, world, and behavioral measurements without treating them as equivalent. The SAN
   model must predict held-out state transitions or action better than neural activity alone and
   identify which measured variable reaches which receiver.
6. **Closed-loop rendering:** Break the action-to-returned-sensation loop while holding sensory input
   as constant as possible. Test whether the proposed three-dimensional body-world state degrades in
   the specific way predicted by the model.
