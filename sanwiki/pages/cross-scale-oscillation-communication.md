**Cross-scale oscillation communication** is a candidate account of how timing at one neural scale changes the excitability or effective connectivity measured at another. It includes cross-frequency coupling within a circuit, interareal synchronization, traveling waves, and state-dependent modulation. These are related phenomena, not one mechanism.

## Scale must be explicit

Possible levels include membrane potential, dendritic compartment, spike train, local population, cortical layer, area-to-area pathway, thalamocortical loop, and whole-organism state. A slow rhythm recorded at the scalp is not automatically the controller of a gamma event in one cortical column.

Cross-frequency coupling can arise from genuine interaction, common input, waveform shape, evoked responses, nonstationarity, filtering, or movement artifact. Phase-amplitude coupling therefore requires controls before it is interpreted as communication.

## Direction is pathway-specific

Bastos et al. found stronger gamma-band feedforward and alpha/beta-band feedback influences across macaque visual areas ([Neuron, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.018)). This is important evidence that directed interactions can have different spectral profiles. It is not a universal rule assigning all bottom-up content to gamma and all top-down prediction to slower activity.

Plasticity also does not simply write a timing match. NMDA receptors, acetylcholine, LTP, and LTD participate in diverse cellular mechanisms whose effects depend on synapse, receptor composition, timing, neuromodulatory state, and history.

## SAN and GCS interpretation

The strongest GCS version defines a circuit-specific relationship among sensory evidence, contextual or predictive activity, proprioceptive and motor state, and returning consequences. A slower or more persistent state may constrain a faster event, and the event may update the state. The source, route, receiver, and consequence must be named at every scale transition.

## Tests

Use directional measures with appropriate reference and volume-conduction controls, separate oscillatory from broadband components, and compare against common-input and waveform explanations. Perturb the proposed source or receiver and predict the direction, latency, behavioral effect, and rescue.
