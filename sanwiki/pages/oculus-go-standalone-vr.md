[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s GDC 2018 hands-on report on the Oculus Go documents the first mass-market standalone VR headset at $199 — a hardware threshold relevant to the SAN agenda of making [[virtual-reality]] a viable substrate for neuroscience-informed immersive experiences.

The Go offered orientation-only tracking (3DOF) and a single controller yet delivered clear LCD visuals, fast app loading, and reliable WiFi multiplayer — demonstrating that the friction barrier to [[neuro-rendering]] applications could be dramatically reduced without full 6DOF tracking.

## SAN relevance

For [[self-aware-networks]] theory, the significance is infrastructural: affordable, frictionless VR lowers the threshold for deploying [[attention]] and perception research in naturalistic settings outside the lab. A headset you "throw into a bag" is a headset that could carry [[brain-computer-interface]] sensors into everyday environments.

## Key observations

- LCD clarity superior to competitors at the time.
- Cross-platform multiplayer with Oculus Rift users demonstrated ecosystem interoperability.
- The $199 price point was paired with plans to acquire Oculus Santa Cruz (6DOF successor) — foreshadowing the Quest trajectory.
