## Position

Read as a definitional consequence of its title in SAN, **[[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]] bridging** describes the mechanism by which a slow [[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]] — a low-frequency, sustained rhythm in a population of neurons — *bridges* multiple smaller assemblies that, in the absence of the bridging rhythm, would oscillate independently or at incompatible phases. When the bridging tonic rhythm is present, the assemblies entrain to it; perturbations in any one of them then propagate along the bridge to the others, which is what licenses the [[functional-fractal-isomorphism]] claim that small-scale perturbations can "tip an entire assembly". The mechanism therefore plays three roles in SAN: it is (i) the *cross-scale coupling element* between micro- and macro-assemblies, (ii) the *carrier* on top of which faster, phasic oscillations carry the [[coincidence-pattern-neural-bit|coincidence-pattern bits]] of conscious content, and (iii) the *amplifier* by which top-down EEG-dipole patterns ([[brainwave-vortex-attractor|alpha/theta/delta/gamma]] bands) exert influence on lower-level assemblies. In the SAN reading, [[oscillatory-binding-problem|binding]] across distant [[cortical-column-oscillatory-collaboration|cortical columns]] is partly underwritten by tonic-oscillation bridging — the binding event happens at fast (gamma/phasic) timescales, but it requires the slow tonic bridge to keep the columns in mutually compatible phase.
