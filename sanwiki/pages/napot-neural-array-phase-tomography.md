**NAPOT** ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Slice Phase Oscillation Tomography) is a SAN hypothesis about distributed neural timing, recurrent transformation, and reconstruction-like updating. Neural signaling includes spike timing, firing rate, waveform, synaptic chemistry, inhibition, neuromodulation, and population state; this page does not replace those mechanisms with phase alone.
> **Boundary note (2026-07-18):** Phase tomography is a SAN framing, not established evidence that phase variation is the qualia substrate or that each array takes literal 2D slices that stack into a 3D percept. The careful formulation is that recurrent neural populations may use timing, phase, amplitude, inhibition, and feedback to update distributed representations; SAN interprets some of those updates as tomography-like. See [[san-scale-ladder-and-claim-boundaries]], [[phase-language-firewall]], and [[screen-metaphor-no-inner-viewer]].

## Candidate mechanism

Cells participate in complementary receive and re-express roles: synapses and dendrites receive and integrate inputs, while axons and presynaptic terminals distribute effects to downstream receivers. Camera, eye, screen, and speaker comparisons are functional metaphors, not claims that a neuron contains optical or audiovisual hardware.

The retina, thalamic and midbrain nuclei, and cortical visual areas have different anatomy and operations. Similar convergence/divergence motifs may recur, but the brain does not literally replicate eye optics at every scale. Cross-scale recurrence is a SAN proposal requiring separate micro, meso, and macro evidence.

SAN proposes that oscillatory shape and timing may contribute information beyond mean firing rate. That is not an exclusive code: rate, timing, synchrony, waveform, inhibition, synaptic state, and population trajectory can all matter. Whether a measured change constitutes perceptual, emotional, or motor content requires a defined decoder, causal use, and controls.

## Historical hypotheses to test

1. **Phase and qualitative content:** SAN proposes a constitutive relationship; current evidence does not establish phase variation as the qualia substrate.
2. **Projection-slice tomography:** slice and CT language is an analogy unless the projection operator, sampling geometry, reconstruction target, and failure modes are defined and measured.
3. **Distributed rendering:** no single neuron contains the percept. The stronger SAN claim that aggregate phase state is constitutive of rendering remains to be distinguished from firing-rate, recurrent-state, predictive-processing, and control accounts.

## Established component anchors

- [Davis et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2802-y) measured traveling cortical waves associated with perceptual performance.
- [Mante et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12742) measured recurrent population dynamics supporting context-dependent computation.

These are component findings, not demonstrations of phase qualia or slice tomography.
