A **multiscale fractal neural oscillator** is the repeating topological circuit firing pattern that recurs isomorphically at multiple spatial and temporal scales within a [[self-aware-network]]. Because the pattern is scale-invariant, the brain can compare and compose patterns across scales — acting as a fractal analogy engine spanning [[dendrite]], circuit, and cortex.

## Core Claim

In [[napot]] terms, each neural oscillator group produces a firing pattern that is topologically isomorphic to oscillators at different scales. The brain treats this isomorphism as a physical analogy — a vector constellation that can be matched or abstracted regardless of absolute signal scale.

## Scale-Invariant Learning

The fractal repetition allows a pattern learned at one scale to be recognized at any other scale. A small cube and a large cube are both "cube" because the shape's spatial pattern is a fractal (scale-invariant) structure. Oscillating neuron groups act as pattern detectors at each scale; dendrites function as fine-grained feature detectors that feed into circuit-scale recognizers.

## Cross-Modal Parallelism

Different cortical areas simultaneously render tempo-spatially and fractally isomorphic frequency patterns. The cross-grid parallelization lets large-scale pattern analogies emerge across multiple sensory modalities — unifying perception, thought, and action under a single fractal topology.

## Phi and Resolution Capacity

[[phi-integrated-information]] in this framework measures not whether consciousness is present but rather the **resolution capacity** of a given brain — how finely it can distinguish and integrate patterns across scales. This reframes IIT's Phi from a binary presence/absence measure to a comparative resolution metric.
