Dopamine's role in effort and motivation: stimulants such as Adderall accelerate dopamine release, producing an initial performance boost, but subsequently deplete available dopamine faster than it is replenished — resulting in a period of lethargy and low motivation (the "zombie effect").

In SAN terms, dopamine functions as a **neuromodulatory gain signal** on phasic activity — increasing the signal-to-noise ratio of phasic bursts relative to tonic baseline (see [[tonic-phasic-oscillation]]). Depletion narrows the dynamic range, reducing the resolution of [[napot]] renderings and impairing selective [[attention]].

## Restoration via brainwave entrainment

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s early observation (2012): audio-visual stimulation (AVS), [[brainwave-entrainment|brainwave entrainment]], music, or aerobic exercise can stimulate additional dopamine synthesis and release, countering post-stimulant depletion. This anticipates the SAN-aligned insight that rhythmic sensory input — by directly driving [[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]] patterns — can up-regulate neuromodulatory tone.
