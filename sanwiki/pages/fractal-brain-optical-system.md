The brain mirrors the structural properties of the eye at multiple nested scales — cortical gray matter functions analogously to the retina (a distributed detection surface), while deeper subcortical structures serve as focal convergence points analogous to the lens. This [[gh-a0206z|self]]-similar optical architecture is a named [[fractal-pattern|fractal pattern]] in [[napot]] theory.

## Key claims

- **Cortex as retina**: Just as the retina contains arrays of photoreceptors sampling a light field, cortical columns sample a field of oscillatory phase patterns propagated up from sensory arrays.
- **Depth as focal length**: Subcortical structures (thalamus, basal ganglia) compress and redirect the "beam" of patterned activity, playing the role the lens plays for light — focusing divergent surface signals into convergent projections.
- **Fractal repetition**: The eye → brain correspondence is not coincidental but reflects a general biological principle: information-processing structures repeat their optical-computational motif across spatial scales.
- **Inverted image correction**: Light forms an inverted image on the retina; the brain's oscillatory rendering "reinverts" this representation to yield upright perception — a phase reversal that itself recurs in the fractal hierarchy.

## Relation to SAN theory

This [[fractal-pattern|fractal pattern]] underpins [[napot]]: if neural arrays are arranged like stacked retinas, then phase-variation signals propagate through them the way light propagates through an optical train — refracted, focused, and ultimately projected into a rendered scene. The conscious "image" is the output of this oscillatory tomography.

It connects to [[fractal-consciousness]] at a physical structural level: the same lens-retina motif appears at the molecular scale (ion channel apertures), cellular scale (dendritic arbors), column scale (cortical layers), and whole-brain scale (cortex / subcortex).
