## Overview

The *History of Super Information Theory (2017-2025)* is a foundational [[gh-b0049y|summary]] document detailing the sequential conceptual progression of [[super-information-theory|Super Information Theory (SIT)]]. It outlines the bottom-up evolution of SIT from a neuroscientific description of coincidence detection into a unified field-theoretic model of gravity and time.

The text emphasizes five major evolutionary phases:

## Phase I: Information as Coincidence (2017)
The theory began with the operational redefinition of a "bit" of information. Instead of a static symbol, information was redefined temporally as a coincidental pattern—a synchronized phase relationship or registered alignment of independent events.

## Phase II: Oscillatory Computation (2022)
SIT expanded into a multiscale framework heavily associated with the [[self-aware-network]] (SAN). It posited that cognition and agency in distributed networks arise from how coincidences compose across scales via phase-based coordination and predictive synchronization. 

## Phase III: Gravity and QGTCD (Late 2022)
The framework was extended into physics with [[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation]] ([[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]). By recognizing time as a structured, dynamical medium resulting from temporal alignment, gravity was mathematically reframed as an emergent property of gradients within this density field.

## Phase IV: Time as a Physical Medium (2024)
SIT transitioned from curvature-first intuition toward a scalar-field-first physical intuition. Time was fully treated as an operational physical medium rather than just a geometric background parameter.

## Phase V: Dissipative Computations (2025)
The theory introduced explicit mechanical engines for equilibration. Computation and phase synchronization were reframed as dissipative processes where localized signals relax into global equilibrium through pure wave-based temporal alignment.

## Future Implications

By formally tracking the shifts in SIT's conceptual layers, this document acts as an anchor for interpreting discrepancies between the [[whitepaper-2019]] early-stating and the definitive 2025 scalar-field structures.
