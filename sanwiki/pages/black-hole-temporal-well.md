In [[dark-time-theory]] / [[super-dark-time|QGTCD]], a black hole is not merely a gravitational well
but a **temporal well** — a region where time density reaches its maximum, collapsing the
information content of infalling particles into a state with no distinguishable "wiggles"
(entropy) from the outside perspective.

## The time tunnel model

Reality, under this framing, is a "time tunnel." As one approaches the center of a black hole:

- Time density increases without bound.
- Particles' energy is at maximum — they are collectively "moving faster in time" relative to
  an external observer.
- From outside, this appears as time frozen at the event horizon; from inside, particles may
  traverse billions of years of internal time before the external universe "catches up."

This explains black hole invisibility: particles inside are effectively moving faster than
light in the external time frame. Light cannot escape not because escape velocity exceeds *c*
in the Newtonian sense, but because the particles' temporal frame has outrun the photon's
capacity to propagate outward.

## Information paradox reframing

Rather than information being destroyed, [[dark-time-theory]] proposes that information is
**compressed** into an incomprehensibly dense temporal state. The "loss" is an observational
artifact: from outside, the information appears frozen. Over cosmological timescales — or via
Hawking radiation — the external universe may eventually "catch up" to the compressed state,
allowing information to re-emerge.

This reframes the paradox from *destruction* to *extreme temporal inaccessibility*.

## AdS/CFT correspondence connection

The AdS/CFT (anti-de Sitter / conformal field theory) framework treats bulk physics as encoded
holographically on a lower-dimensional boundary. Under the Dark Time interpretation:

- Time density variations in the AdS bulk correspond to modified boundary dynamics in the CFT.
- The event horizon acts as a **time-density boundary**, where the CFT encoding must account for
  infinite temporal compression in the bulk.
- Black hole entropy, viewed through [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]], becomes a count of the temporal microstate
  configurations compressed within the high-density time zone — a reinterpretation of the
  Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.

This suggests a novel angle: the holographic principle may be a consequence of time-density
gradient fields, with the boundary CFT providing a projection of interior time-density
microstates.
