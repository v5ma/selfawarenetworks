## Cerebellar prediction becomes a consciousness test

The source-faithful constructive chain from `a0468z.md` is:

```text
current body state + intended movement
-> predicted sensory consequence
-> action and returning sensation
-> prediction error and timing correction
-> revised body estimate and next action
-> possible change in embodied conscious content
```

Primary evidence supports the cerebellum's contribution to active force perception, self-motion,
timing, motor learning, and regionally differentiated cognitive functions. It does not establish
that the cerebellum alone generates consciousness. The decisive SAN test is whether selective
cerebellar perturbation changes embodied content after movement, arousal, attention, report, and
task difficulty are controlled.

## Dynamic self without a hidden observer

The joined source sequence is a development ladder rather than one repeated claim:

```text
2012: self as learned relations among body, memory, environment, and possible action
2013: self as a direction into a recurrent Hebbian loop
2014: successive physical cell groups build, compare, and update a world/self model
2015: current self as an on-the-fly population calculation and embodied prediction
2018: experimentally variable body ownership under altered multisensory contingencies
```

Mante and colleagues' recurrent-dynamics study provides a close established comparison for the
selection-vector language in `a0467z.md` and `a0473z.md`: context-dependent decisions can be
implemented by population dynamics that project relevant variables differently across contexts.
The defensible claim is flexible population computation, not that neurons have no biological
identity or that a line attractor is itself a self.

## Body ownership as controlled mismatch

`a0476z.md` is strongest when translated into experimentally controlled relationships:

```text
visual perspective + visuomotor and visuotactile timing + body form + agency + prior body model
-> degree and stability of avatar/body ownership
-> changed action, confidence, affect, or body estimate
```

The outcome is expected to vary across people, tasks, exposure durations, and clinical contexts.
A body-ownership illusion is not proof of a permanent identity transfer. VR, mirror therapy, and
psychoactive interventions must be evaluated separately under condition-specific trials and
safety rules.

## Developmental waves and sensory grounding

The news source underlying `a0477z.md` is Ackman, Burbridge, and Crair's 2012 Nature study. In live
neonatal mice, spontaneous retinal activity propagated throughout the visual system before eye
opening. Retinal waves were the primary source of activity in midbrain and V1 while modulating
ongoing activity in secondary visual areas. Later causal work showed that the spatiotemporal
properties of retinal waves instruct visual-circuit refinement.

This supports an important part of Micah's 2012 bridge: temporally and spatially structured neural
activity can help organize circuits before ordinary sensory experience. It does not show that the
waves are semantic tokens or that SAN's later PWD mechanism is already proved. The sensory-to-
symbol claim still requires learned generalization, reference, action, and cross-context tests.

## Multisensory hierarchy without a simple gradient

`a0457z.md` asks how multiple senses influence visual processing across cortical levels. Current
evidence supports cross-modal modulation even in primary sensory cortex, with prevalence and route
varying by area, species, task, and state. Some studies find direct or early modulation; others
show stronger convergence in higher areas. The useful SAN model is therefore a distributed,
hierarchy-dependent set of recurrent influences rather than “farther from the eyes means more
multisensory.”

## Choice and the full motor boundary

`a0462z.md` compresses reasons, timing, coincidence, excitation, inhibition, and movement into one
source. The current physiological bridge is:

```text
represented reasons + current state + coincident evidence
-> dendritic, cellular, and circuit transition
-> premotor and motor-pool recruitment
-> motor-neuron action potentials
-> neuromuscular transmission
-> muscle-fiber action potentials and calcium release
-> ATP-powered contraction
-> movement or vocal action
```

Neural timing organizes control and recruitment. It does not supply the chemical energy used by
muscle.
