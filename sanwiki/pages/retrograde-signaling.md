# Retrograde Signaling and Backward Influence

The phrase **backward signaling** can refer to several anatomically different processes. Historical
SAN notes sometimes place these processes next to one another. Recovering the intended idea requires
separating them before asking how they may interact.

## Four mechanisms that must not be collapsed

| Mechanism | Direction and route | Established meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Retrograde synaptic messenger | Postsynaptic cell to a presynaptic terminal across a chemical synapse | A messenger such as an endocannabinoid can change later transmitter release at defined synapses. |
| Backpropagating action potential | From the axon-initial/somatic region into the same neuron's dendrites | A spike can invade dendrites and interact with local input and plasticity mechanisms. |
| Retrograde axonal transport | Along an axon toward the soma | Molecular cargo and injury or trophic signals can travel within the cell. |
| Recurrent circuit feedback | Through other neurons and pathways back toward an earlier processing stage | Network activity can return through a circuit without traveling backward across the same synaptic cleft. |

These mechanisms can participate in one larger feedback architecture, but they are not synonyms.

## Established synaptic example

At defined synapses, postsynaptic endocannabinoids can travel retrogradely and act on presynaptic
terminals, transiently reducing release. This demonstrates that a postsynaptic state can regulate a
future presynaptic contribution. It does not demonstrate that a complete memory, image, or neural
program is transmitted backward across every synapse.

## Recovering the SAN source idea

The 2022 `a0007z` source explores branch- and synapse-specific output, postsynaptic influence,
backpropagating spikes, endocannabinoid signaling, and a prediction traveling "backwards." Some
sentences compress distinct mechanisms into one phrase. The most coherent source-faithful
interpretation is:

```text
receiver and dendritic state
-> local backward or recurrent influence
-> changed readiness of an earlier input or route
-> different response on a later cycle
```

That is a feedback and tuning claim. It should not be rewritten as a literal reversal of ordinary
axonal transmission. The original exploration is preserved in
[Micah Blumberg's `a0007z`](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0007z.md).

## Bounded SAN interpretation

SAN proposes that reception can change future transmission. Retrograde messengers,
backpropagating action potentials, local plasticity, and recurrent pathways are candidate mechanisms
by which the state produced at a receiver can alter later routing or tuning.

The scientifically stronger formulation keeps the route explicit:

```text
named receiver state
-> named backward or recurrent mechanism
-> named presynaptic, dendritic, or circuit target
-> measured change in a later response
```

Without those four items, "backward transmission" remains ambiguous.

## Testable bridge

An experiment should perturb one candidate route at a time and test whether a postsynaptic or
receiver-relative state changes later release, dendritic integration, route selection, or behavior.
Evidence for one mechanism cannot be counted as evidence for all four, and a local modulatory effect
cannot be promoted into network-scale memory reconstruction without additional measurements.
