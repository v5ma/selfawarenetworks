# Coherence-Dispersion Sculpting Hypothesis

The **Coherence-Dispersion Sculpting Hypothesis** is SAN's proposal that neural computation and experienced differentiation depend on an organized relation between cells that repeatedly fire together and cells that maintain separated relative phases. In the simplest case, coherence means that two cells fire together and continue oscillating together until a new event changes their relation. Structured dispersion means that cells maintain separated relative phases under a declared measure. A formal splay state is a population state whose phases are distributed around the cycle, often roughly uniformly; it is not a synonym for every anti-phase, desynchronized, or decoherent state.

Coherent co-activity may recruit, bind, or stabilize an assembly; dispersion, inhibition, and out-of-pattern events may separate, suppress, redirect, or differentiate activity. Neither pole is inherently correct, incorrect, conscious, or unconscious.

This is a proposed SAN mechanism, not an established law of neuroscience. Established research supports neural synchronization, transient desynchronization, timing-dependent plasticity, inhibition, and state-dependent communication as real phenomena. The stronger claim that their spatial organization helps construct qualitative content remains to be tested.

## Source genealogy

### 1949: Hebbian co-activity precursor

Donald Hebb's cell-assembly proposal supplies the historical precursor for the coupling side: repeated co-activity can strengthen the effective relation among participating cells. SAN does not originate the proposition that cells firing together can become more strongly associated.

SAN's proposed addition is a bidirectional population operator. Persistently separated phase relations are not treated merely as failed synchrony or noise; they may help preserve boundaries among assemblies, suppress competing routes, and create differentiated changes against the tonic population context. The further claim that this organized relation contributes to rendered qualitative content is specifically SAN's hypothesis.

## Required timing vocabulary

- **Phase locking** may occur near zero or at a stable nonzero lag.
- **Anti-phase** is a special two-oscillator relation near half a cycle.
- **Splay** refers to a population phase distribution, not all separation.
- **Intermittent synchrony**, **phase slips**, and **desynchronization** are distinct observations.
- **Quantum decoherence** is a separate technical concept and is not imported into this neural
  hypothesis.

## The operator at three scales

### Micro: synapse and neuron

Presynaptic timing, postsynaptic state, dendritic location, inhibition, and neuromodulation influence whether an input changes firing or plasticity. At this scale, "coherence" must be translated into measurable spike timing, membrane-potential relations, synaptic activation, and local oscillatory phase. "Dispersion" must be translated into a declared distribution of timing offsets, omitted spikes, inhibition, or phase spread.

### Meso: assembly and local circuit

Local populations can contain internally coherent subassemblies while remaining separated from competing assemblies by inhibition or phase dispersion. A winner-take-all episode, sparse recruitment, transient desynchronization, and oscillatory gating are possible component mechanisms. No single canonical circuit should be presented as universal across cortex.

### Macro: distributed recurrent network

Sensory, association, memory, body, valuation, and action systems may form temporarily coupled coalitions separated by structured boundaries. SAN proposes that the pattern of both coupling and separation contributes to the current observer-action state. This does not require all brain regions to share one phase or frequency.

## Tonic canvas and differentiated ink

The tonic canvas is the ongoing receiver context at a declared scale. The phasic ink is any consequential departure relative to that context, including:

- unexpected excitation or bursting;
- unexpected inhibition or omission;
- a change in phase relation;
- a change in duration, amplitude, or transmitted quantity; or
- entry into or exit from a locally coherent assembly.

The useful distinction is therefore not activity versus silence. It is expected population organization versus a receiver-detectable difference that changes later processing.

## Plasticity boundary

SAN explicitly proposes a connection between oscillatory coherence or splay-state separation and longer-term LTP/LTD organization. That joined proposal is not inherited intact from Hebb: Hebb supplies the co-activity precursor, while SAN attempts to connect together-firing and apart-firing population relations to both short-term rendering differences and longer-term changes in what routes can be recruited.

Timing-dependent plasticity supplies a plausible biological component bridge, but the current medical literature does not establish a universal mapping in which coherence always produces LTP and decoherence always produces LTD. Plasticity sign and persistence vary with pathway, cell type, delays, prior activity, neuromodulation, and induction protocol. The medically defensible SAN proposition is that histories of relative timing, alignment, separation, excitation, and inhibition bias plasticity in context-dependent ways, thereby stabilizing or weakening later routing.

For example, optogenetically controlled CA3-to-CA1 spike pairings produced timing-dependent potentiation and depression shortly after induction, but stable potentiation after three days for both causal and anticausal paired activity rather than asynchronous activity ([Anisimova et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhac050)). Computational work also shows that spike-timing-dependent plasticity can alter the duration and recurrence of synchronized and desynchronized episodes ([Zirkle and Rubchinsky, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2020.00052)). These results justify studying a two-way relation between timing organization and plasticity; they do not establish SAN's qualia claim.

## Feynman and Friston bridges

The 2022 SAN GitHub record already places components of the coherence-dispersion/plasticity proposal inside a wider difference-reduction synthesis; the 2025 source states the joined account more explicitly:

- **Feynman path-integral analogy:** contributions with different phases can interfere, while stationary-phase neighborhoods contribute coherently. SAN uses this as a mathematical-physical analogy for how many candidate wave relations can reinforce or cancel. A neural population is not thereby proven to calculate a quantum path integral.
- **Karl Friston's free-energy framework:** perception and action are modeled as reducing variational free energy under a generative model. SAN proposes that phase-wave differentials and changing coherence relations could provide part of a physical neural implementation of mismatch reduction. Free-energy minimization does not itself establish SAN's proposed oscillatory implementation.
- **SAN synthesis:** repeated excitation, inhibition, coupling, dispersion, and plasticity form an iterative process in which a current difference changes the network that will receive the next difference.

These are three distinct levels. Feynman's formalism is established quantum physics; Friston's framework is an influential mathematical theory of biological inference; the proposed bridge from both to SAN's neural coherence-dispersion operator is Micah's synthesis and requires independent neural tests.

## Distinction from adjacent theories

| Framework | Primary question | SAN residual |
|---|---|---|
| Communication through coherence | When does phase alignment facilitate effective communication? | What computational role is played by the organized boundaries and dispersions between coupled assemblies? |
| Phase coding | Does event timing relative to an oscillation carry information? | How does a receiver-relative change alter recruitment, suppression, rendering, and later routing? |
| STDP | How does relative spike timing change synaptic efficacy? | How do population-scale coherence and dispersion histories constrain those local events and become stabilized by them? |
| Winner-take-all and inhibition | How are competing responses selected or suppressed? | How do selected and excluded populations jointly structure the distributed rendered state? |
| PWD | What multidimensional event differs from the receiver's expected tonic state? | The coherence-dispersion operator describes how populations organize and sculpt those events across space and time. |

## Central prediction

Perceptual content should depend not on maximal global coherence but on a reproducible spatial and temporal pattern of:

1. locally coherent assemblies;
2. structured phase dispersion or inhibitory separation between assemblies;
3. receiver-relative phasic departures within those relations; and
4. later changes in response readiness or connectivity after repeated exposure.

The hypothesis gains support only if this organization predicts content, discrimination, action, or later plasticity better than matched models using anatomy, firing rate, spectral power, stimulus waveform, arousal, and task state alone.

## Decisive experiment

In a declared sensory circuit, estimate local phase and population coupling without averaging away subassemblies. Present matched stimuli while perturbing:

- within-assembly alignment;
- between-assembly phase dispersion;
- inhibition;
- overall firing rate and power; and
- stimulus timing relative to the receiver's ongoing tonic state.

Measure forced-choice content, confidence, reaction, motor consequence, reafferent updating, and later changes in effective or synaptic connectivity. The strongest SAN prediction is an interaction: the same local event should have different effects depending on its receiver-relative position within the coherence-dispersion pattern.

## Falsifiers and limits

The joined hypothesis is weakened if:

- anatomy, rate, power, and ordinary state variables explain the results without phase-organization measures;
- phase dispersion adds no reproducible information beyond noise statistics;
- perturbing the proposed boundaries does not alter content, routing, behavior, or plasticity;
- the effect does not generalize beyond one stimulation method or task; or
- the observed plasticity has no relation to the proposed short-term organization.

Even positive results would establish a neural coordination mechanism before establishing a phenomenal mechanism. Claims about qualia require an additional bridge from discriminable neural organization to experienced qualitative differentiation.
