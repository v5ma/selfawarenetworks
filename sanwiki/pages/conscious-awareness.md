The phrase **conscious awareness** is often ambiguous. A medically and experimentally useful page must say whether it means perceptual awareness of a stimulus, metacognitive awareness of one's own state, conscious access for report and control, or phenomenal experience.

## No universal dominance threshold

There is no established rule that a neural pattern becomes conscious when it is globally dominant, exceeds one amplitude, enters one frequency band, or crosses one prefrontal oddball threshold. Alpha, beta, gamma, theta, spikes, slow potentials, recurrent activity, and interareal coupling can all vary with stimulus, task, arousal, attention, and report. None is a universal consciousness code.

Similarly, awareness of an object and awareness of being aware are different experimental targets. The second adds metacognitive discrimination and confidence; it should not be inferred from recurrence alone.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can frame conscious awareness as a graded transition in causal availability. A candidate content is represented in particular circuits, preserved or transformed through recurrence, and made consequential for memory, comparison, body state, report, and possible action. The tonic state supplies a local reference and the phasic event supplies a candidate update, but both roles are receiver- and preparation-dependent.

This formulation preserves the theory's emphasis on a distributed observer while avoiding a hidden witness. The network does not first create a render and then become aware of it. The changing causal organization of the network is the proposed observing process.

## Measurement ladder

Evidence should be labeled at the level it actually supports:

1. stimulus information is present;
2. the organism discriminates or acts on it;
3. the content is flexibly accessible;
4. the organism can evaluate its own access; or
5. the pattern is proposed as part of phenomenal experience.

Each transition needs its own controls and failure conditions. A phase-aware SAN model should predict trial-by-trial access or metacognitive accuracy beyond stimulus strength, attention, arousal, report, movement, and simpler neural features.

## Primary anchors

- Watanabe et al. found separable neural signatures of spatial attention and perceptual awareness ([Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 2012](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00016)).
- Hatamimajoumerd et al. showed that report requirements materially affect apparent neural correlates of awareness ([Current Biology, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2022.07.068)).
