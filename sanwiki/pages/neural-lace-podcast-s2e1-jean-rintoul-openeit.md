## OpenEIT background

Electrical impedance tomography (EIT) injects tiny alternating currents and measures the resulting voltage patterns across electrode arrays to reconstruct impedance maps of internal tissue. OpenEIT makes this accessible outside clinical settings: a low-cost wearable device, open firmware, and Python-based analysis stack.

EIT is non-invasive and captures temporal dynamics at millisecond scale — contrast with fMRI (slow, expensive) or MEG (expensive, fixed). This makes it relevant to the SAN goal of closing the loop between brain state and external systems at behaviorally meaningful timescales.
