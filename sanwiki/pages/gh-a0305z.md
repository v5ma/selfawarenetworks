# "Spontaneous Spiking Is Governed by Broadband Fluctuations"

Davis, Muller, and Reynolds's 2022 Journal of Neuroscience article, ["Spontaneous Spiking Is Governed by Broadband Fluctuations"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9236292/), studies spike timing in relation to local field potential signals. The paper reports that broadband local field potential phase predicted spontaneous spike timing more strongly than phase estimates from individual narrow frequency bands.

This result is relevant to [Self-Aware Networks](self-aware-networks.md) and the [SAN brain summary Google Recorder cluster](clusters/san-san-brain-summary-google-recorder-cluster.md) because it supports a cautious reading of neural rhythms as broadband, transient population dynamics rather than as a fixed set of independent narrowband oscillators. The page's original SAN note treated the result as evidence that cortical excitability may depend on the instantaneous state of a broad neural ensemble rather than on allegiance to one frequency band.

The open question for [Neural Oscillatory Tomography](neural-oscillatory-tomography.md) is why brain areas can still appear to prefer particular power bands if moment-to-moment spike coupling is broadband. Possible explanations in the original note include regional morphology, sensory input structure, and local neurotransmitter composition, but those hypotheses remain interpretive and would need direct supporting citations before being stated as established findings.

Davis, Muller, and Reynolds conclude that broader signal representations may describe moment-to-moment cortical fluctuations with higher fidelity than narrowband-only representations. In SAN terms, this is compatible with treating tonic or broadband structure as an important context for phasic events, while keeping the paper's measured finding separate from the theory's broader claims.
