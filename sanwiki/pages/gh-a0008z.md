I remember discussing Stanford's Neurogrid project in 2014. I remember they had a novel approach, they were able to build their own chipset, I can't recall if it was from one of those overseas companies that let people design their own chip, they fabricate it and sell it to you. I think I remember looking into doing my own chip design after reading about Neurogrid. I was surprised to see a 2021 update to Neurogrid. In the paper linked below they break down the algorithm for their artificial [simulation of biology](computational-neuroscience.md). It is a good read.

If Neurogrid's neurons are dimensionless and all the same how can they selectively respond to some [patterns](dendritic-pattern-detection.md) and ignore others?

At first glance the Neurogrid does not appear to model [Plateau potentials](membrane-potential.md), APD [Action Potential Duration](action-potential-duration.md), or the variation in Calcium Ca2+ channel duration that might be caused by the quantity of Potassium K+ and other factors, resulting in a change in the quantity of [vesicle release](multi-vesicle-release.md).

At first glance I'm not seeing Neurogrid model how individual [synapses](synapse.md) on the [Apical dendrite](apical-dendrite.md) (or the neurons exit terminal array) might be up regulated or down regulated based on various factors (such as the synaptic and dendritic activity in the basal [dendrite](dendrite.md), the Soma burst AP [wave shape](action-potential-waveform.md), or cyclic-AMP signalling (up or down regulation of the Apical exit terminal resulting from basal dendritic stimulation, or metabotropic receptors in the basal [dendrite](dendrite.md))

I do not see Neurogrid modelling variation in the number of [vesicles](vesicle-sacks.md) released or the magnitude of [Neurotransmitter release](neurotransmitter.md).

In effect Neurogrid has made the [dendritic branches](dendrite.md) and the [synapses](synapse.md) obsolete by making them wireless. The question is whether essential detail is being discarded. What matters, and I'm not sure their model accounts for it, are factors like the growth and decay of [synaptic connections](synaptic-connectivity-dynamics.md), the up or down regulation of individual [synapses](synapse.md), adaptions to the [morphology](dendritic-morphology.md) of the [dendrite](dendrite.md) that make its responses more adaptive or less adaptive to certain memories, and receptor specific adaptions to or away from certain kinds of sensory stimulus.

The [synaptic](synaptic-configuration.md) and dendritic configuration needs to grow or decay to be responsive to selectively respond to certain [neural patterns](spatial-pattern-coding.md) that it represents with its high dimensional morphology, and it needs to be able to dynamically change its response to the patterns it has learned on a case by case basis.

Virtualizing the [dendritic branches](dendrite.md) and [synapses](synapse.md) into router messages should be fine as long as you are allowing the synapses and branches to grow, decay, and vary in their [high dimensional response](dendritic-computation-pyramidal-representation.md) so that they can be selective in their responses, and capable of tuning their response outputs to the rest of the network.
