# Neural Oscillation Rendering

> **Image status (2026-07-18):** The retained plate is conceptual and is not medically certified. It implies that equal spike counts with different waveform and phase shapes directly create distinct cortical display patches and percepts. Spike count, waveform, phase, synaptic history, and receiver response can differ without any one variable being a direct perceptual medium.

**Neural Oscillation Rendering** is the SAN proposal that perception depends on structured temporal population activity rather than spike count alone. The medically bounded insight is valid: two activity patterns with the same count can have different timing, cell identity, synaptic targets, dendritic consequences, and behavioral effects.

## Where the original claim overreached

Action potentials are not perfectly identical, and presynaptic waveform can affect calcium entry and release in some terminals. But an extracellular waveform can also reflect recording geometry and filtering. A waveform difference is not automatically preserved across an axon, decoded by a synapse, or mapped to experiential content.

Likewise, a local field-potential phase is a population measurement, not a display patch. Distinct phase relations can alter excitability or communication in a particular receiver without composing a literal perceptual field.

## Receiver-defined rendering

A stronger SAN account follows the biological chain:

1. a source population produces a declared spike, burst, or oscillatory pattern;
2. axons and terminals preserve or transform relevant distinctions;
3. release and postsynaptic receptors convert the pattern conditionally;
4. dendrites and local circuits integrate the consequences;
5. recurrent and downstream populations re-express selected relations; and
6. memory, body state, report, or action changes.

Rendering is the distributed causal use of these changing relations, not a picture assembled in cortex. The system itself is the observer-action process.

## Tests

Match spike count while varying one candidate temporal variable. Record at the source, terminal, and receiver; control for rate, cell identity, adaptation, and movement; then perturb the receiver's sensitivity and predict the loss and rescue of a specific discrimination.
