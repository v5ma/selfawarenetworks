## Core Claim

In quantum mechanics, a particle's most probable destination is where the phases of
all possible paths constructively interfere — the stationary points of quantum action.
[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] proposes the same principle operates at the biological scale: when two neurons
fire simultaneously or near-simultaneously, the electromagnetic, thermal, chemical, and
mechanical wave fields between them constructively align. The point on the [[dendrite]]
membrane receiving the greatest convergent excitation becomes the locus where a new
spine spontaneously nucleates and grows toward the source.

This reframes [[long-term-depression]] (LTD) and synaptic plasticity as
phase-interference effects rather than purely biochemical events: growth tracks the
phase gradient through configuration space, not merely chemical diffusion gradients.

## Key Mechanism

- Coincident neuron firing raises local heat / phase coherence in the interstitial field.
- The heated region represents "faster time" — more phase cycles per interval.
- This acts as an [[attractor]] analogous to Feynman's stationary-action paths.
- A [[dendrite]] spine nucleates at the membrane point of maximal constructive interference
  and elongates along the steepest phase gradient — the path that minimizes "quantum
  proper time" between the two firing cells.

This mirrors how [[gravity-as-oscillator-effect]] bends spacetime: mass concentrates
oscillators that warp nearby configuration space, pulling particles toward shorter
paths. Neuron co-firing creates a local version of the same [[attractor]] geometry.
