# Artificial Perception

Artificial Perception is the engineering goal of producing a specific sensory experience through a controlled neural or sensory-interface intervention. The intervention may reproduce part of a natural pathway or teach the nervous system to use a different input route. In SAN it is both a long-term NerveGear objective and a causal test of claims about how perception is constructed.

Perception is not assigned to one terminal cortical screen or localized inner viewer. The proposed result is a distributed change involving sensory, association, body, memory, and action systems. The source-faithful engineering operator is:

**natural or synthetic spatial-temporal input -> receptor, afferent, or targeted intervention -> receiver-specific transformation -> recurrent distribution across thalamic, cortical, body, memory, and action networks -> learned completion and multimodal reconciliation -> report, behavior, or experience -> feedback and adaptation**

## The ordinary-language problem

It is not enough to stimulate a region and observe that a person reports something. A strong artificial-perception program must explain which natural transformations are being replaced, what neural state is causally necessary, how the induced state is measured, and whether the resulting experience is specific, stable, reversible, and behaviorally useful.

The simplest useful test may be much smaller than full vision. The March 2021 SAN conversation proposes a tactile example: create the sensation of a tap at the wrist without a physical tap. That bounded target already requires localization, quality, timing, intensity, state control, and a way to distinguish genuine sensation from expectation or report bias.

## Historical source genealogy

### 2017: observe, decode, write, and reconcile

[[gh-nlp4|Neural Lace Podcast #4]] proposes observing an object and neural activity together, learning a pattern that generalizes despite trial-to-trial variation, and later writing a corresponding pattern back. The conversation also identifies the harder problem: an inserted glass or other object-pattern must be positioned and reconciled with the person's current scene, expectations, and ongoing activity rather than delivered as a context-free packet. It explicitly raises interference, unintended hallucination, and placement as open design problems.

### March 2021: two routes emerge

[[gh-b0309ywhisper|Neurons Stimulation Electrodes]] discusses a learned-substitution route: camera information mapped to stimulation of the tongue or skin, and developmental cross-modal remapping as evidence that useful perception need not always enter through the natural sensory pathway. This is a plasticity and training proposal, not evidence that stimulating any body site automatically produces vision.

[[gh-b0327ywhisper|The Flow of Information in the Brain]] develops the complementary route-matching question. Its bounded example is a felt tap at the wrist without a physical tap. The conversation asks whether a recorded ascending sensory pattern could be replayed near its natural receivers, but it also corrects itself: thalamic activity is sensory input rather than a complete thought or percept, deep thalamic access is risky, and cortical terminals, peripheral interfaces, or other routes may be preferable.

## The read/write ladder

1. **Record:** acquire neural, bodily, stimulus, and environmental signals with known spatial and temporal limits.
2. **Decode:** predict a bounded state within one person and task.
3. **Align:** determine which relational structure persists across time and context rather than expecting one repeated waveform.
4. **Choose a route:** distinguish route-matched replay from learned sensory substitution or remapping.
5. **Target:** identify a causal intervention site, receiver, and variable.
6. **Write:** deliver a calibrated spatial and temporal pattern.
7. **Learn:** when using a synthetic route, train and measure the mapping rather than assuming immediate natural interpretation.
8. **Reconcile:** test how the induced state combines or conflicts with the person's current scene, body, memory, expectation, and action.
9. **Verify:** measure experience, discrimination, behavior, physiology, and adverse effects.
10. **Adapt and generalize safely:** use closed-loop feedback, then test transfer, specificity, reversibility, durability, and failure modes.

Each rung requires separate evidence. Decoding does not imply writing. A report after stimulation does not prove multimodal reconstruction. Learned use of a substitution device does not prove that it recreated the ordinary modality in every respect. One person's successful calibration does not imply a universal code.

## Two distinct engineering routes

### Route A: route-matched replay or substitution

This route tries to intervene near a natural receptor, peripheral nerve, ascending pathway, thalamic relay, or cortical receiver and reproduce enough of the ordinary causal pattern to evoke a bounded percept. Its main questions are anatomical specificity, stimulation variables, receiver state, and recurrent completion.

### Route B: learned sensory substitution or remapping

This route converts information from one source, such as a camera, into a stable pattern delivered through another interface, such as the tongue or skin. The nervous system learns how that pattern relates to objects or actions. Classic tactile-vision substitution and experimentally rerouted visual input in developing ferrets show that useful cross-modal organization is biologically possible, but neither establishes unrestricted adult remapping, sensory equivalence, or a universal code.

The two routes may converge on overlapping distributed task networks, but they are not interchangeable. A decisive research program should compare immediate route-matched effects with performance acquired through training, and should test whether either route recruits modality-specific, supramodal, body, memory, and action systems.

## Candidate intervention routes

- peripheral nerves or sensory receptors;
- spinal or brainstem pathways;
- modality-specific thalamic nuclei;
- primary or association cortex;
- hippocampal or memory-related circuits;
- closed-loop combinations of recording and stimulation; and
- future biological or synthetic interfaces.

The thalamus is important but heterogeneous. It is one family of targets, not the universal port for all semantic content. The companion [[artificial-perception-via-thalamic-stimulation|thalamic stimulation page]] should be read as a candidate design route.

## The multimodal completeness problem

An apparently visual object also carries body position, expected touch, sound, action possibilities, memory, language, and valence. Full NerveGear-style perception would therefore require more than a high-resolution visual pattern. It would need compatible updates across sensory, body, memory, and motor systems, or a principled explanation for why a smaller intervention recruits the rest.

The Broccoli example makes this concrete: color, shape, texture, crunch, taste, smell, location, name, and affordance are related but not stored as one simple packet.

## SAN hypothesis and alternatives

SAN predicts that a write-in consequence may depend on receiver-relative timing and ongoing state, not only which cells are stimulated. The proposal is not that coherence means success and decoherence means failure. Relative alignment may support recruitment or convergence, while structured phase dispersion, inhibition, and out-of-pattern firing may differentiate, suppress, or redirect activity. A strong test should therefore compare controlled patterns of alignment and dispersion with rate-, location-, waveform-, and state-matched controls.

Alternative explanations include ordinary population recruitment, expectation, attention, recurrent completion, representational geometry, and nonspecific arousal. Artificial perception supports the stronger SAN account only if its distinctive variables predict and causally control results beyond those alternatives.

## What established intervention studies currently support

Existing human studies support bounded artificial percepts, not a universal perceptual write code:

- Intracortical microstimulation in the hand region of primary somatosensory cortex evoked localized tactile percepts whose locations followed expected somatotopy; changing stimulation amplitude changed perceived intensity ([Flesher et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.aaf8083)).
- Microstimulation in the human ventrocaudal sensory thalamus evoked focal hand and arm percepts. Electrode location, pattern, and intensity affected projected location, intensity, and naturalness ([Swan et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2017.10.017)). This supports a modality-specific thalamic route, not one generic thalamic port.
- Dynamic sequential stimulation across visual cortex produced recognizable traced forms in sighted and blind participants ([Beauchamp et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.04.033)). This shows that temporal sequence can matter without proving that a phase code is necessary.
- Tactile feedback delivered through somatosensory-cortex stimulation improved robotic-arm control in one participant with tetraplegia ([Flesher et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abd0380)). This demonstrates task-relevant closed-loop sensory feedback, not complete multimodal rendering.
- Tactile-vision substitution has shown that information from an artificial receptor can be delivered through the skin or tongue and learned for bounded tasks ([Bach-y-Rita et al., 1969](https://doi.org/10.1038/221963a0); [Bach-y-Rita and Kercel, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2003.10.013)). BrainPort training studies also report large individual differences and substantial dependence on task knowledge, which limits any claim of automatic or general visual replacement ([Pamir et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/17483107.2020.1821102)).
- In neonatal ferrets whose retinal projections were experimentally routed into auditory pathways, visually responsive auditory cortex developed orientation-tuned organization ([Sharma et al., 2000](https://doi.org/10.1038/35009043)). This is evidence for developmental cross-modal plasticity under a specific manipulation, not evidence that an adult brain can interpret arbitrary stimulation without training or constraints.

Evidence concerning oscillatory phase is not uniform. One visual flicker and transcranial alternating-current experiment found a condition-specific benefit for in-phase theta stimulation over visual cortex ([Somer et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_01539)), whereas a somatosensory experiment found no detectable frequency- or phase-dependent effect of transcranial alternating-current stimulation on near-threshold touch ([Mecarelli et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33051523/)). Differences in modality, target, field strength, depth, task, and method prevent either result from serving as a general verdict on SAN.

Timing-dependent plasticity provides a narrower biological bridge. Precisely timed pre- and postsynaptic activity can produce potentiation or depression, but the sign and persistence depend on cell type, pathway, delays, prior activity, and induction protocol. An optogenetic CA3-to-CA1 study found timing-dependent potentiation and depression after induction, but stable potentiation after three days for both causal and anticausal paired activity, not asynchronous activity ([Anisimova et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhac050)). This supports testing a relation among temporal organization, coupling, and plasticity; it does not justify a universal equation of coherence with LTP or decoherence with LTD.

## Requirements for a future medical illustration

A medically defensible wrist-tap illustration must declare the stimulus location and identify the natural reference route as a simplified pathway rather than one universal touch circuit:

1. **Receptors and peripheral territory:** a tap may recruit more than one class of low-threshold cutaneous mechanoreceptor. The peripheral nerve depends on the exact wrist location and may involve median, ulnar, or superficial radial territories.
2. **Ascending route:** show the dorsal-root ganglion and spinal entry, ipsilateral ascent in the cuneate fasciculus for the upper limb, synapse in the cuneate nucleus of the medulla, sensory decussation through internal arcuate fibers, contralateral ascent in the medial lemniscus, relay in the ventral posterolateral thalamus, and projection to contralateral primary somatosensory cortex ([Qi et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24860443/)).
3. **Distributed consequence:** continue from primary somatosensory cortex into recurrent somatosensory, association, body-state, memory, and action networks. Do not terminate in a screen, object packet, or inner viewer.
4. **Candidate substitutions:** compare peripheral-nerve, ventrocaudal-thalamic, and somatotopically targeted somatosensory-cortex stimulation as distinct routes. Add learned tongue or skin substitution as a separate route, not another point on the same anatomical line.
5. **Controlled variables:** location, delivered charge or field, pulse width, frequency, temporal sequence, receiver state, and relative phase organization. Compare aligned, dispersed, and phase-scrambled patterns without defining any one regime as a genuine percept.
6. **Verification:** require localized report, forced-choice discrimination, behavioral use, physiological response, learning curve, context-conflict tests, reversibility, and adverse-effect monitoring.
7. **SAN residual:** test whether tonic-state measurements and the relative organization of coherence, dispersion, excitation, and inhibition improve prediction and control beyond anatomy, delivered charge, rate, waveform, training, expectation, and recurrent completion. Separately test whether those patterns predict later potentiation or depression.

The figures previously associated with this page failed medical review and are not evidence for the article. Any future plate must contrast established anatomy and intervention evidence with the separately identified SAN timing-and-state hypothesis.

## Safety and epistemic boundary

Artificial perception is an invasive or potentially high-risk research domain. Wiki descriptions are theory and experiment-design notes, not clinical instructions. Any human intervention requires appropriate medical, ethical, regulatory, and informed-consent oversight.
