# fNIRS Brain Imaging

Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) sends near-infrared light through superficial head tissues and estimates changes in oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin from detected light. It is portable and comparatively tolerant of movement, but it is a hemodynamic measurement rather than a direct measurement of spikes, synaptic currents, phase, or neural content.

## What fNIRS preserves

fNIRS can follow task-related cortical hemodynamic changes over seconds. Performance depends on source-detector geometry, wavelength selection, optode-scalp coupling, hair, extracerebral blood flow, motion correction, experimental design, and the reconstruction model. Conventional arrangements are most sensitive to superficial cortex. High-density diffuse optical tomography can improve spatial sampling, but does not remove these physiological and inverse-problem limits.

Calling fNIRS a portable alternative to fMRI describes an engineering trade-off, not equivalence. Both commonly follow hemodynamic consequences of neural activity, but they use different physics, sampling geometries, depth sensitivities, and reconstruction assumptions.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can use fNIRS to test slow, spatially distributed consequences of neural activity during perception and action. A correlation between an fNIRS pattern and a task would not directly demonstrate phase-wave differentials, a tonic canvas, or NAPOT. Those claims require convergent electrophysiology, behavior, anatomy, and an explicit cross-modal model.

The historical Neural Lace and NeurotechX work is relevant as an experimental design lineage: portable optical sensing, body tracking, and spatial analysis could be combined to study embodied tasks. It should not be described as already recovering a neural rendering.

## Evidence boundary

- Established: fNIRS estimates cortical hemodynamic change from near-infrared optical measurements.
- Established: signal quality and inference depend strongly on anatomy, systemic physiology, motion, and analysis choices.
- Proposed by SAN: slow optical patterns may be one observable consequence of recurrent neural-array dynamics.
- Not established: that fNIRS directly reads firing bursts, oscillatory phase, semantic content, or a rendered percept.
