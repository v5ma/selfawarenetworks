# Tonic Powerbands and Reference-Frame Hypotheses

> **Scientific boundary:** Delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma are broad observational frequency ranges, not fixed anatomical channels or universal cognitive roles. Frequency-band power alone does not identify a reference frame, prediction, error signal, perceptual canvas, or represented timescale.

## Why the distinction matters

A powerband summarizes activity observed through a particular instrument, montage, location, reference, time window, and spectral method. Similar frequencies can arise from different generators, and one circuit can express several rhythms. Conversely, a maintained neural context need not be a single narrow oscillation.

A **reference frame** requires a declared variable and what it is measured relative to. A **prediction error** requires a declared prediction, observation, and discrepancy. A **tonic state** requires a maintained receiver condition. A **phasic event** requires a bounded departure. These definitions overlap only when an experiment demonstrates the relation.

## SAN's preserved proposal

SAN proposes that ongoing tonic population activity supplies a shared differentiating context or canvas and that comparatively phasic changes supply differentiated ink. At macro scale, coordinated rhythms may contribute to how distant populations become mutually usable, but SAN does not require one global oscillator or universal phase.

## What would support the hypothesis

Evidence should establish:

1. a reproducible ongoing state in a named population;
2. a bounded incoming or locally generated update;
3. receiver-relative phase, amplitude, or timing that improves held-out prediction;
4. an anatomical route and downstream consequence;
5. selective perturbation causing the predicted loss;
6. compensating rescue.

Band-power correlation alone is insufficient.
