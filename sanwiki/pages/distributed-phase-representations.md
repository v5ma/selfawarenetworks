Consciousness in SAN theory is not localised to any single region but emerges from
"temporally and spatially distributed" phase patterns across neural tissue — multiple
brain areas activate simultaneously, each contributing a phase state, and the aggregate
across those phases *constitutes* perception.

## Core claim

Brain regions like the temporoparietal junction (TPJ) and frontal-parietal networks are
better understood as activity *indicators* — the LED of the network — than as central
controllers. What matters is the ensemble of phase states propagating across distributed
arrays, not any single area's output. This connects directly to [[napot]]'s framing of
neural arrays as projection surfaces for oscillatory patterns.

## Multi-modal signal carriers

Neurons can communicate phase information through at least four physical media
simultaneously:

1. **Action potentials** — discrete spikes that neighbor neurons can "notice"
2. **Chemical neurotransmitter release** — molecular amplitude modulation of phase
3. **Electromagnetic waves** — electric and magnetic field distributions across tissue
4. **Mechanical / acoustic waves** — pressure propagation through neural tissue

All four carriers can encode the same phase pattern at different bandwidths and ranges,
producing redundancy and cross-scale coherence. This multi-modal transmission is a
prerequisite for [[fractal-pattern]] repetition across scales.

## Potassium-sodium dynamics as phase amplification

Inhibited potassium channels prolong action potentials, allowing more neurotransmitter
release per spike and thus creating "brighter" (higher-amplitude) phase representations.
This is a local amplification mechanism — individual synapses can up-weight their
contribution to the distributed phase sum without changing the network's structural wiring.

## History

The localisationist view assigned consciousness to specific regions (V1 for vision, TPJ
for [[gh-a0206z|self]]-awareness). SAN's distributed-phase framing replaces this with an ensemble
model: no region is special; each is one contributor to the phase sum.
