# Electrical Valence: A SAN Hypothesis About Receiver-Relative Drive

The 2022 note [[gh-a0271z|a0271z]] used **electrical valence** while asking how a changing
neural pattern can become behaviorally consequential. Its title linked the psychological word
*valence* with electrical interactions. That wordplay is historical context, not an established
identity between affective valence, valence electrons, field power, and neural action.

The source-faithful SAN proposal is narrower: **electrical valence** names the signed, receiver-
relative effective drive produced when contextual state, excitation, inhibition, timing, waveform,
synaptic efficacy, and pathway state make a downstream transition more or less likely.

## Keep four meanings separate

| Term | What it can mean | What it must not be equated with |
|---|---|---|
| Affective valence | Appetitive or aversive value in behavior and affective neuroscience | Net membrane drive, field strength, or muscle force |
| Electrical potential or field | A measured voltage, current, or field generated by defined tissue and recording geometry | Semantic content, motivation, or an organism-wide causal field |
| Effective neural drive | The net influence of converging excitation, inhibition, neuromodulation, intrinsic state, and timing on a named receiver or motor population | A conserved energy-like substance called "valence" |
| Mechanical energy | ATP-powered molecular work in muscle | The neural control signal that recruits contraction |

## Established physiology

At a receiver, postsynaptic inputs can sum or oppose one another. Whether they influence firing
depends on synaptic location and efficacy, membrane state, inhibition, conductance changes,
conduction delay, and timing. In motor systems, distributed premotor input is transformed by motor
neurons into action-potential patterns, then at the neuromuscular junction into muscle electrical
activity and calcium-dependent contraction. The control signal does not supply the mechanical energy;
ATP does.

- [Farina, Negro, and Dideriksen (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24860172/) describe how
  common synaptic input is reflected in the effective neural drive to muscle under stated model and
  measurement conditions.
- [Hug et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP283698) review common input, recruitment order,
  and motor-neuron functional groups.
- [Liu et al. (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33343299/) review the motor-neuron action
  potential, acetylcholine release, muscle-fiber activation, calcium handling, and contraction route.

None of those results establishes an Electrical Valence variable or a universal phase code for
choice. They establish named components that any such proposal must measure.

## The SAN interpretation

[[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|Tonic context]] supplies a receiver's ongoing reference and
readiness. A [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] is a proposed typed change relative
to that reference. When multiple inputs arrive with compatible timing and sufficient efficacy, their
net effect may raise a receiver or population toward a transition; inhibition, incompatible timing,
or changed state may instead reduce it.

```text
ongoing receiver and route state
-> excitatory, inhibitory, and modulatory contributions with delays and timing
-> receiver-specific net effective drive
-> threshold crossing, suppressed transition, or updated readiness
-> motor-pool recruitment only when the declared circuit supports it
-> action and returned sensory consequence
```

SAN calls the proposed, receiver-relative quantity in this chain electrical valence. It is not a
scalar that can be inferred from a single EEG amplitude, a single phase offset, a single neuron, or
an affective label. Its units and operational definition must be declared in each experiment.

## Why the bat evidence is useful but limited

Bat vocalization experiments measured pre-vocal changes in the **frontal auditory field (FAF)**,
caudate, and auditory cortex. FAF is a bat frontal vocalization area; it should not be relabeled as
prefrontal cortex. The 2020 authors explicitly note that a FAF-prefrontal analogy remains
controversial. These studies measured band-limited power, coupling, spike-field timing, and
statistical directionality. They did not measure an Electrical Valence variable.

- [Weineck, Garcia-Rosales, and Hechavarria (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000658)
  reported call-related pre-vocal beta and high-gamma power changes and theta-band FAF-caudate
  coupling. Gamma spike-LFP locking occurred before vocalization, but differences between call
  conditions had small effect sizes and high variability.
- [Garcia-Rosales et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31230-6) used directed
  phase-transfer-entropy analyses to estimate predominantly FAF-to-auditory-cortex directionality
  before vocalization and an auditory-to-FAF shift after echolocation. This is a directed statistical
  estimate, not direct tracing of axonal traffic.

The SAN extension is that a declared receiver-relative PWD model may organize these component
measures into a testable account of route commitment. It must outperform models using task variables,
firing rate, power, ordinary circuit state, movement preparation, and known anatomy alone.

## A testable version

For a named receiver or motor population, compare a baseline model with a richer SAN candidate.

```text
baseline: task + anatomy + firing rate + local power + known circuit state
SAN candidate: baseline + receiver-relative timing + excitation/inhibition + waveform + route state
```

The Electrical Valence interpretation gains support only if the added, declared variables improve
held-out prediction of route commitment, action timing, or a receiver-specific physiological effect,
and if targeted perturbation changes the predicted result. It is weakened if a simpler model explains
the same data equally well, or if the named variables have no causal effect after controls.
