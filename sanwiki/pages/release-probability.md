# Release Probability

**Release Probability** (Pr) is the per-action-potential probability that a presynaptic [[synapse|synaptic terminal]] releases its docked neurotransmitter vesicles into the synaptic cleft, and within [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] it is treated as a **dynamically tuned**, locally feedback-regulated quantity that participates in [[criterial-causation|criterial causation]] and short-term plasticity rather than as a static parameter — with synapse-by-synapse, region-by-region, and use-dependent modulation. SAN's specific position is that Pr regulates [[multivesicular-release|multivesicular release (MVR)]] decisions, that the dorsal-vs-ventral [[hippocampus|hippocampal]] split in initial Pr is the canonical worked example of regional Pr-tuning consequences, and that potassium-channel dynamics are a candidate mechanism for the link from membrane state to vesicle Pr that the standard calcium-only story does not fully capture.

## Position

Within [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks proper, release probability occupies three positions. First, it is the **switch between facilitation and depression** in short-term plasticity. `b0272ydone--md`, line 59, [[gh-b0272ydone|on github]] gives the structural rule: "synaptic strength is dynamically regulated in a use-dependent manner. At synapses with a low initial release probability, closely spaced presynaptic action potentials can result in facilitation, a short-term form of enhancement in which each [subsequent release is more probable]." `rexnote09--md`, line 226, [[gh-rexnote09|on github]] compresses this to a slogan: "Low initial release probability allows sustained facilitation during high-frequency input." This is the load-bearing functional consequence of Pr in SAN's reading: **low initial Pr is what makes a synapse usable as a high-frequency integrator** rather than as a one-shot relay.

Second, the **dorsal-vs-ventral [[hippocampus]]** split is the canonical regional worked example. `rexnote10--md`, line 28 (and the duplicate at `rexnote12--md`, line 544) gives the dorsal side: "Dorsal hippocampal synapses maintain a relatively low initial release probability, allowing for sustained synaptic facilitation during high-frequency presynaptic input. This facilitation occurs through [Ca²⁺ accumulation and short-term plastic mechanisms]." `rexnote10--md`, line 30 (and `rexnote12--md`, line 546) gives the ventral contrast: "Ventral synapses, conversely, show high initial release probability, leading to rapid depletion of synaptic vesicles and subsequent synaptic depression during sustained high-frequency acti[vation]." The same line continues: "These regional differences in release probability are maintained through specific molecular mechanisms, including differences in calcium channel distribution and vesicle [pool composition]." This is the SAN-original framing reading: dorsal [[hippocampus]] is **wired for sustained integration** (via low Pr → facilitation), ventral [[hippocampus]] is **wired for transient response** (via high Pr → depression), and the two regional roles are *encoded by Pr* rather than by gross anatomy or by neurotransmitter identity.

Third, the **potassium-channel mechanism** is the SAN-original mechanistic conjecture. `vecB--md`, line 1218, [[gh-vecb|on github]] records the chain of reasoning: the standard story attributes Pr modulation to [[gh-a0138zcalcium|calcium dynamics]], but [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s conjecture is that potassium channel dynamics also matter — "Your idea suggests that the release probability might be tightly linked to potassium ion dynamics, reflecting the neuron's 'sensing' of its input. This deterministic c[ontribution would be a SAN-original mechanism]." The same line records the literature search [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] commissioned: "Are there studies that directly link potassium channel dynamics with vesicle release probability? Research into the role of potassium in spike timing and synaptic modulation could provide insights." And the response confirms the gap in the literature: "The relationship between potassium (K⁺) channel dynamics and synaptic vesicle release probability is a nuanced area of neuroscience, with research indicating both direct and indirect influences. While calcium (Ca²⁺) i[s the primary trigger]…" then "Direct Influence of Potassium Channels on Vesicle Release Probability: Research directly linking specific potassium channel activity to changes in vesicle release probability is limited." The SAN-original move is therefore to take this gap as a **target for testable prediction**: K⁺ channel manipulations should produce measurable Pr shifts at synapses where the K⁺ → Pr coupling is operative, separable from the standard Ca²⁺ → Pr pathway.

## Chronology

Pr as a parameter has been part of mainstream presynaptic neuroscience since the 1950s-1970s (Katz and Miledi's quantal-release work, Dodge-Rahamimoff's Ca²⁺-dependence formula). ## Post-SAN

Mainstream presynaptic neuroscience treats Pr as primarily Ca²⁺-driven (number of open Ca²⁺ channels × Ca²⁺-sensor occupancy at synaptotagmin) with longer-term modulation by phosphorylation cascades (PKA, PKC, [[camkii]]), kinase-regulated active-zone proteins (RIM, Munc13), and presynaptic G-protein-coupled receptors. SAN agrees with the Ca²⁺-as-primary-trigger framing (`vecB--md`, line 1218: "While calcium (Ca²⁺) i[s the primary trigger]…") and with the per-terminal heterogeneity finding (`a0221z--md`, line 19), but makes two distinct testable claims the mainstream does not. First, the **K⁺ channel → Pr coupling** (`vecB--md`, line 1218) — that potassium-channel dynamics directly modulate Pr in addition to their indirect effect via [[membrane-potential|membrane potential]] — is a SAN-original prediction that is not standard in the literature ("Research directly linking specific potassium channel activity to changes in vesicle release probability is limited," per `vecB--md`, line 1218). Second, the **dorsal-vs-ventral Pr split as functional encoding** (`rexnote10--md`, lines 28, 30) is read by SAN not as a side-effect of regional anatomy but as the *primary mechanism* by which the dorsal and ventral [[hippocampus]] implement different computational regimes (sustained integration vs. transient response).

Where SAN's framing converges with the published facilitation/depression literature is on the rule that low Pr → facilitation, high Pr → depression (`rexnote09--md`, line 226). Where it goes beyond that literature is in (a) reading Pr as a *participating decision variable* in [[criterial-causation|criterial causation]] rather than as a downstream consequence of presynaptic state, (b) the K⁺ channel mechanistic conjecture (`vecB--md`, line 1218), and (c) the explicit linkage to MVR as a Pr-controlled output (`a0221z--md`, line 40).
