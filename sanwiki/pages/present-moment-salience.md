# Present-Moment Salience

**Present-moment salience** is a SAN proposal about how a change can gain influence within an
ongoing distributed neural state. A relatively maintained context provides the comparison
background; a receiver-relative difference can then alter recurrent processing, attention,
memory, sampling, or action.

This is not a claim that one attention schema, brain region, neuron, oscillation, or scalar selects
the contents of consciousness. Salience, novelty, surprise, attention, awareness, and report are
related but distinct experimental variables.

## Recovered SAN source

The previous Wiki entry was an evidence-free automation stub. The missing operation is explicit in
the November 5, 2023 conversation preserved at [[gh-y0037|Sensory Stabilization, Tonic Context, and
Recurrent Rendering]] and first located in public Git history on January 4, 2024.

The source contrasts common or repeated activity with rare, novel, or differentiated activity. It
describes ongoing tonic activity as background context and a phase-wave differential as a
temporospatial departure that can become prominent "in the present moment, here and now." It then
uses canvas, ink, and clay as functional analogies for maintained context and changing detail.

The source also maps tonic and phasic roles too rigidly onto low-frequency/high-amplitude and
high-frequency/low-amplitude activity. The recoverable SAN operation is broader:

```text
maintained receiver context
-> context-relative sensory or internal difference
-> weighting by novelty, relevance, goals, bodily state, and adaptation
-> changed recurrent routing and effective influence
-> attention, memory, resampling, report, or action
-> consequences update the next receiver context
```

The frequency-band assignment is therefore a historical hypothesis, not a universal physiological
definition of tonic context or phasic difference.

## Established science

Stimulus salience depends on behavioral context as well as novelty and frequency of occurrence. In
an event-related fMRI study, novel visual, auditory, and tactile events recruited a distributed
frontal-parietal-cingulate set of regions rather than one modality-independent "present moment"
center ([Downar et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00636.2001)).

Resting-state fMRI has also distinguished an intrinsic salience network, anchored in the dorsal
anterior cingulate and frontoinsular cortices, from a frontoparietal executive-control network
([Seeley et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5587-06.2007)). This network-level
description does not make the salience network the observer or the sole source of conscious
content.

Novelty, deviance, and involuntary attention are not interchangeable. Auditory experiments found
different electrophysiological and behavioral responses to a rare deviant tone and a novel sound
([Escera et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1162/089892998562997)).

Bayesian surprise is also not the same as physical rarity or Shannon information. It measures how
an observation changes an observer model from prior to posterior beliefs; rare events need not be
surprising to a particular observer, and surprising events can attract gaze
([Itti and Baldi, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2008.09.007)).

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats present-moment salience as **receiver-relative foregrounding** within the distributed
observer-action system. A candidate formalization is:

```text
d_i(t) = x_i(t) - E[x_i(t) | C_i(t)]

S_i(t) = F(|d_i(t)|, precision, learned relevance, goals,
           bodily state, adaptation, and available routes)
```

Here, `C_i(t)` is the declared receiver context, `d_i(t)` is a candidate differential, and `S_i(t)`
is its context-dependent effective influence. These equations are SAN model proposals, not
established neural laws. They explicitly reject the old page's unsupported formula
`salience = input - expectation`.

Within SAN, a [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] may carry several measured
differences, including timing, phase, rate, amplitude, duration, transmitted quantity, variability,
and network position. A PWD becomes relevant to salience only if a declared receiver uses that
difference and a measurable downstream consequence follows. Difference alone does not establish
attention, conscious awareness, or semantic content.

## Scale check

| Scale | Medically bounded operation | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | A receptor, synapse, dendritic branch, or neuron responds differently because of its recent state and inputs. | One cell contains the present moment or recognizes every feature. |
| Meso | A local circuit changes gain, competition, recurrence, inhibition, or routing for some candidate activity. | One canonical cortical column universally selects conscious content. |
| Macro | Distributed sensory, salience, control, memory, thalamic, subcortical, and motor systems alter what controls later processing and action. | One salience network is an inner viewer or complete consciousness mechanism. |

The network itself performs detection, comparison, routing, and action through these distributed
changes. No additional localized observer watches the result.

## Claim ladder

1. **Established:** neural responses depend on context and history; novelty and behavioral
   relevance can change attention and distributed neural activity.
2. **Supported interpretation:** foregrounding is better modeled as a distributed, receiver- and
   task-dependent change in effective influence than as one universal salience scalar.
3. **SAN hypothesis:** tonic receiver context and typed phasic/PWD differences help organize which
   distributed update gains influence over the next rendering-action cycle.
4. **Stronger experimental claim:** a full receiver-relative PWD description will predict later
   attention, memory, report, resampling, or action better than physical rarity, firing rate,
   spectral power, arousal, movement, and task relevance alone.

The stronger claim fails if the PWD variables add no preregistered held-out predictive or causal
value, or if the effect disappears after task, movement, adaptation, arousal, and receiver state
are controlled.
