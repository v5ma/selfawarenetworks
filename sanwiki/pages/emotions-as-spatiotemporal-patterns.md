Emotions are **spatial and temporal patterns** — four-dimensional structures in neural state space rather than discrete symbolic states or continuous chemical gradients. This framing extends [[cellular-oscillating-tomography]] and [[napot]] into the domain of affective experience: if sensory qualia emerge from oscillatory tomography, so do emotional qualia.

## The four-dimensional view

A spatiotemporal representation of emotion captures both *where* in the brain a pattern is active and *when* (its phase relationships over time). This is consistent with SAN's broader claim that conscious experience is the shape of oscillatory waves across neural arrays — emotion is a particular class of those shapes, characterized by global-scale involvement and persistence over longer timescales than perceptual qualia.

## The encoding problem

Individual differences in brain wiring, developmental history, and biology create a **translation problem**: the same "fear" pattern in two people's brains occupies different spatial addresses and slightly different phase signatures. This does not invalidate the spatiotemporal view — it means emotional encoding is personal and non-universal, calibrated to each individual's oscillatory architecture. Transferring emotion across brains or into artificial systems requires solving this translation, not abandoning the spatial-temporal model.

## Bidirectional scale coupling

The same discussion ([[gh-b0252y|b0252y]]) highlights bidirectional feedback between microscale and macroscale neural activity — small oscillations influence large-scale brain waves, which in turn constrain small-scale activity. This coupling is relevant to how emotions (macroscale, global) modulate perceptual qualia (more localized) and vice versa. See [[napot]] for the multi-scale oscillatory hierarchy.

## Implications for BCI and AI

If emotions are 4D patterns, brain-computer interfaces reading only firing rates or single-channel signals miss the essential structure. Full emotional readout would require capturing spatial phase relationships across many nodes simultaneously. Current electrode arrays face this constraint — noted in the same source as a reason for limits on BCI fine motor control, extended here to affective decoding.
