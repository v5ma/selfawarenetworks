# Coincidence Detection: From Synapses to SAN Arrays

Coincidence detection is a family of biological operations in which a receiver's response depends
on selected inputs overlapping within a time window while the receiver is in a particular state.
The receiver may be a receptor complex, dendritic compartment, neuron, or circuit. The consequence
may be a local calcium signal, dendritic event, spike, burst, inhibition, plasticity change, or no
measurable output.

It does **not** follow that every neuron uses one universal coincidence window, that a neuron fires
only when several inputs arrive together, or that every accepted coincidence becomes a conscious
percept. This page explains the physiological bridge. [[coincidence-as-a-bit|Coincidence as a Bit]]
explains SAN's separate proposal that learned, consequential coincidence events can function as
receiver-relative information units.

## One term, several mechanisms

The phrase *coincidence detection* is useful only when the receiver, inputs, window, state, and
consequence are named.

| Scale | Example receiver and input | Possible consequence | What the example does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synapse or spine | Glutamate-bound NMDA receptors plus sufficient local depolarization | Calcium entry and a local biochemical signal | One receptor rule for every synapse or automatic long-term potentiation |
| Dendritic compartment | Basal or proximal drive interacting with distal apical input and a backpropagating action potential in a declared pyramidal-cell class | Dendritic calcium event and, in some conditions, burst output | A universal cortical algorithm or an apical dendrite acting as an axonal terminal |
| Soma and local circuit | Excitation arriving before feed-forward inhibition in a named pathway | A narrowed somatic integration window and selective firing | The same window in every compartment, brain region, or behavioral state |
| Population | Partially overlapping events distributed across many cells | A decodable ensemble state, routing change, or coordinated downstream drive | Tomographic reconstruction, binding, or consciousness without additional evidence |

This diversity matters. A coincidence can be detected locally without producing a somatic action
potential, and a neuron can spike from a strong or intrinsically generated event without first
solving a many-input coincidence problem. Action potentials are commonly initiated near the axon
initial segment; dendrites receive and transform inputs, while the axon and its terminals carry the
cell's principal output.

## Established physiological examples

### NMDA-receptor-dependent spine signals

At many glutamatergic synapses, NMDA-receptor current depends on transmitter binding, a co-agonist,
and enough local depolarization to relieve voltage-dependent magnesium block. Yuste and colleagues
found heterogeneous calcium signals across rat hippocampal spines and enhanced NMDA-receptor-
dependent calcium entry during associated pre- and postsynaptic activation. The result supports one
important coincidence-sensitive mechanism; it does not make every spine equivalent or every local
calcium event a stored bit.

### Dendritic coupling and burst output

Larkum, Zhu, and Sakmann demonstrated nonlinear coupling between separated input zones in particular
layer-5 pyramidal neurons. A backpropagating action potential arriving near distal dendritic input
could facilitate a dendritic calcium action potential and alter axonal burst output. Later work has
shown that morphology, channel distribution, inhibition, cell class, and preparation change this
operation. The medically accurate statement is therefore cell-specific evidence/context
integration, not a generic basal-sensory versus apical-prediction law for every pyramidal neuron.

### Inhibition shapes the window

Pouille and Scanziani found that delayed feed-forward inhibition in rat hippocampal CA1 could narrow
the somatic coincidence window to less than about two milliseconds, while dendritic integration
remained broader. This is a concrete example of inhibition controlling which excitation reaches
spike output. It is not a brain-wide constant and does not imply that all competition is
winner-take-all.

### Timing-dependent plasticity

Relative pre- and postsynaptic timing can bias synaptic strengthening or weakening in specific
preparations, but spike-timing-dependent plasticity rules vary by synapse, cell type, neuromodulatory
state, input history, and protocol. Coincidence detection can therefore participate in learning
without being identical to LTP, LTD, or Hebbian learning.

## Source-faithful SAN development

SAN does not claim to have discovered coincidence detection. The recoverable genealogy is a
donor-and-extension sequence:

- **April 2017:** In the first Neural Lace monologue, Micah explicitly credited Peter Tse's
  *The Neural Basis of Free Will*, treated coincidence as a candidate basis of a neural bit, and
  connected patterns of consequential coincidences to learned criteria and state change.
- **April 22 and November 2, 2017:** Later Neural Lace and NerveGear discussions clarified that the
  event concerns receiver-dependent timing and networks of coincidence patterns, not one isolated
  action potential.
- **June 2022:** *a0215z* described cycles of neural coincidence patterns connected to prediction,
  decision, and coordinated movement.
- **September-November 2022:** the layer-5 and whisker notes explored how dendritic integration,
  inhibition, multilevel sensory arrival, and population structure might supply partial constraints
  for NAPOT reconstruction.
- **June 2025:** *13san.md* called coincidence detection an "engine." That useful summary is a later
  synthesis and must not be backdated into the 2017 or 2022 sources.

One 2022 draft compressed apical dendrite and output-terminal language in a medically confusing way.
The surrounding argument supports the recoverable operation: distal and proximal evidence interacts
with the cell's learned state, changes axonal output, and alters downstream receivers. The apical
dendrite is not an output terminal, and the corrected Wiki should preserve the intended mechanism
without preserving that anatomical conflation.

## The SAN interpretation

SAN asks whether a consequential coincidence can be modeled as a receiver-relative criterion event:

**selected inputs + learned receiver state + bounded timing -> typed local consequence**

The ongoing receiver state supplies [[tonic-phasic-canvas|tonic context]]. A consequential departure
is phasic relative to that context. It becomes a candidate [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave
differential]] only when the source, reference state, spatial route, timing, typed change, receiver,
and downstream consequence are specified. Coincidence alone does not prove phase locking, PWD,
tomography, or neural rendering.

At the network level, SAN proposes that many such events can constrain one another:

1. distributed receivers detect different partial input relations;
2. their outputs alter named downstream cells and populations;
3. recurrent activity redistributes the changed state;
4. the next cycle is conditioned by the updated network and body state; and
5. learned plasticity changes which later overlaps become consequential.

The observer is not a hidden viewer reading these events. The participating receivers,
transmitters, recurrent pathways, inhibitory boundaries, body feedback, and actions collectively
form the observer-action process.

## Scale ladder and bridge burden

- **Micro:** a receptor complex, spine, branch, soma, axon initial segment, or other declared
  compartment responds to temporally and spatially structured input.
- **Meso:** multiple cellular events alter a local circuit, oscillating group, cortical column,
  nucleus, or downstream population.
- **Macro:** distributed activity changes a body-world model, report, choice, or action through
  named sensory, recurrent, and motor pathways.

The abstract receive-test-update motif may recur across scales, but the receiver and measured
consequence must change at every transition. One dendritic coincidence is not itself a reconstructed
scene, traveling wave, conscious percept, or motor command.

## Decisive tests

The distinctive SAN test is not whether coincidence detection exists. A stronger model must show
that receiver state, spatial input arrangement, timing, and typed consequences predict later
routing, reconstruction, or action better than firing rate, spike count, phase, or timing alone.

For the NAPOT bridge, an experiment should:

1. identify distinguishable criterion events in named cells or compartments;
2. measure their effects on named downstream populations;
3. show that their joint pattern improves held-out reconstruction or behavior;
4. selectively disrupt the proposed events without broadly silencing the circuit; and
5. restore the matched mechanism and rescue the predicted consequence.

A failure to outperform simpler models, a failure of selective perturbation, or successful
reconstruction after removing the proposed criterion events would count against the stronger SAN
interpretation.
