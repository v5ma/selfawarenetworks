## Position

In the SAN/NAPOT reading, perception is reconstruction. Light, sound, pressure, smell, and proprioception each enter the [[thalamocortical-network]] as parallel streams of oscillation, and the brain's job is to combine those streams — through [[oscillatory-synchrony-multimodal-integration|phase-locked synchrony]], [[multimodal-integration|multimodal integration]], and the [[fourier-transform-in-consciousness|Fourier-style decomposition and recomposition]] of [[brainwave|brainwaves]] — into a single coherent volumetric model. That model is the *tomographic map of reality*. The framework treats the map as continuous with classical tomography in two senses. First, the data are projections: each [[cortical-column]] sees only a slice of the world, and the [[thalamic-convergence-zone|thalamic hub]] composites the slices into a whole. Second, the reconstruction is wave-based: phase relationships among [[gamma-oscillation|gamma-band]] events carry the geometric information, and [[coincidence-detection|coincidence detection]] across modalities — the [[coincidence-as-a-bit|coincidence-as-a-bit]] arithmetic — registers what aligns. The map is what gets *displayed* on the [[thalamus-as-display-screen|display-screen]] reading of the thalamus, and what is *experienced* as the [[neural-rendering-of-qualia|rendering of qualia]].
