# Phase Signal

**Phase Signal** is historical SAN terminology for a contextualized neural output event. The
action potential remains a real carrier event. Candidate information can depend jointly on count,
rate, precise timing, phase relative to ongoing activity, waveform, release, pathway, receiver
state, and downstream consequence. Phase is therefore not defined here as replacing rate.

```text
contextualized source event
-> transformed axonal and synaptic transmission
```

Established phase-of-firing, spike-timing, ongoing-state, and waveform-dependent-release results
support parts of this operation. They do not show that phase is the only neural code, that phase
is semantic by itself, or that PWD, NAPOT, qualia, or consciousness follows. The governed account

## Position in SAN

The governed phase-signal model keeps four levels separate:

1. **Physical event:** an action potential or burst occurs.
2. **Transformed transmission:** axonal, terminal, waveform, release, and pathway variables shape
   what reaches the receiver.
3. **Receiver-relative effect:** membrane, dendritic, synaptic, inhibitory, and learned state
   shape what difference the event makes.
4. **Population hypothesis:** recurrent contextualized events may contribute to larger
   representations, predictions, actions, Neural Rendering, and conscious experience.

The first three levels can be tested without assuming the fourth. A phase relation becomes
information-bearing only under a declared measurement and receiver test; it does not carry
semantic content merely because a phase can be computed.

## Post-SAN

External research does not require a choice between rate and phase. Phase-of-firing experiments
show that timing relative to ongoing activity can add information beyond spike counts, while
concurrent-code work shows that rate, temporal pattern, population pattern, and phase can carry
complementary information. Presynaptic-state and waveform studies add further variables between
source event and receiver effect.

SAN's extension is consequently not "phase is the message and the spike is only delivery." It is
the testable proposal that a typed, receiver-relative model of the whole transformation predicts
downstream state and behavior better than matched count-, rate-, timing-, or phase-only models.
See [[contextualized-spike-receiver-relative-transformation]] and
[[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential]].
