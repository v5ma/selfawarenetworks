Scale invariant causation is the SAN principle that patterns at any scale can cause effects on patterns at any other scale, enabling both bottom-up and top-down information flow across the neural hierarchy without privileging any particular level.

## Core Claim

In a [[fractal-isomorphic-sensory-arrays|fractal neural hierarchy]], causation is not one-directional. A molecular-scale synaptic event can ripple upward to reshape whole-brain oscillatory states (bottom-up); equally, a large-scale default-network phase shift can modulate the firing threshold of individual synapses (top-down). Neither scale is foundational — each is a valid causal entry point.

## Relation to NAPOT

In [[napot]], [[single-neuron-phase-cascade|phase cascades]] respect no single level of description. A phase pattern at the cortical-column scale reshapes dendritic computations within individual cells; simultaneously a [[dendritic-computation|dendritic computation]] biases which column-level pattern emerges next. This bidirectional causation is what makes the tomographic rendering of experience possible: any level of the hierarchy can write to any other.

## Contrast with Strict Emergence

Strict bottom-up emergence would say: micro-level events produce macro-level experience but not the reverse. Scale invariant causation denies this asymmetry — experience and belief (macro patterns) exert causal power downward on synaptic weights and firing rates.

## Multi-State Metastability Connection

Because causation flows both ways across scales, the brain can occupy multiple stable [[attractor]] states simultaneously at different levels. This is proposed as the basis of [[multi-state metastability]] — the brain's capacity to transition fluidly between cognitive states without any single level locking in a global state.
