# Multi-Timescale Reference Frames

> **Evidence boundary:** Neural systems integrate information over many timescales, and oscillatory bands can be associated with different circuit states. A frequency band does not by itself determine a prediction horizon; observing sub-delta activity alone does not establish that years, centuries, or cultural trajectories are encoded by that activity.

A reference frame must be maintained long enough for a receiver to compare new input with prior state. Different biological processes provide different forms of persistence: membrane and synaptic time constants, recurrent activity, short-term plasticity, neuromodulation, sequence replay, consolidation, and structural plasticity.

## Timescale is not frequency

Frequency and integration window are related only through a specified mechanism. Frequency determines cycle period, `T = 1/f`. It does not, without a model of storage and readout, determine integration time, representational persistence, prediction horizon, or behavioral duration. A 40 Hz signal has a 25 ms cycle, but a circuit using that signal can participate in behavior lasting much longer. Conversely, a slow fluctuation can reflect arousal, sleep, respiration, movement, vascular effects, or task structure rather than a long-range prediction.

SAN's source-faithful proposal is broader than a band-to-horizon lookup table. Nested oscillatory and non-oscillatory states may participate in learned models whose consequences span neural, behavioral, social, and cultural timescales. Long-horizon information could be distributed across recurrent activity, synaptic and structural plasticity, body-world interaction, social transmission, and external records. Whether a specified slow neural variable contributes unique predictive information about such a model remains open and testable.

| Scale | Example mechanisms | Typical question |
|---|---|---|
| Milliseconds | synaptic currents, spikes, conduction delays | Did inputs arrive together? |
| Tens to hundreds of milliseconds | membrane integration, bursts, recurrent transients | Which local pattern is selected? |
| Seconds | working-memory dynamics, action and sensory loops | What state is being maintained or updated? |
| Minutes to hours | neuromodulation, replay, synaptic plasticity | What recent experience changes future processing? |
| Days and longer | consolidation and structural change | What learned model persists? |

These are overlapping ranges, not a one-band-per-horizon codebook.

## SAN interpretation

SAN's tonic canvas and phasic ink distinction is receiver-relative. "Tonic" means an ongoing state against which a particular receiver can register change; it does not mean one permanently slow oscillation. "Phasic" means a transient update relative to that state; it is not synonymous with gamma.

At micro scale, a cell's membrane, dendritic, synaptic, and biochemical state supplies local history. At meso scale, recurrent populations can stabilize or transform activity. At macro scale, loops among sensory, cortical, thalamic, hippocampal, cerebellar, basal-ganglia, and motor systems can maintain a body-world model across perception and action.

NDCA proposes continuity across these updates. To become a scientific mechanism, it must specify the state variables, transformation, timescale, receiver, error measure, and behavioral consequence.

## Testable predictions

A useful multi-timescale model should predict when a representation survives distraction, remaps after context change, generalizes across movement, and fails after selective perturbation. Competing models should include firing-rate recurrence, synaptic plasticity, oscillatory phase, and non-neural confounds. Claims about long-term prediction require longitudinal behavioral and neural evidence rather than extrapolation from a slow frequency label.
