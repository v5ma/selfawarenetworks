# Acausal Patterns: Context-Bearing Representations Beyond the Present Input

![Acausal Pattern contextual prediction map](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/acausal-pattern/acausal-pattern-contextual-prediction-map-20260629-v2.png)

*In this historical SAN usage, an "acausal pattern" is a context-bearing representation used beyond one immediate sensory sample. The figure is a conceptual schematic, not an anatomical pathway; the term does not mean causeless, retrocausal, or exempt from physical causation.*

The source defines an acausal pattern as a notion that extends beyond one place and time and predicts causes by using other concepts. It also explicitly cites an earlier Facebook note, "Causal and acausal patterns in the brain," by Juan Carlos Kuri Pinto. That linked note has not yet been independently recovered, so the term's cited precursor and Micah's joined 2013 argument must remain distinct.

Micah's 2013 extension connects the term to five operations:

1. learned context can activate a representation without the represented object being present;
2. evidence changes the confidence or grade assigned to a belief or prediction;
3. coordinated smaller patterns can participate in a larger compositional concept;
4. active patterns compete for limited processing resources;
5. retrieved memories are reconstructed and can change.

The later SAN vocabulary of NAPOT, phase-wave differentials, and tonic/phasic rendering is not present in this 2013 source and must not be backdated into it.

## Medically grounded interpretation

### Established research

Human concept representations can be both relatively invariant and context-sensitive. Medial-temporal-lobe neurons have responded across different images and even written names associated with the same person or place, while whole-cortex imaging shows distributed semantic maps rather than one localized concept store. Context changes which parts of a concept are emphasized across control and representational networks. These findings support representations that can generalize beyond a single sensory sample, but they do not establish a literal "acausal pattern" cell or a complete belief in one neuron.

Neural representations can also be compositional. Human imaging studies distinguish contributions from prefrontal, temporal, and hippocampal regions when concepts and relations are combined into a structured event. This is a defensible modern parallel to the source's proposal that a quantity modifier and an object representation can participate in one larger concept. It does not establish simple addition of two fixed neuronal armies.

Rapid cross-modal plasticity supplies a bounded version of the blindfold example in the source. After five days of blindfolding and tactile training, normally sighted participants showed tactile recruitment of occipital cortex that was no longer present 24 hours after blindfold removal. The result supports rapid unmasking or reweighting of existing connections; it does not show that occipital cortex is an unrestricted general-purpose processor or that resource competition alone explains consciousness.

Reactivated episodic memories can incorporate later information under bounded experimental conditions. This supports the source's claim that memory is reconstructive and updateable, but not the universal claim that every retrieval necessarily rewrites every memory.

Finally, phase relations can change the effective interaction between neuronal groups, and decision-related neurons can encode graded confidence. Those observations provide measurable comparators for the source's coordination and evidence-grading ideas. They do not show that a belief is identical to synchrony or that confidence is one global neural scalar.

### Source-faithful SAN interpretation

The recoverable SAN operation is:

**current input plus retrieved context -> candidate representation or prediction -> evidence-dependent confidence update -> competition with other active patterns -> changed perception, memory, or action**

At the cellular scale, individual neurons transform inputs according to their current state and connectivity; no generic neuron contains the complete belief. At the circuit scale, coordinated and competing populations can maintain, combine, or suppress candidate representations. At the whole-network scale, distributed semantic, memory, control, sensory, and action systems jointly determine what is represented and acted upon. No stage requires a localized inner viewer.

The 2013 source proposes that beliefs, theories, and facts may use overlapping neural representational machinery while differing in their empirically assigned confidence. That is a SAN philosophical and neural hypothesis, not an established neuroscientific definition of belief, theory, or fact.

### Stronger open hypotheses

The source makes two stronger claims that remain open:

- conscious content consists of dominant, relatively non-compressed patterns winning a resource competition or attractor process;
- coordinated subpatterns form the physical realization of complete beliefs and concepts.

Modern evidence for distributed representation, attractor-like dynamics, phase-dependent interaction, and confidence coding makes these claims testable, but does not yet prove them.

## Discriminating tests

1. Compare models based on immediate sensory input alone with models that add retrieved context. The relevant signal should predict the represented concept, confidence, and behavior when the object is absent or ambiguous.
2. Perturb context-retrieval or semantic-control regions while holding the immediate stimulus constant. The manipulation should selectively alter the context-dependent representation.
3. Perturb inter-population phase relations while preserving mean firing rate as closely as possible. A strong synchrony-identity account predicts a specific loss or transformation of the represented relation beyond ordinary changes in communication efficacy.
4. Test whether resource competition and representational detail predict conscious report better than attention, task demand, arousal, working memory, and recurrent population state alone.

## Scientific boundaries

- "Acausal" is historical SAN terminology, not a standard medical or neuroscience category.
- A representation used away from its original stimulus still has ordinary physical causes.
- Not every cortical representation is necessarily a prediction.
- Synchrony can influence communication without being identical to a belief.
- Abstract or invariant coding does not imply a grandmother cell, a completed image inside the brain, or an inner observer.
- Evidence grades confidence in a proposition; it does not turn beliefs, theories, and facts into interchangeable epistemic categories.
