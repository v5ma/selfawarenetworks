# Dendritic Spine Temporal Memory

Dendritic spines participate in plasticity across multiple timescales. They do not contain an independent clock or a complete temporal memory hierarchy. Their contribution depends on the synapse, dendritic compartment, neuron, circuit, and behavioral context.

## A corrected timescale account

| Approximate scale | Example process | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Milliseconds | Glutamate-receptor currents, voltage-dependent NMDA-receptor gating, and postsynaptic calcium entry | Coincidence sensitivity is pathway- and state-dependent, not a universal neural bit |
| Seconds | Calcium-dependent signaling and delayed biochemical events that can influence plasticity induction | Presynaptic synaptotagmin-mediated vesicle fusion occurs in the axon terminal, not in the postsynaptic spine |
| Minutes to hours | AMPA-receptor trafficking, kinase signaling, local translation, actin remodeling, and spine-volume change | Functional and structural changes are related but are not interchangeable |
| Hours to longer intervals | Stabilization or loss of selected synapses and learning-related circuit reorganization | Persistence varies by region and task; one spine does not equal one memory |

Protein synthesis contributes to some late forms of LTP and memory, but dependence varies with induction protocol, pathway, and time point. LTP is an experimental family of persistent synaptic changes, not a label for every hours-to-days spine event.

## Geometry and timing

Cable properties, active dendritic conductances, branch location, inhibition, and synaptic timing jointly shape dendritic integration. A distal input does not simply arrive later because its spine is farther from the soma, nor does spine position by itself encode temporal order. Dendrites can nevertheless learn temporal selectivity because plasticity changes pathway-specific synapses and local nonlinear interactions.

## SAN interpretation

SAN proposes that spine and dendritic state contribute to a neuron's changed readiness. This can influence which phasic inputs become consequential against ongoing tonic activity. The proposal is compatible with established dendritic computation, but the claim that tonic activity is a shared canvas and phasic departures are receiver-relative “ink” remains a SAN hypothesis requiring direct multiscale tests.

A dendritic spike can alter the probability and timing of somatic output. Calling that event a prediction is a functional interpretation, not a settled identity between dendritic spikes and predictions.
