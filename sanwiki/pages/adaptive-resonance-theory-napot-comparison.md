**[[adaptive-resonance-theory|Adaptive Resonance Theory]] (ART)** is a neuroscience/AI framework developed by Steve Grossberg positing that perception arises from interaction between top-down expectation templates and bottom-up sensory signals, governed by a *vigilance parameter* that sets the threshold for category membership. See [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] for the SAN framework being compared here.

![ART category matching alongside SAN receiver-relative transformation, with dendritic input, axonal output and a separate synapse](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/adaptive-resonance-theory-san-comparison/art-san-comparison-reviewed-20260926-v2.png)

The comparison concerns how incoming activity interacts with learned state. ART's category-matching and vigilance logic and SAN/NAPOT's tonic-context, receiver-relative transformation account address related questions, but they are not identical models. ART also includes biological circuit models; the diagram's simplified category flow is not a claim that ART lacks neuroscience.

## ART Core Mechanism

In a simplified ART category-learning account, bottom-up input and learned top-down expectation interact. A sufficiently good match under the vigilance criterion supports resonance and learning; mismatch triggers reset and search. Vigilance controls how close a match is required, not a universal threshold on biological phase difference. ART was developed to address the **plasticity-stability problem**: retaining learned categories while learning new ones. Its variants and biological extensions have their own assumptions and mechanisms; see [Grossberg's circuit-model account](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2015.00501).

## Overlap with NAPOT

Both ART and [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] share the principle that the brain performs comparisons between internal (top-down) representations and incoming (bottom-up) sensory signals. Key shared intuitions:

- Top-down expectations actively shape perception.
- Bottom-up signals perturb, not simply overwrite, prior state.
- Lateral processing occurs at multiple scales.

## Where NAPOT Diverges

[[napot-overview|NAPOT]] emphasizes a proposed biological implementation through ongoing dynamics and downstream use:

- The baseline is the **[[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]]** — the brain's pre-existing multi-dimensional brainwave pattern, not an abstract prototype.
- Comparisons occur as [[phase-wave-differentials]] propagating through phase-matched, functionally connected regions (e.g., Theta oscillations in hippocampus).
- [[inceptive-field]] names the source's downstream influence, while [[receptive-field]] concerns the conditions to which a receiver responds. Both apical and basal dendrites receive and integrate input; ordinary neuronal output is conveyed through the axon and its boutons, not an apical exit terminal. The functional many-to-one-to-many relation remains the SAN point.

The source [[gh-a0412z|a0412z]] describes convergence toward a cell and divergence toward other cells. That sentence grounds the functional distinction; it does not identify an apical dendrite as a presynaptic output arbor. [Petreanu et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07709) mapped compartment-specific excitatory input to cortical dendrites. [Stuart et al. (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.1997.617ba.x) directly examined axonal action-potential initiation and propagation in rat neocortical pyramidal neurons. These support the anatomical route, not the complete NAPOT interpretation.

## Buzsáki Bridge

This Wiki's Buzsáki bridge highlights the idea that incoming events interact with an already active system. That is a useful comparison with SAN's tonic context, not an identity between an ongoing neural rhythm and an ART vigilance variable. It also does not imply that a nonblank initial state alone prevents forgetting. See [[buzsaki-brain-inside-out]].

## SAN Assessment

SAN's contribution here is the receiver-relative question: what change reaches which receiver, through which route, relative to what ongoing state, and with what consequence? [[phase-wave-differentials]] includes excitation, inhibition, timing and other typed departures; it is not merely a phase-angle mismatch. Matching, recurrence and downstream use remain substantive comparison points without declaring either framework a demonstrated substitute for the other.

**Figure scope:** The neuron and synapse are generic schematics; the traces are illustrations, not measurements. Apical/top-down and basal/feedforward labels give examples, not exclusive compartment assignments. The output footprint is a functional SAN term. Category-learning logic, anatomical observations and SAN's proposed rendering mechanism are different levels of explanation.
