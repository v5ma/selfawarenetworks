# Sensory Cortex Encoding

> **Scientific boundary:** Sensory encoding is modality-, circuit-, layer-, task-, state-, timescale-, and receiver-specific. Evidence for firing-rate, spike-timing, phase, synchrony, or population codes in a named experiment does not establish one universal cortical language or direct proof of SAN/NAPOT.

A sensory code is not a substance carried unchanged from receptor to thought. It is a measured relationship among a physical variable, neural activity, a receiving system, and a condition under which that activity changes what the organism can discriminate or do.

## Different senses begin differently

Vision, audition, touch, proprioception, vestibular sensation, taste, olfaction, and interoception transduce different physical or chemical variables through different receptors and pathways. Their signals are repeatedly transformed before and within cortex.

Even the phrase "primary sensory cortex" is a convenience rather than a universal first stage. Visual, auditory, and somatosensory pathways have different thalamic and cortical organizations, and olfaction does not follow the same initial thalamus-to-primary-cortex route. Primary cortical areas also receive recurrent, modulatory, motor-related, and feedback influences.

The cortex therefore does not simply translate every sense into one common packet and forward it to a central viewer.

## What can count as a neural code

Depending on the experiment and receiver, information may be recoverable from:

- firing rate;
- spike timing and interspike intervals;
- phase of firing relative to a measured field rhythm;
- synchrony or relative timing across cells;
- bursts and pauses;
- adaptation and gain;
- dendritic and synaptic state;
- spatial population patterns and trajectories;
- cross-area and cross-frequency relationships.

Recoverability is not enough by itself. A complete coding claim should identify:

1. the variable being represented;
2. the neural population and measurement;
3. the timescale and behavioral state;
4. the receiver or decoder;
5. the causal consequence of the putative code;
6. the conditions under which the relationship fails.

A statistical decoder can exploit correlations that the biological system does not use. Decoding accuracy therefore establishes recoverable structure, not automatically the brain's native format.

## Phase and frequency are bounded evidence

A macaque V1 study found that spike timing relative to low-frequency local-field-potential phase carried visual information beyond spike counts for the tested movies and preparation. A human MEG study found that tactile spatial attention enhanced stimulus-induced gamma activity in presumed somatosensory cortex while reducing lower-frequency activity elsewhere.

These findings matter. They show that phase and oscillatory state can contribute to neural information and task-dependent coordination. They do not show that:

- all sensory content is phase-coded;
- gamma always means sensory detail;
- alpha or beta always means top-down thought;
- one rhythm carries the same semantics across areas and species;
- a decoded phase variable is directly observed by an inner viewer.

Frequency bands describe ranges in a measurement. Their physiological generators and functional relationships vary with circuit, layer, task, state, species, and recording method.

## SAN/NAPOT interpretation

A medically bounded SAN interpretation is that sensory pathways repeatedly transform and re-express partial state. Each receiving cell or population interprets an incoming event relative to its own ongoing state.

In the tonic-canvas/phasic-ink metaphor:

- **tonic canvas** means the receiver's current state, including baseline activity, gain, adaptation, expectations, and recent history;
- **phasic ink** means a consequential change relative to that state;
- **rendering** means the distributed update and use of those changes, not a literal cortical screen;
- **observer-action** means the aggregate network and body whose state and behavior are changed.

This preserves SAN's relational insight without asserting a universal phase alphabet.

## Scale map

- **Micro:** receptor transduction, synaptic release, dendritic integration, and cellular spiking.
- **Meso:** local population dynamics, recurrent interactions, inhibition, gain, and interareal routing.
- **Macro:** multisensory, memory, body-state, and action systems combine partial evidence into a revisable working model.

A code demonstrated at one scale cannot be promoted to another scale without identifying the intervening transformation and receiver.

## Stronger SAN questions

Instead of asking whether a sensory feature "is" a frequency, ask:

- Which circuit changes with the feature?
- Which aspect of its activity carries additional information?
- Which downstream receiver is sensitive to that aspect?
- Does causal disruption impair the relevant discrimination or action?
- Is the result specific to a behavioral state or task?
- Does the SAN variable explain variance beyond a conventional population model?

These questions turn the phase-wave language into a testable research program.

## Failure conditions

A claimed SAN code should be rejected or narrowed when its putative receiver is unspecified, when a decoder succeeds only through information unavailable to biological circuits, when causal perturbation leaves the predicted behavior intact, or when the same result is fully explained without the proposed phase or field variable.

## Primary evidence anchors

- Montemurro and colleagues found additional visual information in phase of firing relative to low-frequency V1 field activity under a specific macaque recording condition: [Phase-of-firing coding of natural visual stimuli in primary visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2008.02.023).
- Bauer and colleagues measured attention-related gamma enhancement in human somatosensory cortex during a tactile task: [Tactile spatial attention enhances gamma-band activity in somatosensory cortex](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5228-04.2006).
- Bastos and colleagues measured distinct frequency relationships for feedforward and feedback influences in primate visual cortex: [Visual areas exert feedforward and feedback influences through distinct frequency channels](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.018).
- Zhou and colleagues demonstrated that local visual responses can carry border-ownership information dependent on wider context: [Coding of border ownership in monkey visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.20-17-06594.2000).
