Virtual Models Theory holds that consciousness is not the electrical activity of neurons itself, but rather the [[non-physical-information|non-physical information]] patterns that emerge from and move flexibly throughout neural tissue. Like a musical piece that can be played on multiple pianos in different locations, the same concept or percept can be instantiated across different neural substrates — the *pattern* is primary, not the specific hardware executing it.

This view contrasts with substrate-identity theories: what matters is the dynamic relational structure of the information, not which particular neurons carry it at any moment. The pattern is "virtual" in the sense that it floats above the physical medium, maintained by coordinated activity rather than by any fixed set of cells.

## Relationship to SAN Theory

In [[self-aware-network]] theory, virtual models are the unit of conscious content. A [[gh-a0206z|self]]-aware network]] maintains a running model of itself and its environment; this model is precisely a virtual information pattern sustained by [[neural-magnification]] and [[multi-scale-neural-processing]]. The model can migrate across cortical regions without losing identity, explaining perceptual stability and memory re-instantiation.

## Decoder Requirement

Virtual Models Theory requires both encoder and decoder processes for meaningful consciousness — a pattern with no mechanism to be read back out carries no experiential content. This distinguishes brains from rocks: neural architectures provide continuous dynamic decoding through [[napot]] oscillations, whereas passive physical substrates have no such read-out mechanism.

## Relationship to Dissipative Systems

Virtual models exhibit properties of [[dissipative-systems]] — they are [[gh-a0206z|self]]-organizing patterns far from thermodynamic equilibrium, continuously exchanging energy with their environment while maintaining dynamic structure. The whirlpool analogy applies: the form persists even as the material through it constantly changes.
