# Deep Learning Neural Networks

**Deep Learning Neural Networks** (DNNs) are, in the [[Self-Aware-Networks|Self-Aware Networks]] (SAN) corpus, the contemporary engineering analogue against which the project's biological account of [[cortex|cortical]] computation is repeatedly checked: many-layered [[artificial-intelligence|artificial-intelligence]] systems whose [[convolutional-neural-networks|convolutional neural-network]] and successor architectures the project treats as a partial software shadow of [[neural-array|neural arrays]] in real [[Cortical-columns|cortical columns]]. The corpus introduces them in podcast form — "Have you ever wanted to know what deep learning neural networks are? We go into detail about what deep learning neural networks do in this podcast and I help to explain how understanding artificial neural networks" maps onto biology ([[gh-a0006z|a0006z]]--md, line 24) — and then uses them throughout as the comparison object for [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]], [[Predictive-Coding]], and the [[memory-prediction-framework|memory-prediction framework]] of [[Jeff-Hawkins]] / [[Jeff Hawkins]].

The clearest equivalence the corpus draws is layered hierarchy: "The layer by layer accumulation of information in Artificial Deep Learning Neural Networks, or DNNs is a good indicator that neural arrays, or layers in the brain, are learning information that is play[ed back]" (a0115z--md, line 70). This grounds the SAN reading that the [[3d-neural-network-brain|3D neural-network brain]] is best modelled not as a flat sheet but as a stack of [[neural-arrays|neural arrays]] performing successive transformations — the same intuition the project pairs with [[3d-semantic-segmentation|3D semantic-segmentation]], [[apical-dendrite|apical dendrites]], [[Cortical-columns]], [[cortical-column]], and [[columnar-organization|columnar organisation]].
