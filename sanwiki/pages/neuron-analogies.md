A neuron has been compared to a telegraph, a clock, and a geiger counter — each metaphor capturing one aspect of how neurons detect and transmit signals (per Stephen Strogatz's *Sync* and Jon Lieff's *The Secret Language of Cells*).

The history of neuron analogies tracks the dominant technology of each era. Each analogy constrains what questions scientists ask: the telegraph model foregrounds point-to-point signaling; the clock model foregrounds rhythmic firing; the geiger counter foregrounds stochastic event detection.

## SAN critique and replacement

[[Self-Aware Networks]] theory proposes a richer dual metaphor: neurons are simultaneously **cameras** (detecting incoming [[phase-wave-differentials]]) and **LED pixels** (re-transmitting transformed phase patterns to downstream arrays). This replaces one-directional send/receive with bidirectional sensing-and-rendering — closer to what [[napot]] requires.

The camera-pixel model is not simply a better analogy for the same function; it reframes the neuron's role from signal relay to *pattern transducer* within a distributed oscillatory network.
