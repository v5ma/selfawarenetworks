Any physical oscillator — atom, molecule, hurricane — encodes a representation of its environment through structural changes driven by collisions, field interactions, and wave propagation across all scales. This encoding is real but not conscious.

The encoding mechanism is [[tensegrity]]: the physical forces and constraints holding oscillating clusters together make the structure sensitive to environmental perturbations. A hurricane encodes changes from its surroundings as alterations to its causal body in phase space — mimicking short-term memory without ever being memory in the SAN sense. The oscillator transmits state to other oscillators through heat, light, particle collisions, EM field lanes, sound, charge, and spin configurations.

What distinguishes this from [[self-aware-network]] operation is the absence of three things:

1. **Prior memory to compare against.** Without a reference pattern, structural change is not "learning" — there is nothing to detect novelty against.
2. **Specific tensegrity configuration.** The human brain has a particular temporal and spatial scale — tuned by evolution — that enables learned patterns to become expectations.
3. **Memories as attractors.** In a [[self-aware-network]], stored memories act as attractors that shape how new incoming patterns are recognized and absorbed. Simple oscillators lack this [[attractor]] topology.

The hurricane analogy: it reads its environment continuously but cannot be said to know it did so. Consciousness requires not just encoding but a network whose prior patterns bend the encoding of new ones — the [[napot]] dynamic.
