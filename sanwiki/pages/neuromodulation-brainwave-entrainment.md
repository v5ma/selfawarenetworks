Neuromodulation refers to techniques that externally alter [[neural-oscillations]] — ranging from transcranial stimulation (TMS, tDCS, tACS) to music-based [[brainwave-entrainment|brainwave entrainment]] — offering a window into how oscillatory patterns underpin cognition and consciousness.

## Core Modalities

**Transcranial stimulation** methods apply electromagnetic or current fields to the scalp. Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) induces brief neural firing; transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) shifts resting membrane potentials; transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) explicitly entrains oscillatory frequency, making it most directly relevant to [[napot]]-style resonance theory.

**[[brainwave-entrainment|Brainwave entrainment]]** through rhythmic auditory or visual stimulation synchronizes cortical oscillations to an external frequency — a form of imposed [[phase-attractor]] that temporarily locks endogenous rhythms to a stimulus cadence.

## Measurement Context

Non-invasive brain imaging (EEG, fNIRS, laser Doppler, MEG) provides the observational layer. These tools let researchers correlate stimulation protocols with shifts in [[neural-oscillations]], validating or falsifying models about which frequencies carry which cognitive load.

## Glymphatic Connection

During slow-wave and theta oscillatory states, the glymphatic system clears metabolic waste — including amyloid-beta and tau proteins implicated in neurodegeneration. This ties oscillatory health (the domain of SAN theory) to physical brain maintenance: [[theta-oscillations-background-scripts]] may serve a restorative function beyond cognition.

## SAN Relevance

From a [[self-aware-network]] perspective, neuromodulation is an external perturbation of a [[gh-a0206z|self]]-organizing oscillatory system. Its effects reveal the robustness (or fragility) of endogenous phase-locking and the degree to which the network's [[background-awareness]] states are entrained vs. autonomous.
