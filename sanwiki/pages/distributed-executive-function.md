## Executive function is a family of operations

Executive function is not one anatomical object. The term groups partly separable operations such
as maintaining a task rule, using [[working-memory|working memory]], suppressing or delaying a
response, switching between rules, accumulating evidence, monitoring conflict or error, planning,
and selecting an action.

The prefrontal cortex is important to many of these operations, but it does not work alone. Its
contribution depends on task-specific interactions with parietal, cingulate, insular, sensory,
motor, thalamic, striatal, limbic, cerebellar, brainstem, autonomic, and bodily systems. The relevant
network and causal direction can change during preparation, evidence sampling, commitment,
execution, and feedback.

This is why distributed executive function is more precise than either of two extremes:

- **One central commander:** all choice is assigned to one prefrontal site.
- **Every component decides:** every receptor, neuron, or oscillating group is assigned the organism's complete goal and agency.

Distributed control instead means that named components make different, bounded contributions to
the state transition that eventually changes the organism and its environment.

## What current neuroscience establishes

### Prefrontal context and flexible control

Prefrontal systems can maintain and apply task-relevant context through interactions with other
brain systems. Miller and Cohen's integrative account proposed that prefrontal activity biases
processing elsewhere according to goals and task demands
([Miller and Cohen, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.neuro.24.1.167)). This is a network-control
account, not evidence for a little observer in prefrontal cortex.

Human network studies distinguish partly separable control systems and show task-dependent changes
in connectivity. A dual-network framework separated adaptive, rapidly changing control from more
stable task-set maintenance ([Dosenbach et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2008.01.001)).
Frontoparietal connectivity patterns also reconfigure across tasks, supporting the idea of flexible
hubs rather than one fixed command path
([Cole et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3470)). These measurements support distributed and
dynamic control, although functional connectivity alone does not establish a causal route.

### Thalamic and basal-ganglia contributions

The thalamus is not merely a passive relay. In mice, mediodorsal thalamic input amplified local
prefrontal connectivity and sustained rule-specific representations during attentional control
([Schmitt et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22073)). Other experiments showed thalamic
regulation of switching between cortical representations
([Rikhye et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0269-z)). These are bounded circuit
findings, not proof that the [[thalamus]] is a unitary chooser.

Basal-ganglia pathways participate dynamically in action selection rather than acting as a single
binary gate. Direct and indirect pathways can interact during selection
([Li and Jin, 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.87644)), while competing neural choice
representations can shape evidence accumulation
([Bond et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.85223)). A complete account must therefore include
both cortical representation and subcortical selection, as well as the downstream machinery that
actually produces movement or autonomic change.

### Deep cortical layers and higher-order thalamocortical systems

Deep-layer corticothalamic and corticofugal populations are diverse. One recent experimental line
found that layer 6b can influence brain state through apical dendrites and higher-order
thalamocortical circuitry
([Zolnik et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.11.021)). A later article develops a
specific layer 6b theory of attention
([Zolnik et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.11.024)). This supports taking deep
layers and [[thalamocortical-network|thalamocortical networks]] seriously. It does not establish
that all layer 6 neurons, all cortical areas, or one delta-frequency process perform every executive
operation.

## Recovering the 2022 SAN proposal

The 2022 sources contain compressed anatomy, speech-transcription errors, analogies, and open
questions. Source-faithful review asks what operation those passages were trying to express before
classifying them as errors.

### Arrays as stateful receivers

On June 10, 2022, Micah described cells, arrays, circuits, cortical columns, and oscillating groups
as receiving and transforming patterns from other populations. The note explicitly says that its
sensory anatomy needs correction. Its recoverable operation is not that every region literally sees
the whole brain. It is that a downstream population encounters a transformed neural state as its
own input and can change what happens next.

This is the array-to-array ancestor of [[functional-connectivity|receiver-specific effective
influence]]: no array needs to know its ordinal layer, and no array contains the complete percept or
goal.

### The prefrontal and olfactory passage

The June 16 audio transcript contains obvious recognition errors, including "prefront of cortex,"
"olfactory bolt," "visor cortex," and "enthrina cortex." It also calls prefrontal cortex a primary
sensory area while referring to a 2020 paper in which olfactory information reaches prefrontal
regions.

A plausible intended paper is Wang et al. (2020), which measured odor identity in piriform cortex,
transient learned value in orbitofrontal cortex, and persistent learned value in medial prefrontal
cortex ([Wang et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.07.033)). This supports the
source's deeper point: prefrontal regions are stateful receivers and transformers of processed
sensory and learned-value information. It does **not** make all [[prefrontal-cortex|prefrontal
cortex]] primary olfactory cortex. The transcript does not name the paper, so the identification
remains a strong candidate rather than a proved source binding.

### Bottom-up criteria and top-down context

On June 27, 2022, Micah proposed that choice combines bottom-up thresholds in cells and bodies with
top-down learned expectations maintained in ongoing population activity. The source equated
receptor configuration too directly with LTP. The recoverable, testable formulation separates
receptor composition, synaptic efficacy, dendritic structure, intrinsic conductance, inhibition,
recent state, and longer-term plasticity.

On September 9, 2022, the source integrates an earlier corticothalamic-loop proposal with task
assembly recruitment, competition, inhibition, and frequency-sensitive coordination. The lasting
SAN idea is that learned state and current context can temporarily change which distributed route
becomes effective. A single resonance frequency or one canonical layer stack is a possible model
to test, not universal anatomy. See [[neural-tuning|Neural Tuning]].

## A scale-declared control model

For a bounded component `i`, let `x_i` be its current state, `u_i` its current input, `m_i` its
history-dependent parameters, and `b` the relevant body or global task context. A general
state-transition model is:

```text
x_i(t + Delta t) = F_i(x_i(t), u_i(t), m_i(t), b(t))          [1]
```

Equation [1] does not call the component conscious or autonomous. It only declares that the next
state depends conditionally on more than the immediate input.

For candidate route `k`, a phenomenological effective-drive variable can combine contributions from
several sources:

```text
q_k(t) = sum_i w_ki(t)y_i(t) - h_k(t) + c_k(t)               [2]
```

Here `y_i` is a source contribution, `w_ki` is its current effective weight, `h_k` collects named
inhibitory or opponent influence, and `c_k` collects task, memory, value, or body context. The terms
must be replaced by measured quantities in any real experiment.

An organism's action policy is then a constrained function of the candidate drives, not necessarily
a winner-take-all maximum:

```text
a_t = Pi({q_k(t)}, body_t, task_t, safety_t, history_t)       [3]
```

`argmax_k q_k` is one special competition model, not a universal law of neural choice. Several
routes can cooperate, alternate, remain unresolved, or be vetoed downstream.

The action changes the body and environment, which creates the next inputs:

```text
(body_(t+1), world_(t+1), u_(t+1))
    = E(a_t, body_t, world_t)                                 [4]
```

Equations [1]-[4] define a closed executive loop. A state is executive in the operational sense
only when it changes what information, rule, route, or action can causally affect a later state.

## The SAN extension

SAN joins five ideas that must remain separately testable:

1. **Local criteria:** cellular and circuit states constrain available transitions. See [[criterial-causation|Criterial Causation]].
2. **Learned context:** ongoing tonic state and memory alter response readiness.
3. **Dynamic coalition:** excitation, [[inhibitory-interneurons|inhibition]], disinhibition, thalamic regulation, basal-ganglia pathways, and network state temporarily change effective influence.
4. **Receiver-relative update:** a [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] can be one typed contributor when it predicts receiver state beyond rate, power, common input, movement, and prior state.
5. **Embodied return:** motor, autonomic, endocrine, and environmental consequences update the distributed system. See [[self-as-motor-sensory-rendering|Self as Motor-Sensory Rendering]].

The compact SAN chain is:

```text
local criteria and learned tonic context
-> several partially active candidate routes
-> sensory evidence, memory, value, inhibition, and thalamocortical regulation
-> temporary distributed coalition and changing effective drive
-> commitment, motor or autonomic execution
-> changed body and world
-> sensory and internal return
-> updated readiness, memory, and possible plasticity
```

SAN further proposes that receiver-relative timing and structured coherence-dispersion can
contribute to coalition formation. One possible increment is:

```text
q_k,SAN(t) = q_k(t) + beta_k * PWD_k(t)                       [5]
```

Equation [5] is useful only if `PWD_k` is operationally defined and improves held-out prediction or
causal control beyond the components already in Equation [2]. It does not make one frequency band a
command channel. The [[consideration-sandwich|Gamma Consideration Sandwich]] and
[[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|tonic-phasic]] vocabulary are SAN models to test against
named alternatives.

## Cooperation and competition coexist

Executive control cannot be reduced to the first neuron to fire. Local winner-take-all and
feedback-inhibition motifs exist, but a behaving organism also requires integration across sensory,
memory, valuation, motor, autonomic, and error-correction systems. Depending on the circuit and
task, relevant dynamics can include soft competition, divisive normalization, sequential sampling,
parallel preparation, mutual inhibition, disinhibition, recurrent stabilization, or cooperative
summation.

The distributed account therefore asks four separate questions:

1. Which population represents or transforms a candidate?
2. Which circuit changes the candidate's effective influence?
3. Which pathway commits the body to an action?
4. Which returned consequence updates the next state?

No answer alone identifies a localized observer or the complete cause of an action.

## Discriminating tests

1. Name the executive operation, circuit, species, task phase, receiver, and behavioral endpoint.
2. Compare localized-PFC, distributed-network, thalamic-control, basal-ganglia-selection, and embodied-feedback models using held-out data.
3. Separate representation, functional connectivity, causal influence, motor preparation, action, and returned consequence.
4. Perturb one proposed route and test a directional loss, while checking compensatory routes and arousal or movement confounds.
5. If PWD is added, compare against rate, power, phase alone, common input, prior state, and prediction-error variables; test selective rescue where feasible.
6. Test whether the same mechanism generalizes across tasks before describing it as an executive principle.
7. Reject a universal layer, band, or first-to-fire rule if control survives its selective removal or is better predicted by an alternative model.
