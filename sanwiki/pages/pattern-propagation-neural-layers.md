In SAN theory, the primary medium of neural communication is **coincidence patterns** — the specific temporal signature of co-firing neurons. The brain does not pass raw signals; it passes patterns of patterns. Each layer of the brain receives an incoming pattern and re-represents it, passing it forward.

## The propagation mechanism

1. A neuron fires when it detects a recognized coincidence pattern from its inputs.
2. Its firing is itself a pattern — it broadcasts to downstream neurons.
3. Each successive layer re-represents the pattern at a **different temporal stage**.

This gives the brain a built-in mechanism for tracking objects moving through space or time: a visual object moving left-to-right is represented by different spatial positions across successive brain layers, with each layer holding a slightly later temporal snapshot. The cascade of representations constitutes [[temporal-tracking]].

## Synaptic weight as protein structure

When a neuron fires, new [[protein-synthesis]] occurs at the activated synapses. Additional protein increases structural mass, which increases electromagnetic density at that synapse. Higher density = higher likelihood of the same pattern re-triggering future firing. Learning is thus literally the accumulation of protein — a physical embodiment of pattern frequency history.

## Neurotransmitter role

[[neurotransmitters]] (dopamine, GABA, [[acetylcholine]]) stored in [[vesicle-sacks]] at the axon terminal are released into the [[synaptic-cleft]] when a neuron fires. They bind to receptors on downstream neurons, shifting those neurons' phase and firing probability. This is the mechanism by which one layer's temporal pattern is handed to the next.

## Connection to NAPOT

[[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography) formalizes this: the brain is an array of neural arrays, each projecting its phase pattern forward. Pattern propagation is the propagation of phase-encoded information across the tomographic projection hierarchy.
