# SAN Golden Corpus Map

The SAN Golden Corpus Map is the control surface that connects every preserved source artifact to concepts, chronology, evidence status, comparisons, experiments, and downstream writing. Nothing in the SAN archive is classified as disposable. Priority determines where material is introduced and how much explanation it receives, not whether it survives.

## Why this map exists

The SAN archive is much larger than any one manuscript. It includes public talks, podcast transcripts, Git history, research notes, book plans, paper drafts, downloaded chats, diagrams, and later interpretations. A useful wiki must do more than return keyword matches. It must answer six questions quickly:

1. What idea is present?
2. Where is the earliest recoverable source?
3. How did the idea change?
4. Which parts are established neuroscience, SAN interpretation, or open conjecture?
5. Which other SAN ideas and external theories does it connect to?
6. Where should it be used in the book, a paper, an experiment, or a comparison?

It includes 573 a-series files, 205 b-series files, 13 SAN manuscript files, 11 Neural Lace Podcast files, 21 PDFs, and smaller named families. All 1,036 files are inventory indexed.

After excluding those 43 representations and the 134 exact reviewed anchor files, the intellectual-content review queue contains 859 files: 682 a/b/c theory-sequence files, 157 supporting text files, and 20 binary or reference files. Lexical candidates remain in that queue until a human semantic review accepts or rejects their routes. The front-spine queue is zero. Every artifact remains preserved.

## The four-layer graph

### 1. Source layer

Every source receives an exact path, hash, source family, available date fields, and text-readability status. An event date, claimed development date, first Git date, publication date, and recovery date are kept separate.

### 2. Concept and genealogy layer

Source passages are connected to named concepts, precursor formulations, later refinements, revision deltas, examples, and engineering implications. This is where the wiki records that a mature term may have older conceptual ancestors without silently backdating the term itself.

### 3. Adjudication layer

Each important statement is labeled by posture: established background, SAN synthesis, stronger SAN hypothesis, historical claim, comparison finding, or open conjecture. The Comparison Wiki owns external priority and similarity verdicts; the SAN Wiki preserves the internal concept and source genealogy.

### 4. Output layer

Accepted graph routes point into SAN Book 2, papers, evidence atlases, experiment designs, figures, and public explanations. An output consumes the graph; it does not become the sole surviving copy of the underlying knowledge.

## The no-surprise test

A newly recovered SAN artifact is integrated only after it has:

- an immutable source identity or hash;
- its available dates separated by type;
- at least one concept or question route;
- a relation to earlier and later formulations;
- an evidence posture and overclaim boundary;
- a downstream book, paper, comparison, or experiment route; and
- an explicit unresolved status when interpretation is still uncertain.

Passing this test does not mean every source has equal front-page prominence. It means no source becomes invisible merely because its filename, vocabulary, or date differs from the current manuscript.
