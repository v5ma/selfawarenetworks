# SAN Residual After Prior-Art Subtraction

**SAN Residual After Prior-Art Subtraction** identifies what remains scientifically distinctive after established neuroscience, earlier theories, and strong engineering alternatives receive full credit.

This is not an exercise in removing every familiar component from SAN until nothing remains. Scientific systems are often distinguished by a specific joined mechanism, set of constraints, or testable operator chain. The purpose of subtraction is to prevent SAN from claiming familiar components as inventions while making its proposed synthesis precise enough to test.

## Subtract Relations, Not Vocabulary

A term match is not enough. Two accounts may use different words for the same operation, or the same word for different operations. Compare bounded relations:

| Relation | Question |
|---|---|
| Input | What physical or computational state reaches the mechanism? |
| Transform | What operation changes, selects, integrates, or predicts that state? |
| Output | What measurable event or state follows? |
| Receiver | Which cell, circuit, model, or organism can use the output? |
| Scale | Is the claim cellular, local-circuit, systems-level, behavioral, or phenomenal? |
| Test | What result would discriminate this account from its strongest alternative? |

Surface divergence does not erase a shared causal chain. Conversely, a shared word such as *rendering*, *synchrony*, *prediction*, or *field* does not establish equivalence without a matching operation and endpoint.

## Foundations SAN Does Not Own

SAN should explicitly credit at least these established or earlier foundations:

- learned cell assemblies, recurrent completion, and attractor memory;
- hierarchical prediction and prediction-error models;
- active thalamocortical and corticothalamic routing;
- sparse, distributed, topographic, and semantic population representations;
- oscillatory phase coding, coherence, traveling waves, and synchronization;
- nonlinear dendritic integration, coincidence sensitivity, and compartment-dependent computation;
- inhibitory competition and context-dependent route selection;
- global-access, recurrent-processing, integration, and self-model theories of consciousness;
- endogenous electric fields and bounded ephaptic effects;
- causal perturbation and complexity measurements such as TMS-EEG; and
- multisensory causal inference, body models, and sensorimotor control.

Representative primary sources include [Hopfield's recurrent-memory model](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.79.8.2554), [Rao and Ballard's hierarchical predictive-coding model](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580), [Saalmann and colleagues' attention-dependent thalamic routing results](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1223082), [Huth and colleagues' semantic-map study](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17637), [Anastassiou and colleagues' ephaptic-coupling experiments](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2727), and [Massimini and colleagues' TMS-EEG perturbation study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1117256).

These foundations constrain SAN. They do not automatically validate or invalidate the joined SAN proposal.

### Scope of the representative evidence

- **Hopfield, 1982.** Establishes that a formal recurrent network can recover a stored pattern from a sufficient partial cue. **Boundary:** this does not establish that mammalian memory uses the exact Hopfield architecture.
- **Rao and Ballard, 1999.** Establishes that a hierarchical model can use feedback predictions and feedforward residual errors to account for selected visual response properties. **Boundary:** this does not establish that all cortical feedback carries prediction or that every mismatch is a SAN PWD.
- **Saalmann et al., 2012.** In a monkey visual-attention task, pulvinar activity and synchrony helped regulate transmission between interconnected cortical areas. **Boundary:** this does not establish one universal thalamic routing algorithm.
- **Huth et al., 2016.** Voxel-wise fMRI models revealed distributed semantic selectivity during natural-speech listening. **Boundary:** this does not establish single-neuron semantic maps or cellular mechanisms.
- **Anastassiou et al., 2011.** Weak extracellular fields changed membrane potential and entrained spikes in rat cortical slices under bounded conditions. **Boundary:** this does not establish a global electromagnetic field that performs perception or consciousness.
- **Massimini et al., 2005.** TMS-evoked cortical propagation differed between wakefulness and non-REM sleep. **Boundary:** TMS-EEG alone does not identify conscious content or determine consciousness in every condition.

## The Proposed SAN Residual

After subtraction, the residual is not simply "brains oscillate" or "brains predict." It is the proposed joined architecture in which:

1. living cells and populations maintain ongoing, receiver-relative tonic states;
2. differentiated phasic events alter that ongoing context as measurable updates rather than decorative "ink" alone;
3. learned cellular morphology and circuit organization constrain what each receiver detects and transmits;
4. many partial projections are recurrently gathered, transformed, and redistributed across micro, meso, and macro scales;
5. the distributed tonic field or canvas and its phasic changes are proposed to participate in the system's own perception, without a localized inner viewer;
6. memory, prediction, sensory registration, action selection, and reafferent consequences repeatedly modify the same distributed working state; and
7. artificial implementations must expose these operations as inspectable variables and outperform capacity-matched alternatives before receiving a SAN-specific engineering interpretation.

Each item must remain scale-labeled. A dendrite receiving synaptic input is not by itself a cortical tomographic reconstruction. A local oscillating assembly is not automatically a whole-brain observer. A distributed representation is not automatically conscious. The SAN claim concerns a proposed multiscale relation among these operations.

## What Would Make the Residual Real?

The residual earns scientific force only when its joined predictions outperform component theories and flexible baselines.

| Proposed SAN relation | Discriminating evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Tonic context changes interpretation of phasic input | Receiver-specific prediction beyond rate, power, waveform, delay, and recent-history controls |
| Recurrent projections perform NAPOT-like reconstruction | Held-out latent-state reconstruction plus projection-specific perturbation and rescue |
| Neural tuning changes effective routing | Measured tuning change predicts later route selection beyond ordinary plasticity and state-space baselines |
| Distributed field-cell activity contributes causally | Perturbation changes a declared perceptual or action variable after synaptic, firing-rate, and common-input controls |
| Joined architecture adds engineering value | Better calibration, robustness, adaptation, efficiency, or causal generalization under matched data, capacity, compute, and tuning budget |

Failure to beat those alternatives narrows the SAN residual. It does not erase Micah Blumberg's historical source genealogy, but genealogy and biological validity remain separate questions.

## Five Different Achievement Levels

Do not collapse these outcomes into one label:

1. **Historical priority:** a dated SAN source contains a bounded relation.
2. **Content convergence:** another work later expresses an equivalent relation.
3. **Component support:** an experiment supports one mechanism used by SAN.
4. **Joined-mechanism support:** evidence favors the coupled SAN operator over decomposed alternatives.
5. **Phenomenal interpretation:** evidence supports a claim about consciousness or subjective experience.

Progress at one level does not automatically establish the next.
