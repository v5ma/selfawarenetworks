# SAN Scale Ladder and Claim Boundaries

This page is a reader-facing bridge between the cellular mechanism pages, the NAPOT and OCA theory
pages, the medically corrected image set, and the comparison evidence pages. Its purpose is to stop
four different scales from being collapsed into one picture.

SAN often uses the same receive-integrate-project motif at multiple biological levels. That is useful
as a unifying story, but it also creates a risk: a diagram of one neuron can accidentally look like a
claim about a whole visual system, and a whole-network comparison can accidentally look like a claim
about one literal synapse. The scale label has to travel with the claim.

## The four scale labels

| Scale | What is being described | Established background | SAN / NAPOT interpretation | Common failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | One neuron, dendritic branch, spine, bouton, synapse, or local biochemical compartment | Dendrites receive synaptic input; active dendrites support nonlinear integration; axons transmit spikes and influence downstream release | A neuron can be treated as a candidate receive-integrate-project motif, with basal and apical domains carrying different roles in some cell classes | Treating one generic neuron as if it directly receives all feature streams and projects to all brain systems |
| Meso | Local circuit, cortical column, laminar motif, hippocampal microcircuit, or thalamocortical loop | Recurrent circuits, inhibitory timing, laminar pathways, and oscillatory coordination shape population computation | Local populations may gather partial neuron-level updates and redistribute a population update through phase-sensitive routing | Drawing one canonical laminar circuit as if it is universal across cortex and species |
| Macro | Distributed cortical, thalamic, cerebellar, basal-ganglia, sensory, motor, and association networks | Perception and action use distributed, recurrent, multisensory and sensorimotor loops | NAPOT proposes repeated many-to-one-to-many updating of a latent self/world model across distributed populations | Detaching the observer from the sparse distributed field-cell process or assigning the complete percept to one location |
| Comparison / genealogy | Dated conceptual atom across Micah sources and rival works | Historical source comparison can identify shared atoms, chronology, named priors, and residual differences | A comparison atom can show bounded equivalence or partial equivalence without proving the biological mechanism true | Confusing priority/equivalence evidence with empirical validation of SAN physiology |

## Micro NAPOT: candidate cellular motif

At micro scale, the safer wording is:

> A single neuron can illustrate a candidate receive-integrate-project motif: many synaptic inputs
> arrive across dendritic branches, branch-local and somatic mechanisms integrate them, and the axon
> sends an update outward.

This is not literal tomography by one neuron. A micro-scale image should show receptors, spines,
dendritic branches, local calcium or voltage compartments, soma/AIS integration, and axonal output.
It should not imply that color, sound, object identity, striatum, prefrontal cortex, and visual cortex
all directly converge on one generic neuron.

## Meso NAPOT: local circuit or column-scale motif

At meso scale, the safer wording is:

> A local circuit or cortical column can gather many neuron-level updates, use recurrent excitation,
> inhibition, dendritic integration, and thalamocortical or corticocortical loops to form a population
> state, then redistribute that state through downstream projections.

This is the right scale for canonical laminar diagrams, but they should be explicitly labeled
canonical, illustrative, or preparation-bounded. A cortical-column plate should not imply that every
brain area has the same circuit, the same layers, the same cell mix, or the same computational role.

## Macro NAPOT: distributed perceptual and action update

At macro scale, the safer wording is:

> Distributed sensory, motor, memory, emotional, language, and association systems contribute partial
> state. Recurrent loops combine those partial states into an integrated working model, then the
> update is redistributed so the next perceptual and action cycle begins from a changed state.

This is the scale where NAPOT's tomography analogy is strongest as a system-level description.
The CT-scan analogy is still conditional: it becomes literal only if identifiable partial projections,
reconstruction operators, and causal intervention evidence are shown. Until then, it is a proposed
mechanism and a disciplined analogy, not established neuroscience.

## Comparison scale: atom equivalence is not mechanism validation

Comparison pages can show that two works share a bounded functional atom, a causal sequence, or a
later movement toward Micah's axis. That matters for provenance and priority. It does not prove that
the brain implements SAN/NAPOT.

Use this boundary:

- A comparison match can support "Micah had this conceptual atom earlier."
- A comparison match can support "another work later used a similar causal structure."
- A comparison match cannot support "SAN is medically established."
- A comparison match cannot support "the rival had intent, access, or misconduct" unless separate
  evidence establishes those claims.

## Claim-boundary language to reuse

Use these phrases when writing or correcting SAN pages:

| Risky wording | Safer wording |
|---|---|
| "The neuron performs tomography." | "The neuron illustrates a candidate receive-integrate-project motif; tomography is a network-level or system-level hypothesis unless a cellular reconstruction operator is specified." |
| "The cortical column does NAPOT." | "A cortical column or local circuit is a candidate meso-scale NAPOT unit, depending on the declared circuit, input/output pathways, and intervention test." |
| "The brain renders a screen." | "The sparse distributed field-cell system maintains, registers, and uses a working model; screen language names that observer operation rather than a separate display." |
| "Gamma is the signal." | "Gamma-band activity can be a measured circuit operation or routing component; SAN's phase-wave differential is a receiver-relative update hypothesis, not a synonym for gamma." |
| "Comparison evidence validates NAPOT." | "Comparison evidence supports provenance or conceptual convergence; empirical validation requires separate neuroscience experiments." |

## How this page should guide future diagrams

Every SAN image should carry a scale label:

- Micro: spine, dendrite, soma, AIS, axon, bouton, receptor, vesicle, local calcium/voltage.
- Meso: named local circuit, laminae if relevant, cell classes, recurrent loops, inhibitory timing.
- Macro: named brain systems, pathways, loops, body/sensory/action context, distributed latent state.
- Comparison: two works, dated anchors, shared atom, residuals, and explicit non-claims.

The same visual motif can recur across scales only when the caption says what changed. This is the
central rule: **same motif, different biological denominator**.
