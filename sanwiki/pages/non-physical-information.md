Non-physical information is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s claim that the coincidence patterns neurons detect have no mass or energy yet constitute a real causal force in neural computation.

## The ontological claim

Standard physics holds that causation requires the exchange of mass or energy. Blumberg argues that a **tempo-spatial coincidence pattern** — e.g., neurons A, B, C firing within ~3 milliseconds of each other — is itself meaningful information: it carries no mass or energy, yet when it matches a neuron's [[criterial-causation|firing criteria]] it triggers a phasic burst and propagates downstream effects. The pattern's identity (what it *is*) drives causation, not only the physical substrate carrying it.

This is a top-down causal claim: higher-order pattern structure reaches back and selects which lower-order physical events proceed.

## Distinction from purely physical accounts

A thermostat responds to temperature via simple energy threshold — no pattern matching, no criteria selection. Neural systems, Blumberg argues, add a second layer: they encode and detect *information* (pattern) with criteria that can themselves be reset by incoming information. This is what separates goal-directed behavior from mechanical response.

## Role in SAN theory

Non-physical information provides the substrate for [[virtual-patterns-in-the-brain]] to carry causal weight without invoking dualism. The pattern is physically instantiated, but the *causal handle* is the form, not the matter.

- Explains why two physically identical brains diverge immediately when placed in different environments — current sensory coincidences (non-physical information) reconfigure criteria in real time.
- Grounds [[millisecond-based-identity]]: identity at any moment is the live set of active criteria, not stored engrams.
