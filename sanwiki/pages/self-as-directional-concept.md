The **[[gh-a0206z|self]] as directional concept** is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s minimal definition of selfhood: at the most primitive level, the [[gh-a0206z|self]] is not a place or a substance but a *direction in space* — the one consistent forward-facing observer point that does not change relative to the changing environment.

## Core claim

A brain exists for highly coordinated movement. The minimal [[gh-a0206z|self]] that such movement requires is a direction: "forward" (where *you* go) as distinct from left, right, backward, or rotating. This direction is the one invariant in a changing world — you change rooms but the directional [[gh-a0206z|self]] is always present in every room.

This avoids the hard-problem framing: rather than asking what produces phenomenal experience, [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]-style [[gh-a0206z|self]] theory asks what the *minimum structural requirement* for selfhood is, and locates it in spatial directionality rooted in locomotion.

## Nested causality context

The note situates the [[gh-a0206z|self]] within [[nested-causality]]: from proteins → cells → organs → whole organism → gravity → cosmic forces. Each level has genuine causal patterns not reducible to the one below. The [[gh-a0206z|self]] emerges at the organismal level as a directional node integrating multi-scale parallel computations.

## Properties of the directional self

As organisms become more complex the direction object acquires properties:
- Body-schema: arms, legs appended to the direction vector.
- Mirror recognition: the organism notices the match between its own direction and reflections or other moving beings.
- [[gh-a0206z|Self]]/non-[[gh-a0206z|self]] boundary: the [[gh-a0206z|self]] direction changes slowly (cellular level), while the environment changes fast — yielding a natural inside/outside distinction.
