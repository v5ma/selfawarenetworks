## Post-SAN

Mainstream cognitive [[neuroscience]] has, since Libet, treated volition as a phenomenon to be either explained away (epiphenomenalism) or recovered via downstream "veto" mechanisms; predictive-processing accounts (Friston-style active inference) recover it as the agent's own generative-model outputs. The SAN framing differs in two testable directions. First, it predicts that volitional reports should be *phase-locked* to the [[dominant-phase-wave-differential-rhythm|dominant phase-wave differential rhythm]] in motor and [[prefrontal-cortex|prefrontal cortex]] — a claim adjacent to but stronger than mainstream readiness-potential work, since SAN says the readiness signal is the visible edge of a [[criterial-tempo-spatial-waves|criterial tempo-spatial wave]] rather than a separate motor-prep code. Second, it predicts that disorders of volition (schizophrenic passivity phenomena, alien-hand, Parkinsonian akinesia) should map onto specific failures of [[oscillatory-computational-agency|oscillatory computational agency]] at named [[criteria|criterial]] thresholds — a stronger pathology-mapping claim than the symptom-level descriptions in current clinical literature. Where Libet-tradition work treats volition as a binary about whether free will "exists," and active-inference treats it as the agent's prior over its own [[actions]], SAN treats it as the *experience* of one [[memory-prediction-cycle|memory-prediction cycle]] reading another's outputs.
