# Tomography

**Tomography** is, in [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] usage, the umbrella term for the family of [[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography (NAPOT) hypotheses — the SAN-original claim that brain oscillations, both [[tonic-oscillation|tonic]] and phasic, project sensory data across [[neural-arrays|neural arrays]] so that conscious content is *reconstructed* from oscillatory cross-sections in the same way that medical tomography reconstructs a 3-D image from many 2-D projections. NAPOT is one of SAN's two flagship [[neuroscience]] claims, sitting at the centre of the [[napot-revision-3-anti-spike|NAPOT-revision]] line and adjacent to [[cellular-oscillating-tomography|cellular oscillating tomography]] and [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differentials]] within the broader SAN account.

## Position

Within [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks, *tomography* is the structural metaphor that organises the entire NAPOT family. The medical analogy is exact: tomographic imaging reconstructs a 3-D body from many 2-D slice projections, and NAPOT claims that conscious experience is reconstructed from many oscillatory projections of sensory data across the [[neural-array-projection|neural array]] of [[cortex]] — the [[cortical-traveling-waves|traveling-wave]] [[phase-pattern|phase pattern]] at any given moment is one such projection, the [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] is the difference between successive projections, and the unified percept is the back-projected reconstruction. SAN positions NAPOT as the parent of two specialisations: [[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]] (the C2 cycle in the SAN exposition) and [[phase-wave-differentials|Phase-Wave Variations Tomography]] (the C4 cycle), with both treated as "extensions of the NAPOT [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography|Neural Array Projection Oscillation Tomography]] hypothesis" (`04san--md`, line 87, [[gh-04san|on github]]). The framing also yokes NAPOT to a particular oscillatory commitment: "brain oscillations (both tonic and phasic) project sensory data across [[neural-arrays|neural arrays]]" (`03san--md`, line 28) — so [[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillations]] supply the carrier and phasic oscillations supply the modulation, with the [[brainwave-vortex-attractor|brainwave]] field as the integrated standing-wave the reconstruction is read off of. In SAN's accounting, NAPOT is "one of the core arguments of the SAN [[self-aware-networks-theory|Self Aware Networks Theory]] of Mind" (`04san--md`, line 87, [[gh-04san|on github]]) — making this page the entry point to the brain-side flagship claim.

## Post-SAN

Mainstream cognates run from the broader literature on [[cortical-traveling-waves|cortical traveling waves]] (Muller, Sejnowski et al.) and [[oscillatory-binding|oscillatory binding]]-by-synchrony (Singer, Engel, Fries) to Friston-style predictive-coding accounts. Where the traveling-wave literature documents oscillatory propagation across [[cortex]] without committing to a *reconstruction* claim, SAN's NAPOT framing makes the reconstruction explicit: the conscious percept is the back-projection of many oscillatory cross-sections through the cortical sheet, in the same algebraic shape as Radon-transform tomography. The testable difference is reconstructive: NAPOT predicts that a sufficient number of recorded oscillatory cross-sections (across distinct [[cortical-traveling-waves|traveling-wave]] phases) should reconstruct the corresponding sensory content — which is what licenses the SAN extension toward [[brain-computer-interface|BCI]]-class decoding — whereas pure traveling-wave or oscillatory-binding accounts make no such reconstruction claim. The [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave variations tomography]] specialisation extends the same framing to the differential between successive frames, predicting that motion content should be readable specifically off phase-wave differences rather than off raw amplitudes.
