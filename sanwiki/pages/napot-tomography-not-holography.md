# NAPOT: Tomography, Not a Literal Hologram

NAPOT uses **tomography** as a reconstruction hypothesis: multiple partial, differently situated,
and time-varying signals may jointly constrain a working model that no one input contains. It does
not propose that the brain projects a literal optical hologram or stores a complete miniature world
in one interference pattern.

## Why tomography is the preferred analogy

Tomography emphasizes a causal problem:

1. partial evidence is sampled from different receivers or routes;
2. transformations preserve some relations while losing others;
3. recurrent populations combine constraints;
4. a working estimate supports prediction or action;
5. new evidence updates the estimate.

The analogy becomes a biological mechanism only when the page identifies the projections, latent
variable, reconstruction operator, receiver, error model, and intervention test. Without those
elements, tomography remains a disciplined explanatory analogy.

## Why "not holography" needs a boundary

Holography and tomography are mathematical and engineering families with several meanings. Neural
population codes can be distributed without being literal holograms, and wave interference can be
measured without proving holographic storage. NAPOT's central contrast is therefore not that all
holographic models are impossible. It is that SAN intends a repeated partial-evidence reconstruction
and update cycle rather than an optical plate watched by an internal observer.

## Scale ladder

- **Micro:** one neuron participates through synaptic reception, nonlinear integration, axonal
  output, and downstream effects. This is a candidate motif, not tomography by label.
- **Meso:** a named local circuit or population may combine partial inputs and redistribute a
  population update. A reconstruction claim needs identifiable variables and held-out tests.
- **Macro:** distributed sensory, thalamic, association, memory, body, and action systems may maintain
  a revisable body-world model. This is where the tomography analogy is strongest.

No bright central point, neuron, column, or cortical area contains the completed percept. The same
distributed system that constructs the state is altered by it and produces its consequences.

## Five recovered 2022 routes

The tomography proposal should preserve five connected routes while keeping their evidence levels separate:

1. **Array-to-array transformed representations:** each receiving population transforms a projection according to its learned and current state. It does not receive a semantic copy.
2. **Cortical-column assumptions:** a column can be one candidate circuit context, but no universal repeated cortical-column algorithm is established.
3. **Object and location coding:** object, location, body, and action relations can be distributed across populations rather than contained in one neuron or one PWD.
4. **Apical/basal evidence-expectation comparison:** distinct dendritic domains make contextual comparison biologically plausible, but basal does not universally mean evidence and apical does not universally mean expectation.
5. **Distributed rendering:** the participating network performs and undergoes the reconstruction; no localized viewer receives a completed picture.

Two 2022 process chains connect these routes. Dynamic task-assembly selection proposes context or preparation -> frequency alignment -> inhibition of competing routes -> a selected burst, rendering, or action assembly. Oscillating-group completion proposes cue -> recruitment of a learned oscillating group -> pattern re-expression -> internal or motor output. Each arrow requires its own measurement and failure condition. See [[oscillating-group-completion|Oscillating-Group Completion and Re-expression]].

## Established component evidence

The following evidence makes portions of the reconstruction problem biologically concrete without confirming NAPOT:

- Context outside a classical receptive field altered later macaque V1 responses, supporting recurrent influence rather than a single feedforward pass ([Zipser, Lamme, and Schiller, 1996](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.16-22-07376.1996)).
- Recurrent or additional computation better explained delayed object solutions for images that challenged feedforward models ([Kar et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0392-5)).
- Eye position, head position, and anticipatory remapping alter spatially relevant population responses, demonstrating that body-world relations need not reside in one fixed coordinate frame ([Andersen, Essick, and Siegel, 1985](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.4048942); [Duhamel, Colby, and Goldberg, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1553535); [Brotchie et al., 1995](https://doi.org/10.1038/375232a0)).
- Visual and vestibular cues can be weighted according to reliability in a measured multisensory population and behavior ([Fetsch et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2983)).
- Changing direction-selective or face-selective neural populations can bias relevant perceptual judgments, establishing downstream causal use without locating an entire percept in the stimulated cells ([Salzman, Britten, and Newsome, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1038/346174a0); [Afraz, Kiani, and Esteky, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04982)).

These studies support recurrence, coordinate transformation, reliability-sensitive integration, and causal use. They do not show that the brain performs tomography, that oscillation is the unique reconstruction mechanism, or that a reconstructed state is conscious.

## Minimum evidence burden

A literal NAPOT tomography claim requires:

1. multiple partial projections with known or estimable transforms;
2. an identifiable target state or relation;
3. reconstruction performance on held-out conditions;
4. comparison with non-tomographic alternatives;
5. selective perturbation of one projection or transform;
6. a predicted degradation and, where possible, rescue;
7. separate conclusions for representation, action, report, and consciousness.

The decisive comparison must include a strong non-tomographic recurrent model. NAPOT gains support only when removing or corrupting a declared projection or transform produces a predicted reconstruction deficit that cannot be explained by generic state capacity, and restoring that projection or transform rescues the declared receiver or behavioral endpoint.
