# From Sensory Difference to Symbol: Learned Neural Grounding

## The problem in plain language

How can many different encounters become one reusable concept such as `apple`, and how can a word
refer to that concept without a hidden reader inside the brain?

A concept is useful when part of a learned pattern can recruit enough of the rest to support
recognition, prediction, communication, or action. The word, sight, smell, grasp, and expected taste
of an apple do not share one raw sensory format. Learning establishes transformations and
associations among them.

```text
world event
  -> modality-specific sensory changes
  -> learned distributed neural state
  -> recognition, prediction, label, or action
  -> returned consequence
  -> updated routes for later reinstatement
```

## The recovered SAN contribution

`a0225z.md`, internally dated November 6, 2012, argues that a concept is a learned and
reactivatable neural pattern rather than a mystical inner object. It also proposes that nonhuman
animals form concepts without human language and that words and colors become meaningful through
their learned relations rather than through different kinds of neural substance.

The historical single-neuron or single-dendrite wording is too narrow. The governed version is a
distributed, multiscale learned disposition. A spoken label is another route into and out of that
system, not the sole source of the concept.

`a0218z.md` adds multimodal feature "notes" within larger sequences. The later broccoli case makes
the read/write problem concrete: two brains can recognize the same object without identical neural
codes, while a BCI would have to reconstruct enough of the relevant learned relations to produce
recognition or experience rather than only evoke a label.

`a0411z.md`, internally dated October 15, 2012 and first Git-fixed in June 2022, asks whether a
semantic token can be a learned relation among above-threshold features, whether an edge relation
can recur across scales, and whether multisensory waves gain usable meaning through prediction and
movement. It also treats inherited circuit organization and plastic learning as complementary.
Other named speakers in the discussion remain separately attributed.

`a0419z.md`, internally dated May 16, 2014, adds the possibility that learned category distances
differ across people and contexts. The defensible modern version is a person- and task-dependent
representational geometry, not a literal directory tree inside the cortex.

`a0132z.md` adds a direct carrier-change example: Braille, visual language, sound, and imagined
heat-based Morse signals can preserve a learned order or meaning even though receptor and local
neural activity differ. [[crossmodal-pattern-invariance-reexpression|Crossmodal Pattern Invariance
and Re-Expression]] governs that proposal without asserting one identical code across the senses.

## Established science and SAN hypothesis

Established research supports distributed semantic memory, contributions from sensory, motor,
affective, linguistic, and heteromodal systems, and concept learning in nonhuman animals. It does
not establish one concept neuron or SAN's specific phase mechanism.

SAN adds the hypothesis that partial cues are repeatedly transformed through learned dendritic,
synaptic, inhibitory, membrane, and oscillatory state until a larger pattern is reinstated and can
affect further perception and action. Phase-Wave Differential (PWD) is one candidate description
of receiver-relative timing and waveform differences in that process; PWD is not a synonym for
the concept itself.

## Developmental-pattern source

[[gh-a0477z|a0477z]] is an internally dated October 2012 bridge among streaming patterns,
semantic tokens, category geometry, action, and developmental retinal waves. The underlying
[Ackman et al. study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23060192/) establishes patterned spontaneous
activity throughout the neonatal mouse visual system before eye opening. It does not establish
that retinal waves are semantic tokens or prove the later PWD mechanism.

## What would distinguish the model

1. Require category generalization to held-out exemplars and actions.
2. Separate nonverbal recognition, lexical access, property experience, confidence, and report.
3. Compare firing-rate, population-state, connectivity, phase-relation, and hybrid models on the
   same trials.
4. In BCI write tests, include sham, mismatched-pattern, and label-only controls.
5. Test human and nonhuman category behavior without assuming language is either necessary for all
   concepts or irrelevant to human conceptual structure.

## Meaning changes the organism because it changes reachable action

[[gh-a0563z|a0563z]] distinguishes the physical effect of hearing or seeing a word from the learned
semantic effect of understanding it. [[gh-a0579z|a0579z]] adds sensorimotor grounding: a learned
meaning constrains possible action, bodily consequence, spatial relation, memory, and
communication. The useful operation does not require two literal global cortical grids, nor does
it reduce all meaning to movement.

This gives the SAN account a complete bridge:

```text
physical signal -> learned relation -> altered prediction and route accessibility
-> possible action and consequence -> updated meaning
```
