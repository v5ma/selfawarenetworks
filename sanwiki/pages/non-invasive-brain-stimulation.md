Non-invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) refers to a family of techniques that modulate [[neural-oscillations]] and neuronal excitability from outside the skull — no surgery required. In SAN theory, these methods are significant because they demonstrate that brain [[oscillatory-neural-coupling]] is externally influenceable, consistent with the view that the brain is a pattern-resonance system.

## Key Modalities

**TMS (Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation)** uses rapidly changing magnetic fields to induce electrical currents in cortical neurons. Applied repetitively (rTMS), it can either suppress or enhance activity in targeted regions. Safety incidents exist — patients have reported lasting adverse effects, and TMS is distinct from (though occasionally compared to) electroconvulsive therapy (ECT).

**tDCS (Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation)** delivers weak constant current via scalp electrodes to raise (anodal) or lower (cathodal) cortical excitability. Low-cost and portable, but spatial resolution is coarse.

**tACS (Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation)** entrains [[brain-rhythms]] by oscillating current at a target frequency. A 2022 Nature study found tACS produces long-lasting improvements in working memory — and notably showed greatest benefit for individuals with the worst baseline cognitive function, suggesting it amplifies deficient oscillatory synchrony rather than overwriting healthy patterns.

**[[brainwave-entrainment|Brainwave entrainment]] / music / auditory stimulation** are lower-intensity NIBS-adjacent methods: rhythmic stimuli synchronize endogenous oscillators via [[neural-entrainment]].

## Clinical Relevance

NIBS is explored for Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's (ribosomal protein s15 / LRRK2 pathway), and post-COVID cognitive rehabilitation. HD-tDCS combined with respiratory rehabilitation was trialed in critically ill COVID-19 patients (HD-RECOVERY RCT). The underlying hypothesis: improving oscillatory coherence accelerates [[neuroplasticity]] and waste clearance.

## Relation to SAN Theory

SAN posits that consciousness arises from hierarchical oscillatory phase patterns across spatial and temporal scales. NIBS provides direct empirical leverage: if you can tune an oscillatory pattern externally and observe cognitive or perceptual change, it supports the claim that those patterns carry [[functional-information]]. See also [[napot]] and [[oscillatory-neural-coupling]].

Complementary brain-interface approaches — optical coherence tomography, fNIRS, laser Doppler cerebral blood-flow imaging, MEG — provide readout of the oscillatory state that NIBS methods attempt to modulate. Together, stimulation + readout form the closed-loop framework relevant to [[neural-lace]] design goals.
