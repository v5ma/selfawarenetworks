The cerebral cortex follows a **small-world network** topology: the vast majority of synaptic connections are short-range (local within a cortical column or nearby region), with a sparse minority of long-range connections. This architecture maximizes information density per wiring cost while enabling rapid global broadcast.

## Structural features

- Most neurons respond primarily to directly connected neighbors via action potentials.
- Long-range connections (inter-region axonal projections) are rare relative to local synaptic density.
- Cortical columns of ~10,000 neurons form the basic local processing units.
- Synaptic complexity within each column is enormous: over 1,000 different proteins per synapse combine to produce vast local computational power.

## Relevance to SAN

SAN's [[napot]] framework depends on this topology. Oscillatory phase patterns travel along the dense local connections, while the sparse long-range projections enable the global synchrony that constitutes [[entification]]. The small-world structure is not incidental — it is the architectural substrate that allows [[tonic-oscillation|tonic slow-wave fields]] to organize distributed column activity while [[phasic-tonic-relationship|phasic bursts]] carry local representational detail.

The short-range density also explains why synaptic LTP (see [[camkii-microtubule-ltp]]) can serve short-term memory efficiently: the local [[attractor]] basin is small enough to be captured by a single synaptic change, while long-term structural change (new protein synthesis to the nucleus) is needed to anchor patterns against the high local turnover.
