A neuron has been compared across the scientific literature to three types of devices: a **telegraph** (discrete signal relay), a **clock** (oscillatory timing), and a **geiger counter** (event-rate detector). Each analogy captures a different aspect of how neurons encode information.

Sources include Strogatz's *Sync* and Jon Lieff's *The Secret Language of Cells*, as well as multiple papers in the neural coding literature.

## SAN Relevance

In [[cellular-oscillatory-tomography]] (COT), the clock analogy is foregrounded: neurons as coupled oscillators that synchronize to form unified sensing arrays. The geiger-counter analogy maps to rate-coding models SAN argues are insufficient; the telegraph analogy maps to the Perceptron / all-or-nothing model SAN replaces with the [[metatron]].

The [[phasic-tonic-relationship]] in SAN is best understood through the *clock* analogy: the [[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]] is the clock's baseline tick; phasic bursts are irregular disturbances that encode information in their deviations from regular timing.
