The [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] Inverted Expansion Model reframes cosmological expansion: rather than spacetime uniformly expanding, **mass concentrates (shrinks) local space** while the surrounding spacetime appears stretched by contrast — analogous to crumpling a napkin, which compresses the center and extends the outer edges.

## Core Claims

**Mass as local space-compressor**: Every oscillating mass creates a region of compressed space around it by virtue of locking oscillations together. This is the gravitational well seen from the outside, but from inside it is simply denser-packed space.

**Time density gradient**: Dense mass regions slow time (compressed oscillation periods), while sparse interstellar space runs faster clocks. This is [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]'s restatement of gravitational time dilation — but derived from oscillator mechanics rather than curvature of a spacetime manifold. See [[qgtcd]] for the full framework.

**No heat death**: Because every mass concentration compresses local space and stretches surrounding space, the universe never reaches uniform equilibrium. Local oscillators always generate new gradients. The universe cycles perpetually between condensation and expansion phases rather than ending in entropic flatness.

## Relation to SAN Theory

The same logic applies to neural computation. A dense oscillatory network (e.g., a cortical column during active processing) compresses local information space — more state transitions per unit time — while quieter regions run at background clock speed. [[napot]] proposes that consciousness is the rendering produced by navigating this time-density gradient across the neural array. ## Relation to Standard Cosmology

Standard model: dark energy drives uniform spacetime expansion. [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]: expansion is relative — each mass is compressing its neighborhood while observing distant neighbors as "expanding away." This potentially reframes dark energy as a measurement artifact of gravitational time dilation at cosmological scale.
