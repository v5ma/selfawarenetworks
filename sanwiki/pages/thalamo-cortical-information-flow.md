**Thalamo-cortical information flow** is the pathway by which the thalamus relays and gates sensory signals to the cortex, and is identified in SAN as the key target for high-resolution brain-computer interfaces designed to evoke [[artificial-perception|artificial perception]].

## SAN Framing

The central claim is that to emulate perception artificially — whether via electronic implants or [[bioelectronics]] — one must understand and replicate the exact mechanism by which the thalamus communicates perceptive signals to the cortex. This is the design target for [[neural-array-projection-oscillatory-tomography]] (NAPOT): rather than stimulating cortex arbitrarily, the approach should mirror the thalamic relay format.

This connects directly to [[cellular-oscillatory-tomography]] (COT), where each cell acts as a local oscillatory encoder — the thalamus encodes sensory streams into a format the cortex "expects."

## BCI Implications

Two time-horizon positions are outlined:
- **Now**: EEG + eye-tracking can yield on/off click signals. Useful but low-bandwidth.
- **Future**: Intracranial BCIs targeting the thalamo-cortical interface will deliver the resolution needed to replace smartphones and integrate natively with AR/VR.

The preference for **[[bioelectronics]]** (rather than metal electrodes) is motivated by resolution constraints: metal electrodes cannot achieve the spatial resolution needed for full perceptual bandwidth; biological substrates can.

## Research Priority

Understanding the thalamus → cortex signaling protocol — spike timing, oscillatory phase coding, relay-cell burst modes — is identified as the foundational scientific problem that unlocks genuine [[artificial-perception|artificial perception]]. Progress on this directly advances the SAN theory's application to neurotechnology.
