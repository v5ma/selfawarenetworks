A **wavefront collision** is the critical timing event in [[napot]] that defines a discrete instantiation of the [[perceptual-screen]]. It is not the peak voltage of a sine wave but the moment when propagating oscillatory wavefronts from multiple distributed [[neural-array|neural arrays]] meet, producing a local spike of constructive interference and maximal informational coherence.

This SAN plate maps wavefront collision as a relational synchrony event: distributed phasic wavefronts converge over a tonic substrate, constructive interference crosses a coherence threshold and refreshes one perceptual-screen frame, while destructive interference cancels and produces no new frame.

## Mechanism

Traveling high-frequency phasic waves radiate across cortical surfaces. When two or more of these wavefronts intersect, their phase relationships determine the outcome:

- **Constructive interference** — phases align, amplitude amplifies. This "collision" marks a transient window of high-phase synchrony across participating arrays, which the [[ndca|NDCA]] readout layer interprets as a bounded perceptual frame.
- **Destructive interference** — phases cancel. No screen refresh occurs.

The moment of collision is therefore both the *clock tick* (when the screen refreshes) and a *content carrier* (what oscillatory pattern is coherent at that instant determines what is perceived).

## Relation to phase alignment

Wavefront collisions are a special case of [[perceptual-screen|phase alignment]]: they are the spatiotemporally localized events at which the otherwise diffuse oscillatory activity achieves the coherence threshold required for [[entification]]. The variable refresh rate of the [[perceptual-screen|perceptual screen]] follows directly from the probabilistic, non-metronomic nature of these collisions — they occur when the non-linear interaction of phasic and tonic waves produces a suitable convergence, not on a fixed clock.

## Distinction from raw voltage peaks

The SAN account specifically rejects raw voltage crests as timing signals. Membrane voltage peaks are locally unsynchronized across the cortex and do not correspond to moments of global informational integration. Wavefront collisions, by contrast, are inherently relational — they only occur when at least two traveling waves occupy the same location simultaneously, making them a reliable index of distributed synchrony.
