# Dendritic Spike

A **dendritic spike** is a regenerative electrical event initiated within a dendritic compartment. The term names a family of mechanisms, not one waveform or one universal computation. A dendritic spike must be distinguished from a subthreshold postsynaptic potential, an optically measured calcium transient, a backpropagating action potential, and an axonal action potential.

![Dendritic spikes comprise several mechanisms with several possible outcomes](../assets/generated/san/dendritic-spike/dendritic-spikes-mechanisms-and-conditional-outcomes-20260720-v1.png)

> **Medical boundary:** No generic duration, branch location, propagation distance, effect on somatic firing, or plasticity rule applies to every dendritic spike. A local regenerative event is not automatically a semantic gate, conscious selection, NAPOT sample, or axonal output.

## Established event families

Different preparations use "dendritic spike" for events dominated by different conductances:

- **NMDA spikes or plateaus** arise from regenerative NMDA-receptor current following sufficiently organized glutamatergic input. They can remain branch-local and produce substantial local calcium entry.
- **Sodium dendritic spikes** depend strongly on voltage-gated sodium channels, are faster, and may propagate within the dendritic tree or toward the soma with state-dependent reliability.
- **Calcium spikes or plateaus** depend on voltage-gated calcium conductances, can be broad, and are prominent in particular dendritic compartments and cell classes.
- **Mixed events** recruit several receptor and channel mechanisms. A single label may conceal interactions among sodium, calcium, NMDA, and potassium conductances.

Direct dendritic recordings in mouse visual cortex found sensory-evoked local dendritic spikes that contributed to orientation-selective somatic responses ([Smith et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24162850/)). Human layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons can generate sodium-channel-mediated dendritic spikes, but that finding does not make every human dendrite equivalent to a rodent layer 5 apical dendrite ([Gooch et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36260998/)).

Single dendrites of cortical pyramidal neurons have also discriminated the order and velocity of experimentally controlled synaptic-input sequences. That sensitivity appeared in local calcium signals, somatic depolarization, and sequence-selective spike output ([Branco, Clark, and Hausser, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20705816/)). This supports local spatiotemporal pattern selectivity; it does not establish semantic recognition by a branch.

In a mouse whisker-detection task, calcium activity in the apical dendrites of a subset of layer 5 pyramidal neurons in primary somatosensory cortex correlated with perceptual threshold, and causal manipulation of those dendrites shifted the threshold ([Takahashi et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28008068/)). This is evidence that active dendrites can contribute causally to perception in a specified circuit and task, not evidence for a universal dendritic consciousness gate.

## Separate events, not a fixed pipeline

These events can interact without forming an obligatory sequence:

1. synaptic activation can produce an EPSP and local ion entry without a dendritic spike;
2. a dendritic spike can remain local without an axonal action potential;
3. a dendritic spike can depolarize the soma and change axonal-spike probability;
4. an action potential initiated near the axon initial segment can backpropagate into dendrites;
5. a backpropagating action potential can alter the threshold for a dendritic calcium event; and
6. some combined events can evoke somatic bursts.

In rat layer 5 pyramidal neurons, appropriately timed backpropagating action potentials and distal apical input can support backpropagation-activated calcium spike firing ([Larkum, Zhu, and Sakmann, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10588751/)). This is an important preparation-specific mechanism, not a canonical operation of every cortical neuron.

History and inhibition also matter. Voltage and channel state can open or close transient windows for dendritic-spike propagation ([Park et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37398232/)). Dendrite-targeting inhibition can control initiation, duration, propagation, or termination depending on the pathway.

## Plasticity is conditional

Dendritic events can influence plasticity, but "dendritic spike equals LTP" is false.

- NMDA spikes supported associative LTP in rat CA3 pyramidal-cell dendrites without requiring backpropagating action potentials in one preparation ([Brandalise et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27848967/)).
- Local NMDA-dependent dendritic spikes induced input-specific LTD in mouse visual-cortex layer 5 pyramidal neurons in another protocol ([Holthoff et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15319420/)).
- Sodium dendritic spikes contributed to distal synaptic LTP in rat hippocampal CA1 neurons ([Kim et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26247712/)).
- Branch-specific calcium spikes were associated with task-specific spine potentiation during mouse motor learning ([Cichon and Gan, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25822789/)).
- Hippocampal plateau potentials can act as instructive signals for behavioral-timescale synaptic plasticity across seconds, a different rule from ordinary millisecond-scale spike timing ([Bittner et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28883072/)).

The outcome depends on cell class, branch, input location, conductances, inhibition, neuromodulation, prior synaptic strength, and induction protocol. No one figure should assign every dendritic spike a mandatory memory-write outcome.

## SAN interpretation: a branch-relative state transition

SAN sources emphasize that dendrites perform active, learned transformations rather than passively summing scalar inputs. The medically bounded interpretation is:

> **SAN hypothesis:** A dendritic regenerative event is a branch-relative state transition that changes how a local spatiotemporal input pattern influences later cellular and network activity. Its informational value is defined by the branch's prior state, the receiving cell, the downstream pathway, and the measured consequence.

Under this interpretation, a dendritic spike can be treated as a candidate phasic departure from an ongoing local dendritic state. It becomes relevant to PWD or NAPOT only if the event's timing, waveform, propagation, and receiver-specific consequences predict a later population state beyond ordinary voltage, firing-rate, and anatomical variables.

## Recovered SAN operation: learned branch criteria

Older SAN sources used phrases such as "pattern recognition" and "criteria causation" for dendritic processing. Read literally and without their surrounding mechanism, those phrases can sound as if one branch recognizes a whole object or consciously makes a decision. The recoverable operation is narrower and testable:

> **Learned branch-state operator:** **Input and state:** pathway-specific synaptic input plus branch morphology, synaptic strengths, receptor and channel state, inhibition, and recent activity. **Local transform:** a regenerative event or no event. **Conditional output:** a change in somatic or axon-initial-segment firing, bursting, plasticity, or later recurrent recruitment.

In this bounded sense, a branch can implement a **local biophysical criterion**: its learned structure and current state make some spatiotemporal input patterns more consequential than others. That is compatible with established dendritic sequence discrimination and stimulus selectivity. It is not conscious deliberation, a semantic object judgment, or proof that the branch contains a complete percept.

Basal, perisomatic, apical, and tuft inputs can carry distinct pathway-dependent information in particular pyramidal-cell circuits. Their interaction can alter output probability or bursting. SAN's source-level intuition should therefore be preserved as **evidence-and-context interaction across dendritic domains**, not as a universal rule that basal dendrites always receive sensory evidence, apical dendrites always receive predictions, or the apical dendrite is an axonal output terminal.

The scales must remain separate. A local dendritic event is a candidate micro-level transformation. The cell's axonal output is a distinct cellular consequence. Meso- or macro-level recurrent recruitment is a distributed network result. Neither one branch nor one generic neuron contains, transmits, or views a complete rendered image. Hippocampal BTSP also remains a preparation-specific plasticity mechanism; it does not establish one universal cortical learning rule.

## Bounded tests

1. **Mechanism identification:** combine dendritic voltage recording, calcium imaging, somatic/AIS recording, and conductance-specific perturbation to classify the local event.
2. **Conditional output:** estimate whether waveform and branch state predict local containment, somatic depolarization, axonal firing, bursting, or no detectable output.
3. **Plasticity alternatives:** test LTP, LTD, no durable change, and heterosynaptic effects rather than assuming one sign.
4. **SAN incremental test:** compare a branch-state model with point-neuron, somatic-voltage, and firing-rate baselines on held-out prediction of later population recruitment and behavior.
5. **Causal requirement:** perturb the identified branch event while matching somatic firing as closely as possible; test whether the predicted later circuit state changes.
6. **Falsifier:** if branch-resolved variables do not add predictive or causal information beyond somatic and synaptic baselines, narrow the SAN branch-transition claim.
