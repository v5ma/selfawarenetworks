# Action-Potential Magnetic Field

The historical SAN sources asked a broader question than whether an action potential has a magnetic
field. They proposed that one neural event produces several coupled physical consequences, each
with a different range, delay, receiver, and possible effect:

`neural event -> electrical + ionic + magnetic + mechanical + thermal + chemical/metabolic state changes`

This is a **multimodal event hypothesis**, not a claim that five interchangeable copies of one
message travel through the brain. The magnetic branch remains the least established as an
endogenous neural-computation channel.

> **Scientific boundary:** Action-potential magnetic fields are measurable. That does not establish that mammalian cortical cells decode them, that fields reset at nodes of Ranvier, or that magnetic patterns reconstruct memory or consciousness. SAN's stronger claim requires a named biological receiver and a field-specific causal effect at physiological strength.

## The recovered operation in plain language

When a neuron fires, voltage and ionic currents change first. Those changes can be accompanied by a
weak magnetic field. In some preparations, neural activity also produces measurable membrane
motion, transient heat changes, calcium and metabolic responses, and extracellular chemical
signals. These consequences do not have identical waveforms or meanings.

The medically bounded SAN proposal is:

`shared neural event -> modality-specific transfer -> receiver-specific effect -> changed local or network state`

Electrical and chemical transmission already have established neural receivers. Mechanical,
thermal, and metabolic effects also have identified biological pathways in bounded preparations.
The unresolved magnetic question is whether an endogenous action-potential-scale field reaches any
neural receiver strongly enough to change its state after ordinary electrical, ionic, chemical,
mechanical, and thermal effects are controlled.

## One event, several physical coordinates

A neural event can be represented without treating its consequences as duplicates:

`X_e = [V_e, I_ion,e, B_e, M_e, Q_e, C_e]` [1]

Here:

- `V_e` is membrane and extracellular voltage.
- `I_ion,e` is transmembrane and axial ionic current.
- `B_e` is the magnetic field generated by current.
- `M_e` is mechanical displacement or stress.
- `Q_e` is heat production or absorption.
- `C_e` is the accompanying chemical and metabolic state.

The coordinates are coupled because they arise from overlapping processes, but each has its own
units, geometry, transfer function, and measurement limits. A receiver response is therefore:

`Y_r = F_r(X_e, C_r, H_r)` [2]

where `C_r` is the receiver's current cellular context and `H_r` is its recent history. Equation [2]
does not assume that every receiver senses every coordinate.

## What magnetic measurements establish

Axial and transmembrane currents necessarily generate magnetic fields. Barry and colleagues used
nitrogen-vacancy diamond magnetometry to measure action-potential magnetic signals close to giant
marine-worm and squid axons and outside an intact worm. One reported signal had a peak-to-peak
amplitude of about 4.1 nT, and the detector was about 10 micrometers from the preparation. The study
also recovered waveform and propagation direction
([Barry et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1601513113)).

A later diamond-sensor study measured weak magnetic fields associated with compound action
potentials in mouse corpus-callosum slices
([Hansen et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-39539-y)). These experiments establish an
external measurement channel. They do not identify an endogenous mammalian neural magnetic
receiver.

At myelinated axons, action potentials are regenerated at nodes of Ranvier by concentrated ion
channels and cable properties. A node is not a magnetic reset device. Branching also does not divide
one semantic field vector into fractions, although branch geometry and local channels can affect
electrophysiological propagation.

## The other modalities are not merely decorative

Rejecting duplicate encodings must not erase the source's more useful point: one firing event can
have several experimentally detectable consequences.

- In cultured sensory axons, action-potential firing caused microscopic swelling associated with nonvesicular ATP release through volume-activated anion channels. Blocking the swelling or those channels disrupted this axon-to-glia signaling route ([Fields and Ni, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.2001128)).
- Rabbit vagus-nerve experiments measured positive heat followed by reabsorption during a single impulse, with the timing related to depolarization and repolarization ([Howarth, Keynes, and Ritchie, 1968](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1968.sp008434)).
- Sustained synaptic activity can create a local presynaptic energy deficit. AMPK-PAK signaling can recruit and anchor mitochondria, helping maintain ATP supply, calcium clearance, and synaptic efficacy ([Li et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33020662/)).

These findings do not prove a universal mechanical or thermal neural code. They do show why
mechanical, thermal, and metabolic consequences should be tested rather than dismissed as
figurative language.

Two ATP mechanisms must also remain separate. Mitochondria synthesize ATP for cellular work.
Extracellular ATP can be released through identified membrane pathways and act as a signal. Neither
operation is evidence that an action-potential magnetic field stimulates mitochondria.

## The flavin and mitochondrial branch

The earliest recovered SAN source proposed that flavin-containing neural or mitochondrial
mechanisms might detect magnetic changes and alter ATP production. There is a bounded precedent for
a flavin protein affecting neural responses to an external magnetic field: in Drosophila
motoneurons engineered to express cryptochrome, a 100 mT applied field potentiated a
blue-light-dependent response
([Giachello et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2140-16.2016)).

That experiment does **not** establish the SAN route. It used ectopic Drosophila cryptochrome, blue
light, and a 100 mT field. The applied field was roughly eight orders of magnitude larger than a
1 nT action-potential field, before accounting for geometry, distance, tissue, molecular identity,
or whether the relevant mammalian protein is present in the proposed compartment.

The scientifically open SAN hypothesis is therefore specific:

`endogenous AP magnetic field -> identified neural sensor -> metabolic or excitability change`

It remains unconfirmed until all three links are demonstrated at physiological scale.

## Tonic context and phasic difference

The August 2022 SAN source treated electrical, magnetic, mechanical, and other state changes as
co-occurring modalities across micro, meso, and macro scales. Its deeper proposal was that
modality-specific consequences might jointly affect an ongoing tonic context while unusual events
produce phasic departures.

That idea can be retained without treating a magnetic field as a hidden viewer. The network itself
is the candidate observer-action system: cellular receivers transform available state changes,
recurrent circuits distribute their consequences, and the resulting activity changes perception,
memory, or action. A modality belongs in that account only to the extent that a receiver actually
uses it.

At the **micro scale**, equation [2] concerns a named molecule, membrane, organelle, synapse, axon,
or cell. At the **meso scale**, it concerns interactions in a local circuit or population. At the
**macro scale**, it concerns distributed population and bodily measurements. Evidence at one scale
must not be promoted automatically to another.

## Tests for a causal magnetic channel

For a candidate magnetic receiver `r`, define its incremental contribution as:

`Delta_B = P(Y_r | X_e) - P(Y_r | X_e without B_e)` [3]

This is a conceptual comparison, not permission to remove a physical field while unintentionally
changing the current that produced it. A decisive experiment would:

1. Measure voltage, current, magnetic field, calcium, mechanical displacement, temperature, metabolic state, synaptic output, and receiver response in the same preparation.
2. Name the proposed sensor and show that it is present in the relevant compartment.
3. Add, cancel, or reorient the local magnetic component at physiological amplitude while matching direct electrical stimulation, current, temperature, vibration, light, and chemical state.
4. Show a receiver-specific change that disappears when the candidate sensor is removed or disabled.
5. Demonstrate an effect on a defined computation, plasticity outcome, or behavior beyond the established electrical and chemical pathways.

Failure to identify a physiological receiver or a field-specific causal effect would reject the
magnetic bridge. It would not reject ordinary action-potential biophysics or the established
mechanical, thermal, chemical, and metabolic consequences of neural activity.

## Scientific layers

**Established physiology:** action-potential current generates a weak magnetic field; firing can
co-occur with mechanical, thermal, calcium, chemical, and metabolic changes; neural activity can
alter ATP demand and mitochondrial positioning.

**Evidence-supported interpretation:** one event can be described by coupled physical coordinates
whose different transfer functions and receiver pathways matter experimentally.

**Open SAN hypothesis:** receiver-used combinations of these modality-specific consequences help
shape tonic context, phasic departures, local tuning, and later network state.

**Stronger unconfirmed claim:** endogenous action-potential magnetic fields are detected by a
mammalian cortical flavin, cryptochrome, mitochondrial, ion-channel, or other receiver and thereby
change computation.

**Rejected as established:** magnetic resets at nodes of Ranvier, semantic field-vector fractions at
axon branches, five duplicate memories, or a magnetic field serving by itself as the substrate of
NAPOT or conscious experience.
