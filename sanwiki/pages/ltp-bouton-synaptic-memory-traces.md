Long-term memories in the brain are encoded through **structural changes at synapses** — specifically via bouton addition or removal driven by [[synaptic-computation-ltp|Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)]] and Long-Term Depression (LTD). The central open question: do old memory patterns persist as latent "ghost traces" within new synaptic configurations, or are they completely overwritten?

## Bouton-level encoding

Long-term memories correspond to **active synapses formed via bouton addition or removal** — physical structural additions (en passant boutons, terminal boutons) or their retraction. This is distinct from short-term memory, which operates through volatile changes to existing receptor states without structural commitment.

RNA synthesis is a gating mechanism: RNA malfunctions lead to malformed synapses during LTP-based protein synthesis, disrupting memory consolidation. This links molecular-level expression to macroscopic memory stability.

## The ghost-trace question

Memory storage may operate as **cumulative modification** rather than complete rewrite. Each new learning event alters existing synaptic configurations without fully erasing prior states — leaving structural "ghosts" of previous patterns embedded in the new arrangement. The alternative: memories are sequentially overwritten and prior configurations are truly lost.

In SAN theory this connects to [[napot|NAPOT]]'s multi-layer phase projection: if older synaptic configurations leave residual phase-wave signatures, they can weakly influence ongoing oscillatory patterns — surfacing as déjà vu, priming effects, or implicit memory.

## Buzsáki's alphabet framework

[[gyorgy-buzsaki|Buzsáki]] proposes the brain reorganizes genetically learned neural patterns analogously to constructing words from an alphabet — pre-wired base patterns are recombined and annotated rather than generated from scratch. This frames memory formation as reconfiguration of an inherited repertoire, which would predict that "ghosts" of prior arrangements are systematically reused, not discarded.

## Research path

Optogenetics provides a route to empirical resolution: selectively activating or silencing specific LTP- or LTD-tagged synaptic populations in neural organoids allows direct observation of whether deactivated configurations leave residual structural or functional signatures. This distinguishes full overwrite from cumulative trace accumulation.
