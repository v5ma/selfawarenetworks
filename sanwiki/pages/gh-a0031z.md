What does the Neurofeedback Salon experience entail?

The source describes one down session, called Inhibit, and one up session, called Excite. Each session lasts 35 minutes. It says the short-term effects can feel pleasant and may last up to a week, and it presents the intended next-day effect as feeling noticeably better. It also describes hoped-for longer-term effects after several sessions, including brain optimization, improved focus, relaxation, creativity, and problem solving. The source compares the equipment to technology used in other offices for autism, addiction, attention deficit disorder, and post-traumatic stress syndrome. It also says neurofeedback technology has been used by athletes, high-performing students, marksmen, corporate executives, and professional golfers to optimize mind and mind-body states.

The Salon includes brainwave entrainment aligned with the participant's brainwaves, which the source describes as a smooth, stimulating, surreal, and profound experience. It says the two sessions mentioned above were inspired by the Len Ochs Protocol, which the source says began showing success in 1992 using equipment that then cost half a million dollars. The source also says other sessions were backed by credible research that would be listed on the website later. It describes the equipment as commercial and medical grade, costing thousands of dollars, while presenting it as an upgrade over equipment that previously cost much more.

When a participant enters the Neurofeedback Salon, they can take a seat while the equipment is introduced. The source describes the neurofeedback piece as a black plastic headset with fourteen sensors that sits on the participant's head to scan electromagnetic brainwave patterns and transmit them wirelessly to a computer. The next pieces are blue or white goggles. Blue is described as less intense, while white blinking light is recommended for the full experience because it reflects the participant's brainwaves combined with the entrainment signal. The source says blue goggles can still create a strong session because receptors in the human eye are especially responsive to blue light. The goggles can also be used with eyes closed by covering the outside with removable goggle tape. The participant also receives clean silicone earbuds and uses high-quality headphones.

Next, the source says the participant is shown graphs charting their brainwaves. The additional feedback is described as helping create useful associations inside the mind between regions. The operator checks that all sensors are lighting up green, starts the session, and watches the screen while the participant sits back and watches as well. After 35 minutes, the second half of the program begins. Another 35 minutes later, the participant removes the equipment.

Afterward, the source encourages people to sit quietly for up to fifteen minutes so they can absorb the experience. It says the session graph can be emailed with an electronic bill payable through online banking, Google Checkout, PayPal, or a credit card.

Other types of Neurofeedback

Neurofeedback sessions are described as coming in two varieties.

1. The first type trains the participant to manipulate objects on a screen using brainwaves. This requires decoding brainwaves and translating them into software movement according to the rigid structure of programmed controls. The source says current software limitations create a sharp learning curve for many people. Sensors have to be aligned precisely, and the source says differences in brain size or shape can make the predefined software controls harder to use. It says many people become frustrated with that type.

2. The second type of neurofeedback experience, used as part of the Neo Mind Cycle Experience, sends brainwave patterns back to the participant after converting them to light and sound. The source says this approach does not have to translate the patterns through rigid software because the converted signal is returned to the participant and decoded by the brain. It describes the process as a feedback loop from senses to brainwaves, to machine, to senses again, and back through the cycle. The source describes this feedback loop as similar to expanded self awareness.

3. The source expresses hope that predictive intelligent software from Numenta will become more useful for decoding brainwaves and translating them into on-screen movement. It says the Salon can support both the first and second types of neurofeedback experience, but that the first method would wait until software technology improved.

The source compares the unfolded neocortex to a large dinner napkin or table placemat that is six layers deep. It describes the wiring as three-dimensional rather than chaotic, but says EEG signals are difficult to decode because the surface is folded inside the skull. It also says EEG signals need to be separated from ECG interference, such as the heartbeat, and from other body sounds that are usually not noticed but can be loud when measuring brainwaves.

The source describes neurofeedback as a high-tech mirror for the brain and compares its current resolution limits to a lower-resolution camera. It also compares fMRI to a lower-resolution camera because it measures blood flow rather than brainwaves. The source argues that even a lower-resolution view of one's own brainwaves can still be useful when no higher-resolution option is available.

The source also describes this mirror as supporting an informed form of brainwave entrainment. It presents the approach as different from traditional binaural beats because the beat pattern is informed by live brainwaves, creating what the source calls a surreal and highly synchronous experience.
