# Sensor-Array Consciousness: Distributed Sensing, Coupling, and the SAN Single-Sensor Hypothesis

## Recovered operation

SAN's "single sensor" language describes a proposed **system-level function**, not the
anatomical fusion of many receptors into one receptor and not a hidden viewer:

```text
many locally sensitive units
-> recurrent signal exchange
-> maintained timing and state relationships
-> propagation of local departures
-> population estimate or coordinated response
-> action
-> returned sensory consequence
```

No one neuron, cortical column, or brain location contains the complete observation.
The participating receivers, transmitters, recurrent routes, body, and consequences
collectively perform the sensing and response.

## What neuroscience establishes

- Visual, auditory, and somatosensory signals can converge in particular neurons and
  circuits. This is pathway-specific multisensory integration, not proof that one
  generic neuron directly receives every named feature
  ([Meredith and Stein, 1986](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1986.56.3.640)).
- Human perceptual estimates can combine visual and haptic cues according to their
  relative reliability
  ([Ernst and Banks, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/415429a)).
- The phase relation between rhythmic neural groups can modulate their effective
  interaction
  ([Womelsdorf et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1139597)).
- Fireflies can synchronize flashing through coupled timing and feedback
  ([Buck and Buck, 1968](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.159.3821.1319)).
  Synchronization does not by itself demonstrate shared perception or consciousness.
- Perturbational complexity can distinguish some conscious and unconscious conditions
  by measuring responses that are both distributed and differentiated
  ([Casali et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294)).
  It is a relevant comparator, not proof of the full SAN mechanism.

## Source-faithful anatomical corrections

### Hippocampal analogy, not cortical identity

The later rough b0005y annotation calls the hippocampus a "special cortical column."
That wording reflects a functional hierarchy analogy associated with Jeff Hawkins and
Sandra Blakeslee's *On Intelligence*, not literal anatomy. The hippocampal formation
contains allocortical structures with organization distinct from a six-layer
neocortical column, and it interacts extensively with entorhinal and association
cortices ([Schultz and Engelhardt, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1159/000360925)).

The medically accurate SAN interpretation is a comparison among **receive-transform-project
operations** in hippocampal-entorhinal systems and neocortical circuits. It is not a
claim that the hippocampus is anatomically one cortical column.

### Pyramidal routes, not all-to-all wiring

Pyramidal neurons participate in diverse local, corticocortical, corticothalamic,
corticostriatal, corticospinal, and other corticofugal pathways. The source's
"whole-brain pyramidal connections" should therefore be read as distributed long-range
projection architecture, not as one class of neuron uniformly contacting the entire brain.

### Timing sensitivity, not planetary resonance

The 2022 audio speaks of a system configured to perceive patterns at their relevant
rates. A later text annotation added a 6-8 Hz alpha/Earth-resonance passage and a quote
attributed to Nikola Tesla. That passage is not in the authoritative retranscription,
6-8 Hz is not the standard adult alpha range, and the Tesla attribution has not been
verified. It is therefore not evidence for this page.

The source-faithful scientific interpretation is narrower: neural responses depend on
membrane and synaptic time constants, oscillatory state, relative phase, and the temporal
structure of incoming signals.

### Substrate independence remains a hypothesis

"Dead things can be conscious" is the source's strong substrate-independence conjecture.
It does not mean that inert matter has been shown to be conscious. The testable proposal
is that material category alone may be insufficient; a candidate system would need the
relevant sensing, transmission, recurrence, differentiation, integration, persistence,
and action or perturbational consequences.

## SAN interpretation

SAN proposes that a sensor array becomes functionally more than a collection of isolated
detectors when its components recursively change one another's future states. In the
strong version:

```text
distributed sensing
-> receiver-relative change
-> recurrent propagation
-> selective coupling and structured separation
-> integrated but differentiated system state
-> coordinated observer-action
```

The tonic population state supplies the ongoing relational context. Phasic departures,
including PWD candidates, supply differentiated updates. A returned consequence changes
later sensing, so the "observer" is the closed recurrent sensor-action system itself.

This joins established observations into a SAN-specific hypothesis. It does not establish
that synchrony, recurrence, complexity, or collective behavior alone is sufficient for
consciousness.

## Scale boundary

- **Cellular:** receptors and synapses sample local conditions; dendrites integrate
  selected inputs; spikes and synaptic release influence particular downstream targets.
- **Meso circuit:** recurrent excitatory and inhibitory populations combine, separate,
  route, and update selected signals. Coupling is receiver- and state-dependent.
- **Whole organism:** distributed brain and body systems coordinate perception, memory,
  valuation, action, and returned sensory evidence without a central inner viewer.
- **Supra-organism analogy:** fireflies, hives, networks, and cities can display collective
  sensing or coordination. Whether any such system is conscious requires discriminating
  evidence beyond synchrony or aggregate output.

## Discriminating tests

A useful test should compare the full coupled-system hypothesis against simpler models:

1. Measure local sensor states, recurrent coupling, timing relations, and downstream action.
2. Perturb one component or route while preserving total input and average activity.
3. Test whether coupling and receiver-relative timing improve held-out prediction of the
   population estimate, action, and recovery.
4. Compare against models using sensor count, firing rate, anatomical connectivity, or
   ordinary cue integration alone.
5. For any consciousness claim, require evidence that distinguishes conscious integration
   from nonconscious synchronization, reflexive control, and aggregate behavior.

The strong SAN claim is weakened if recurrent timing and differentiated propagation add no
predictive or causal value beyond those simpler accounts.
