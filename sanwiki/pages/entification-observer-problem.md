# Entification and the Distributed Observer

> **Core architectural rule:** A [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] (PWD) is a typed, receiver-relative departure from a declared tonic or reference pattern. It is foundational throughout SAN's recurrent operation. A departure may involve excitation, inhibition, a missing expected event, delay, or an interaction among them. PWD is not excitation minus inhibition, and it is not a later or secondary stage.

> **Image status:** No image is accepted on this page. Any future visual containing brain anatomy, cells, membranes, pathways, currents, fields, proprioception, or physiology must pass a separate medical-grade image certification before installation.

## Governing question

How can many spatially separated and temporally changing processes become one bounded system that
senses, transforms, remembers, selects, acts, and registers the consequences of its own activity?

**Entification** is Micah's term for the proposed formation and maintenance of that entity-level
organization. It names the operation by which differentiated cells, arrays, recurrent pathways,
body states, and action-return loops may function as one causally bounded observer-action system.

The term does not solve the problem by renaming emergence. It asks which relations make the proposed
entity measurable, which interventions should disrupt it, and what recovery would count as evidence
that the joined organization rather than one isolated component is doing the work.

Entification is therefore stronger than simultaneous activity, synchrony, recurrence, embodiment,
or a self-model considered alone. It is also not, by itself, evidence that every system satisfying a
subset of the relations is conscious, a continuing person, or a bearer of moral status.

## What the term keeps separate

| Not sufficient by itself | Retained Entification question |
|---|---|
| Activity occurring at the same time | Do the activities have receiver-specific, reciprocal consequences inside a bounded system? |
| Maximum synchrony | Does coordination preserve the differentiated relations needed for routing, memory, choice, and action? |
| Generic recurrence | Does recurrent traffic update a continuing self-related control state through returned evidence? |
| A boundary drawn by an observer | Does the proposed boundary predict perturbation, transfer, disruption, and recovery better than competing boundaries? |
| A self-model stored somewhere | Does the model participate in sensing, selection, action, and correction by the same distributed process? |
| The word `emergence` | What exact causal operation joins the parts, and what observation could show that the proposed joining failed? |

## Eight candidate measurable relations

The eight relations below are SAN's current eligibility test. They are intended to be jointly
investigated. Passing one row does not establish the rows beneath it or certify the complete theory.

| Relation | Operational question | Candidate evidence | Failure condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Bounded but permeable organization** | Which cells, pathways, body variables, effectors, and timescales belong to the candidate system for this task? | A declared boundary that improves causal and predictive fit across intervention and recovery. | Arbitrary boundary changes make no difference, or an external partition predicts the data equally well. |
| **2. Recurrent access to internal and external state** | Can consequences of prior internal, sensory, and bodily states alter later reception within the proposed system? | Traced recurrent routes with receiver-specific delays and state-dependent effects. | Activity is feed-forward only, or the alleged return has no discriminable consequence for later receivers. |
| **3. Integration across relevant scales** | Do local events affect larger coordinated states while larger states constrain local reception? | Cross-scale perturbation and decoding with direction, timing, and receiver declared. | Apparent integration reduces to common input, volume conduction, averaging, or an analysis artifact. |
| **4. Selective propagation and differentiation** | Which relations are transmitted, inhibited, delayed, omitted, amplified, or kept separate? | Route-specific changes that preserve both coordination and content-bearing differentiation. | Universal synchrony, undifferentiated rate, or nonspecific arousal explains the result equally well. |
| **5. Memory-dependent transition** | Does learned history change what the same incoming relation causes next? | State- and history-dependent response, plasticity, retention, and matched forgetting or disruption. | Current input alone predicts the transition and learned state adds no explanatory value. |
| **6. Prediction or consideration of alternatives** | Does the system maintain and compare action-relevant possibilities before selection? | Alternative-sensitive activity whose perturbation changes a specified discrimination or action. | The signal is only post hoc motor, reward, or report activity with no prospective consequence. |
| **7. Action and returned evidence** | Does selected activity change the body or world and return sensory or proprioceptive evidence to the changed system? | A closed intervention chain from neural state to action to measured return to neural update. | The proposed loop omits the effector, return path, or effect of the return on later state. |
| **8. Continuing self-related control** | Does a persistent but revisable coordinate organize memory, body state, goals, and correction across episodes? | Transfer, damage, recalibration, and recovery predicted by one continuing control model. | A momentary episode or externally supplied controller explains performance without a continuing internal relation. |

These are eligibility relations, not a single scalar score. A valid experiment must declare the
candidate entity, receivers, reference pattern, scale, task, effectors, endpoint, and causal strength
before interpreting the result.

## Joined causal operation

1. A candidate boundary, receiver, timescale, task, and tonic or expected relation are declared.
2. Sensory, bodily, recurrent, synaptic, electrical, and chemical influences reach cells and arrays.
3. Excitation, inhibition, missing expected activity, delay, and interaction can each change what a
   receiver encounters relative to its tonic or expected relation.
4. SAN calls that typed, receiver-relative difference PWD.
5. Learned membrane, dendritic, synaptic, cellular, and network state transforms the consequence.
6. The changed receiver projects a target-specific consequence into later cells and arrays.
7. [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] repeatedly reconstructs relations through receive, transform, project,
   compare, and return operations. It uses PWD throughout recurrent operation, not only at a final,
   late, or optional stage.
8. Memory preserves selected consequences while prediction and consideration constrain alternatives.
9. Action changes the body or environment, and sensory and proprioceptive evidence returns to the
   already changed system.
10. The return updates later readiness, routing, memory, self-model, and action selection.
11. Entification names the proposed entity-level organization when this joined operation is causally
    bounded, differentiated, recurrent, memory-bearing, embodied, and continuing.
12. The same distributed process performs the observer action; the model is not presented to a
    second witness hidden behind the network.

This chain is the causal middle. Omitting it produces a strawman in which Entification is only a
name for synchrony, integration, emergence, or a passive internal picture.

Architectural rank is distinct from page order, historical date, brainwave frequency, cortical layer,
and the order in which a diagram is drawn.

## Coordination requires difference

A tonic or recurring oscillatory relation can provide context, readiness, or a temporarily effective
route. The meaningful event is not restricted to added excitation. It can be an excitatory advance,
inhibitory delay, suppressed or missing expected event, burst, duration change, phase shift, or an
interaction whose consequence depends on the receiving system's current and learned state.

Synchrony can increase effective interaction in one preparation while structured phase dispersion,
inhibition, or separation preserves sequence and selectivity in another. SAN therefore treats
alignment and differentiation as complementary coordinates, not as a contest in which maximum
synchrony is always the goal.

The phrase **distributed observer** describes the joined causal work of participating receivers. It
does not imply that every cell has the complete represented scene, that every cell is independently
conscious, or that one global waveform contains all content without receiver-specific transformation.

## Levels of observer language

| Level | Operational meaning | What must not be inferred automatically |
|---|---|---|
| **Local receiver** | A cell or array changes because a particular relation reaches it in its current state. | One receiver is not thereby the complete observer or a miniature person. |
| **Distributed observer-action system** | Many partial receivers jointly transform, route, remember, act, and register returned consequences. | Distributed activity alone does not identify the correct entity boundary. |
| **Self-model** | A revisable internal relation predicts aspects of the system's body, capacities, or future state. | Successful self-modeling alone does not establish phenomenal consciousness. |
| **Conscious episode** | SAN's stronger proposal that the active distributed organization constitutes a rendered and detected episode for that system. | One episode is not automatically a complete continuing person. |
| **Continuing person** | Diachronic biological, mnemonic, embodied, social, and control relations persist and update across episodes. | Temporary coupling, partial transfer, or a similar output does not automatically transfer personhood or moral status. |

Keeping these levels separate allows evidence at one level to support a component without being
promoted into proof of every higher-level claim.

## Four-level claim ladder

1. **Established components:** neural populations coordinate, differentiate, inhibit, disperse,
   route, remember, predict, and contribute to action in preparation-specific ways.
2. **Documented SAN synthesis:** learned receivers use tonic context and typed PWD relations while
   recurrent arrays receive, transform, project, act, and register returned evidence.
3. **Entification extension:** those operations may form one functionally bounded actor when all
   eight relations and their causal dependencies are jointly present.
4. **Consciousness and identity claim:** SAN proposes that the appropriately organized process is
   the observer rather than a display for another witness. Conscious episode, continuing person,
   transfer, temporary coupling, and moral status retain separate eligibility tests.

No lower level proves the level above it. This separation preserves both the strongest SAN proposal
and the actual inferential strength of each scientific result.

## Competing explanations and decisive tests

| Competing account of a result | Required discriminator |
|---|---|
| Mean rate, spectral power, or common input | Match those variables while changing the proposed receiver-relative timing or route. |
| Volume conduction or measurement mixing | Demonstrate directed, delayed, receiver-specific consequence under perturbation. |
| Nonspecific arousal, movement, reward, or report | Match behavior and state variables while selectively disrupting the proposed Entification relation. |
| Generic recurrence | Show memory-, alternative-, boundary-, and action-return-specific predictions beyond recurrence alone. |
| A centralized controller | Remove or isolate the candidate center and test whether distributed control and recovery follow the declared model. |
| External scaffolding | Remove the external controller or cue and test whether the system preserves and revises its own control relation. |
| A self-model without joined entity organization | Preserve model output while disrupting its causal participation in sensing, selection, action, or correction. |
| A post hoc boundary | Compare candidate partitions and require the selected boundary to win prospectively under disruption and rescue. |

A decisive Entification program should:

1. declare the proposed entity boundary, task, timescale, receivers, effectors, and self-related state;
2. measure all eight relations rather than synchrony or complexity alone;
3. trace the ordered chain from local difference through receiver consequence, memory, action, and return;
4. reversibly disconnect, inhibit, delay, or retime one proposed integrating route while preserving
   gross activity and matched nuisance variables;
5. predict a specific discrimination, action, continuity, recalibration, or recovery failure;
6. restore the declared relation and test matched recovery;
7. compare the full organization with controls preserving mean rate, power, movement, transient
   synchrony, external coupling, and task performance where possible; and
8. reject or revise the proposed boundary when the joined model provides no incremental prediction
   or perturbation-specific consequence beyond those controls.

## Interpretation recovery rule

An apparent mismatch cannot be promoted directly into a SAN-versus-science contradiction or used to
invalidate an accepted source reading. It first enters `INTERPRETATION_RECOVERY_REQUIRED`.

Before any revision can supersede a governed interpretation, the reviewer must:

1. read the complete Micah source neighborhood rather than an isolated sentence;
2. reconstruct the strongest complete claim and its ordered causal chain;
3. separate speaker, date, later commentary, repository fixation, and current public visibility;
4. test for strawman compression, missing context, terminology drift, and architectural-rank drift;
5. read the matching primary scientific source at its actual species or substrate, preparation,
   intervention, measure, endpoint, scale, and causal strength;
6. state the positive formulation in which the SAN claim and scientific component occupy their
   correct semantic levels; and
7. submit the proposed revision to the same source, science, narrative, protocol, and release checks
   required of the interpretation it would replace.

A later challenge has no automatic invalidation or supersession authority. It can add a review record,
but the governed interpretation changes only after the complete revision checks pass.
