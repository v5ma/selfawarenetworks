# Hofstadter and Hawkins

I learned two important things from author Douglas Hofstadter. The first is the power of using analogies for effective communication. The second is his argument that the [self](gh-a0206z.md) can be understood as a strange loop. Hofstadter did not provide a neurological definition of how that loop functions in the brain, so I read later neuroscience and cognitive-science work as a way to refine the analogy rather than treat it as a finished model.

Jeff Hawkins's books On Intelligence and A Thousand Brains helped me embrace concepts such as predictive coding, biologically plausible neural networks, sparse distributed representations, and reference-frame functions involving cortical columns, grid cells, and place cells.

Gyorgy Buzsaki's book Rhythms of the Brain, combined with Hawkins's On Intelligence, gave me a useful way to think about the brain as a learning feedback loop. That learning-feedback-loop idea, shaped by the combination of Hofstadter's and Hawkins's concepts, is part of why I named my neurofeedback salon [Neo Mind Cycle](neo-mind-cycle.md).
