# Addressing Criticism of "Humans Are Metal Robots" (2018)

Micah Blumberg's September 24, 2018 response clarified what the phrase "humans are
metal robots" was and was not meant to claim. The phrase was a deliberately provocative
physicalist analogy: human neurons depend on charged metal ions and organized
biological machinery, while perception, feeling, and action arise from the activity of
the living network. It was not a claim that a transistor feels, that neurons are simple
binary switches, or that any machine with the same outward behavior has a human mind.

## What the response actually clarified

### 1. The metal language concerns ions, not a metal chassis

Sodium, potassium, and calcium are chemical elements classified as metals. In nervous
tissue they occur mainly as dissolved charged ions, including Na+, K+, and Ca2+. Their
concentration gradients and membrane conductances help produce electrical signaling.
The analogy does not make a biological cell equivalent to a steel robot or a silicon
transistor.

### 2. Coincidence was proposed as a basis of an information unit

The response explicitly rejected "one neural firing equals one bit." The intended
operation is receiver-relative: selected inputs arrive within a relevant spatial and
temporal window, interact with the receiver's present state, and produce a consequential
distinction. That consequence may be subthreshold, dendritic, somatic, synaptic,
plastic, or circuit-level. See [[coincidence-as-a-bit|Coincidence as a Bit]] for the
fully source-recovered formulation.

### 3. The brain was not reduced to a purely digital device

The response says that digital-versus-continuous coding was a subtopic, not its central
thesis. Neural systems contain both event-like and continuously varying variables:
action-potential initiation can be thresholded, while membrane voltage, conductance,
dendritic integration, timing, waveform, transmitter release, neuromodulation, and
population state vary over continuous or probabilistic ranges.

The 2018 article cited Tee and Taylor's then-current discrete-representation preprint.
That paper proposed a communications-theory argument; it did not settle all neural
coding as purely digital. The source-faithful SAN recovery is a hybrid
continuous-discrete system, not "the brain is a computer made only of ones and zeros."

### 4. Substrate matters through the operations it can support

Micah described a position close to, but not identical with, unrestricted
functionalism. Different materials might support a human-like mind, but only if the
substrate can implement the relevant spatial, temporal, recurrent, embodied, and
internal representational operations. Behavior alone was not offered as sufficient.

### 5. Perception was distributed, not watched by an inner viewer

The response described concepts as rendered by the brain to other parts of the brain in
feedback loops. Read source-faithfully, this means that recurrent cellular and network
activity changes the state of other participating receivers. It does not install a
small observer, screen, or final display inside the head. The distributed network is
the perceiving and acting system.

### 6. Feelings as spatiotemporal patterns was a hypothesis

The response proposed that feelings, knowledge, and intuitions have analyzable spatial
and temporal organization and might someday be reproduced by an engineered system that
matches the necessary biological functions. This is a computational and philosophical
hypothesis. Established neuroscience supports measurable neural, bodily, behavioral,
and reported correlates of affect; it does not yet provide a complete pattern identity
for a feeling or a validated procedure for transferring one to a machine.

## Physiological corrections that preserve the argument

The original source contains compressed wording and at least two recoverable technical
errors. Correcting them does not require discarding the underlying proposition.

- **Both Na+ and K+ are positively charged cations.** The original phrase "negatively
  charged potassium K+" is a sign error. Potassium can have a negative equilibrium
  potential relative to a chosen voltage reference, and the cell interior can be
  negative relative to the exterior, but the K+ ion itself is positive.
- **Neurotransmitters are not positive and negative ions sent as neurotransmitters.** A
  presynaptic terminal releases transmitter molecules. Receptor activation then changes
  postsynaptic conductances, ion flow, membrane state, and sometimes biochemical or
  plasticity pathways.
- **Neurons, not synapses, generate conventional axonal action potentials.** Spikes are
  commonly initiated near the axon initial segment and can propagate along the axon;
  somatic spikes can also backpropagate into dendrites. Dendrites can generate local
  regenerative events, but the site, channel mechanism, cell type, and consequence must
  be named.
- **Coincidence is not one universal gate.** Dendritic location, synapse type,
  inhibition, morphology, recent activity, neuromodulation, and cell class alter the
  effective integration rule.

Classic voltage-clamp work established voltage- and time-dependent sodium and potassium
conductances in action-potential generation
([Hodgkin and Huxley, 1952](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1952.sp004764)).
Somatic action potentials were shown to propagate actively into neocortical pyramidal
cell dendrites
([Stuart and Sakmann, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1038/367069a0)).
Single cortical dendrites can discriminate the temporal order of synaptic activation,
with consequences in local calcium and somatic depolarization
([Branco, Clark, and Hausser, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1189664)).
These results support the richer cellular substrate that the response was trying to
protect; they do not make every coincidence a fixed bit or every dendrite a complete
mind.

## The recovered architecture

The closing 2018 synthesis can be translated without treating its engineering analogies
as literal anatomy:

    electrochemical biological substrate
    + receiver-dependent coincidence and subthreshold computation
    + learned network transformations
    + spatially organized internal representation
    + recurrent feedback among participating systems
    -> distributed perception, feeling, decision, and action

The source summarized this with the engineering terms digital signal processor, deep
learning network, 3D point cloud, and fractal feedback loops. Those terms identify
candidate functional requirements. Merely combining four software components does not
prove that a human mind or consciousness has been constructed.

## Claim ladder

### Established physiology

- Neural signaling depends on ion gradients, channels, membrane conductance, chemical
  synapses, dendritic integration, and action potentials.
- Dendrites can perform nonlinear, state-dependent spatial and temporal integration.
- Recurrent neural populations can build task-relevant internal representations and
  affect behavior.

### Historical 2018 interpretation

The machine analogy is useful when "machine" means an organized physical system whose
material, internal state, timing, feedback, and embodiment jointly matter. Coincidence
can serve as a candidate receiver-relative unit of distinction without equating one
spike with one Shannon bit.

### Later SAN interpretation

Later SAN work develops tonic context, phasic differences, PWD, NAPOT, distributed
observer-action, and multiscale rendering. Those later terms may clarify the 2018
architecture, but they must not be presented as words or fully formed mechanisms already
contained in the September 2018 response.

### Stronger experimental claim

A candidate engineered substrate would need to reproduce more than task behavior. It
should preserve declared internal causal organization: receiver-specific integration,
recurrent state transitions, perturbation responses, embodied feedback, and the
relationships among neural or machine state, report, and action. Similar behavior with
different internal causal structure would not by itself establish experiential
equivalence.

## Scale boundary

- **Cellular:** ions, channels, receptors, dendritic branches, soma, axon initial
  segment, axon, terminals, and local plasticity have distinct roles.
- **Circuit:** recurrent excitation and inhibition select, combine, suppress, and
  redistribute population activity; no one cell contains the complete representation.
- **Network and organism:** distributed sensory, association, memory, body-state, and
  motor systems jointly support report and action; a complete observer is not localized
  to one screen or module.
