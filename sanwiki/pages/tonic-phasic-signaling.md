# Tonic-Phasic Signaling

**Tonic-phasic signaling** applies SAN's canvas/ink distinction to communication between identified
biological senders and receivers. The canonical mechanism and its medical boundaries are maintained
at [[tonic-phasic-canvas|Tonic and Phasic Roles: Canvas and Ink]].

## Signal definition

A sender's sustained and transient activity can jointly affect a receiver, but the receiver does
not read an abstract shared clock. It responds through its receptors, dendrites, membrane state,
inhibition, intrinsic dynamics, and recent history. The relevant signal may involve rate, timing,
phase, burst structure, waveform, release probability, transmitter, or population geometry in a
particular preparation.

Calling the relationship a channel requires:

1. a named sender and receiver;
2. a measured tonic reference and phasic variable;
3. recoverable information at the receiver;
4. causal use beyond correlated variables;
5. a failure condition.

The older shared-tonic-clock, thalamic-display, and bare-carrier-as-awareness language is SAN
provenance, not established neurophysiology. Multimodal integration is distributed and does not
require one thalamic screen or one frequency carrier.
