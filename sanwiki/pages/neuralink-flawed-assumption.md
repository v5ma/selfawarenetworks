## Position

Within [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks the critique has three components. First, the *signal-model* objection: Neuralink's electrode arrays sample voltage spikes at high temporal resolution but treat each channel as a relatively independent rate carrier. Under [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography|NAPOT]] the relevant variable is the *phase relationship* across many channels at once — and a decoder built on per-channel rate statistics will leave most of the [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave-differential]] structure on the table. Second, the *form-factor* objection: the [[neural-lace-ar-vr-no-glasses]] / [[nervegear-vision]] thesis is that the consumer endpoint should be non-invasive ([[non-invasive-brain-stimulation|non-invasive]] read/write through skull) rather than implanted. The [[neural-lace-podcast]] interviews ([[neural-lace-podcast-ep2-blaze-sanders]], [[neural-lace-podcast-ep3-eric-matzner]], [[neural-lace-podcast-4-andre-watson]], [[neural-lace-podcast-5-jules-urbach-otoy]]) document the alternative-pathway argument. Third, the *write-side* objection: Neuralink-class read-only or low-channel write devices cannot perform the [[neural-rendering]] that [[brain-computer-interface-neural-readwrite]] argues a SAN-aligned BCI would need to deliver true [[augmented-reality-bci|AR/VR]] experiences without [[neural-lace-ar-vr-no-glasses|glasses]].
