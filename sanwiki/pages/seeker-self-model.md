The **seeker** is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s term for the [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model that arises from a brain's ability to predict its own neuronal firing patterns in order to coordinate movement and understand causality.

Rather than selfhood being a unified observer, the seeker is an emergent prediction process: neurons anticipate their own activation, and this anticipatory loop constitutes a motor-grounded sense of agency. The seeker "seeks" outcomes by simulating its own control signals before executing them.

## Connection to NAPOT and SAN

The seeker [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model is consistent with [[napot]] — the brain's oscillatory activity encodes predictions and their errors. In SAN terms, [[self-aware-network]] emerges when the network can model its own future states, and the seeker is the minimal unit of that [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modeling capacity.

Predictive inversion — where the brain inverts motor predictions to infer sensory causes — links the seeker concept to [[predictive-coding]] and to the SAN notion that [[consciousness]] is a continuous forward-model of the body's interaction with the world.

## Embodied grounding

The seeker is inherently embodied: coordinated physical activity (motor commands + sensory feedback loops) is the foundation, not an abstract symbol system. This grounds selfhood in [[tonic-oscillation]] that maintains body posture and spatial orientation rather than in cortical content alone.

## History

Earlier SAN formulations emphasized the [[gh-a0206z|self]] as a distributed observer across [[neural-code-functions]]. The seeker framing foregrounds motor prediction as the genetic root of [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modeling, adding a developmental and evolutionary grounding that the pure observer framing lacked.
