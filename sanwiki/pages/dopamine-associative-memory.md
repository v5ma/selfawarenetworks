## Position

Within the SAN frame, an "associative memory" is a recovered link between patterns: when one [[neural-rendering-of-qualia|rendered]] pattern partially activates, the network regenerates the patterns previously co-active with it. The [[criterial-causation|criterial-causation]] account of single-neuron decision says each [[pattern-neurons|pattern neuron]] only fires when its criteria are met; what dopamine appears to add — given its neuromodulatory function in published neuroscience and given SAN's positioning in [[dopamine-as-pattern-selector]] — is a gating signal that biases *which* of several locally-criterial patterns gets written through into a longer-lived synaptic configuration. In SAN-internal terms, dopamine biases the [[pattern-competition-consciousness|pattern competition]] in favor of the patterns associated with motivationally significant rendering, so that the [[multi-perspective-pattern-convergence|cross-array convergence]] that would normally average out a transient pattern instead consolidates it.

This positioning has structural consequences for the SAN stack. It implies that [[memory-as-regenerable-pattern|regenerable memory]] is biased rather than uniform — the patterns most likely to be regenerated are those that the dopamine system "tagged" during their original rendering. It connects directly to [[dopamine-effort-motivation]], because the same neuromodulatory signal that gates encoding also gates the effort-allocation decision for retrieval. And it sits adjacent to [[adderall-dopamine-dynamics]] insofar as pharmacological perturbation of the dopamine system would, on this view, perturb not only [[attention]] but the *associative structure* of memory, by changing which patterns get co-tagged.
