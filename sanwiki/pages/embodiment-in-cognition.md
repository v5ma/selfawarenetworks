# Embodiment in Cognition

> **Scientific boundary:** Embodied cognition is a broad family of positions supported by sensorimotor, proprioceptive, interoceptive, affective, and environmental coupling. It does not establish one universal mechanism, prove that every concept is a motor simulation, or independently validate SAN/NAPOT.

Embodiment means that cognition is performed by a living system whose body constrains sensing, memory, valuation, prediction, and action. The body is not merely an input device attached to a disembodied controller.

## Several body-related variables must remain distinct

A medically careful account separates:

- **body schema:** action-oriented estimates of body configuration;
- **body image:** perceptual and conceptual knowledge about the body;
- **proprioceptive location:** where a body part is estimated to be;
- **body ownership:** whether a body or part is experienced as mine;
- **agency:** whether I am experienced as causing an action or event;
- **self-location:** where the perceiving self is experienced to be;
- **interoceptive state:** signals and inferences concerning internal bodily condition.

These variables interact but can dissociate. Rubber-hand and virtual-body experiments show that visual, tactile, proprioceptive, spatial, and temporal evidence can alter ownership and location. They also show that proprioceptive drift, ownership reports, and agency should not be used as interchangeable measures.

## The body participates in perception

Movement changes the evidence available to every sense. Reaching changes visual and proprioceptive relations. Walking changes optic flow, vestibular input, load, and body position. Breathing and cardiac cycles change internal signals and neural state. Action therefore does not occur only after perception; it helps generate the next perceptual sample.

This gives embodiment a concrete control structure:

1. the organism maintains a current body-world state;
2. sensory and internal changes update that state;
3. goals and predicted consequences constrain possible actions;
4. action changes the body or environment;
5. reafferent and exafferent consequences update the next state.

Different nervous-system and bodily loops operate at different speeds. There is no requirement that one executive location own the entire cycle.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats the self as a distributed motor-sensory rendering produced by the participating network and body. "Rendering" here means a revisable, action-usable state, not a picture shown to an inner witness.

The positive observer mechanism is:

- cells and receptors register consequential differences;
- local and distributed circuits transform those differences;
- tonic activity supplies each receiver's current comparison context;
- phasic events alter that context and are re-expressed to other receivers;
- body state and action consequences constrain the next cycle;
- the aggregate operation produces observer-action.

The system itself is changed by what it senses. That participation, rather than a hidden viewer, is the proposed basis of observation.

## Ownership, agency, and statistical matching

SAN's statistical-matching idea is useful when stated as multisensory causal inference rather than a single binding event. A candidate body part becomes more likely to be treated as mine when visual, tactile, proprioceptive, temporal, anatomical, and motor evidence support a common cause.

That does not make ownership, location, and agency identical. A SAN model should represent them as related outputs or state variables with partly distinct evidence and failure modes. It should predict when they agree and when they separate.

## The body as a chemical network

Neural, endocrine, immune, metabolic, cardiovascular, respiratory, and visceral processes interact. It is therefore reasonable to treat cognition as physiologically embodied and multi-timescale.

The stronger phrase "the body is a chemical neural network" remains a SAN substrate hypothesis. Chemical signaling outside the nervous system is not automatically neural computation, and shared feedback form does not prove identical representation, code, or observer function. Each proposed continuity must name its cells, signal, receiver, timescale, and causal consequence.

## Scale map

- **Micro:** receptors, synapses, cells, neuromodulators, and local physiological state.
- **Meso:** spinal, brainstem, cerebellar, thalamic, cortical, autonomic, and organ-level loops.
- **Macro:** the whole organism acting within an environment and maintaining a distributed body-world model.

Embodiment is strongest when these scales are connected explicitly rather than compressed into one body-as-brain metaphor.

## Implications for SAN Book 2

The body-world control problem should precede the largest consciousness claim. This creates a defensible narrative ladder:

1. bodies must regulate and move;
2. movement requires state estimation and prediction;
3. sensing is altered by action;
4. distributed systems maintain an action-ready body-world model;
5. SAN interprets the participating field-cell organization as observer-action.

The first four steps can be evaluated using established sensorimotor evidence. The fifth is the SAN constitutive hypothesis and requires separate tests.

## Testable questions

SAN should predict, for a named task:

- which body variable is being estimated;
- which sensory and motor signals should be combined or kept separate;
- which circuit and receiver should carry the consequential update;
- how tonic context changes the effect of the same phasic event;
- whether perturbation changes ownership, location, agency, action, or several independently;
- what result would distinguish the SAN interpretation from standard multisensory inference and control models.

## Primary evidence anchors

- Botvinick and Cohen demonstrated that synchronized visual and tactile stimulation can alter felt ownership and proprioceptive localization: [Rubber hands feel touch that eyes see](https://doi.org/10.1038/35784).
- Ehrsson, Spence, and Passingham associated illusory hand ownership with premotor activity: [That's my hand! Activity in premotor cortex reflects feeling of ownership of a limb](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1097011).
- Samad, Chung, and Shams tested a Bayesian causal-inference model of body ownership: [Perception of body ownership is driven by Bayesian sensory inference](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0117178).
- Rohde, Di Luca, and Ernst demonstrated that proprioceptive drift and ownership need not move together: [The rubber hand illusion: feeling of ownership and proprioceptive drift do not go hand in hand](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0021659).
- Imaizumi and colleagues experimentally dissociated agency and body ownership after visuomotor temporal recalibration: [Dissociation of agency and body ownership](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnint.2015.00035).
