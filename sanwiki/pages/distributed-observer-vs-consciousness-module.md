This page compares two explanatory emphases: a model that assigns a privileged role to a bounded
awareness-modeling process and SAN's proposal that observer-action is performed by sparse,
distributed, recurrent brain-body activity. The comparison must not turn either account into a
straw man.

## What Attention Schema Theory actually proposes

Attention Schema Theory (AST) proposes that the brain constructs a simplified internal model of
attention and uses that model to help control attention. The content of that model contributes to
claims and judgments of awareness. AST is not adequately summarized as "the TPJ is a consciousness
module." Temporoparietal systems enter parts of the theory through attention, body modeling, and
social cognition, but the theory describes interacting processes rather than a single anatomical
box that manufactures experience.

- [Webb and Graziano (2015), the Attention Schema Theory](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00500)

## The SAN alternative

SAN proposes that no separate module receives a finished representation and then becomes the
observer. Instead:

1. many cellular and circuit receivers register partial differences;
2. learned structure and current state determine which differences matter;
3. recurrent neural and body loops maintain and revise a working body-world model;
4. the same distributed process produces recognition, prediction, report, autonomic response, and action;
5. those consequences change the next state of the system.

The observer is this aggregate receive-transform-re-express-update operation. It is sparse because
only a task- and state-dependent subset is effective at a given time. It is distributed because no
single cell, column, hub, or cortical area contains the complete body-world relation.

## A bounded comparison

| Question | Attention Schema emphasis | SAN emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| What is modeled? | The process and state of attention | Body, world, internal state, possible action, and the system's changing relation to them |
| Why model it? | Improve control of attention and support awareness judgments | Coordinate perception, consideration, autonomic regulation, action, and feedback |
| Where is the observer? | Not reducible to one TPJ box; implemented by interacting modeling and control processes | No separate observer location; observer-action is the distributed operation itself |
| Main unresolved step | Whether an attention schema explains phenomenal experience rather than reports and control | Whether tonic field-cell registration is constitutive of observation or consciousness |

These accounts can overlap on learned self-modeling and control while differing on what work a
model of attention can explain.

## Anatomical firewall

- The **TPJ** participates in heterogeneous attention, multisensory, body, and social processes. It
  should not be drawn as the universal peak of consciousness.
- **Layer 5 and Layer 6 pyramidal neurons** have diverse projection patterns. Their long-range
  outputs do not by themselves define the default-mode network or a consciousness hierarchy.
- **Rich-club organization** is a graph property of highly connected nodes, not a dedicated observer organ.
- The **corpus callosum** supports interhemispheric communication, but callosal coupling is neither
  necessary nor sufficient evidence for one unified observer.
- A cortical laminar motif may be useful as a canonical example, but cortical areas, cell types,
  subcortical nuclei, cerebellar loops, brainstem systems, and bodily feedback are not one universal column circuit.

## Self-model learning without a special spectator

SAN treats self-related models as learned relations among vision, touch, proprioception,
interoception, action, memory, and social feedback. Learning these relations does not require a
separate consciousness module. It also does not show that every oscillator, neuron, or local array
is individually conscious. A unit contributes to observer-action only insofar as its state is
received, transformed, and used in the larger loop under declared criteria.

## What would discriminate the accounts

A useful comparison should manipulate a defined attention-model variable while separately
measuring sensory representation, attentional control, confidence, report, body ownership, action,
and no-report physiological outcomes. SAN additionally predicts that disrupting the relevant
distributed receive-transform-update relation should impair observer-action even when no single
awareness module is removed. Neither prediction is supported merely by observing activation in a
hub or by decoding a report.
