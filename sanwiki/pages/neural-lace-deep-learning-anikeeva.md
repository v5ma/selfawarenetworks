May 2018 SVGN article profiling [[polina-anikeeva|Polina Anikeeva]] (MIT) on the hardware gap in brain-machine interfaces: AI advances rapidly while neural recording/stimulation hardware lags. Anikeeva's approach: magnetic nanoparticles (20,000× smaller than coffee grounds) injected via IV that receive external magnetic fields and convert them to local neural stimulation.

## Core mechanism

Ion concentration controls neural firing. Anikeeva's lab targets ion channels directly — nanoparticles transduce externally-applied magnetic fields into localized ionic current, providing wireless, cell-scale stimulation without implanted electrodes. This is a minimal-invasiveness path to the [[neural-lace|neural lace]] concept.

The central interface problem she frames: "how do you connect soft biological systems and electronics in a way that is meaningful and organic?" — the mismatch between rigid electronics and compliant tissue is the physical root of electrode drift, scarring, and signal loss. Her answer is to eliminate the rigid probe entirely.
