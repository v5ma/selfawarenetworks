The observation that stimulants like Adderall cause an initial performance boost followed by a
motivational crash — the "zombie" effect — because they accelerate [[dopamine]] release faster
than the brain can replenish it.

## Core observation

Adderall forces a rapid expenditure of available [[dopamine]]. The initial phase yields heightened
focus and energy; once the reserve is depleted, the user experiences lethargy and reduced
motivation — the opposite of the intended effect. This relates to the brain's finite
[[neurotransmitter]] budget and the cost of [[forced-attention]].

## Countermeasure: stimulation restores dopamine

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s 2012 note proposed that giving the brain high-bandwidth sensory stimulation — physical
exercise, [[brainwave-entrainment|brainwave entrainment]], audiovisual stimulation — could prompt additional [[dopamine]]
synthesis and release, partially counteracting the depletion phase. This aligns with later SAN
thinking about [[gravity-of-attention]]: the brain's reward signal is tied to novel, high-salience
input, and varied stimulation raises the dopamine floor.

## Relation to SAN

Within [[self-aware-network]] theory, [[dopamine]] is not just a "pleasure chemical" but a
precision-weighting signal that modulates which [[neural-oscillations|neural oscillations]] dominate at any moment. A
depleted dopamine pool means fewer high-confidence predictions win the competition for cortical
representation — manifesting as cognitive flatness and loss of goal-directed behavior.
