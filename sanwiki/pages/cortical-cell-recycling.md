**Cortical cell recycling** is the phenomenon in which cortical cell types serve multiple distinct functions during brain development and are then *reused* — "recycled" — to perform different functions in the adult cortex.

During development, cortical circuits are not constructed by a single deterministic programme. Instead, they pass through multiple phases shaped by [[evolutionary-mechanisms]], activity-driven changes in developmental programmes, epigenetic mechanisms, and environmental feedback. Individual cell types take on different roles at each phase.

The implication is that the same cell can occupy different functional roles depending on context — a property analogous to [[self-aware-network]] nodes that reconfigure their learned responses based on incoming signals.

## Category Theory and Multi-Level Cell Functions

[[category-theory]] is proposed as an appropriate framework for mapping the multi-level communication patterns of cells (and atoms, brain activity, fluid mechanics) because biological nodes cannot be fully inspected: we need abstraction rules that capture relationships between invisible functions.

The key move: **cellular communication is Turing complete**. Chemical message-passing can simulate any kind of information, just as nodes in a [[graph-neural-network]] communicate. A cell, through evolutionary trial and error, accumulates learned sequences of functions it executes in response to environmental signals — the same logic as a trained artificial neuron.

This allows a three-level abstraction ladder:
- Ordinary cell → abstracted as a neuron
- Neuron → abstracted as an artificial neuron
- Multi-cell network → abstracted as a graph neural network

## Relevance to SAN

In [[napot]] and related SAN frameworks, [[neural-array-projection]] depends on neurons reading each other's firing patterns as "rendered" representations. Cortical cell recycling grounds this — the same physical substrate serves representational functions at multiple timescales, consistent with SAN's claim that the brain is a [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modelling system rather than a fixed-function machine.
