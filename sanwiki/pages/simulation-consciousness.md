# Simulation and Consciousness: Internal Rendering, Artificial Implementation, and Evidence Gates

**Simulation and consciousness** names two related but different questions in Self-Aware
Networks (SAN):

1. How does a living brain construct and continually update the experienced model of body and
   world?
2. Could a nonbiological implementation reproduce the causally relevant process closely enough
   to become a candidate for consciousness?

SAN answers the first question with a physical, distributed rendering proposal. It does not place
a screen or a small viewer inside the brain. Tonic population activity supplies maintained
context; phasic and [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] events supply
receiver-relative changes; cellular receivers, transmitters, recurrent routes, plasticity,
action, and returned sensory consequences collectively perform the observer-action process.

SAN treats the second question as an engineering and scientific hypothesis. A computer can
simulate declared neural equations and circuit dynamics. That fact alone does not establish that
the simulation is biologically equivalent or phenomenally conscious.

## The source-faithful SAN proposition

The strongest bounded formulation is:

```text
measured or hypothesized biological state
-> explicit computational variables and update rules
-> receive, transform, and project operations
-> recurrent integration and redistribution
-> tuning, memory, and route change
-> embodied action and reafferent consequence
-> renewed internal rendering
```

SAN proposes that this joined causal organization may be implementable in another physical
substrate. The word *may* matters. The proposal remains open until experiments identify which
cellular, synaptic, field, chemical, morphological, and population variables are causally
necessary and show that an artificial implementation preserves their relevant effects.

## Three claims that must not be collapsed

| Claim | What evidence can establish it | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Model equivalence** | A simulation reproduces declared measurements within stated error bounds. | That it reproduces unmodeled biology, implements the same causal process, or experiences anything. |
| **Causal or implementation equivalence** | Matched perturbations, ablations, delays, and rescues produce the predicted internal and behavioral consequences. | That functional or causal similarity is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness. |
| **Phenomenal identity** | No generally accepted experiment currently proves that an artificial implementation has first-person experience. Theory-derived indicators can support or weaken a case. | A definitive positive or negative verdict from fluent behavior, complexity, synchrony, self-description, or SAN conformance alone. |

This distinction prevents two opposite errors. It does not dismiss artificial consciousness merely
because its substrate is unfamiliar, and it does not declare a system conscious merely because it
uses neural vocabulary or reproduces selected outputs.

## What simulation is already able to do

Established computational neuroscience shows that biological mechanisms can be represented at
several levels:

- Hodgkin and Huxley expressed bounded membrane-current dynamics as quantitative equations.
- Reconstructed microcircuit models can combine anatomical and physiological constraints and
  reproduce selected circuit behavior.
- Large data-driven visual-cortex models can compare point-neuron and biophysical descriptions
  against experimental recordings.
- Closed-loop agents can use recurrent internal state to predict, choose, act, and update.

These achievements validate modeling as a method. They do not imply that a model contains every
causally relevant feature of its biological target. A point-neuron model and a compartmental model
may match one firing-rate statistic while differing sharply in dendritic, waveform, metabolic,
field, or plastic consequences.

## SAN's internal-rendering claim

Within SAN, *rendering* is not only a convenient analogy to computer graphics. It is a proposed
operation performed by living tissue:

```text
maintained tonic context
-> sparse excitatory or inhibitory difference
-> receiver-relative phase, timing, waveform, or release consequence
-> distributed recurrent detection
-> changed model, readiness, memory, or action
```

The resulting percept is not stored at one coordinate and then watched. The percept exists, in
the SAN hypothesis, as the sparse and distributed organization of differences and responses across
the network. The tonic action field is the maintained receiver context or canvas; phasic activity
is the changing ink; and the same network that carries the rendering also uses it to select action.
See [[brain-as-rendering-engine|Brain as Rendering Engine]], [[neural-rendering|Neural
Rendering]], and [[entification-observer-problem|Entification and the Distributed Observer]].

This is stronger than the established claim that perception is constructive and recurrent. The
specific tonic-canvas, PWD, distributed-observer, and NAPOT account remains a SAN hypothesis that
requires discriminating tests.

## What must cross from biology into an implementation

A serious artificial SAN experiment should declare which members of this set it implements:

1. **Receiver state:** local context that changes how the same arriving event is interpreted.
2. **Typed difference:** timing, phase, duration, waveform, rate, release, or other dimensions with
   explicit units and reference states.
3. **Projection:** a defined transformation from a source population into named receiver states.
4. **Recurrence:** later activity changes earlier or parallel processing through specified routes.
5. **Tuning and plasticity:** recent history changes later gain, timing, routing, or connection
   strength.
6. **Distributed rendering:** no inner viewer; the maintained and changing network state is used
   by the system itself.
7. **Embodiment:** sensors, internal condition, effectors, action, and returned consequence close
   the loop.
8. **Cross-scale constraint:** cellular, local-circuit, and population claims are not treated as
   interchangeable merely because they share a frequency label.

An implementation may discover that some biological details can be replaced while preserving the
relevant operation. It may instead discover that ionic, chemical, field, metabolic, glial, or
morphological variables are necessary. Substrate independence is therefore a testable scope claim,
not a premise that allows biology to be discarded in advance. See
[[substrate-independence-neural-computation|Substrate Independence of Neural Computation]].

## Consciousness evidence does not transfer automatically

Clinical and experimental consciousness measures are calibrated on biological systems under
specific conditions. For example, perturbational complexity has distinguished several conscious
and unconscious conditions in humans by measuring distributed and differentiated cortical
responses after perturbation. That does not mean the same numerical score, computed from arbitrary
software activity, has the same interpretation.

Consciousness science also remains theoretically unsettled. A large preregistered adversarial test
reported results that supported some predictions while challenging central predictions of both
integrated information theory and global neuronal workspace theory. Recent machine-consciousness
work therefore recommends converging, theory-derived indicators rather than one decisive metric.

For SAN, an artificial-consciousness assessment should include:

- functional performance without treating performance as experience;
- causal tests of the proposed SAN operations;
- indicators drawn from several consciousness theories, including evidence against each;
- developmental and embodiment history;
- robustness to deceptive or prompted self-report;
- uncertainty about unmeasured biological variables; and
- welfare and governance precautions proportional to the evidence and stakes.

## A decisive SAN experiment

Build three capacity-matched embodied systems:

1. a strong recurrent world-model baseline;
2. a SAN-structured model with declared receiver state, typed differences, projection, recurrent
   redistribution, tuning, action, and reafference; and
3. ablated SAN variants in which each proposed operation is removed or replaced.

Evaluate them on multimodal reconstruction, delayed perturbation, novelty, self-state estimation,
action-conditioned prediction, recovery after route disruption, and transfer to new environments.
Record internal variables, not only final behavior.

The SAN mechanism gains support only if its named operations make preregistered, reproducible,
causal contributions beyond matched alternatives. If the same results survive every SAN-specific
ablation, the claimed mechanism is not supported. Even a positive mechanism result would establish
an implementation result, not by itself phenomenal consciousness.
