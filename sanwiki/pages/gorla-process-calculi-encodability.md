## Position

Gorla Process Calculi Encodability is the formal yardstick this *precise sense* would have to meet to be defensible. Gorla's five [[criteria]] (compositionality, name invariance, operational correspondence, divergence reflection, success sensitiveness) supply the conditions under which one process calculus can be said to *faithfully encode* another; SAN imports these as the conditions under which one biological-substrate encoding can be said to faithfully encode another. ## Post-SAN

Mainstream concurrency-theory literature treats Gorla's [[criteria]] as the de-facto standard for separation-and-encodability results in process algebra. Adjacent biological-modeling work using Petri nets, stochastic pi-calculus (Cardelli, Priami), and category-theoretic systems biology shares the formal-equivalence concern that SAN's invocation of Gorla addresses. SAN's distinct move is to import the encodability framework into a *cross-substrate* setting — using it not (only) to compare process calculi to one another but to compare biological substrates (cells, neurons, ensembles) against process-calculus models of them. Where SAN makes a methodological commitment that adjacent literature does not is in requiring that any [[fractal-functional-isomorphism|cross-scale isomorphism claim]] meet operational-correspondence and divergence-reflection [[criteria]], not only structural ones — a commitment that bears directly on which claims about [[every-cell-chemical-neural-network|all-cell computation]] can be defended formally.
