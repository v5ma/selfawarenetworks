# Astrocytes

![Astrocytes tripartite synapse endfeet calcium syncytium diagram](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/astrocytes/astrocytes-tripartite-endfeet-calcium-syncytium-20260629-v1.png)

*Figure status: conceptual SAN infographic, not an anatomical atlas. It places several documented astrocyte operations in one teaching image. Individual astrocytes, brain regions, and preparations need not perform every pictured operation at once, and the SAN label "slow tonic control" remains a hypothesis to test rather than an established cellular identity.*

**Astrocytes** are a major class of central-nervous-system [[glial-cells|glial cell]]. Their fine processes contact synapses, blood vessels, other glia, and the extracellular space. Depending on region and state, astrocytes help regulate potassium, remove neurotransmitters, supply metabolic substrates, influence blood flow, and alter synaptic transmission and plasticity. They communicate through local voltage, calcium, metabolic, molecular, and gap-junction-coupled changes rather than by behaving as ordinary spiking neurons.

Astrocytes do not "vastly outnumber" neurons throughout the human brain. Human glial and neuronal totals are of the same broad order, and the fraction represented by astrocytes varies by region and measurement method. The scientifically useful point is functional diversity, not an inflated cell-count claim.

## Local cellular operation

At many synapses, a fine **perisynaptic astrocyte process** approaches part of the presynaptic terminal and postsynaptic membrane. This arrangement motivates the term **tripartite synapse**, although astrocytic coverage is incomplete and differs across synapses and brain regions.

A bounded causal chain is:

```text
local transmitter release and extracellular potassium change
-> a nearby astrocyte process detects local chemical and electrical conditions
-> astrocyte voltage, calcium, transporter, or metabolic state changes
-> transmitter clearance, ionic balance, synaptic coverage, or plasticity conditions change
-> the next neuronal event occurs in a modified local environment
```

Experiments support individual links in this chain. Local astrocyte calcium responses can detect sparse hippocampal synaptic activity; astroglial potassium clearance can contribute to short-term plasticity; and neuronal activity can cause rapid, focal depolarization of peripheral astrocyte processes that changes glutamate uptake. These results do not establish one universal astrocyte algorithm or a brain-wide calcium-wave controller.

## Astrocyte networks and timescales

Astrocytes can be coupled through connexin gap junctions, forming spatially extended networks for ions and small molecules. Calcium events can remain highly local or spread more broadly, and their timing ranges from subcellular transients to slower state changes. The word **syncytium** describes coupling; it should not be read as proof that the network computes a single global average or maintains one uniform voltage.

Astrocytes also participate in neurovascular and metabolic coupling through endfeet near blood vessels. This supports the energetic and homeostatic conditions in which neuronal computation occurs. It does not by itself show that an astrocyte field is the content of consciousness.

## Place in Self-Aware Networks

SAN's glial argument predates the recent astrocyte-ensemble literature. In the May 5, 2017 [[neural-lace-podcast-source-genealogy|Neural Lace Podcast #4]], Micah Blumberg argued that a useful brain snapshot would need astrocytes as well as neurons and described a neural network and a "glionet" interfacing with one another. The transcript was publicly committed in 2022. In June-August 2022, SAN sources proposed that phase-coincidence relations could influence growth and removal of neuronal and glial connections and explicitly placed astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, and other cells in the COT-to-[[napot-theory|NAPOT]] architecture.

The medically aligned SAN interpretation is not that astrocytes are neurons or that they carry canonical neuronal action potentials. It is that a changing brain representation may depend on **multicellular state**: neurons provide rapid, spatially specific electrical and synaptic events, while astrocytes can alter the slower local conditions under which those events are received, transmitted, stabilized, or recalled. SAN asks whether those glial conditions contribute to the [[tonic-phasic-canvas-of-consciousness|tonic context]] against which [[phase-wave-differential|phase-wave differentials]] have receiver-relative effects.

That final step is a SAN hypothesis. It requires direct comparison against neuronal rate, calcium, arousal, cell identity, and general network-state explanations.
