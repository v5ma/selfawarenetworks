# CaMKII, Microtubules, and LTP

![Medically corrected CaMKII-microtubule LTP plate separating local CaMKII-actin plasticity, dynamic microtubule entry and cargo transport, local translation, nuclear signaling, and the unproven SAN cytoskeletal-encoding hypothesis](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/camkii-microtubule-ltp/camkii-microtubule-ltp-coordinated-plasticity-medically-corrected-20260715-v2.png)

This plate separates measured plasticity mechanisms from the proposed idea that a tubulin lattice could carry an additional durable code. Synaptic calcium, CaMKII, receptor trafficking, actin remodeling, local translation, cytoskeletal transport, and synapto-nuclear signaling are related processes, but they are not one linear write-to-tubulin pathway.

## Established plasticity mechanisms

In many experimentally studied forms of excitatory plasticity, NMDA-receptor-dependent calcium entry binds calmodulin and activates CaMKII near the postsynaptic density. CaMKII can alter receptor function and trafficking and interact with actin-regulatory processes. Actin is the dominant structural cytoskeleton inside mature dendritic spines.

Microtubules are prominent in the dendritic shaft. Dynamic plus ends can transiently enter a subset of spines and influence spine morphology and postsynaptic organization ([Jaworski et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2008.11.013); [Hu et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2445-11.2011)). This supports coordination between microtubules and actin during plasticity. It does not show that CaMKII writes a firing-frequency code into tubulin.

Protein synthesis also needs a scale boundary. Dendritic ribosomes can support local translation, while separate signaling pathways can communicate synaptic activity to the nucleus and alter transcription. A newly synthesized synaptic protein does not ordinarily need to travel to the nucleus to become a memory trace.

Early and late forms of LTP also differ across preparation and protocol. It is too broad to say that all LTP requires new protein synthesis or that one molecular pathway explains all durable memory.

## Behavioral-timescale plasticity

Behavioral-timescale synaptic plasticity (BTSP) extends the relevant temporal window beyond a simple millisecond coincidence rule. In CA1 neurons, Jain and colleagues detected delayed, stochastic dendritic CaMKII activation associated with calcium events 10-100 seconds after BTSP induction. Blocking CaMKII during a later 15-30 second window impaired potentiation ([Nature, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08021-8)).

That result shows that CaMKII can participate in a delayed dendritic process. It does not show a tubulin memory code, a universal six-bit molecular symbol, or an intracellular copy of a complete place field.

## The tubulin-lattice proposal

Craddock, Tuszynski, and Hameroff proposed that CaMKII phosphorylation patterns on microtubule lattices could provide a molecular memory mechanism ([PLoS Computational Biology, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002421)). Micah's "six-bit hammer" metaphor belongs to this hypothesis family: patterned synaptic activity might leave a structured molecular consequence rather than changing only a scalar synaptic weight.

This is a useful mechanistic question, but it remains unestablished. A convincing demonstration would need to show:

1. activity-dependent, reproducible phosphorylation or structural states at identified tubulin sites;
2. persistence over a declared memory interval;
3. more information than expected from general cytoskeletal turnover or cell state;
4. selective readout by a known molecular or cellular receiver;
5. loss or alteration of a declared learned variable after targeted disruption;
6. restoration by writing the relevant state back;
7. prediction beyond conventional synaptic, actin, transport, and transcription models.

A correlation between CaMKII activity and microtubule dynamics would not by itself satisfy that chain.

## SAN interpretation

The strongest current SAN bridge is **plasticity-conditioned cellular readiness**. CaMKII, actin, receptor trafficking, local translation, microtubule transport, and nuclear signaling can jointly change how a neuron responds later. A tubulin code is one possible additional mechanism, not a prerequisite for NAPOT or for the distributed observer-action account.

The cell does not become a hidden viewer because it contains a persistent molecular state. Molecular changes alter later cellular transformations; populations and recurrent routes use those altered transformations in perception, memory, and action.
