**Neural rendering of qualia** is SAN's strongest interpretation of the distributed working model:
the relevant field-cell activity does not merely support reports about color, sound, pain, taste,
body, or emotion but helps constitute what those experiences are like. This is an identity
hypothesis, not an established medical fact.

## Four claims that must remain separate

1. **Representation:** neural state covaries with a stimulus, body variable, or internal content.
2. **Decoding:** a measurement pipeline can recover some content from that state.
3. **Causal use:** a biological receiver uses the state to change perception, control, report, or action.
4. **Phenomenal identity:** the state or process constitutes the experience itself.

Evidence for one claim does not automatically establish the next. A recognizable reconstruction is
not a native neural picture, and causal contribution to report is not by itself a complete theory of
qualia.

## SAN proposal

SAN proposes that receiver-relative differences alter an ongoing, distributed field-cell state and
that the same embodied system carries those changes into memory, prediction, autonomic regulation,
and action. The observer is this aggregate operation. The further claim that its intrinsic
organization is redness, pain, taste, or another quality requires discriminating tests and a clear
identity criterion.

## Whisker testbed

`a0296zWhisker.md` proposes that active touch supplies a concrete route from changing internal
state and sensory input to distributed tactile experience. The cited physiology supports
state-dependent synchrony, fast dendritic integration, reciprocal whisker sensory-motor loops, and
closely timed S1/S2 responses. It does not show that desynchronization is tactile qualia, that tonic
activity absorbs a phase-wave differential, or that the whisker system performs tomography.

The SAN identity claim becomes testable only if a declared coherence/dispersion or receiver-relative
state predicts and causally changes tactile discrimination after movement, membrane state, firing
rate, power, connectivity, and ordinary sensorimotor models are controlled.

## Choice boundary

Competition among neural populations can influence action before reportable awareness, and planning
can constrain later choices. It does not follow that one dominant phase rhythm is the choice, that
all decisions have two fixed preconscious stages, or that high-bandwidth activity defines
phenomenological consciousness. Choice, confidence, report, motor preparation, and experience must
be measured separately.

## Test direction

A strong test should compare content-specific field-cell geometry with matched rate, power,
waveform, movement, arousal, and recurrent-state alternatives; perturb the proposed receiver or
relation; predict a selective change in experience and behavior; and test recovery or rescue.
