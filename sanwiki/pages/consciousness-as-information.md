Information language enters consciousness research in several different ways. A neural response can carry information about a stimulus for an external decoder; a system can use a distinction in control; a theory can define an intrinsic causal organization; or an author can make a phenomenal identity claim. These meanings must not be collapsed.

## Represent IIT fairly

Integrated Information Theory (IIT) does not merely say that any information integration produces consciousness, and Phi is not a measure of picture resolution. IIT proposes that consciousness corresponds to an intrinsic, maximally irreducible cause-effect structure. Its mathematical core and proposed posterior cortical implementation are specific and controversial claims.

SAN may reject IIT's axioms, measures, or biological implementation, but it should compare the actual causal architecture rather than a generic information-processing position.

## The SAN position

SAN emphasizes implemented biological relations: receptors, learned cellular structure, dendritic and synaptic transformations, recurrent populations, body state, memory, and action. Oscillation can modulate those relations, but an oscillatory substrate is not sufficient by itself. NAPOT is a candidate reconstruction account, not a demonstrated readout that converts information into experience.

The strongest SAN distinction is between information available to an investigator and information causally available to the organism. A classifier may decode content that no downstream biological receiver uses. Conversely, an organism may use a state that current measurements cannot decode well.

## Evidence ladder

For each claim, state whether evidence supports:

1. statistical dependence between neural activity and a variable;
2. external decoding;
3. receiver-specific biological use;
4. integration across a declared system boundary; or
5. phenomenal identity.

Moving up the ladder requires additional evidence. Neither high decoding accuracy nor high integration proves experience.

## Current comparative boundary

The 2025 COGITATE adversarial collaboration tested preregistered biological predictions of IIT and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory. Results aligned with some predictions while substantially challenging key claims of both, including sustained posterior synchronization predicted for IIT and aspects of prefrontal ignition predicted for GNWT. The study supports discriminating tests, not a simple victory for information, workspace, oscillation, or SAN ([Nature, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1)).
