The "[[gh-a0206z|self]]" is not a persistent, bounded entity — it is a vortex-like cycling of brainwave activity, harmonized from component parts. This early SAN [[gh-formulation|formulation]] treats personal identity as an oscillatory construct rather than a stable subject.

## Core claim

In a 2015 exchange, [[gh-resnote04|Micah Blumberg]] characterizes the [[gh-a0206z|self]] as "a sort of illusion functioning as a vortex-like cycling of brainwave activity from component parts harmonized." The [[gh-a0206z|self]] has no fixed substrate; it is a pattern sustained by the ongoing oscillatory synchrony of distributed neural assemblies. When the synchrony breaks - through sleep, anesthesia, psychedelics, or death - the construct dissolves.

This is the philosophical corollary of [[napot]]: if the oscillating group of cells is the observer, and the observer is the [[gh-a0206z|self]], then the [[gh-a0206z|self]] *is* the oscillatory pattern — nothing more and nothing less.

## Implications for identity modification

If selfhood is a cycling pattern rather than a fixed entity, then brain modification technologies (neurofeedback, psychedelics, BCI) can alter the cycling pattern — and thereby alter personality and identity. The question "is it still the same person?" becomes a question about pattern continuity, not substrate continuity.

This reframing also complicates mind uploading: uploading a brain state would capture the pattern at a moment, but the [[gh-a0206z|self]] is the *cycling* — the ongoing vortex — not the snapshot. Whether a re-instantiated pattern resumes the same vortex or begins a new one is an open question in SAN philosophy.

## Synchronization analogy

The discussion references metronome synchronization — isolated metronomes placed on a shared surface spontaneously phase-lock through mechanical feedback. This is offered as a physical analogue: the "[[gh-a0206z|self]]" emerges from mutual entrainment of neural oscillators, not from any single oscillator. See [[brainwave-vortex-attractor]] for the formal SAN description.
