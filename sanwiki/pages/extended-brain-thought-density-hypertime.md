# Extended Brain and Thought-Density Hypertime

## The idea in plain language

A calculator, search engine, or AI assistant can help a person without becoming part of that
person's continuing cognitive process. SAN's **Extended Brain** proposal sets a higher bar: the
human and artificial system must repeatedly sense, predict, correct, and adapt to one another
during a task.

The associated **thought-density hypertime** hypothesis asks whether such coupling could increase
the number of useful, distinguishable cognitive events that the person can access, use, and later
remember during the same amount of clock time.

```text
reciprocal coupling
  -> more useful human-integrated cognitive events
  -> finer subjective temporal resolution
  -> possibly more experienced duration per clock interval
```

This is not physical time dilation, biological life extension, or a claim that machine operations
automatically become human thoughts.

## What counts as an Extended Brain?

The system needs more than speed or convenience:

- the human state changes the artificial state;
- the artificial state changes later human inference or action;
- both effects survive controls for common input and shared workspace;
- the person can inspect, reject, or reverse the coupling;
- resulting information is accessible enough to guide behavior and memory; and
- the architecture preserves consent, agency, and ownership boundaries.

## The October 2021 scenario ladder

The `b0140y` source adds a useful architecture without proving that the architecture exists:

```text
added cortex-like workspace connected to a living brain
-> collective substrate joining formerly embodied participants
-> conjectured copying, recovery, and virtual continuation
-> technology later internalized into re-embodied people
```

The problem comes before the term. A machine becomes a candidate cognitive extension only when it
does more than provide useful output: the person's state must change the machine, the machine must
change later human inference or action, and both must adapt through repeated feedback.

The source also says that a brain-generated model is not the external world itself. Predictive
coding provides an established example of recurrent internal-model computation, while cognitive
offloading, closed-loop decoder adaptation, bidirectional BCI, and limited brain-to-brain
interfaces establish separate component operations. These comparisons stop before merged
consciousness, whole-person backup, identity continuity, or observer-created physical reality.

The exact 2021 source does **not** state the later autonomous-artificial-brain distinction or the
program's consent, ownership, access, and disconnection requirements. Those remain important
results of the wider Neo Mind Cycle and Artificial Neurology genealogy, not retroactively imported
transcript atoms.

The stronger SAN hypothesis is testable: measure reciprocal causal contribution, human
accessibility, retained consequence, agency, and recovery through connection, disconnection, and
re-embodiment. A collective process should be called a new joint agent only if it predicts
outcomes beyond the participants and their shared input.

The source's observer-centered cosmology and theology remain preserved as tentative later-physics
and metaphysical genealogy. They are not BCI or neuroscience evidence.

## How to test it

Compare reciprocal adaptive coupling with one-way assistance, yoked replay, and human-only task
performance. Measure bidirectional influence, accuracy, novelty, retained human recall,
calibration, cognitive load, temporal judgment, and post-session agency.

The hypothesis fails if common input explains the apparent coupling, if gains remain outside the
person's accessible cognition, or if subjective duration changes do not depend on reciprocal
coupling.

### Artificial developmental acceleration is a separate branch

A May 2021 Artificial Neurology source asks whether an artificial learner could undergo many more
training episodes per wall-clock interval before interacting at human speed. That proposal should
not be collapsed into human Extended Brain hypertime.

- accelerated computation means more updates per wall-clock interval;
- accelerated development requires durable, generalizable learning; and
- subjective hypertime requires separately justified evidence of temporally structured experience.

More training does not guarantee wisdom, kindness, consent, or personal continuity. If credible
machine-experience evidence later emerges, forced acceleration becomes a possible welfare exposure
rather than a shortcut to maturity.
