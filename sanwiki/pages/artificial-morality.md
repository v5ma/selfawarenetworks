# Artificial Morality

**Artificial morality** can mean several different things: learning preferences, following
constraints, representing stakeholder interests, judging intentions and consequences, explaining
choices, or asking whether an artificial system itself has moral status. These problems interact,
but they must not be collapsed into one scalar called "good."

The source-faithful SAN proposal is narrower and more specific. An embodied agent may learn an
internal map that relates its condition and goals to predicted consequences in the world. That map
can organize approach, avoidance, cooperation, inhibition, and later learning. It is a candidate
mechanism for **agent-relative valuation** and a possible input to moral judgment. It is not an
objective moral law.

## The Recovered May 2024 Proposal

The page originated in a [May 27, 2024 exchange](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/dcbe9b8f47f0e1036e47741c77e0bd658a1ac8d1/cnote%20artificialmorality.md)
between Logan Thrasher Collins and Micah Blumberg.

Collins proposed that objective morality might be a continuously varying manifold corresponding to
positive and negative qualia across spacetime, conditional on physicalism and panpsychism. Micah's
reply changed the scope. He proposed that a biological or artificial agent could form a measurable,
dynamic information pattern that maps felt or functional good and bad onto detected environmental
conditions and the agent's own goals.

Micah used Michael Levin and colleagues' idea of **agential matter**: living cells and collectives
can pursue goals, solve problems, and reach similar outcomes by more than one path
([Dade-Robertson, Levin, and Davies, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/btd.2023.1)). The historical SAN
note called this "Agent-al Material." The scientifically grounded term is *agential matter* or a
*goal-directed system*. The concept does not by itself establish consciousness, morality, or
rights.

The examples of extreme heat and cold were boundary conditions: a state that threatens an
organism's integrity can acquire strongly negative value, and agents facing a common threat may
cooperate to avoid it. Calling such a condition "evil" was moral language applied to an
agent-relative threat, not a medical claim that temperature is intrinsically evil.

The recovered operator is:

**internal and body state + learned goals + sensed situation + predicted consequences + social and
developmental history -> distributed valuation and moral-model state -> action selection,
inhibition, or cooperation -> experienced and environmental consequences -> model updating**

## Reward, Valence, Morality, and Welfare Are Different

| Term | Bounded meaning |
|---|---|
| Reward | A training or control signal |
| Action value | Estimated return under a model or policy |
| Homeostatic or allostatic value | Relevance to maintaining or anticipating bodily needs |
| Affective valence | Experienced or behaviorally expressed pleasantness and unpleasantness |
| Learned preference | A model of judgments supplied by people or inferred from behavior |
| Moral judgment | Evaluation involving norms, intentions, harms, rights, duties, relationships, or consequences |
| Welfare | A subject's possible positive or negative experience or condition |
| Moral status | A normative judgment about whose interests deserve consideration |

Optimizing reward does not establish that a system values the outcome in a human sense. Protecting
an operating range does not establish feeling, morality, rights, or personhood. Conversely, moral
judgment is not reducible to one pleasant-unpleasant axis.

## What Neuroscience Supports

Several established findings make parts of the proposal biologically plausible without proving the
complete SAN interpretation:

- Human population-imaging work found sensory-specific and cross-modal patterns that tracked a
  continuous pleasant-to-unpleasant axis
  ([Chikazoe et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3749)). This supports measurable
  population-level valence, not one morality center or an objective moral code.
- Human fMRI and comparative anatomy support a large-scale allostatic-interoceptive system linking
  modeling of bodily state to visceromotor regulation
  ([Kleckner et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-017-0069)).
- Mouse basolateral-amygdala population activity changed when learned reward and punishment
  contingencies reversed, and neural changes tracked behavioral changes
  ([Zhang and Li, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07679-9)). This is learned valence, not
  morality.
- Human moral judgments use information beyond valence. Neural patterns can distinguish
  intentional from accidental harm
  ([Koster-Hale et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1207992110)), and moral preferences can
  alter the valuation of profit obtained by harming another person
  ([Crockett et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4557)).
- Moral-foundation judgments have shown both shared social-cognition involvement and dissociable,
  experience-sensitive cortical patterns
  ([Hopp et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01693-8)). A later naturalistic fMRI study
  reported a low-dimensional space containing several moral dimensions rather than one good-bad
  line ([Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2026.121724)).

These studies do not identify a moral truth in neural tissue. They show that bodily regulation,
affective value, learned consequences, intentions, social concepts, and moral judgments have
measurable but partly distinct population-level representations.

## The SAN Interpretation

SAN treats valuation as part of a distributed observer-action process rather than a message read by
an inner judge.

At the **micro scale**, receptors, synapses, ion channels, neuromodulators, and plasticity affect how
body and environmental signals change particular receivers. At the **meso scale**, distributed
cortical and subcortical populations combine interoceptive state, exteroceptive events, memory,
social inference, predicted consequences, and candidate actions. At the **macro scale**, a person
or artificial system acts within an environment containing other agents, institutions, norms, and
shared risks.

In SAN terms, tonic activity may supply a receiver-relative working context while phasic departures
update the distributed value-and-action state. That is a proposed operational interpretation, not
a clinically established morality code or a sentience meter. A low-dimensional manifold is also a
model of regularities within high-dimensional activity; it is not a literal surface stored in one
brain region.

## Shared Constraints Without Objective Morality

The historical source proposed that two or more agents facing the same measurable danger could
develop partially convergent valuations and cooperate. This is a testable coordination claim:

**shared constraint + sufficiently overlapping needs, models, and communication -> partially
aligned predictions and actions**

The overlap can be measured through choices, reports, physiology, learned policies, and
generalization to new situations. It may explain why some values feel intersubjectively stable.
It does not prove that two agents have identical qualia, that every goal is morally good, or that
agreement creates objective morality. Agents can share a temperature limit while disagreeing about
risk, rights, responsibility, or the acceptable means of avoiding harm.

## Artificial-System Evidence Gates

An artificial system should pass separate gates:

1. **Regulation:** does it maintain declared variables under perturbation?
2. **Valuation:** does an internal state predict choices across held-out contexts better than a
   reward-only description?
3. **Causal use:** does intervening on that state change action and learning in the predicted
   direction?
4. **Social inference:** does it represent who is affected, what they intended, and which
   constraints or rights apply?
5. **Normative governance:** can stakeholders inspect, contest, and revise objectives and
   prohibited outcomes?
6. **Welfare or sentience:** is there evidence beyond language, self-protection, optimization, or
   human-like behavior that the system can undergo positive or negative experience?

Passing an earlier gate does not establish a later one.

## Discriminating Tests

A source-faithful research program would:

- compare reward-only, homeostatic, affective-valence, social-inference, and full moral-model
  predictors on held-out situations;
- manipulate bodily or operating state, environmental danger, social intention, and learned norms
  separately;
- test whether an inferred low-dimensional state generalizes across sensory modalities without
  erasing morally important dimensions;
- test whether agents with shared constraints converge only where their needs and information
  overlap;
- intervene on the candidate state and measure later choice, cooperation, inhibition, memory, and
  learning; and
- test biological and artificial systems without assuming that similar behavior means similar
  experience.

The strong SAN version would be weakened if the candidate state does not generalize, has no causal
effect on action or learning, adds nothing beyond ordinary reward and prediction variables, or
cannot distinguish threat avoidance from judgments involving intentions, other agents, and norms.

## Governance Test

An artificial moral system should document:

- who selected the objectives, examples, raters, and stakeholder groups;
- which rights, constraints, and prohibited outcomes sit outside learned reward;
- how disagreement and minority interests are represented;
- how uncertainty and distribution shift are handled;
- which versions, updates, memories, and tools produced an action; and
- how affected people can contest or appeal consequential decisions.

Human feedback is evidence about selected human judgments, not universal moral truth.
