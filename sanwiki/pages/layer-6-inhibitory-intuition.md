Layer 6 (the deepest layer) of the cortical column contains high-level neurons that can fire and simultaneously inhibit the entire chain of neurons beneath them. When a Layer 6 neuron fires this way, it acts as a **sparse distributed representation (SDR)** of the cluster it just silenced — embodying the whole in a single activation.

## The intuition mechanism

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] proposes that this Layer 6 suppression is the neural substrate of *intuition*: those moments when a "big prediction" seems to pop directly into consciousness without a conscious step-by-step derivation. The intuition doesn't travel the full bottom-up hierarchy (Layer 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6); instead, sufficiently urgent or familiar patterns route directly to Layer 6, bypassing intermediate processing.

The Layer 6 neuron's SDR thus represents the whole sub-cluster's pattern at a high level of abstraction — the same way a compressed model encodes context without replaying every detail. This explains both the speed and the "certainty" quality of intuitions: the full hierarchy is not consulted, but the high-level pattern match is strong.

## Layer 1 ↔ Layer 6 dialog

A challenge this model must answer: how can Layer 1 (sensory input) communicate urgency to Layer 6 when normal ascending signals lose detail at each step? The conventional binary-pulse model (ANN-style) cannot encode urgency or granularity — a spike is a spike. SAN theory's answer is that the *waveform shape* of the [[action-potential|action potential]] (the [[action-potential-waveform-encoding]]) carries meta-information about urgency, source depth, and pattern type, allowing Layer 6 to distinguish an urgent v1 message from a routine v5 message.

## Neuronal shape as information

This connects to a broader SAN claim: the structural shape of a neuron — its dendritic geometry, the precise configuration of synapses — is itself significant information that binary ANN models erase. If all neurons are treated as identical threshold units, the spatial-temporal program encoded in dendritic morphology is lost. In SAN, each neuron's shape is treated as a topological descriptor of its learned history, analogous to a differential equation in a categorical representation. See [[mixed-selectivity]] and [[dendritic-architecture]].
