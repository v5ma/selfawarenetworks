# Proprioceptive Feedback

> **Scientific boundary:** Proprioception includes receptor, spinal, brainstem, cerebellar, thalamic, cortical, and motor-prediction processes. It is not a single cortical stream. The claim that all proprioceptive feedback is transmitted through Layer 5 parvalbumin interneurons and merged by gamma through the Gamma Consideration Sandwich is a SAN hypothesis, not established anatomy.

Proprioception is the nervous system's sensitivity to body position, movement, and force. It is indispensable for posture and action, but it is only one contributor to a larger body model.

## Sources of proprioceptive evidence

Important peripheral sources include:

- muscle spindles, which are sensitive to muscle length and change in length;
- Golgi tendon organs, which are sensitive to muscle tension;
- joint and cutaneous afferents that contribute under particular conditions;
- signals generated by active movement and interaction with external loads.

These are not the same as vestibular signals about head motion and orientation, interoceptive signals about internal bodily condition, or exteroceptive touch. The systems interact, but the distinctions matter medically and experimentally.

## Pathways and loops

Proprioceptive information participates in several partially parallel operations:

- spinal reflex and pattern-generating circuits;
- ascending spinal and brainstem pathways;
- cerebellar state estimation and movement calibration;
- thalamic and primary somatosensory cortical processing;
- posterior parietal and premotor body/action representations;
- descending motor control and the sensory consequences of movement.

The same signal can affect rapid control without becoming reportable, contribute to body position without producing ownership, or alter a movement prediction without constituting the complete sense of self.

## Active proprioception includes prediction

During active movement, sensory consequences arrive with delay. Motor-related prediction can therefore improve estimates of limb state. Human cerebellar-patient studies show that cerebellar damage can selectively impair aspects of active proprioception and force perception, supporting a role for prediction in body-state estimation.

This does not mean that proprioception is generated only by prediction. Passive receptor evidence remains important, and cerebellar contributions vary with task and lesion.

## Correction to the earlier SAN microcircuit claim

Parvalbumin-positive interneurons are local inhibitory neurons. They can influence timing, gain, and oscillatory activity within cortical circuits. They are not peripheral proprioceptive afferents, and current evidence does not establish one universal Layer-5-PV route that transmits all body feedback into consciousness.

Likewise, gamma activity can accompany attention, sensory processing, and local circuit coordination, but a gamma-band association does not identify the source of proprioceptive information or prove a single binding mechanism.

The Gamma Consideration Sandwich should therefore be presented as a SAN integration hypothesis: named sensory evidence, motor predictions, goals, and reafferent consequences may interact through task-specific recurrent circuits. The theory must specify those circuits rather than assigning entire information classes to frequency bands.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats proprioception as part of observer-action rather than a status message delivered to an inner controller:

1. receptors and local circuits register changing body relations;
2. motor commands and predictions alter the expected state;
3. cerebellar, cortical, subcortical, brainstem, and spinal systems compare and transform partial evidence;
4. the distributed body model changes action selection and ongoing control;
5. action changes the body and environment, creating the next sensory update.

The network itself participates in the comparison. It does not render a body for a separate viewer.

In tonic/phasic terms, the tonic canvas is the receiver's current body-and-task state; phasic ink is the new difference produced by movement, load, error, touch, or internal change. The relevant baseline is receiver-specific and need not be one global oscillation.

## Scale map

- **Micro:** receptor transduction, synapses, dendrites, interneurons, motor neurons, and cellular integration.
- **Meso:** spinal, cerebellar, thalamic, cortical, and local recurrent circuits.
- **Macro:** a distributed body-world model linking posture, ownership, self-location, agency, goals, and action.

The scales interact, but no one receptor, interneuron, cortical layer, or frequency band contains the complete body model.

## Testable SAN questions

A stronger SAN account should name:

- the body variable being changed;
- the receptor and ascending pathway;
- the predicted motor consequence;
- the receiving population and timescale;
- the measured tonic context and phasic update;
- the behavioral effect of causal perturbation;
- the result that would separate GCS or NAPOT from standard sensorimotor prediction.

A prediction specifically about Layer-5 PV cells must state cortical area, projection target, cell type, task, frequency measurement, and expected behavioral deficit. Without those constraints, the claim is not anatomically testable.

## Primary evidence anchors

- Bhanpuri, Okamura, and Bastian found an active proprioceptive deficit associated with cerebellar damage and unpredictable dynamics: [Predictive modeling by the cerebellum improves proprioception](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0784-13.2013).
- Bhanpuri, Okamura, and Bastian also found impaired active force perception in cerebellar patients: [Active force perception depends on cerebellar function](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00983.2011).
- Weeks, Therrien, and Bastian measured reduced proprioceptive acuity during movement after cerebellar damage: [The cerebellum contributes to proprioception during motion](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00417.2016).
- Wardman and colleagues used selective muscle and cutaneous afferent stimulation to distinguish contributions to human sensorimotor cortical activity: [Cerebral, subcortical, and cerebellar activation evoked by selective stimulation of muscle and cutaneous afferents](https://doi.org/10.1002/phy2.270).
