# Dendritic Coincidence Detection

**Dendritic coincidence detection** describes the enhanced interaction of inputs that arrive within a relevant spatial and temporal window. It is one form of dendritic integration, not a universal rule that isolated synaptic inputs fail while simultaneous inputs always trigger a burst.

## Mechanism

Excitatory postsynaptic potentials have finite rise and decay times. Inputs close enough in time can overlap, and inputs close enough on a branch can share local voltage. NMDA receptors add a voltage-dependent nonlinearity because depolarization helps relieve their magnesium block. Voltage-gated sodium or calcium channels can add further regenerative amplification.

The result depends on more than simultaneity:

- a single strong input can matter;
- repeated inputs can facilitate or depress presynaptically;
- inhibition can veto, delay, shunt, or reshape excitation;
- inputs on different branches may sum more linearly than clustered inputs;
- membrane voltage and recent activity change the effective window.

[Polsky, Mel, and Schiller (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1253) experimentally demonstrated branch-dependent nonlinear summation in rat neocortical pyramidal neurons. [Xu et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11601) linked coincident sensory and motor-related input with dendritic plateau activity in layer 5 pyramidal neurons during an active whisker task in mice.

## What it does not measure

Coincidence is not automatically synchrony across an entire neural array. A somatic burst is not a direct percentage-confidence readout, and an "80 percent match" cannot be inferred from a firing threshold without an experimentally defined code. Coincident excitation can also be canceled or redirected by inhibition.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can treat coincidence sensitivity as one mechanism by which distributed arrivals alter a cell's future contribution to a network. A valid phase-based extension must measure phase, local dendritic voltage, synaptic identity, and output together. It must outperform explanations based only on rate, temporal overlap, or membrane depolarization.

The relevant observer is not a soma reading a confidence meter. The neuron and circuit change state through the physical interaction of synaptic currents, membrane conductances, recurrent activity, and downstream consequences.
