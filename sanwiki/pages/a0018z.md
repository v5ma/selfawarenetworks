# Dorsal and Ventral Vision as Complementary Inputs to Neural Rendering

*Micah Blumberg's 2022 `a0018z` note used computer-vision systems as analogies for partially specialized visual pathways. It did not establish the posterior parietal cortex as a literal neural radiance field or the inferior temporal cortex as a literal semantic-segmentation engine. The medically defensible SAN question is whether overlapping visual pathways contribute differently transformed information to a recurrent, distributed perceptual and action system.*

## Source recovery

The current idea developed in four dated stages:

1. On June 8, 2022, the first public Git version of [[gh-a0018z|a0018z]] described information paths forming "holograph patterns" and the mind as a computed phase field. The note presented this inside a deliberately poetic and speculative neuron-spacetime discussion, not as established visual anatomy.
2. On July 31, 2022, Micah visibly revised "holographic" to "tomographic." This records a conceptual move from a stored hologram analogy toward reconstruction from distributed projections. It does not by itself establish biological tomography.
3. On August 19, 2022, the note introduced the two-stream computer-vision comparison. It called the posterior-parietal/NeRF line "humor" and said the ventral and dorsal streams were **analogous** to semantic-segmentation and spatial-reconstruction systems.
4. On September 4, 2022, [[gh-a0004z|a0004z]] refined the proposal as a conjecture: the parietal stream may focus more on spatial rendering or "where," the temporal stream more on semantics or "what," and both may perform some of each operation.

The source chronology therefore does not support the later automated claim that one note established a strict, anatomically grounded division of labor. The recoverable innovation is a qualified analogy about complementary transformations and their integration.

The source also contains speculative quantum-gravity and teleportation branches. They remain part of its historical record, but they are not medical evidence for the visual-stream proposal and are not required for this page's neural mechanism.

## Medically grounded interpretation

### Established visual neuroscience

The major geniculostriate route carries retinal signals through the lateral geniculate nucleus to early visual cortex. From there, distributed and recurrent processing includes ventral occipitotemporal and dorsal occipitoparietal pathways. These are useful functional tendencies, not two isolated pipelines with exclusive contents.

The classic perception-action distinction has bounded experimental support. Patient DF could accurately shape her grasp to objects whose orientation and width she could not report normally, supporting partly dissociable neural processing for visual perception and visually guided action. The finding does not imply that the dorsal pathway is a complete scene renderer or that the ventral pathway alone contains object meaning.

Object and shape information occur in both pathways. Human imaging studies found hierarchically organized object information in ventral and dorsal cortex, dorsal sensitivity to three-dimensional object structure, and shape-sensitive responses that challenge a strict ventral-"what"/dorsal-"where" split. Task demands also affect the pathways differently: ventral representations can be comparatively stable across task changes, while dorsal representations can be more adaptive to what the participant is doing with the information.

The pathways are embedded in recurrent networks. Macaque tracing demonstrates dense, weighted, directed interareal cortical connectivity. Electrophysiology also shows feedforward and feedback influences between visual areas with different frequency biases. These results support recurrent exchange; they do not prove that oscillatory phase performs NAPOT reconstruction or that one frequency band has one universal computational meaning.

### Source-faithful SAN interpretation

The recovered SAN operation is:

**retinal and bodily evidence -> partially specialized spatial, object, and action transformations -> recurrent cross-pathway exchange -> distributed working percept and action readiness -> movement and new sensory evidence**

The computer-vision comparison can be retained if its limits stay visible:

- **Semantic-segmentation analogy:** ventral and associated temporal populations can contribute relatively stable object, feature, category, and contextual information.
- **Spatial-reconstruction analogy:** dorsal and associated parietal populations can contribute viewer-relative geometry, depth, location, movement, task demands, and action-relevant transformations.
- **Integration claim:** neither pathway contains the completed percept. Visual, thalamic, medial-temporal, association, attention, and sensorimotor populations jointly maintain and update the represented scene.

In the bounded [[napot-theory|NAPOT]] interpretation, these are complementary projections entering a many-to-many recurrent reconstruction process. "Rendering" means a distributed working neural representation used by the same network for perception and action. It does not mean a literal screen, a stored NeRF volume, or an inner viewer.

### Scale boundaries

- **Cellular scale:** a neuron integrates inputs according to its dendritic, membrane, inhibitory, and neuromodulatory state. No generic neuron receives the complete dorsal and ventral scene.
- **Local-circuit scale:** populations can emphasize different features, spatial relations, task variables, and action affordances through recurrent excitation and inhibition.
- **Large-network scale:** partially specialized pathways exchange information with visual, thalamic, memory, attention, and motor systems. The aggregate network activity is the observer-action process.

The source's computed phase-field proposal is a stronger SAN hypothesis. Current neuroscience supports phase-sensitive communication and recurrent population dynamics in bounded preparations, but it does not establish one brain-wide physical phase field as the completed visual percept.

## Discriminating tests

1. Present unfamiliar three-dimensional objects while independently varying identity, geometry, viewpoint, task, and intended action. Test whether ventral and dorsal population models contain overlapping but differently weighted information.
2. Use time-resolved recording and pathway-specific perturbation to determine whether recurrent exchange improves held-out prediction of perception and action beyond feedforward, firing-rate, and task-only models.
3. Compare ordinary population-state models with a declared SAN model that adds receiver-relative phase and timing. The stronger SAN account requires added predictive and causal value under matched power, rate, movement, arousal, and sensory input.
4. Test integration directly: perturb one pathway and ask whether the other retains its specialized information while the joint perceptual-action judgment loses a specific component. A strict two-engine mapping and a distributed-overlap model make different predictions.

## Scientific boundaries

- Posterior parietal cortex is not anatomically a NeRF.
- Inferior temporal cortex is not anatomically a 3D semantic-segmentation network.
- "What," "where," and "how" are useful summaries, not exclusive labels for every neuron or area in a pathway.
- Dorsal object and shape coding does not make the dorsal and ventral pathways identical.
- Recurrent visual exchange does not establish NAPOT, consciousness, or a universal oscillatory code.
- A computed phase field is a SAN hypothesis, not an established medical description of visual awareness.
- The note's quantum-gravity speculation cannot be used as evidence for the neural claims on this page.
