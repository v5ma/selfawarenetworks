## The core mechanism

An atom forms when the "place field" (a substrate of oscillation potential) folds recursively until it creates a stable cyclone. This cyclone oscillates and radiates field lines in all directions — imagined like rays around a drawn sun. These radial lanes align particle motion vertically (toward/away from the atom) rather than horizontally.

Because more traffic flows vertically, horizontal movement is slowed in proportion to distance from the atom's center. This directional asymmetry *is* gravitational time dilation — particles near atoms take longer to complete horizontal traversals. The mechanism requires no additional force: it follows from the geometry of aligned oscillation lanes.

## Gravity as a reverse fan

The analogy offered: a fan pushes air away; gravity is a reverse fan pulling space inward. At the atomic scale, the cyclonic structure creates centripetal flow. Multiple atoms oscillating at the same frequency constructively combine into a larger effective oscillator with a stronger inward lane structure — explaining why bulk matter produces stronger gravity than isolated atoms.

## The place field

Space itself is conceived as a "place field" — a metronome-like substrate that iterates through states of oscillation. It has no intrinsic size or fundamental time; it is defined only by what is oscillating within it. Atoms are regions where the field has settled into a stable oscillatory [[attractor]] (the cyclone). Empty space has near-uniform distribution of potential directions; atoms break this symmetry by aligning field lines.

This anticipates the [[super-dark-time]] [[gh-formulation|formulation]] of local time density ρₜ: the place-field's oscillation density corresponds to ρₜ.
