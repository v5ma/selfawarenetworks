[Choice](choice.md) refinement theory tries to reconcile free will with determinism by suggesting that deterministic [self](gh-a0206z.md)-aware agents can continually refine their choices with each successive iteration of computation. In this framing, each iteration incorporates new variables into the awareness equation and can improve later choices with new information. The claim applies adjacent-possible theory to [self](gh-a0206z.md)-awareness and phenomenal consciousness.

Richard Feynman's delivery in the Feynman lectures can sound as if each major rhetorical point is a powerful particle collision in his mind. In SAN terms, that image raises a speculative question: could some brain-level collisions be activated soliton-like wave collisions, where waves from phasic firing neural paths intersect with oscillating edge communities?

At the mesoscopic scale, edge communities can be read as arrays of neurons and as sections of cortical layers inside cortical columns.

At the brain-network level, [choice](choice.md) may involve interactions between oscillating groups of cells across cortical and subcortical structures. This framing also relates to information scaling, [fractals](gh-map-intro-fractal.md), and top-down and bottom-up information flow in the brain.

# "Cortical-Subcortical Interactions in Goal-directed Behavior"

The 2023 *Physiological Reviews* article "Cortical-Subcortical Interactions in Goal-directed Behavior" supports the page's network-level framing by arguing that behavior emerges from cooperative cortical and subcortical interactions, including bottom-up thalamic interactions, rather than from isolated functional modules.
https://journals.physiology.org/doi/abs/10.1152/physrev.00048.2021

The 2022 *Cerebral Cortex* article "Bidirectionally connected cores in a mouse connectome: towards extracting the brain subnetworks essential for consciousness" is relevant to the page's consciousness claim because it studies bidirectionally connected subnetworks that may be sufficient to support conscious experience.
