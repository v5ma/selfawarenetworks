## Primary-source corrections

- [Narayanan et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3549) found common medial-frontal
  low-frequency mechanisms for adaptive control in humans and rats. This supports a bounded
  post-error control route, not the claim that realizing any personal mistake produces one
  universal low-frequency state.
- [Stickgold et al. (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.290.5490.350) reported stereotyped
  Tetris imagery at sleep onset, including in amnesic participants. It does not show that tonic
  oscillations store complete Tetris images.
- [Rose et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah7011) reactivated a task-relevant latent
  working-memory item with TMS. It supports a latent-to-active reinstatement question, not a
  universal tonic-wave memory store.
- [Sibille et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004137)
  supports an astrocytic potassium-homeostasis contribution to neuronal excitability. It does not
  show that glia distribute newly produced ion channels according to thought content.
- [Fu et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10844) found clustered dendritic-spine formation
  during repeated motor learning. It does not establish frequency-matched electromagnetic or
  spacetime guidance of spine growth.
- [Song et al. (2017)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/html/Song_Semantic_Scene_Completion_CVPR_2017_paper.html)
  and [Litany et al. (2018)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Litany_Deformable_Shape_Completion_CVPR_2018_paper.html)
  provide bounded machine shape/scene-completion donors, not biological neural-rendering evidence.
- [Hunt and Schooler (2019)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2019.00378) controls the General
  Resonance Theory source. The copied 2020 article records comparison material, not Micah
  authorship or independent SAN validation.
