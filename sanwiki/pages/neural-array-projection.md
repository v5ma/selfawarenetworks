**Neural Array Projection** is SAN's name for the idea that upstream population activity does not simply appear unchanged in a downstream population. Anatomical projections carry effects through axons and synapses; the receiving cells transform those effects according to dendritic integration, inhibition, neuromodulation, recurrent input, plasticity, and current state.

## Projection and transformation are different operations

In established anatomy, a neural projection is a set of axons from a source population to one or more target populations. Transformation occurs because transmission and reception are selective and state-dependent:

1. Source cells generate axonal action potentials.
2. Action-potential waveform, terminal voltage, presynaptic calcium, release machinery, vesicle pools, and recent history influence release at each bouton.
3. Transmitter acts on receptors at the receiving synapse.
4. Dendritic location, receptor composition, inhibition, membrane state, and coincident inputs determine the local postsynaptic effect.
5. Dendritic and somatic integration alter axon-initial-segment output.
6. Recurrent and feedback pathways update both source and target populations.

The transformed downstream pattern is therefore a population consequence, not a single wave packet that carries a complete rendered image from Array A to Array B.

## Where the weights live

Synaptic efficacy has presynaptic and postsynaptic contributors. Presynaptic release probability and vesicle availability vary across boutons. Postsynaptic receptor number, spine geometry, dendritic conductances, and local inhibition alter the receiving effect. Plasticity can change both sides, but a postsynaptic spine is not ordinarily a remote control that directly sets the release pattern of every incoming axon.

A special trans-synaptic mechanism has been demonstrated under bounded conditions: [Ucar et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04125-7) found that enlargement of a spine that physically pushed its facing bouton enhanced presynaptic exocytosis in rat slice culture. This supports possible local mechanical feedback, not a universal spine-to-bouton programming architecture.

Bouton-specific release and multivesicular capacity are also real but probabilistic. [Duerst et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-33565-6) found substantial release-probability differences among Schaffer collateral boutons and more multivesicular release under high-release-probability conditions.

## Dendritic interaction, not a radiating interference picture

Dendritic branches combine conductance changes nonlinearly through membrane voltage, active channels, electrotonic structure, and local spikes. The existing figure's concentric colored waves should be read as a metaphor for interacting inputs. The page does not establish a radiating dendritic field that spatially interferes like light, nor does it establish phase as the exclusive neural code.

## Scale declaration

- **Micro:** one cell receives many synaptic effects, transforms them, and distributes axonal consequences.
- **Meso:** a local circuit or canonical laminar motif transforms population activity through recurrent excitation, inhibition, and cell-type-specific routing.
- **Macro:** distributed cortical, thalamic, subcortical, cerebellar, and body loops update an action-relevant working model.

A canonical laminar diagram is a teaching abstraction. Cortical areas, cell classes, thalamic nuclei, and noncortical systems do not all share one identical layer-by-layer projection operator.

## SAN interpretation

SAN proposes that recurrent, weighted projection contributes to a distributed render-update cycle. To make that proposal scientific, a page or experiment must name:

- source and target populations;
- anatomical pathway and direction;
- measured carrier, such as spikes, membrane voltage, synaptic current, field potential, or transmitter release;
- receiver and time window;
- transformation or decoder;
- behavioral or motor consequence;
- alternative explanation and failure condition.

Calling the target pattern a rendering is a SAN interpretation. Ordinary synaptic transmission and downstream population transformation are established component mechanisms; they do not by themselves demonstrate NAPOT tomography, phase qualia, or a complete perceptual reconstruction.
