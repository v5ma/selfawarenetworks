The "humans are metal robots" thesis holds that human cognition and consciousness are fully reducible to physical information-processing operations: all knowledge, feelings, emotions, insights, and intuitions decompose into [[tempo-spatial-patterns]] that brains — and in principle computers — can process. The claim is not metaphorical; metallic ions (calcium, potassium, sodium) physically carry neural signals.

This position sits adjacent to functionalism but is class-constrained: different substrates can host minds, yet certain structural properties (feedback loops, coincidence detection, hierarchical temporal integration) are required. It rejects emergentist "special sauce" explanations in favor of pattern-based accounts of [[consciousness]].

## Core mechanisms

- **[[coincidence-detection]] as the basis of a bit**: neurons fire when inputs arrive within a temporal window; this is the fundamental discrete step of computation in biological tissue.
- **Tempo-spatial patterns**: the invariant currency of mind, shared across neural, silicon, and any sufficiently structured substrate.
- **[[neural-backpropagation]] and dendritic action potentials**: sub-threshold events that shape which coincidences get detected, linking micro-circuit dynamics to representational content.

## SAN framing

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] argues that consciousness emerges when a digital signal processor is combined with a deep learning neural network, a three-dimensional [[point-cloud]], and a fractal of feedback loops — exactly the architecture of [[self-aware-networks]] theory. The "metal robot" framing demystifies mind without eliminating it.

## Criticism addressed

Emergentists object that functional equivalence cannot capture phenomenal experience. The SAN reply: phenomenal experience IS the temporal interference pattern — there is nothing additional to explain beyond the pattern's [[gh-a0206z|self]]-referential structure (see [[napot]]).
