[[adaptive-resonance-theory|Adaptive Resonance Theory]] (ART, Stephen Grossberg) and [[napot|SAN:NAPOT]] both describe
top-down/bottom-up comparison mechanisms in neural systems, with different model
structures and explanatory emphases.

## ART's Core Idea

In a simplified ART account, bottom-up input and learned top-down expectation interact. Vigilance sets the required match: adequate matching supports resonance and learning; mismatch initiates reset and search, potentially recruiting a new category. This addresses stability and plasticity under the model's assumptions, not a claim that arbitrary biological learning can never forget. [Grossberg's biological circuit account](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2015.00501) develops mechanisms beyond this simplified flow.

## Buzsáki Bridge

The [[buzsaki-brain-inside-out|inside-out perspective]] raises a related question: how does sensory input interact with a brain that already has organized activity? This is an editorial bridge, not an identity between a rhythm and an ART category template, or between every sensory input and a mismatch. Micah's [[gh-a0206z|source discussion]] likewise starts from an organized, learned system rather than a blank slate. An ongoing initial state supplies context; it does not by itself establish ART's matching rule or prevent forgetting. See [[tonic-oscillation]].

## Where SAN:NAPOT Diverges

[[napot|SAN:NAPOT]] agrees that the brain performs a continuous comparison between
internal baseline patterns and incoming signals, and proposes a physiological route to test:

- The comparison substrate is the **multi-dimensional [[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]]** — e.g., theta
  rhythm in the hippocampus.
- Incoming sensory data arrives as **[[phase-wave-differential|phase wave differentials]]**
  riding on top of that tonic ground.
- The comparison happens not just across layers but **across brain regions** via phase-matched
  functional connectivity — oscillating feedback loops, not static templates.
- **[[apical-dendrite|Apical]] and basal dendrites** receive and integrate inputs. The ordinary output route runs through the axon initial segment, axon, boutons, transmitter release and downstream receptors. [[inceptive-field]] is SAN's name for that downstream influence, not an apical output terminal.

[[gh-a0412z|The original convergence/divergence note]] states the functional many-to-one-to-many distinction. The anatomical route is supported by separate studies of [dendritic input organization](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07709) and [axonal initiation and propagation](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.1997.617ba.x); those studies do not establish the full NAPOT synthesis. See [[receptive-inceptive-dendritic-projection]] for the source and compartment context.

## Key Contrast

ART's match/resonance/reset logic and SAN's tonic-reference, transformation and downstream-use account can be compared without treating one as a complete implementation of the other. The shared question is how current activity interacts with learned state. PWD includes excitatory and inhibitory departures, timing, waveform and receiver context, not only phase alignment.

**Figure scope:** The anatomical scene is generic, not a laminar wiring map. Dendritic input and axonal output are distinct; the small synapse separates fusion, cleft and receptor effects. The functional inceptive-field definition and the broader NAPOT interpretation remain SAN proposals. Illustrated patterns are not experimental recordings.
