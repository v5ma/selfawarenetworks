The **[[virtual-models-theory|Virtual Models Theory]]** holds that neurons fire in patterns representing **virtual models** of reality — information structures that are non-physical in character yet require physical substrate to exist and transmit. Consciousness is the experience of these virtual models moving across neural regions, not of the neurons themselves.

## Core claim

Information encoded in firing patterns is not the same as the physical neurons doing the firing. The patterns are "virtual" in that they can migrate across different neural regions without losing identity — a point directly illustrated by the split-brain literature (corpus callosum severing changes *which* regions host a virtual model, but doesn't destroy the model-forming capacity). The key implication: consciousness is not localized to fixed hardware but is a mobile, pattern-level phenomenon.

This framing is consistent with [[napot]] (Neural Array-Projection Oscillatory Tomography), where successive neural arrays re-encode and project the same informational pattern forward in time and space, producing unified perspective from distributed hardware.

## Decoding requirement

Consciousness requires not just *encoding* of a virtual model but a *decoder* capable of extracting meaning from it. This is why [[self-aware-network]] theory treats the brain as a system of interacting encoder-decoder oscillator arrays: each array encodes in one phase and is decoded by the receiving array in the next phase.

## Distinction from standard information theory

Standard information theory treats signals as content-neutral. The [[virtual-models-theory|Virtual Models Theory]] adds semantic weight: the patterns that constitute virtual models carry meaning because they isomorphically map to environmental states. Consciousness emerges at the point where a system can not only encode the map but use it to navigate — i.e., where there is both a virtual model and a model-user (the [[self-aware-network]]).

## Consciousness as process, not state

Consistent with SAN's [[four-dimensional-phase-tracking]] account, the [[virtual-models-theory|Virtual Models Theory]] treats consciousness as a **dynamic process** — virtual models are continuously re-rendered by oscillating arrays — rather than a binary on/off state. Disruptions to the physical substrate (anesthesia, split-brain surgery, microtubule collapse) interrupt the rendering process, not the information itself.
