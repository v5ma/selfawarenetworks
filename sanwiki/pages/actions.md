# Actions

> **Boundary note (2026-07-18):** Actions are produced by distributed sensorimotor systems, not by brainwaves alone. Oscillations, traveling waves, dopamine, replay, engrams, and dendritic events can influence selection, timing, learning, or execution, but they are not interchangeable causes. A careful claim must name the circuit, behavior, timescale, measured variable, and alternative control account. See [[body-model-as-control-surface]], [[phase-language-firewall]], and [[comparison-evidence-is-not-biological-validation]].

An **action** is a closed-loop body-world trajectory. It includes selection, preparation, execution, sensory consequence, rapid correction, and learning. No single spike, rhythm, neuron, or cortical region is the action by itself.

## Established control architecture

- **Selection and policy:** cortical and basal-ganglia loops bias which behavior is expressed, suppressed, or sustained.
- **Preparation and coordination:** premotor, supplementary motor, parietal, cerebellar, and other populations configure task-dependent control states.
- **Execution:** motor-cortical and brainstem descending pathways interact with spinal interneurons, motor neurons, muscles, and biomechanics.
- **Feedback:** proprioceptive, cutaneous, visual, vestibular, and interoceptive signals permit rapid correction while movement is still underway.
- **Learning:** synaptic plasticity, reinforcement signals, cerebellar error-based adaptation, and cortical reorganization change later actions.

These components overlap in time. A reach, saccade, posture change, or spoken word is not produced by a simple pipeline with one final gate.

## The SAN interpretation

### Recovering "the loop of you"

An early SAN source compressed the self-maintaining loop as **senses -> brainwaves -> actions -> new sensory input** and called it "the loop of you." In that source, *brainwaves* meant the changing physical activity of the nervous system, not an EEG frequency band acting as a motor command. The medically recoverable operation is:

**current sensory, proprioceptive, interoceptive, memory, and task state -> distributed neural-state update and action selection -> descending motor, autonomic, or vocal execution -> changed body and environment -> reafferent and exafferent sensory return -> updated self-world state**

This makes action part of the next observation rather than an output appended after perception. It also preserves the early SAN proposal that a continuing self is partly constituted by a learned observer-action loop, while avoiding the claim that every feedback loop is conscious or that one oscillation is the self.

### The loop at three scales

- **Cellular:** Dendritic, somatic, axonal, and synaptic state condition whether and how a neuron contributes to a selected route. One neuron does not contain the complete action, intention, or self.
- **Circuit and population:** Cortical, basal-ganglia, thalamic, cerebellar, brainstem, spinal, and peripheral systems jointly select, prepare, execute, suppress, and correct behavior. There is no universal cortical gate or single brainwave command center.
- **Organism and environment:** Movement, speech, autonomic change, and environmental consequences generate sensory return that updates the next neural and bodily state. A closed sensorimotor loop alone does not establish conscious awareness.

In this framing, **criterial causation** is not a single executive threshold. It is the distributed fact that cells and circuits respond conditionally to combinations of input, state, history, and inhibition. A local criterion can contribute to an action without containing the complete choice or its meaning.

SAN's observer-action claim is therefore distributed. The network registers differences, transforms them against its current state, changes the body and environment, and receives the consequences. The resulting sensory return participates in the next observation. Many actions, including reflexive and automated actions, do not require conscious awareness.

### Source-faithful claim boundary

- **Established neuroscience:** actions depend on distributed selection, preparation, execution, feedback, and learning systems; self-generated movement changes later sensory input; predictive and sensory-feedback signals can modify perception and control.
- **Supported SAN interpretation:** "brainwaves" can be recovered as shorthand for changing neural population state, including but not limited to oscillatory organization, rather than a literal EEG instruction sent to muscle.
- **SAN-specific hypothesis:** the learned sense-state-action-return trajectory is constitutive of the continuing observer-action process called the self.
- **Stronger unproven claim:** a named phase relation, PWD pattern, or tonic/phasic organization is necessary or sufficient for a particular conscious action. That requires circuit-specific perturbation and behavioral testing.

## Oscillations and actions

Oscillations can organize excitability, timing, communication, and population dynamics. They are not an action command language by default. A rhythm stopping when a behavior stops does not show that the rhythm encoded or terminated that behavior. A strong claim requires a causal perturbation that preserves movement opportunity, firing-rate information, arousal, and relevant connectivity while selectively changing the proposed oscillatory relation.

## Tonic canvas and phasic ink

For action, the tonic canvas is the receiving control system's current state: posture, task rule, prediction, body estimate, and readiness. Phasic ink is a consequential update such as a sensory error, target change, reward signal, or branch-local event. These are functional roles relative to a receiver and timescale, not fixed frequency bands.

## Testable SAN question

Does a receiver-defined phase relation add causal predictive power for action selection or online correction beyond firing rate, broadband activity, kinematics, common input, and known feedback-control variables? The answer must be measured in a named circuit and behavior.
