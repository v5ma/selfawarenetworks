# Cortical Traveling Waves: Measured Phase Gradients and the SAN Hypothesis

> **Scientific boundary.** Cortical traveling waves are measured spatiotemporal patterns in specific preparations. They are not automatically phase-wave differentials (PWDs), prediction errors, tomographic reconstructions, or conscious contents. SAN proposes a stronger relationship that must be tested rather than assumed.

## What researchers measure

A traveling wave is a spatially organized change in the phase or activity of a neural signal over time. With electrodes or imaging sites arranged across tissue, researchers can ask whether nearby locations show a systematic phase progression rather than unrelated local oscillations.

Several quantities must remain distinct:

| Quantity | What it describes |
|---|---|
| Oscillation frequency | How quickly a local signal cycles in time |
| Phase | A location's position within that cycle |
| Spatial phase gradient | How phase changes across measured locations |
| Propagation direction and speed | The fitted movement of that spatial pattern |
| Wave strength or consistency | How well and how reliably the observations fit a traveling-wave model |
| Spectral power | Signal energy within a frequency range; not the same variable as propagation |

A phase gradient measured on the scalp is not automatically a direct map of propagation along the cortical surface. Electrode geometry, referencing, source mixing, volume conduction, signal-to-noise ratio, and the anatomical scale of the preparation all affect the interpretation.

## Bounded evidence

| Preparation | Finding | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Human intracranial ECoG during a memory task | Theta- and alpha-range clusters showed spatial phase gradients whose direction and consistency related to task events and performance. | Direct cortical recordings covered selected clinical electrode locations, not the entire brain. |
| Human intracranial recordings during memory encoding and recall | Posterior-to-anterior and anterior-to-posterior directions differed with the cognitive operation. | Direction depended on task and recording coverage; it was not one universal hierarchy. |
| Macaque lateral prefrontal and posterior parietal arrays | Local beta traveling-wave properties reflected recent reward history; prefrontal wave strength also related to behavioral sensitivity. | The authors reported different frequencies across areas and treated the waves as likely local rather than one continuous frontoparietal wave. |
| Human scalp EEG after DMT | Estimated forward and backward traveling-wave measures changed relative to placebo. | Scalp direction estimates do not by themselves identify a cortical route, a PWD, NAPOT, or a consciousness mechanism. |
| Awake macaque V1 voltage-sensitive-dye imaging | A suppressive recurrent wave shaped the cortical representation of apparent motion. | This was a bounded visual-cortex preparation, not a universal predictive or perceptual mechanism. |
| Human intracranial predictive-learning task, preprint | Alpha-wave direction, phase reset, high-gamma activity, and trial-to-trial model updating covaried in the reported task. | This remains a preprint result and does not establish SAN's joined interpretation. |

Together, these studies show that traveling-wave geometry can relate to perception, memory, prediction, and action in particular experiments. They do not show that every oscillation travels, that every traveling wave carries one semantic object, or that wave direction alone identifies feedforward versus feedback computation.

## The source-faithful SAN genealogy

The traveling-wave proposal developed in stages:

1. **September 18, 2022 - recurrent propagation ancestor.** The public *Neural Array Projection Oscillation Tomography Theory, 5th Revision* video described bidirectional transmission through synapses, neurons, assemblies, thalamic and hippocampal loops; array-to-array transformation; reconstruction; and eventual motor consequences. It did not yet establish the later equation of this architecture with cortical traveling waves.
2. **January 4, 2024 - explicit traveling-wave/PWD join.** *Hex 7* described traveling waves as possible high-information phasic departures against a tonic context and connected their movement between neural arrays with PWD and NAPOT. This is the first explicit Git-fixed join recovered for this page.
3. **October 5, 2024 - routing extension.** *04san.md* proposed that predictive information might use cross-cortical traveling waves to coordinate distributed processing and flexible routing. This later extension must not be backdated into the 2022 formulation.

The accurate source-recovery result is therefore not that a 2022 source already used all of the current traveling-wave vocabulary. It is that the 2022 recurrent receive-transform-project architecture supplied an ancestor, and the explicit traveling-wave interpretation was added in 2024.

## The SAN hypothesis

SAN does not need one universal frequency band to define tonic and phasic roles. The distinction is receiver-relative:

- **Tonic context** is the receiving population's ongoing organized state.
- **Phasic change** is a consequential departure from that expected state.
- **A traveling PWD candidate** is a phasic departure whose spatial phase geometry propagates across declared tissue and produces a measurable receiver-specific consequence.

The proposed operator is:

**receiver-specific tonic state -> spatially structured phasic event -> propagation through constrained anatomy -> state-dependent effects in receiving populations -> recurrent redistribution -> possible perceptual, memory, or action consequence**

This preserves the stronger SAN idea while keeping the terms testable. A measured traveling wave is not a PWD by definition. It becomes a PWD candidate only when its timing, phase, frequency, amplitude or power, duration, spatial path, receiver state, and downstream consequence are jointly specified. It becomes a NAPOT candidate only if the recurrent propagation contributes demonstrably to reconstruction or coordinated updating from partial signals.

## Interaction is not a literal collision detector

Counterpropagating or differently directed patterns may superpose, interfere, change excitability, or reflect multiple sources. Those possibilities must be distinguished experimentally. Two wavefronts meeting does not by itself compute a match or error, synchronize cortical columns, or render a perceptual slice.

Likewise, a posterior-to-anterior or anterior-to-posterior fitted direction is a measured spatial relationship. Calling it feedback or feedforward requires anatomical, temporal, and causal evidence in the declared task. The predictive-learning and DMT findings are valuable because they motivate bounded tests of direction and function, not because they prove one universal mapping.

## Scale ladder

- **Micro scale:** membrane and synaptic events shape local excitability and timing. A single action potential or one dendritic event is not by itself a cortical traveling wave.
- **Meso scale:** phase or activity can propagate across a local cortical map or electrode array. This is the scale of many directly measured cortical traveling waves.
- **Macro scale:** coordinated patterns can be estimated across larger regions or the scalp, but anatomical-source claims become harder and require explicit controls.
- **Whole-system consequence:** perception and action depend on recurrent sensory, association, subcortical, and motor systems. SAN proposes that selected wave patterns participate in this distributed process; it does not assign the complete observer to one wave or location.

## How to test the added SAN claim

A decisive study should compare nested models on held-out trials:

1. local power, firing rate, task state, anatomy, and connectivity;
2. those variables plus wave direction, speed, phase-gradient consistency, and spatial extent;
3. those variables plus receiver-relative tonic state and the typed PWD event variables;
4. those variables plus a declared NAPOT reconstruction or redistribution outcome.

Causal perturbation should alter wave geometry while matching total power, rate, arousal, sensory input, and task demand as closely as possible. The SAN extension is weakened if wave variables add no reproducible prediction beyond conventional local and network variables, if changing wave geometry produces no selective receiver or behavioral consequence, or if typed PWD/NAPOT variables do not improve prediction of reconstruction or coordinated updating.
