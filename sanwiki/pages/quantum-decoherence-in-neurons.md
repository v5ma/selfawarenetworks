# Quantum Decoherence in Neurons

**Quantum decoherence** is the loss of phase relationships that allow a selected quantum state to display interference or other coherent behavior. A claim about decoherence is incomplete unless it names the physical degree of freedom, its environment, the preparation, the coupling model, and the lifetime required by the proposed biological function.

## The Tegmark calculation

Tegmark analyzed specific models of neural firing and kink-like polarization excitations in microtubules. His calculation produced decoherence times of roughly 10^-13 to 10^-20 seconds, far shorter than the 10^-3 to 10^-1 second neural timescales used for comparison ([Physical Review E, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)).

This is a serious challenge to models that require those selected states to remain coherent over neural-processing intervals. It is not an experimental measurement of every possible quantum state in a living neuron. Later replies disputed the chosen state variables, geometry, and environmental coupling and proposed longer times for different models.

The dispute creates a specification requirement, not a shortcut. A new proposal must state exactly which state Tegmark's calculation does or does not apply to and must measure that state's lifetime under relevant conditions.

## What later microtubule experiments do not settle

In-vitro work has measured electronic photoexcitation migration and collective ultraviolet-emission behavior in prepared tubulin or microtubule systems ([Kalra et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114); [Babcock et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c07936)). These findings show nontrivial protein photophysics. They do not by themselves demonstrate a long-lived neuronal superposition, entanglement, quantum computation, or objective reduction.

"Coherence" also has several meanings. Optical coherence, quantum-state coherence, synchronous neural firing, EEG coherence, phase locking, and a stable cognitive representation are not interchangeable measurements.

## Required evidence for a neural quantum role

A functional quantum-neural claim should provide:

1. a mathematically defined state and observable;
2. a measurement in intact, functioning neurons;
3. a lifetime long enough for the declared operation;
4. evidence that neural machinery prepares or controls the state;
5. a known biological receiver or coupling path;
6. a selective perturbation that changes a declared neural computation;
7. a classical control model that fails where the quantum model succeeds;
8. replication across preparations.

Only after those steps would it be reasonable to ask whether the mechanism contributes to consciousness.

## Relation to SAN

SAN's tonic/phasic, NAPOT, and distributed-observer claims can be formulated using classical cellular and network dynamics. They do not depend on maintaining a microtubule superposition.

A future quantum contribution could alter molecular or cellular readiness while the observer remained distributed across cells, fields, recurrent routes, body signals, and action. Demonstrating quantum coherence at molecular scale would not imply that a microtubule sees, chooses, or contains a percept.

The SAN comparison should therefore keep three verdicts separate:

- **physical state detected**;
- **neural function established**;
- **consciousness contribution established**.

No current result should be promoted from the first verdict to the third without the intervening causal evidence.
