A **burstlet** is an incomplete synchronized neural burst — a partial firing event across a neural ensemble that falls below the threshold for a full [[brainwave]] oscillation cycle. In SAN theory, burstlets are proposed as **the unit of information in the brainwave pattern**, analogous to the bit in [[information-theory|Shannon's information theory]].

## Definition

Where a full burst represents a completed synchronization across a cell assembly, a burstlet is a truncated version: enough synchrony to propagate a signal, but not enough to constitute a committed, high-amplitude oscillation. The distinction matters because the brain encodes information not just in the presence or absence of a spike, but in the *degree of synchronization* achieved.

## Connection to SAN Concepts

Burstlets fit within SAN's framework of [[neural-oscillatory-tomography]] (NOT): if full oscillations are how the brain renders its 3D model of sensory input, burstlets represent sub-threshold updates — candidate predictions that are partially confirmed. This connects to [[predictive-coding]] models where the brain continuously generates and tests hypotheses.

The burstlet concept also informs [[napot|NAPOT]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography): not every projection cycle completes; burstlets are the evidence that projection is probabilistic, not deterministic.

## Relation to Information Theory

Framing burstlets as the information unit parallels Shannon's entropy formalism: information content is inversely related to predictability. A burstlet signals that a full burst was *expected* but only partially delivered — high information content precisely because it violated the prior.
