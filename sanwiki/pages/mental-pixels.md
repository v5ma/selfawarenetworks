In SAN theory, **mental pixels** are the granular units of conscious experience — momentary loci of heightened activation produced when intersecting energy wave patterns create a measurable local difference in neural state. Consciousness, on this model, is not a continuous field but a discrete rasterization: a dynamic display assembled from discrete wave-difference events.

## Definition

An "energy wave difference" occurs when two or more oscillatory signals — carrier waves at different frequencies or phases — create constructive or destructive interference at a neural site. The constructive interference peak is the *pixel*: a brief, location-specific burst of increased firing or phase coherence that stands out against the background oscillatory noise.

Because the brain continuously sweeps intersecting oscillations across neural arrays (see [[napot|NAPOT]]), the mental display is rebuilt moment-to-moment from millions of such difference events.

## Connection to NAPOT

[[napot|NAPOT (Neural Array Projection Oscillation Tomography)]] provides the mechanism: high-frequency phasic waves intersect low-frequency tonic carrier waves, and every intersection point is a candidate mental pixel. The brain's "3D internal screen" (see [[volumetric-3d-internal-screen]]) is the aggregate of all current pixels across the full neural volume.

Mental pixel density and coherence determine perceived clarity: high-coherence intersections produce vivid qualia; low-coherence intersections produce vague or marginal experiences at the edge of awareness.

## Relation to Burstlets

[[burstlets|Burstlets]] (incomplete synchronized bursts) represent *partial* pixels — wave differences that fail to reach full constructive interference, contributing sub-threshold information rather than full conscious registration.
