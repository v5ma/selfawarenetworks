The **[[neo-mind-cycle|Neo Mind Cycle]] three-phase protocol** is the structured neurofeedback session
design used in [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s 2011–2012 neurofeedback practice. It organizes
brainwave training into three distinct phases applied in sequence.

## The three phases

1. **Inhibit** — suppresses overactive brainwave frequencies to reduce noise and
   create neuroplasticity headroom. A 35-minute standalone session at the short tier.
2. **Excite** — stimulates target frequencies to strengthen desired cognitive states.
   Together with Inhibit, this forms the standard 70-minute two-part session.
3. **Maintain** — a consolidation phase that stabilizes the new oscillatory pattern
   established by the Inhibit + Excite sequence. The full 105-minute three-part
   session is described as the "intense feel good" tier.

## Three-technology integration

Each session combined:
- **Neurofeedback EEG** — real-time brainwave visualization creating brain [[gh-a0206z|self]]-awareness.
- **Binaural audio beats** — [[brainwave-entrainment|brainwave entrainment]] to drive plasticity.
- **Light machine** — photic stimulation synchronized to the patient's actual brainwave
  pattern, creating a closed-loop feedback system between internal rhythm and
  external stimulus.

The combination is significant: rather than delivering fixed stimulus frequencies,
the light machine adapts to the client's own oscillations — a hardware instance
of the [[brainwave-vortex-attractor]] feedback principle.

## Historical significance

This 2011–2012 protocol is a practical precursor to the theoretical frameworks
developed later in [[neo-mind-cycle]] and [[napot]]. The Inhibit–Excite–Maintain
structure maps roughly onto the tonic–phasic–inhibited oscillatory motif central
to [[neural-functional-fractal]].
