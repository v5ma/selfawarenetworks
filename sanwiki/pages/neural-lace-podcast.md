# Neural Lace Podcast

Micah Blumberg launched the Neural Lace Podcast in April 2017 as a public research conversation
about brain-computer interfaces, neurophysics, spatial computing, medical imaging, artificial
intelligence and consciousness. The recovered series is not just an interview archive. It records
the transition from the hands-on Neo Mind Cycle and EEG period into the mechanism questions that
later became central to Self-Aware Networks.

## What the series asks

The recurring problem is how to build a bidirectional interface with a living brain. That requires
more than detecting a coarse brain state. A system would have to:

1. observe the relevant event in the world and the neural response together;
2. identify a repeatable relation despite changing biological detail;
3. understand where and how distributed neural signals are transformed;
4. distinguish reading a state from safely causing a controlled state change; and
5. close the loop through perception, action and feedback.

Those questions lead the conversations into thalamic convergence, dendritic and synaptic
computation, hierarchical recognition, coincidence patterns, VR embodiment, neurofeedback,
volumetric rendering, EIT, EEG, fNIRS and optical imaging.

## Recovered scope

The downloaded source family contains eleven transcripts spanning **2017-04-11 through
2020-05-12**. Season 1 includes Episodes 1-5, Episode 7 and the NerveGear episode. The recovered
Season 2 files include Episodes 1-4, but Episode 3 was recorded after Episode 4. No exact Episode 6
transcript is present in this family.

## Why it matters to SAN

- **Artificial perception:** Episodes 1-4 develop the problem of recognizing and writing
  spatial-temporal neural patterns, including the broccoli example.
- **Neural mechanism:** The conversations move from network hierarchy toward synapses, dendrites,
  plasticity, timing, phase and feedback.
- **Neural Rendering:** OTOY, light fields, volumetric capture and VR provide an engineering
  vocabulary for capture, transformation, projection and update.
- **BCI history:** EEG-driven VR, NeurotechSF, OpenEIT, WebVR and the Noisebridge implementation
  document a sustained practical research lineage.
- **Instrument discipline:** The later episodes make clear that EEG, EIT, fNIRS and optical imaging
  have different observables and limitations.

## From mirrored feedback to a candidate external cortex

The series connects two engineering stages that should not be collapsed. [[neo-mind-cycle|Neo Mind
Cycle]] returned transformed EEG-related variation as changing light and sound so the participant's
brain could learn the closed loop. The 2018 NeurotechSF and Noisebridge work then routed changing
electrical measurements into WebVR, making the returned consequence spatial and action-linked.

That sequence motivates the [[neo-mind-cycle-neurofeedback-bci|external-cortex bridge]]: a machine
is not a cognitive extension merely because it displays neural data. It becomes a candidate
temporary extension only if reciprocal, task-relevant transformation is learned and causally
necessary for the coupled operation. Delay, scrambling, remapping, removal, sham feedback, and
artifact-aware controls provide ways to test that stronger claim.

This is a transition in the SAN research program, not proof that the historical systems decoded
thoughts, reconstructed a complete percept, or incorporated software into biological cortex. The
[[bci-instrument-atlas|BCI Instrument Atlas]] tracks the later move toward multimodal measurement,
world-body co-registration, and controlled intervention.

## Evidence boundary

The recordings establish public intellectual history. Automated transcripts help discovery but
can misidentify names and speaker turns. Guest claims, Micah's claims, questions and speculation
must remain separate, and all biological or device-capability claims still require independent
evidence.
