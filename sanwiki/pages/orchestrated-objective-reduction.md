# Orchestrated Objective Reduction

**Orchestrated Objective Reduction**, usually abbreviated **Orch OR**, is the Penrose-Hameroff theory proposing that biologically orchestrated quantum states in neuronal microtubules undergo objective reduction and contribute to conscious moments. The complete theory is described on [[orch-or|Orch OR]].

## Do not compress the theory into one finding

Orch OR contains multiple dependent claims:

1. microtubules support a relevant physical state;
2. that state persists under biological conditions;
3. cellular processes organize or "orchestrate" it;
4. objective reduction occurs by the proposed physical rule;
5. neural systems causally use the event; and
6. the event explains a declared consciousness target.

A result at an earlier step does not prove a later one. Microtubule dynamics in dendritic spines concern cytoskeletal participation in plasticity. Optical effects in purified tubulin concern the physical behavior of a preparation. Drug effects on loss of righting reflex concern an anesthesia-associated behavioral endpoint. None is, by itself, a direct observation of objective reduction or conscious content.

## SAN comparison

SAN and Orch OR both insist that consciousness must be physically realized, but they place the explanatory burden differently. Orch OR assigns a central role to microtubular quantum states and objective reduction. SAN assigns the observer-action role to distributed causal organization across cellular receivers, recurrent networks, body state, memory, and action. SAN therefore neither needs Orch OR nor treats a neuron or microtubule as a hidden observer.

The comparison is mechanistic rather than rhetorical. Experiments should state the scale, measured variable, biological receiver, perturbation, behavioral or report target, and alternative explanations. See [[microtubules-anesthesia-and-orch-or-evidence-boundary|Microtubules, Anesthesia, and Orch OR: Evidence Boundary]].

## Primary theory source

Hameroff and Penrose's review states the mature Orch OR proposal and its responses to major criticisms ([Physics of Life Reviews, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2013.08.002)).
