Single-neuron phase cascade is the mechanism in which the firing of one cell simultaneously changes the phase states of a large group of cells — the propagation step that makes individual action potentials meaningful at the array level in [[napot]] theory.

## Core Observation

A single neuron's [[action-potential|action potential]] does not merely update one downstream target. Via lateral, recurrent, and diverging axon branches (see "exit terminals branch radially in every possible direction"), it entrains or disrupts the oscillatory phase of many neighboring and downstream cells at once. The result is a coordinated phase shift across an entire neural array from a single firing event.

## Synchronization and Desynchronization

When one oscillator "kicks" others, the outcome bifurcates:
- **Synchronization**: the kicked oscillators adopt the phase of the driving neuron, producing a coherent wave front.
- **Desynchronization**: if the kick arrives at an incompatible phase offset, the driven oscillators scatter to varied phases, disrupting existing patterns.

Which outcome occurs depends on coupling strength, ion-channel state, and the relative phase at the moment of the kick. This is the microscale basis for the [[oscillatory-binding-problem|binding]] and unbinding of neural representations.

## Inhibitory "Bad Points"

Neurons whose phase state cannot absorb incoming signals (non-absorbing or refractory states) act as "bad points" that interrupt synchronization chains. They filter which firing events propagate through the array and which do not — a form of inhibition-as-gating that shapes the topology of phase cascades.

## SAN Significance

In [[napot]], the phase cascade is the information-transmission event — not the [[action-potential|action potential]] per se, but the ripple of phase adjustments it initiates across the array. Conscious experience is the integrated tomographic rendering of all ongoing phase cascades.
