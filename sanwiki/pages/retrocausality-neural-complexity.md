**Retrocausality** is the hypothesis, supported by quantum entanglement experiments, that future states can influence past states — "the future can change the past." In SAN's early theoretical framing, this raises the possibility that the brain's future existence participates in shaping causal structure, not merely receiving it.

## Neural complexity as a cosmic upper bound

The source proposes that the number of synapses in a single brain may exceed the number of stars in the visible universe, making the brain the most complex structure we know of. SAN theory uses this as a grounding claim: any theory of mind must account for a substrate of enormous combinatorial richness, far beyond what classical computing models assume.

## Retrocausality and consciousness

Quantum entanglement experiments have demonstrated that measurement outcomes can appear to influence prior states (see: quantum eraser experiments). The source connects this to consciousness by suggesting that if time is a spatial dimension (general relativity) and the future can define the past, then conscious observers are embedded in a causal web that is not strictly forward-in-time. This speculative framing anticipates SAN's interest in [[oscillatory-neural-coding]] as a time-symmetric signal — oscillations do not have a privileged temporal direction.

## Memory as cognitive restructuring

The source also introduces memory processing as a tool: reframing difficult memories as data rather than lived experience is a form of top-down modulation. This connects to SAN's model of [[predictive-processing]] and top-down dendritic gating.
