# Thalamic Convergence: Bounded Integration, Not One Global Zone

> **Page role:** "Thalamic convergence zone" is a Wiki bridge label, not a separately established SAN anatomical term. This page distinguishes experimentally observed convergence in named thalamic circuits from the unsupported idea that all sensory and conscious content is forced through one whole-brain bottleneck.

## Two meanings of convergence

**Anatomical or functional convergence** means that identified inputs influence the same neuron, nucleus, or bounded circuit. This can be tested with tracing, connectomics, simultaneous recording, synaptic physiology, and pathway-specific perturbation.

**Global experiential convergence** would mean that all relevant information is compressed into one thalamic location or unified message. Current evidence does not establish such a zone. Sensory, motor, limbic, attentional, arousal, and higher-order thalamic nuclei have different inputs and outputs, and olfaction is an important exception to the simple "all senses relay through thalamus" slogan.

## Demonstrated bounded examples

- In mouse higher-order posterior medial thalamus (POm), electron-microscopy connectomics found convergent layer-5 input from somatosensory and motor cortex on identified thalamic neurons. The result supports cell- and nucleus-specific integration, not universal multimodal fusion ([Sampathkumar et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34282018/)).
- In macaque visual networks, pulvinar activity coordinated communication between interconnected cortical areas according to attentional demands. This is a bounded visual-attention result ([Saalmann et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3714098/)).
- In mouse prefrontal circuits, mediodorsal thalamus amplified local cortical connectivity during attentional control without carrying the categorical rule representation itself ([Schmitt et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5570520/)).

The three mechanisms are not interchangeable: cellular convergence, attention-dependent coordination, and connectivity amplification answer different questions.

## Source-faithful SAN interpretation

SAN's 2021-2022 [[thalamic-bridge-consciousness-portal|thalamic bridge]] sources propose that partial states can be gathered, transformed, and returned through distributed routes. The medically coherent interpretation is **many bounded convergence operations embedded in recurrent loops**:

`selected inputs -> local thalamic integration or modulation -> pathway-specific output -> cortical and embodied consequence -> returned input`

On this interpretation, no node contains the complete percept. Different circuits may align, separate, sustain, suppress, or redirect partial states. SAN's stronger hypothesis is that receiver-relative tonic and phasic organization across those loops contributes to a distributed rendered state.

## Scale boundaries

- **Micro:** synapses and dendrites on an identified thalamic neuron integrate selected inputs.
- **Meso:** a named nucleus and its cortical partners regulate a bounded task or state.
- **Macro:** many thalamic, cortical, subcortical, and body-return routes jointly change the organism's distributed state.

Evidence at one scale does not automatically prove the others. A convergent POm dendrite does not prove a conscious display; a whole-brain imaging correlation does not identify the synaptic integration rule.

## Testable SAN increment

For one declared circuit, compare a rate-only model, a connectivity model, a phase/timing model, and a full receiver-relative SAN model. Simultaneously measure the relevant inputs, thalamic receiver state, cortical output, and behavior. Then perturb one input class or thalamic output route and test predicted loss and rescue. A result counts for this page only if it identifies where convergence occurs, what variables are transformed, which output changes, and how the organism's behavior or report is affected.

## Provenance boundary

No dated source packet has been found for the literal phrase **Thalamic Convergence Zone**. The page is retained because it repairs a real structural ambiguity in the Encyclopedia: adjacent SAN sources discuss thalamic bridges, convergence, and distributed rendering, while the medical literature demonstrates only circuit-specific forms of convergence. This bridge must not be used as independent priority evidence for a historical SAN term.
