## Established sensorimotor physiology

Voluntary movement depends on distributed cortical, basal-ganglia, cerebellar, brainstem, spinal, peripheral, and sensory loops. Premotor and motor cortical populations contribute to planning and execution; basal-ganglia loops influence selection and vigor; cerebellar circuits contribute prediction, timing, calibration, and error-based updating; spinal and brainstem circuits transform descending and sensory input into coordinated muscle activity.

Proprioceptive, cutaneous, vestibular, visual, and interoceptive signals report different aspects of body-world state. Corollary-discharge and forward-model mechanisms can predict sensory consequences and help distinguish expected from unexpected change. None of these findings places a complete self-model in one neuron or region.

## The SAN/NAPOT proposal

SAN proposes that a usable self is the continuously updated relation among motor intention, body state, sensory consequence, memory, value, and possible action. NAPOT supplies a candidate many-to-one-to-many process by which partial signals are integrated and redistributed across participating populations.

This is not a little viewer watching an internal screen. The proposed observer is the distributed process itself. In SAN language, a population's **tonic action field** is its current organized operating state or canvas. Phasic synaptic and cellular changes are differences registered against that state. Those differences alter subsequent field, cellular, and motor activity. Across recurrent cycles, the network's aggregate registration, transformation, redistribution, and action is the observer-action.

The electromagnetic-field interpretation is a SAN hypothesis. Established physiology supports membrane potentials, synaptic currents, extracellular fields, oscillations, recurrent population activity, and sensorimotor feedback; it does not by itself establish that a field is the phenomenal observer.

## Scale ladder

| Scale | Established mechanism | SAN interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | A neuron integrates synaptic and dendritic inputs and changes its axonal output | A candidate receive-integrate-project motif; not a complete self or tomography by one cell |
| Meso | Local circuits and columns coordinate selective population activity | A local body-state or action-state update |
| Macro | Distributed cortical, subcortical, brainstem, spinal, and body loops maintain and correct behavior | A recurrent working self-model whose observer-action is distributed across the loop |

The same abstract relation may recur across scales, but the anatomy and causal variables differ. Similarity of motif is not proof that every scale performs the same computation.

## Choice and action

SAN treats choice as a bounded, prediction-sensitive selection process. Candidate actions are shaped by learned value, current body state, environmental constraints, remembered outcomes, and competing control policies. The selected trajectory remains revisable as sensory consequences arrive. This makes an action a closed-loop process, not a single cortical command or threshold crossing.

## Test burden

A testable self-rendering claim must identify the participating populations, measured tonic and phasic variables, direction of information flow, behavioral consequence, and competing explanation. Useful experiments should ask whether disrupting a proposed relation impairs body-state prediction, sensory attribution, online correction, or action selection beyond effects explained by firing rate, arousal, connectivity, or generic task difficulty.
