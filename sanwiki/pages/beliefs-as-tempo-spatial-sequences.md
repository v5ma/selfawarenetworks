This plate shows belief as a neural tempo-spatial sequence: a remembered causal pattern is replayed through hippocampal-cortical circuits, projected into future thought, tagged by emotion, and updated through evidence accumulation and LTP/LTD.

A **belief** in SAN theory is "a sequence of tempo-spatial patterns that represents a remembered causal relationship now projected into the future as thought" ([[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]], 2013). Emotions such as fear are not fixed states but **temporal sequences** — the brain plays forward a learned causal pattern and treats its prediction as present reality.

## What this means

The same neural machinery that stores memory plays it forward in time to generate anticipation. Fear is a memory-projection: the hippocampus and [[amygdala]] activate the same pattern that was associated with past threat, and the motor cortex prepares responses as if the future event is already occurring. The emotion *is* the prediction.

This dissolves the distinction between memory and expectation: both are tempo-spatial sequences encoded in [[synaptic-frequency]] patterns across [[cortical-columns]]. A belief is just a sequence that runs persistently.

## Democratic moral arbitration

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s related claim (same source): "Our neurons collect data systematically, democratically, when the evidence is accumulating in a clear direction they surge forth with energy — this is how the brain chooses what is moral."

Moral judgment is not the output of a special module but the outcome of evidence accumulation across a distributed [[self-aware-network]]. When convergent evidence exceeds a threshold the network fires collectively — identical to [[napot]]'s description of how [[attention]] and action are selected.

## Neuroplasticity grounding

Because beliefs are sequences of synaptic patterns, they are subject to [[ltp]] / [[ltd]] over time. The brain is always changing itself (see Norman Doidge, *The Brain That Changes Itself*). Changing a belief means replacing one tempo-spatial sequence with another through accumulated experience — not "conquering" it externally but allowing the network's own evidence-accumulation to update the pattern.
