**Quantum noise in cognition** is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s position, first articulated in 2014, that quantum-scale effects in neural tissue produce only random noise — not structured signal — and therefore cannot serve as a mechanism for consciousness or as a channel for a "quantum soul."

The argument: the [[observer-effect]] in quantum physics means that *measurement disturbs quantum states*, not that consciousness creates or governs physical reality. Quantum fluctuations at the level of microtubules and synapses add an element of chance — noise — that the brain's patterned signaling must *overcome*, not exploit. Any purported soul communicating via quantum fluctuations would be "interfering and being defeated by the brain's systems on a regular basis."

This directly counters [[orch-or]] (Penrose–Hameroff Orchestrated Objective Reduction), which posits that microtubule quantum coherence generates conscious experience. In the SAN framework, classical synaptic computation — shaped by natural selection — is sufficient; quantum effects are a substrate-level nuisance, not a feature.

## Implications for SAN

The SAN account of [[self-aware-network]] behavior relies on [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography): patterns of oscillation propagating across neural arrays at classical timescales. The "noise floor" set by quantum fluctuations is far below the signal-to-noise threshold at which NAPOT patterns operate, making quantum effects irrelevant to the theory's core claims.

## Related critiques from the same source

- **Pineal gland mysticism**: no credible peer-reviewed evidence supports the pineal gland as a locus of consciousness or DMT secretion. The claim fails to trace to hard research.
- **Lanza biocentrism**: the "turtles with shells" framing — consciousness carries spacetime — cannot explain sleep, blackouts, or personality changes from brain injury. Consciousness is *not* independent of physical spacetime.
