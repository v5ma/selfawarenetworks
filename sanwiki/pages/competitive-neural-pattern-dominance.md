This image frames pattern dominance as an emergent cortical competition: rival distributed patterns are reinforced or suppressed through excitatory drive, inhibitory shaping, thalamo-cortical feedback, and plasticity until one sparse coherent ensemble becomes dominant without a central controller.

Competitive neural pattern dominance describes how cortical patterns compete for expression by inhibiting neighboring patterns until one reigns supreme — an early precursor to [NAPOT](napot-overview.md) formalized by [Micah](gh-resnote04.md) in a 2012 discussion.

## Core model

[Micah](gh-resnote04.md) describes neural activity as "packs of wrestling dogs competing for dominance by inhibiting their neighbors until only one pattern reigns supreme, sort of like a three-dimensional game of GO." This is explicitly *not* a centralized imposition of order but an emergent, bottom-up process shaped by the tension between inhibitory and excitatory connections.

The primary arena is the neocortex. Life experiences strengthen or weaken rival pathways, and the competition between energy surging through those pathways constitutes most of the subjective experience of being human. Lower structures (thalamus, subcortical) participate, but they shape rather than dominate this competition.

## Inhibitory/excitatory tension

Ben Thomas (interlocutor) frames the same dynamic: neither inhibitory nor excitatory activity is more centralized — it is the *tension* between the two that emergently generates complex thought. The thalamo-cortico-thalamic circuit's inhibitory cortico-thalamic connections provide a canonical example of shaping (not controlling) excitatory activity in goal-directed fashion.

## Relation to consciousness-as-prediction

From the same discussion: consciousness can be reduced in function to "a memory-based prediction resulting in the coordination of our whole structure." This prediction-coordination view anticipates [NAPOT](napot-overview.md)'s oscillatory phase encoding. See also [self-aware-network](self-aware-network.md).

## Fractal symmetry note

Ben Thomas distinguishes vertebrate neural architectures from simpler informational networks (fungi, ant colonies) by the presence of **fractal symmetry in functional nodal architectures** — visible even in simple organisms like C. elegans. This fractal structure criterion is consistent with [intro-fractal-consciousness](intro-fractal-consciousness.md).
