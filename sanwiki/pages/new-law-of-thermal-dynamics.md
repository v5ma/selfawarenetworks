All physical systems tend to dissipate electromagnetic, thermodynamic, or **phase-wave differences** over time. This generalizes the second law of thermodynamics from heat and entropy to wave-phase gradients across all physical substrates.

This principle extends Karl Friston's [[free-energy-principle]] beyond biological systems: where Friston's FEP describes living systems minimizing prediction error through internal modeling, the wave dissipation law applies to non-living systems as well — the driving mechanism is wave-based signal dissipation, not surprise minimization specifically.

## Brain as structured dissipation machine

The brain is a **structured dissipation machine**: neural circuits organize the natural tendency toward phase equilibrium. Learning occurs not by storing representations but by managing [[phase-wave-differentials]] — incoming waves alter synaptic weights to partially cancel future incoming waves of the same frequency and phase. This provides a bottom-up physical account of predictive coding.

Neural circuits that survive are those that most efficiently route dissipation — evolution selects for dissipation architecture, not just information capacity.
