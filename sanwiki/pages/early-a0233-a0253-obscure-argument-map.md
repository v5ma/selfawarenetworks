## Main result

The tranche produced one newly governed concept:
[[state-relative-differential-neural-write-in|State-Relative Differential Neural Write-In]].

The public June 11, 2017 `a0249z.md` article states that a future sensory BCI should first listen
to the pattern present in the current person and environment, then send only the difference
between that pattern and the desired pattern. The defensible modern form is a closed-loop control
protocol:

```text
current measurements + context
-> uncertain estimate of current task-relevant state
-> declared reachable target
-> minimum safe intervention predicted to reduce target error
-> measured neural, behavioral, and reported consequence
-> update or stop
```

This does not mean raw EEG can be subtracted from a stored experience. The measurement, latent
state, desired target, actuator-reachable state, and reported percept must remain distinct.

## Other durable recoveries

### Observer as a summary of expectations

`a0239z.md` gives an internally dated 2012 formulation in which the singular observer is a useful
summary of many distributed expectations and predictions. The governed route does not call every
neuron a complete observer. It asks when distributed prediction, recurrence, body state, action,
and returned consequence jointly support an observer-level capacity.

### Inhibition defines active content by exclusion

`a0246z.md` and the public `a0250z.md` treat inhibition as computationally positive: it can reroute
activity, suppress alternatives, and help define the temporary pattern available for attention or
action. This strengthens SAN's Choice mechanism. It does not imply that all inactive cortex is
unconscious content, that total electrical equilibrium is conserved across regions, or that every
inhibitory event produces an avalanche.

### Multiscale transformation rather than exact copying

`a0236z.md` and `a0252z.md` describe information being transformed across dendritic, circuit, and
larger-network scales. The strong term “fractal functional isomorphism” is not assumed. A valid
cross-scale claim needs a declared mapping, retained task variable, transformation loss, and
downstream consequence.

## Primary-source boundaries

- [Little et al. (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23852650/) predates the 2017 article with
  adaptive DBS using a neural biomarker. SAN does not claim to have invented closed-loop neural
  stimulation.
- [Gu et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9414) formalized brain-network controllability
  before `a0249z.md`. Its linear structural model is prior art and does not establish arbitrary
  percept writing.
- [Zrenner et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29191438/) and
  [Schaworonkow et al. (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30268710/) later show that TMS effects
  depend on instantaneous EEG-defined state.
- [Bolus et al. (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8356067/) later implemented online
  state estimation and target tracking for optogenetically driven neural activity.
- [Xu et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11601) governs the active-sensing dendritic
  calcium finding cited in `a0236z.md`; it does not establish PWD or NAPOT.
- [Grossman et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.05.024) demonstrated temporal
  interference stimulation in mice. It did not demonstrate noninvasive artificial perception or
  whole-brain semantic write-in.

## Medical and physics quarantine

`a0233z.md`, `a0242z.md`, and `a0251z.md` preserve valuable research questions but include
uncontrolled personal reports, supplement hypotheses, broad disease-cause claims, and uncertain
receptor/rhythm statements. They are not current medical advice and do not become SAN premises
without primary evidence and expert review.

`a0234z.md` and `a0253z.md` preserve the route from neural oscillation questions into QGTCD and SIT.
Their spacetime, mass-frequency, observer, and gravity claims belong to physics manuscripts and
require independent mathematical and physical review. They do not validate the neural model.
