**Gravitational modulation of entanglement** is the testable prediction that emerges from [[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation]] ([[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]): if local gravitational potential alters the rate at which a quantum particle's internal phase cycle evolves, then entangled particle pairs held in *different* gravitational wells should exhibit measurable deviations from standard quantum-mechanical correlation predictions.

## The QGTCD Mechanism

In [[quantum-supertimeposition]], each particle's internal clock runs at a rate faster than any human-scale instrument can resolve. [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] adds a gravitational dimension: that internal clock rate is not constant — it is modified by local spacetime curvature, just as macroscopic clocks run slower in stronger gravitational fields (general relativistic time dilation).

Consequence: two entangled particles in different gravitational potentials will have their phase cycles evolving at slightly different rates. Because entanglement correlations are phase-lock correlations (see [[quantum-supertimeposition]]), a differential clock rate should shift the relative phase of the locked pair, producing a small but detectable change in the correlation statistics.

## Proposed Off-World Experiments

To test the prediction, [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] proposes conducting Bell-inequality or quantum entanglement experiments in regions with significantly different gravitational potentials from Earth:

- **Moon** — ~0.16 g surface gravity
- **Mars** — ~0.38 g
- **Jovian moons** (e.g., Europa, Callisto) — varying weak-field environments
- **Orbital vs. surface** — comparing results at high altitude / low gravity vs. ground station

If results deviate from standard QM predictions, the deviation would confirm that gravitational time dilation modifies internal phase cycles — a bridge between general relativity and quantum mechanics not present in standard quantum field theory.

## Significance

This distinguishes [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] from a purely interpretive restatement of quantum mechanics. An interpretation that makes the same predictions as standard QM is empirically equivalent and unfalsifiable. The off-world prediction makes [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] falsifiable — it becomes a physical theory, not just a reinterpretation.

Implications if confirmed: refined gravitational lensing predictions, quantum computing designs that account for local gravity (orbital quantum computers vs. ground stations), and a new empirical bridge between quantum mechanics and general relativity.
