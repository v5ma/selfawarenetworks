This SAN plate shows noise-to-coherence as a biological phase-organization mechanism: scattered sensory energy is pooled through dendrites, gated by interneurons, and aligned through plastic recurrent coupling into a stable coherent oscillation.

Neural noise-to-coherence is the process by which the brain organizes an initially random distribution of sensory energy into coherent oscillatory patterns. In SAN, this is a foundational mechanism: [[self-aware-networks]] emerge from noise, not despite it.

## Core Claim

The brain begins each thought cycle in a noisy, high-entropy state — diverse neurons firing without global coordination. [[neural-plasticity]] and [[oscillatory-coupling]] then act on this noise, selectively amplifying correlated activity until a coherent wave-pattern dominates. The coherence is not imposed top-down; it emerges bottom-up from local interactions, analogous to metronome synchronization on a shared platform.

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s 2013 [[gh-formulation|formulation]]: *"a system that organizes sensory noise creating coherency from an initially random distribution of energy."*

## Emotion as Axon-Nerve Frequency

An early SAN claim from the same note: **emotion and pain are axon-nerve frequencies** that calibrate the sensitivity of the intelligence system. Rather than being orthogonal to cognition, emotional tone is encoded in oscillatory frequency patterns along axons, making the network fine-tuned to salient stimuli. This prefigures the [[napot]] treatment of affect as a frequency-domain signal.

## Synchronization Analogy

The metronome synchronization phenomenon (Huygens, 1665) — where independently ticking metronomes on a shared surface entrain each other — was invoked in the same discussion as a physical analogy. A "hidden force in the network" (mechanical vibration through the shared platform) drives coherence. In neural terms, the shared platform is the extracellular medium and [[field-coupling]] between neurons.
