# Pyramidal Cell

A **pyramidal cell** is an excitatory projection neuron found in the cerebral cortex, hippocampus, and related structures. The name refers to its characteristic soma and polarized dendritic tree, but pyramidal cells are not one uniform cell type. They differ by cortical area and layer, gene expression, morphology, physiology, input, and axonal projection target.

## Core anatomy

A cortical pyramidal neuron commonly has:

- **basal dendrites** extending around the soma;
- an **apical dendrite** that ascends toward superficial layers and may form a tuft;
- a **soma** that receives the combined membrane influence of dendritic and local inputs;
- an **axon initial segment**, where many action potentials begin; and
- an **axon** with local collaterals and, for projection neurons, longer-range targets.

Dendrites are receiving and integrating compartments. They can support active local events, but they are not output terminals. The axon is the principal route by which the cell sends action potentials to other neurons. Calling an apical dendrite an "exit terminal" reverses this anatomical distinction.

## Diversity matters

Layer 2/3 intratelencephalic neurons, layer 5 intratelencephalic neurons, layer 5 pyramidal-tract neurons, corticothalamic neurons, and hippocampal pyramidal neurons have different projection patterns and physiology. A generic cortical pyramid drawn across layers should therefore be treated as a teaching abstraction, not a universal canonical circuit.

Apical input can influence perception and behavior in specific cell classes. For example, [Takahashi et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-0677-8) manipulated apical dendritic activity in identified mouse layer 5 somatosensory projection classes during tactile detection. [Xu et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11601) studied nonlinear integration of sensory and motor-related inputs in mouse layer 5 pyramidal dendrites during active touch. These results do not license a universal layer-to-function map for the whole cortex.

## Cellular output

Synaptic and dendritic activity changes membrane voltage. When conditions at the axon initial segment cross the relevant threshold, an action potential is initiated and propagates along the axon; it may also backpropagate into the soma and dendrites. Bursts can arise in some pyramidal classes through interactions among somatic, axonal, and dendritic conductances.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can treat a pyramidal cell as one participant in a receive-integrate-project motif: dendritic compartments gather and transform inputs, the AIS converts cellular state into action potentials, and axonal branches distribute effects to other cells. No single pyramidal neuron contains a full percept, and no one layer is the hidden viewer. Observer-action arises from the recurrent activity of many cells, sensory pathways, internal state, and motor consequences.
