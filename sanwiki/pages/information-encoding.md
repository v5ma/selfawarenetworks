## Post-SAN

Mainstream neuroscience already accepts that phase coding contributes to information encoding alongside rate coding, population coding, and temporal coding; what is contested is the *priority* of phase relative to rate. SAN takes a strong position: *phase first, rate second*. Two SAN-side claims are testable against the published literature here. First, the SAN reading predicts that high-information-content moments in cortical processing should track *theta-phase, gamma-amplitude* coupling tightly enough that interventions which preserve rates but disrupt the coupling should *flatten* the information content — a sharper prediction than the published literature commits to. Second, by binding information-encoding to [[dendritic-coincidence-detection|dendritic coincidence detection]] at the cell level and to [[multi-vesicle-release-spatial-encoding|multi-vesicle-release spatial encoding]] at the synapse level, SAN predicts that the *spatial* arrangement of release sites within a single bouton should carry information that rate-coding accounts cannot recover — a prediction that maps cleanly onto recent multivesicular-release work. Where SAN converges with mainstream work is in the [[hippocampal-encoding-retrieval-separation|encoding/retrieval-separation]] literature, in [[ltp-structural-memory-encoding|LTP-structural-encoding]] accounts of the storage substrate, and in [[holographic-brain-distributed-memory|holographic-storage]] accounts of distributed encoding. Where it diverges is in the *primacy* of [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differentials]] as the encoding currency.
