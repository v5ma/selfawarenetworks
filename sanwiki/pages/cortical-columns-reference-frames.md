# Cortical Columns and Reference Frames

A moving organism must recognize a stable object, place, or situation while its sensory samples continuously change. Cortical populations can contribute to this problem by maintaining context, registering new features relative to that context, and exchanging updates with other populations and motor systems.

> **Evidence boundary:** Columnar and laminar organization are established features of neocortex, but columns vary across areas and species. Treating every column as a grid-cell module, physical ring, complete object model, universal NAPOT projector, or identical six-layer circuit is a computational hypothesis rather than established anatomy.

## Established organization

A cortical column is a local anatomical and functional motif, not a sealed processor with one fixed circuit. Neurons in a cortical patch may share inputs or response properties, while axons and dendrites also cross proposed column boundaries and connect across layers, areas, thalamic nuclei, and other subcortical systems.

- Layer 4 is a major thalamorecipient layer in primary sensory cortex, but thalamic and cortical inputs also reach other layers.
- Layers 2/3 support extensive local, horizontal, callosal, and corticocortical interactions.
- Layer 5 contains diverse pyramidal projection classes with cortical, striatal, brainstem, and other subcortical targets.
- Layer 6 contains diverse corticothalamic and corticocortical projection populations.
- Layer 1 contains apical dendrites, interneurons, neuromodulatory inputs, and long-range axons; it is not merely an empty cap.

The phrase **canonical cortical circuit** is a comparative template. It does not mean that visual, auditory, somatosensory, association, motor, and prefrontal cortex instantiate one unchanged wiring diagram.

## Five objects that must not be conflated

1. **Cortical column:** a proposed local anatomical or functional grouping.
2. **Reference frame:** a coordinate origin, axes or relations, transformation rule, and update process that allow one variable to be represented relative to another.
3. **Orientation hypercolumn:** a visual-cortex organization in which local populations span orientation preferences. Its map varies across species and is not a universal cortical ring.
4. **Ring attractor or ring manifold:** a circular topology in population state space. Neurons need not be physically arranged in a circle.
5. **Oscillatory phase:** timing relative to a periodic reference. Phase can modulate communication or encode information, but it is not automatically orientation, location, or position on a neural manifold.

The original image collapsed all five into one anatomical object. A scientifically usable account must measure them separately.

## Hawkins reference-frame hypothesis

Hawkins and colleagues propose that cortical columns use grid-cell-like location codes to represent features relative to objects and combine models across columns. This gives movement and sensorimotor change a direct role in object-centered knowledge. Cortical spatial tuning, mixed selectivity, and grid-like signatures make parts of the proposal testable, but they do not establish that every column implements one grid mechanism.

Hawkins's proposal is the immediate conceptual donor for Micah's 2022 reference-frame notes. SAN then adds a different question: whether a relatively persistent local or large-scale neural state can function as the receiver-relative context against which faster sensory, mnemonic, inhibitory, and motor-related changes are registered.

## Source-faithful SAN interpretation

### 1. Nested contexts rather than one master frame

SAN proposes that reference-dependent processing may recur across scales:

**ongoing receiver state + sampled input + movement or task context -> local transformation -> recurrent redistribution -> revised receiver state -> action and returned consequence**

At the micro scale, a neuron integrates inputs relative to membrane, synaptic, and dendritic state. At the meso scale, a local population can occupy a task-dependent activity state. At the macro scale, distributed cortical, hippocampal, thalamic, subcortical, bodily, and sensory systems exchange partial state. Functional similarity across scales does not imply identical anatomy or one universal algorithm.

### 2. Persistent context and faster differences

Established research shows that cortical areas differ in intrinsic timescale and that feedback and feedforward influences can favor different frequency bands in specific primate visual tasks. SAN's additional proposition is that a receiver's relatively persistent state can serve as context while faster deviations revise represented content.

This must not become a fixed frequency table. A low frequency does not by itself prove a longer represented horizon, and no named EEG band is universally a reference frame. The relevant measurements are the receiver population, state persistence, represented variable, update event, direction of influence, and behavioral consequence.

### 3. Ring topology as a candidate state geometry

Population recordings have revealed ring-like attractor manifolds for particular variables such as head direction. This supports the distinction between physical layout and population-state topology. SAN's 2022 persistent-ring intuition can therefore be tested as a geometry of activity: does the receiver return to nearby learned states, maintain a state until an informative change, and move along a reproducible manifold when context changes?

The answer cannot be inferred from a circular drawing, orientation pinwheel, oscillatory waveform, or grid-like fMRI signature alone.

### 4. Receiver-relative coordination

A local population does not align a private coordinate map and send it to an inner viewer. Cross-column and long-range interactions can change which signals are effective at each receiver, which population state is maintained, and which motor route reaches threshold.

SAN's tonic-canvas/phasic-ink language names proposed operational roles:

- **tonic context or canvas:** the receiver's ongoing, differentiating population state;
- **phasic difference or ink:** a departure that changes the receiver, routing, or downstream action;
- **recurrent update:** the changed state returned through local and long-range loops;
- **observer-action:** the coordinated network's perception and consequence, not a separate entity watching a map.

The proposed [[phase-wave-differentials|PWD operational definition]] adds a measurable burden. It must name the carrier, reference state, receiving population, event window, timing, magnitude, duration, and downstream effect. "Phase" cannot stand simultaneously for orientation preference, a manifold coordinate, spike timing, and field-potential phase.

## What the quarantined image was trying to say

The failed image was trying to show a useful operation:

**local reference-dependent populations -> cross-population coordination -> distributed perceptual update**

It failed medically and conceptually because it depicted three identical laminar modules with literal hypercolumn rings, treated orientation and phase as the same ring coordinate, assigned universal input/alignment/output roles to L4, L2/3, and L5/6, and ended at a separate coherent perceptual map.

A future replacement should place four layers of explanation side by side rather than merging them:

1. measured area-, layer-, and cell-specific anatomy;
2. an abstract population-state geometry;
3. receiver-relative timing and communication;
4. the SAN nested-context hypothesis and its behavioral test.

The rejected image remains quarantined and must not return to publication.

## Discriminating tests

1. **Timescale test:** During controlled changes in identity, viewpoint, movement, and task rule, determine whether a slower or more persistent population state improves decoding of faster feature samples beyond generic recurrent-network models.
2. **Geometry test:** Test whether the declared population occupies a ring, torus, line, discrete cluster set, or unconstrained manifold. Do not infer topology from anatomy or a schematic.
3. **Phase-routing test:** Perturb a measured phase relation while controlling rate and stimulus. Test whether receiver-specific communication and behavior change as predicted.
4. **Nested-frame test:** Hold local sensory content constant while changing object-relative, body-relative, or task-relative coordinates. Determine which populations encode each relation and how transformations propagate.
5. **Cross-scale test:** Compare the same receive-context-transform-update signature at cellular, local-population, and distributed-network scales. Reject a proposed functional isomorphism where the causal interfaces or predictions do not match.
6. **Action test:** A reference-frame model must predict route selection, movement, report, or another downstream consequence, not only decodable neural geometry.

## Correction record

The earlier scientific rewrite correctly removed a universal hypercolumn ring, fixed laminar input/output overgeneralization, and rejected the image's final perceptual-map implication. It under-preserved four SAN operations: nested reference contexts, persistent context versus faster differences, ring-like state persistence, and cross-scale functional self-similarity.

This revision restores those operations with their actual chronology and separates them from the later tonic-canvas language. The old neural-network/quantum-field-theory analogy is not restored as a cortical mechanism because no source-grade anatomical or physiological bridge was recovered. Neural manifolds remain on the page only as measured population-state geometry.
