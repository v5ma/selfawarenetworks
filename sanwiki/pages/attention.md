# Attention as Distributed Selection

> **Scientific boundary:** Attention is not one beta command, gamma gate, inhibitory winner, thalamic switch, cortical spotlight, or consciousness mechanism. Experiments identify several forms of selection whose anatomy and measured effects depend on stimulus, task, species, circuit, and behavioral endpoint.

## Operational definition

Attention is a change in which information has enough causal influence to alter later neural processing, judgment, memory, sampling, or action under otherwise matched conditions.

This definition does not make attention identical to looking, recognition, confidence, report, conscious access, or phenomenal consciousness. It also does not require unattended candidates to disappear.

The recovered SAN idea is therefore not a spotlight watched by an inner viewer. The attended state is the distributed change in which sensory, mnemonic, evaluative, and motor relations can affect the network's next state.

## Established component mechanisms

### Competition and gain

When competing stimuli occupy a broad receptive field, attention can shift a recorded response toward the pattern produced by the attended stimulus ([Moran and Desimone, 1985](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.4023713); [Reynolds, Chelazzi, and Desimone, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.19-05-01736.1999)). Mean firing, normalization, shared variability, and behavioral performance are related but separable measurements.

### Movement-related bias

Subthreshold frontal-eye-field stimulation can enhance location-matched V4 responses without evoking a saccade ([Moore and Armstrong, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01341)). This establishes one causal sensory-motor relation, not a central executive or a complete theory of attention.

### Thalamic routing

Pulvinar interactions with connected visual cortical areas change with spatial attention ([Saalmann et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1223082)). Other thalamic circuits adjust gain or effective cortical connectivity in different tasks. These are circuit-specific operations, not one thalamic chooser inspecting completed representations.

### Midbrain selection

Reversible superior-colliculus inactivation impaired which sensory signal controlled a covert perceptual judgment even without a required orienting movement ([Lovejoy and Krauzlis, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2470)). A distributed account must therefore include cortical and subcortical routes and distinguish an attention-correlated signature from causal control of behavior.

## Oscillations, inhibition, and dendrites

Oscillatory coherence, inhibitory and disinhibitory circuits, dendritic state, neuromodulation, recurrent input, and common drive can all change effective influence. None is a universal synonym for attention.

In macaque V4, attention to a behaviorally relevant stimulus increased gamma-range synchronization and reduced lower-frequency synchronization at nearby sites ([Fries et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1055465)). In macaque V1, however, attention reduced stimulus-driven gamma power and spike-field coherence in a different task and preparation ([Chalk et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.03.013)). Gamma is therefore a circuit- and task-dependent observable, not a universal selected-content carrier.

Beta activity can participate in top-down control without becoming a one-band law of thought. In one macaque spatial-attention study, beta-band influence from parietal area 7a to V1 preceded and covaried with gamma-band influence from V1 to V4 ([Richter et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3771-16.2017)). That result supports a specific cross-frequency route. It does not establish that all beta originates in prefrontal cortex, that cortical layer 4 is intrinsically a beta "thinking layer," or that all feedforward evidence is gamma.

The same discipline applies to inhibition and dendrites. An inhibitory neuron does not mechanically reroute one spike down a different axon; inhibitory and disinhibitory circuits alter gain, timing, competition, and accessibility in target populations. Dendritic integration can alter whether and how a cell responds, but no one canonical laminar circuit is universal across cortical areas, cell types, species, and tasks.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats selection as receiver-relative change within the distributed observer-action system. Its source-faithful task-assembly hypothesis can be stated as:

```text
context, preparation, and current tonic state
-> several partially active candidate relations or assemblies
-> phasic evidence, learned tuning, feedback, inhibition, and disinhibition
-> receiver-specific changes in gain, timing, and effective connectivity
-> transient recruitment of a task-relevant sensory-to-action route
-> judgment, memory, report, resampling, or movement
-> sensory and bodily consequences update the next cycle
```

The tonic canvas is a proposed maintained receiver context, and phasic ink is a proposed differentiated update. SAN further proposes that phase-compatible PWD events can increase the effective drive of one recruited route while structured phase separation and inhibition reduce competing influence. That proposal must be tested against firing-rate, normalization, recurrent-state, connectivity, and movement-based explanations. No dominant phase differential automatically becomes conscious content.

### From selection to action

Bat vocalization supplies a concrete, bounded example of oscillatory state preceding action. Before vocalization, frontal and dorsal-striatal beta and high-gamma activity, theta coupling, and frontal gamma spike-LFP phase locking differed according to whether the next act was echolocation or communication ([Weineck, Garcia-Rosales, and Hechavarria, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000658)). A companion study found predominantly frontal-to-auditory information flow before vocalization and stronger auditory-to-frontal flow after an echolocation pulse returned ([Garcia-Rosales et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31230-6)).

Those studies measured pre-action power, phase relation, coupling, action class, and directional flow. They reported correlates and predictions, not proof that the oscillations caused the call. SAN's added hypothesis is that these jointly measured variables can be treated as an operational trace of building **electrical valence**: signed, receiver-specific effective drive through which a distributed timing pattern gains control of a motor route. The complete route still includes subcortical and brainstem motor circuits, motor nerves, neuromuscular transmission, calcium release, and ATP-powered muscle contraction. Neural coincidence organizes recruitment; it does not supply the mechanical energy of movement.

## Attention is not conscious access

Rapid identity decoding, attentional modulation, late recurrence, confidence, broad access, explicit report, and no-report proxies are different endpoints. Gamma-band activity and the P3 can track post-perceptual task and report demands rather than awareness itself ([Pitts et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.07.024)).

A SAN experiment must independently vary evidence, relevance, distractor strength, motor mapping, report demand, and visibility. It should specify the receiver and causal consequence, then test selective perturbation and rescue.

## Image status

The earlier phase-coherence selection-gate image is preserved in the failed-medical-review archive and is not embedded here. It incorrectly universalized a layered cortical microcircuit, assigned beta and gamma fixed anatomical roles, depicted a winner as directly suppressing alternatives, and made one coherent phase pattern enter conscious processing. Its useful recoverable idea is a new, medically bounded figure showing **distributed candidate populations -> circuit-specific competition and routing -> selected sensory or motor consequence -> feedback**, with the historical SAN map-to-action and Neural Tuning hypotheses labeled separately from established physiology.
