Phase-Differential Rendering is the SAN proposal that a nervous system constructs and updates perceptual content through receiver-relative changes in ongoing neural activity. It combines a broad, established fact - neural responses are contextual and time-dependent - with a stronger hypothesis about phase, magnitude, inhibition, and recurrent routing.

## Start without the jargon

The brain is already active before a new sensory event arrives. A new event changes activity relative to that ongoing state. Different cells and circuits respond according to their learned structure, current excitability, inhibition, feedback, and task context. Their outputs alter downstream and recurrent activity, producing a new state that can guide memory, report, and action.

SAN calls the repeated construction and revision of that usable state [[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]]. Rendering is not a literal display watched by a separate observer. The cells that change state are part of the system whose later recognition and behavior are changed.

## What a phase-wave differential means

A [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] (PWD) is a proposed difference between an ongoing reference state and an event-related neural pattern, evaluated by particular receiving cells or populations. Candidate variables include relative timing, phase, frequency content, amplitude, burst duration, inhibition, waveform, and spatial propagation.

The term does not mean that one frequency equals one percept. It also does not license combining every timing-related variable into one undefined quantity. Each experiment must specify which variable is measured, at which scale, by which receiver, and with what predicted effect.

## Tonic and phasic are relative roles

In the strongest usable formulation, tonic and phasic are functional roles relative to a current baseline, not permanent labels for fixed frequency bands.

- A tonic state is the comparatively persistent activity against which a change is evaluated.
- A phasic event is a transient, consequential departure that updates or challenges that state.
- Inhibition, disinhibition, slow potentials, spikes, bursts, and traveling waves can contribute differently; they are not synonyms.
- Magnitude must be operationalized. Amplitude, duration, spike count, release probability, and postsynaptic consequence are related but distinct measurements.

This preserves the earlier mental-ink and canvas insight without assigning all slow activity to canvas and all fast activity to ink.

## Receive, transform, and project

The NAPOT version of the account uses a recurring operation:

1. dendrites and receptors receive structured input;
2. learned cellular state determines which combinations are consequential;
3. local integration and inhibition transform the response;
4. axonal output, terminal dynamics, chemical release, and bounded local field effects project or
   modulate consequences, while glial and vascular state can alter the surrounding conditions;
5. downstream and recurrent populations interpret those consequences relative to their own state; and
6. action and new sensory feedback update the next cycle.

Ephaptic coupling may contribute locally alongside synaptic, axonal, gap-junction, glial, vascular, and neuromodulatory routes. It should not be treated as the sole global stabilizer.

## Redness and broccoli as different tests

[[theory-of-redness|Theory of Redness]] asks whether this architecture can explain one qualitative distinction without defining the experience into existence.

[[broccoli-shared-recognition-artificial-perception|The Broccoli example]] asks whether the same architecture can bind color, shape, texture, smell, taste, language, location, and action while allowing differently organized brains to recognize the same public object. It then turns the model into a BCI problem: what causal intervention could recreate one part of that distributed state?

## Thalamic boundary

Thalamic nuclei are major, heterogeneous participants in sensory relay, cortical state, attention, and recurrent coordination. They are not one universal semantic start and endpoint. Different sensory pathways, cortical and subcortical loops, and direct peripheral or cortical intervention targets must be analyzed separately.

A thalamic phase-intervention experiment is one possible test of SAN. Failure or success at one nucleus would not settle every form of artificial perception.

## Alternatives and falsifiers

The stronger PWD account must be compared with firing-rate models, recurrent population-state models, synchrony measures, predictive-processing accounts, representational geometry, and ordinary effective-connectivity explanations.

Evidence would be stronger if a preregistered phase-aware model:

- predicts held-out perception or action better than matched alternatives;
- survives controls for firing rate, stimulus information, attention, and movement;
- identifies a receiver-specific causal perturbation; and
- predicts the direction and recovery of the induced change.

If phase-aware variables add no explanatory or causal value after those controls, the stronger rendering account must be revised.
