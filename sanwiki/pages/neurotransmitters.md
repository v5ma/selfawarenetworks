# Neurotransmitters

**Neurotransmitters** are chemical messengers released at [synapses](synapse). Standard neuroscience describes them as molecules packaged into vesicles, released from a presynaptic cell, bound by receptors on a postsynaptic cell, and then removed or degraded after signaling ([NCBI Bookshelf, *Neuroscience*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10795/)). In the [Self Aware Network](self-aware-network) framing, their quantity, timing, and identity are treated as the molecular layer where [SDR](sparse-distributed-representation)-like code becomes a physical synaptic event.

## Origin

The SAN packet ties neurotransmitters to the earliest [NAPOT](napot-neural-array-phase-tomography) account of molecular coding. The `06san` source says that local field potentials are adjusted by "varying quantities of neurotransmitters" at regular and irregular intervals (`06san--md`, line 1, [on GitHub](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/06san.md)). The `a0001z` note connects variation in [action-potential duration](action-potential-duration) to variation in neurotransmitter release and says that NAPOT Revision 2 makes those varying levels carry the SDR exit-terminal code (`a0001z--md`, line 71, [on GitHub](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0001z.md)).

The mechanism is stated through the exit-terminal picture in `a0008z`: open terminals determine the quantity of vesicle sacks filled with neurotransmitters that are released (`a0008z--md`, line 33, [on GitHub](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0008z.md)). A second early note places the process in a broader electrochemical substrate, describing neurotransmitters binding to open channels while ion movement changes polarization (`a0002z--md`, line 30, [on GitHub](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0002z.md)).

## Position

Inside the SAN architecture, neurotransmitters are the molecular event by which [waveform-encoded](action-potential-waveform-encoding) differences become synaptic output. The `07san` chapter describes a cell outputting new information as a change in neurotransmitter release (`07san--md`, line 11, [on GitHub](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/07san.md)). The page therefore treats change in release, not only baseline release, as the informational unit that SAN maps upward into [brainwave](brainwave) and [consciousness](consciousness) claims.

The same packet connects SAN to mainstream synaptic-efficacy language. The `a0007z` source says synaptic efficacy depends on release probability, amount of neurotransmitter released, and postsynaptic receptor activation (`a0007z--md`, lines 308-309, [on GitHub](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/a0007z.md)). NCBI's discussion of presynaptic receptors similarly describes synaptic transmission efficacy as governed by release probability, transmitter amount, receptor type and number, and receptor response ([NCBI Bookshelf, *Biology of the NMDA Receptor*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK5275/)).

SAN also uses neurotransmitter species as distinct possible signal channels. The packet states that neurons can release more than one neurotransmitter type and that this matters to SAN only if different transmitters carry different signals at different times (`a0001z--md`, line 71). It also names [GABA](gaba) as an inhibitory neurotransmitter sensed through ionotropic GABA-A and metabotropic GABA-B receptors (`a0007z--md`, line 919), placing inhibitory transmission inside the same [ion-channel](synaptic-ion-channel-tone) and release framework.

Robert Lawrence Kuhn's 2024 Cell Press review, *A landscape of consciousness*, includes neurotransmitter release among cellular calcium-ion processes that can trigger further release (`dl-oct6-2025--A_landscape_of_consciousness_Toward_a_taxonomy_of_explanations_and_implications_Robert_Lawrence_Kuhn_1-s2.0-S0079610723001128-main`, paper-external).

The contrast is therefore interpretive rather than merely terminological. Mainstream sources use transmitter release as one variable in synaptic and cellular computation, while SAN treats release magnitude, timing, and transmitter identity as the proposed encoding currency beneath conscious rendering. That stronger SAN claim remains a hypothesis, but it is anchored in real synaptic mechanisms rather than in an abstract spike-rate-only model.
