# Criteria Causation

**Criteria Causation** is, in the [[Self-Aware-Networks|Self-Aware Networks]] (SAN) corpus, the SAN-flavored reading of Peter Tse's *The Neural Basis of Free Will: [[Criterial-Causation|Criterial Causation]]* (MIT Press, 2013) — the thesis that neurons act as informationally configurable [[criteria-causation-single-neuron-pattern-detection|single-neuron pattern detectors]] whose firing [[criteria]] are themselves set by past activity, so that subsequent choices and [[actions]] are caused by *what counts as a match* rather than by a billiard-ball bottom-up cascade. The term sits as a variant beside [[Criterial-Causation]], [[criterial-causation]], [[criterial causation]], [[criterial-causation-atp-neurons|criterial causation (ATP/neurons)]], and [[criterial-causation-quantum-retrocausality|criterial-causation with quantum retrocausality]] — collectively a hub topic the project treats as central to its account of [[choice]], [[Consciousness]], and the [[illusion-of-choice|illusion of choice]].

The mechanism is described concretely in the corpus at the synapse: "post synapse adjusting the firing criteria for the post-synaptic [[dendrite]]. This is the mechanics of criteria causation or information based change. The information that makes up thought is causing biological" change ([[gh-a0166z|a0166z]]--md, line 11).
