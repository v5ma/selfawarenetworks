# Oscillating-Group Completion and Re-expression

**Oscillating-group completion and re-expression** is a 2022 SAN proposal for how a partial cue may recruit a learned distributed group and produce a reconstructed internal or motor consequence.

The proposed chain is:

```text
partial cue
-> recruitment of a learned oscillating group
-> completion or reconstruction under current state
-> pattern re-expression
-> internal, physiological, or motor output
```

The complete chain is a SAN hypothesis. Its component mechanisms have experimental support, but observing an engram, oscillation, ripple, burst, or learned movement does not by itself confirm oscillating-group completion.

## What may be retained locally

Rejecting a literal stored movie does not require treating a dendrite as generic readiness with no structured contribution. Learning can change the number, position, strength, and coordination of synapses on selected dendritic branches.

Motor learning in mice produced clustered new dendritic spines that were more persistent than isolated new spines ([Fu et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10844)). Sleep after motor learning promoted task-related spine formation on selected branches ([Yang et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1249098)). Different motor tasks recruited branch-specific dendritic calcium spikes, and those events produced persistent plasticity at coactive spines ([Cichon and Gan, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14251)). Learning-related spinogenesis can also preserve functional clustering of task-related inputs ([Hedrick et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01086-6)).

These results support learned, spatially organized dendritic constraints. They do not demonstrate that one dendrite contains an arbitrary lossless three-dimensional image.

The bounded SAN proposal is that a learned dendritic configuration may function as:

- a local relational constraint;
- a nonlinear feature conjunction;
- an addressable projection into a larger learned circuit;
- a cue-sensitive transformation; or
- one basis contribution to distributed reconstruction.

Its capacity depends on synapse count, branch nonlinearities, state richness, noise, effective rank, correlated inputs, and redundancy across the network. Gross numbers of synapses are not equivalent to independently recoverable voxels.

## Group recruitment is not a permanent address

Engram experiments establish different operations: learning-recall overlap, necessity, sufficiency, induced association, access rescue, and systems reorganization. They do not show that one tagged group is the entire remembered experience.

Direct activation of learning-tagged dentate-gyrus cells can elicit a learned behavioral response under controlled conditions ([Liu et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11028)). Distributed engram ensembles across multiple regions can contribute jointly to recall ([Roy et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29384-4)). Exact cellular representations can also drift while population information or behavior remains useful ([Ziv et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329); [Driscoll et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.07.021)).

An oscillating group should therefore be defined through a declared state, timing relation, membership rule, receiver consequence, and null model. It need not have immutable membership.

## Completion is not playback

Hippocampal population events can re-express, reverse, compress, and construct ordered trajectories. Some decoded events reroute around new barriers rather than replaying only paths previously traveled ([Widloski and Foster, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.02.002)). This supports constructive sequence generation, not a complete sensory recording played to an inner viewer.

In the SAN interpretation, a cue changes the same distributed system that subsequently predicts, remembers, reports, or acts. The reconstructed state is the network's changed operation. There is no separate observer watching an internal display.

## Re-expression must name an output

Re-expression may be measured as:

- a dendritic or somatic event in a downstream receiver;
- changed spike probability, latency, or burst structure;
- changed transmitter release or postsynaptic response;
- recruitment of another population;
- an internal decision variable;
- a physiological response; or
- a motor action.

A neuron need not transmit the complete spatial arrangement of its dendritic inputs as an axonal image payload. The testable proposal is that activation of a learned local configuration changes a typed output which helps recruit a larger distributed state.

## Recovering the 2022 "photocopier" proposal

The historical word *photocopier* names expansion through gain and routing, not literal duplication of a complete memory from one neuron into every downstream cell. In the medically bounded reconstruction, the operation is:

```text
learned local cellular state
-> changed timing, burst structure, or output to actual postsynaptic targets
-> circuit-specific excitatory and inhibitory recruitment
-> competition and ordered activation of further receivers
-> distributed pattern re-expression
-> internal, physiological, or motor consequence
```

Micah's [June 10, 2022 a0111z revision](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/84f9d0a56a47fec4b427ac6422062902f39776dd/a0111z.md#L18-L22) describes a local memory-related difference being "magnified" through downstream timing and inhibition into an oscillating-group phase rendering. The [June 11, 2022 a0129z revision](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/ed4d6d5341bed45eff614967634dbdc617893f5a/a0129z.md#L2-L24) expands that intuition into a sequence of receiving arrays, phasic recognition events, inhibition, changing phase relations, and proposed dissipation.

The source's direct-inhibition wording must be read through real circuit anatomy. Most cortical pyramidal neurons are excitatory; where they suppress competitors, they ordinarily do so by exciting inhibitory interneurons or through other circuit-specific routes. In layer 5 neocortex, trains from individual pyramidal cells can recruit Martinotti-cell-mediated disynaptic inhibition of neighboring pyramidal cells ([Silberberg and Markram, 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17329212/)). In the dentate gyrus, identified PV and SST interneurons have distinct, use-dependent effects on winner-take-all dynamics ([Hainmueller et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42013854/)). Neither result licenses a universal rule for every neuron, region, or oscillation.

Three stronger statements remain SAN hypotheses rather than established consequences: jointly inhibited cells need not become one stable tonic oscillator; desynchronization does not automatically induce LTD; and a phase-structured cascade is not proved merely because activity spreads. SAN predicts that receiver-relative timing, tonic context, and the complete PWD description will explain or control re-expression beyond firing rate, connectivity, and ordinary sequence models.

## Five 2022 routes

The proposal should retain five connected routes without turning them into settled anatomy:

1. **Array-to-array transformation:** each receiver transforms incoming activity according to its current state.
2. **Cortical-column assumptions:** a column may supply one circuit context, but there is no established universal column algorithm.
3. **Object and location coding:** object, location, body, and action relations are distributed across populations.
4. **Apical/basal comparison:** separated dendritic domains support contextual integration, but basal does not universally mean evidence and apical does not universally mean expectation.
5. **Distributed rendering:** participating cells and fields undergo the reconstruction; no localized viewer receives it.

These stages are related but not interchangeable. The later group-completion, NAPOT, and typed-PWD formulations are not backdated into the earliest light-field note.

## Decisive experiment

Compare capacity-matched models using current state and history, firing rate, a conventional latent state, engram membership, ordered sequence features, dendritic or branch-constraint features, oscillating-group recruitment, phase-only timing, the complete typed PWD event, and the full SAN reconstruction model.

The SAN model gains support only if it improves held-out reconstruction of declared object, location, body, action, or future-endpoint relations; generalizes across sessions despite membership drift; and predicts selective perturbation and rescue. Perturbations must separate dendritic constraint, group recruitment, sequence order, burst structure, phase relation, and generic excitability.

Failure of the complete model would not show that memory is unstructured or that timing never matters. It would reject the tested SAN conjunction at the declared scale and endpoint.
