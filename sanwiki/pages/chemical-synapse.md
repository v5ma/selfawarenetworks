# Chemical Synapse

A **chemical synapse** is a receiver-specific transformation boundary. A presynaptic electrical event changes calcium entry and the probability and timing of vesicle fusion. Released transmitter then encounters a cleft, uptake processes, receptors, membrane voltage, and a postsynaptic cell whose present state conditions the result. The synapse therefore does not copy an unchanged voltage waveform, semantic token, or phase object from one neuron to another.

Its medically established operation is:

```text
presynaptic terminal event and terminal state
-> voltage-gated calcium entry
-> probabilistic vesicle fusion and transmitter release
-> cleft concentration, diffusion, and clearance
-> receptor-specific conductance and intracellular signaling
-> local postsynaptic response
-> dendritic, cellular, and circuit consequence
```

This staged transformation is the source-faithful interpretation of the early Self-Aware Networks claim that the chemical synapse is both electrochemical and computational.

## What the original SAN wording was trying to say

### 1. A 2012 charge-transformation question

An owner-dated note says it was created in September 2012; its wording first entered the current public Git history on June 8, 2022. Micah asked whether a chemical change in the brain could count as computation and wrote that transmitter changes charge conditions in the sending and receiving neurons, making a chemical synapse "also really an electrical synapse."

That phrase should not be retained as an anatomical equivalence. Chemical and electrical synapses are different junction types. The medically recoverable operation is that chemical transmission **transduces one electrochemical state into another**: presynaptic voltage controls release, transmitter controls receptor conductance, and receptor conductance changes postsynaptic current, voltage, signaling, and later excitability. The response in the source itself already distinguishes the two synapse classes and points to the richer sign, gain, receptor, and state dependence of chemical transmission.

### 2. A 2017 synapse-as-computation proposal

In the April 13, 2017 Neural Lace Podcast #2, whose transcript entered the current public Git history on October 22, 2022, Micah contrasted direct gap-junction current with chemical transmission and then asked whether "the synapse is like a computer" and whether the dendrite performs further computation.

The defensible operation is not that transmitter molecules carry more semantic information merely because they are chemically complex. It is that a chemical synapse implements a stateful, nonlinear, and receiver-dependent transform. Release probability, short-term facilitation or depression, cotransmission, receptor subtype, reversal potential, dendritic location, membrane state, and recent activity can all change the consequence of the same nominal presynaptic spike.

### 3. A June 2022 computational-density claim

On June 13, 2022, `a0249z.md` argued that comparisons between one synapse and one transistor miss both pre- and postsynaptic complexity and the parallelism of the larger network. The important scientific translation is not a transistor-equivalence number. It is that the synapse is an active computational component whose state and target help determine what a presynaptic event does.

### 4. An August 2022 population-phase extension

On August 11, 2022, `a0270z.md` proposed that chemical and electrical transmission ultimately contribute to phase changes in an oscillating population relative to its tonic activity. The sentence is too literal when read as saying that a phase change is the physical entity crossing a chemical cleft.

The recoverable SAN operation is cross-scale:

```text
local synaptic transformation
-> changed probability, sign, latency, and timing of receiver activity
-> changed excitatory-inhibitory interaction across a circuit
-> possible phase advance, delay, locking, dispersion, or route change in a declared population
```

Primary studies show that chemical inhibitory and excitatory conductances can perturb spike phase and help organize gamma-frequency synchrony in specific preparations. That supports a local-to-population bridge. It does not establish one universal brainwave carrier, NAPOT, PWD, or consciousness.

### 5. An August 2022 waveform-to-release extension

A recording transcript publicly fixed on August 28, 2022 connected presynaptic potassium-channel state, action-potential waveform, calcium entry, and vesicle release. Later physiology supports the central direction of this proposal: bouton waveform and calcium-channel state can regulate synaptic efficacy. The transcript's suggested fixed vesicle-number range is not retained as a universal code. Release is probabilistic, synapse-specific, history-dependent, and potentially multivesicular.

### 6. A later developmental conjecture

A later recording, transcribed and summarized in 2023, conjectured that an electrical synapse was once "inside" every chemical synapse. The medically accurate bounded relation is narrower. Transient electrical coupling can precede or influence chemical synapse formation in some developing or regenerating circuits, and mixed chemical-electrical contacts exist. This does not show that every chemical synapse passes through that sequence or retains a hidden gap junction.

## Established physiology

At a declared synapse, a useful schematic dependency is:

```text
p_fusion(t) = F(terminal voltage, terminal calcium, vesicle-pool state,
                recent activity, presynaptic modulation)
```

For a receptor class `r`, one conventional current description is:

```text
I_r(t) = g_r(t) [V_m(t) - E_r]
```

Here `g_r` depends on transmitter exposure and receptor kinetics, `V_m` is local membrane voltage, and `E_r` is the receptor's reversal potential under a declared sign convention. These equations are dependency summaries, not universal single-synapse laws.

Established results include:

- Vesicle release is quantal and probabilistic rather than a deterministic one-spike/one-packet transfer.
- Transmitter release depends nonlinearly on presynaptic calcium, vesicle availability, coupling geometry, and recent activity.
- Presynaptic action-potential waveform can change calcium entry and release efficacy; the bouton waveform need not equal the somatic waveform.
- Cleft concentration is transient and preparation-specific. Receptor binding, unbinding, desensitization, transporters, diffusion, and extracellular geometry shape the postsynaptic current.
- The same transmitter can have different effects through different receptor subtypes, reversal potentials, compartments, and cellular states.
- Short-term facilitation and depression make a synapse sensitive to recent input history. Connection and target-cell classes can have different dynamics.
- Electrical synapses pass current through gap-junction channels. Chemical synapses use transmitter release and receptor transduction. Both can coexist in a circuit, but they are not interchangeable structures.

## The bounded SAN hypothesis

SAN can retain a stronger, testable proposition without treating phase as cleft cargo:

> A chemical synapse is a stateful receive-transform-project element. Its terminal waveform, release history, molecular state, receptor identity, receiver state, and relative timing jointly determine a distribution of postsynaptic consequences. Across an identified circuit, those transformations may alter receiver-relative phase, coupling, structured separation, and effective routing.

In this formulation, **tonic** means the declared receiver's current local and network context. A **phasic phase-wave differential** is a measured departure relative to that context. Neither is a molecule or waveform that remains unchanged while crossing the synapse.

The distinctive SAN question is whether a typed receiver-relative description adds predictive and causal value beyond conventional variables such as presynaptic spike time, firing rate, bouton waveform, release probability, receptor kinetics, short-term plasticity, connectivity, and postsynaptic state.

## Micro, meso, and macro scales

- **Micro:** terminal waveform, calcium, vesicles, cleft transmitter, receptors, conductance, local voltage, and intracellular signaling.
- **Meso:** identified excitatory and inhibitory circuit interactions, synaptic delays, short-term plasticity, spike timing, population phase, coupling, and dispersion.
- **Macro:** perception, memory, action, and observer-action require distributed recurrent systems, body state, and behavior. One synapse is neither a percept nor an observer.

A micro-scale synaptic event may contribute to a meso-scale rhythm, and a meso-scale rhythm may contribute to macro-scale behavior. Each transition needs its own measurement and causal evidence.

## A discriminating experiment

1. Declare one synapse class, postsynaptic cell class, circuit, task or preparation, frequency range, and behavioral or physiological endpoint.
2. Measure presynaptic terminal voltage, calcium, release or transmitter exposure, receptor-mediated current, postsynaptic voltage or spiking, and population timing where technically possible.
3. Fit a baseline model using spike time, rate, waveform, connection identity, short-term plasticity, receptor kinetics, and receiver state.
4. Add typed SAN variables such as receiver-relative phase, waveform-release covariance, tonic-state dependence, and signed phase advance or delay.
5. Perturb presynaptic potassium channels, calcium-release coupling, receptor kinetics, or inhibitory/excitatory timing separately and test predicted mediation at each stage.
6. Require held-out prediction, selective intervention, and mechanism-matched rescue. If the SAN variables add no reliable value or the proposed intermediate does not mediate the effect, narrow or reject the phase-routing extension for that preparation.

No result at this page's scale automatically establishes NAPOT, a transferred qualia, or consciousness.

## Image audit

The quarantined 2026 figure correctly attempted to show the terminal-calcium-release-cleft-receptor-response sequence. Its failure was the final shortcut from one postsynaptic voltage change to a boxed "phase event" or "brainwave/NAPOT signal."

It also drew one generic excitatory ionotropic route without labeling it as a bounded example, omitted release probability and receiver state, and collapsed micro-scale transduction into a meso-scale population result. The medically recoverable image concept is a **transformation ladder with an explicit scale boundary**: first show the stochastic, receptor-specific chemical synapse; then show separately how many identified synapses can alter the timing and phase organization of a measured circuit. The failed PNG and SVG remain quarantined. No replacement art will be generated while image work is paused.
