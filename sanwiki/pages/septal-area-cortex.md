# Septal Cortex and Septal Nuclei in the Olfactory-Limbic Network

> **Terminology recovery:** The phrase **septal area cortex** in the 2021 source fuses two neighboring but distinguishable labels. **Septal cortex** refers in some neuroanatomical descriptions to subcallosal cortical tissue in the septal region. **Septal area** or **septal nuclei** refers to deeper subcortical structures. The source was exploring both while moving between diagrams; it was not proposing a new cortical territory.

## What the source was trying to determine

In a November 6, 2021 discussion, Micah Blumberg was trying to trace how olfactory information reaches the rest of the brain. The conversation compared the olfactory bulb and tract, primary olfactory cortex, septal region, hippocampal and limbic structures, mediodorsal thalamus, and orbitofrontal or prefrontal cortex. The repeated question was anatomical and functional: which routes are direct, which are relayed, and where do smell, memory, value, emotion, and action begin to interact?

The wording being read closely matches a University of Wisconsin neuroanatomy handout that calls the subcallosal cortical component **septal cortex**, then separately lists the **septal area** among adjacent deep limbic structures ([Olfactory and Limbic handout](https://www.neuroanatomy.wisc.edu/coursebook/neuro3%282%29.pdf)). The recording then shifts to a search result that calls the septal area subcortical. The most source-faithful diagnosis is therefore a **compressed label fusion**, not evidence that Micah intended to rename the septal nuclei as cortex.

The discussion repeatedly says that the route is still being worked out. It should be read as an exploratory source question, not a settled SAN anatomical model. The source was recorded in 2021, first publicly Git-fixed in June 2022, and is preserved at [[gh-b0069y|b0069y]] ([immutable Git version](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/4e8cde5f687cc575b9c99dc181f53ea68305c4b4/b0069y.md)).

## The anatomical distinction

The **septal region** includes cortical and subcortical structures near the medial wall of the anterior forebrain. Clinical neuroanatomy distinguishes subcallosal septal cortex from deeper septal nuclei ([Hahn et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A2123)). The lateral septum is a midline subcortical structure, not neocortex; human single-nucleus work also shows that it contains multiple molecularly distinguishable neuronal populations ([Phillips et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.111820)).

These labels should not be collapsed:

- **Septal cortex or subcallosal cortical region:** cortical tissue beneath the rostrum of the corpus callosum in some limbic-system descriptions.
- **Medial septum and diagonal band:** basal-forebrain nuclei with cholinergic, GABAergic, and glutamatergic populations and strong hippocampal relations.
- **Lateral septum:** a predominantly subcortical relay and transformation region receiving major hippocampal input and projecting toward hypothalamic and brainstem systems.
- **Septum pellucidum:** a thin midline membrane, not a synonym for septal cortex or septal nuclei.

Anatomical proximity in a sagittal illustration does not establish a direct synaptic route or a shared function.

## Supported olfactory-limbic routes

Olfactory output is branching and recurrent rather than one mandatory chain.

1. **Bulb to primary olfactory and limbic targets.** In macaque tracing, olfactory-bulb axons reached multiple primary olfactory areas, including piriform, amygdalar, entorhinal, and related regions. The same study found limited bulbar labeling in the horizontal diagonal band and projections from primary olfactory cortex toward orbital and medial prefrontal areas ([Carmichael, Clugnet, and Price, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.903460306)).
2. **Direct and transthalamic routes to orbitofrontal cortex.** Human effective-connectivity work supports both a direct piriform-orbitofrontal relation and an indirect route through mediodorsal thalamus whose coupling changes with olfactory attention ([Plailly et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5607-07.2008)). This does not make thalamus the obligatory first relay before primary olfactory cortex.
3. **Olfactory relations with basal forebrain are subgroup-specific.** Viral tracing in mice found broad olfactory input to horizontal-diagonal-band and neighboring basal-forebrain cholinergic populations, whereas medial septum/vertical diagonal band received predominantly hippocampal input in that experiment ([Zheng et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2018.00099)). The septal system should therefore not be drawn as one uniform olfactory station.
4. **Septohippocampal timing is established in bounded preparations.** Identified medial-septal GABAergic neurons fire at distinct phases of hippocampal theta and can rhythmically organize hippocampal inhibition ([Borhegyi et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1413-04.2004)). Separately, rat recordings show that lateral septum can respond to the magnitude of hippocampal ripple-associated population activity and route it toward subcortical targets ([Tingley and Buzsaki, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.10.012)).

Together, these findings support a network of distinct cortical, basal-forebrain, thalamic, hippocampal, and subcortical routes. They do not support a single olfactory-bulb-to-septum-to-prefrontal serial pathway.

## Source-faithful SAN interpretation

The recoverable 2021 SAN operation is **route discovery across a distributed sensory-memory-value-action network**. No one location receives a complete smell. Different receiving populations transform partial information according to their anatomy, current state, learned connectivity, attention, memory, and behavioral context.

Later SAN language can interpret this as a receive-transform-project architecture in which distributed populations update one another and influence later sampling and action. That later interpretation must not be backdated into this recording as though the 2021 discussion already specified NAPOT, PWD, a tonic canvas, or one septal mechanism.

The bounded SAN hypothesis is that receiver-specific transformations across these recurrent routes contribute to the network's aggregate observer-action state. The established studies support component anatomy and physiology; they do not establish SAN's joined rendering account or consciousness claim.

## What this page does not claim

- The septal nuclei are not reclassified as cortex.
- Septal cortex and septal nuclei are not treated as one structure.
- Diagram adjacency is not treated as evidence of direct connectivity.
- Primary olfactory cortex is not equated with orbitofrontal or prefrontal cortex.
- Olfaction is not thalamus-free; it lacks an obligatory first-order thalamic relay before primary olfactory cortex.
- One neurotransmitter, one frequency, or one septal population is not assigned the identity or valence of an odor.
- A component circuit result is not presented as proof of NAPOT, PWD, qualia, or consciousness.
