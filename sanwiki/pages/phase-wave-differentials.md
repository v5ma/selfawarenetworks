## What the rejected figure was trying to say

The recoverable SAN idea is that an ongoing [[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|tonic context]]
defines a receiver's current readiness, while phasic changes can alter a named downstream receiver.
Compatible excitation, inhibition, timing, waveform, and recurrent state may support or suppress a
transition; their receiver-specific net consequence is related to the proposed
[[electrical-valence-receiver-relative-drive|Electrical Valence]] bridge.

The medically accurate version must not turn that proposal into one stable phase reference across
the cortex, a universal laminar circuit, an absolute open-or-closed communication channel, or a
single rendered field that is automatically conscious. Each scale requires its own cells,
population, anatomical route, reference signal, measurement units, and downstream consequence.

A **phase-wave differential (PWD)** is SAN terminology for a typed, receiver-relative change in
neural activity that has a measurable downstream consequence. Candidate dimensions include phase
or timing, frequency or event rate, amplitude, duration, transmitted quantity, variability, and
spatial or network state. It is not a synonym for phase difference, synchrony, traveling wave,
gamma burst, electric field, or functional connectivity.

The word **differential** is essential. SAN's differentiated system includes waves of excitation,
waves of inhibition, and their interaction; a particular measurement may expose one or both. PWD
therefore does not mean phase-wave excitation or phase-wave inhibition, and the differential must
not be silently redefined as excitation minus inhibition. What matters is the typed difference from
the receiver's declared tonic pattern and the consequence of that difference for the receiver.

This wording is governed by Micah Blumberg's August 28, 2026 author clarification. It preserves the
joined meaning of the earlier sources but is not backdated into their exact 2022 language.

## Minimum definition

A defensible PWD statement has this form:

> Relative to reference state R, source system A changes along a declared route to receiver B. The change has a declared typed feature vector, biological route, and measurable consequence C.

The differential is the declared change relative to the declared tonic reference, including the
excitatory and inhibitory organization when those variables are part of the measured system. The
wave component requires evidence of spatial organization or propagation. The information claim
requires a receiver that preserves and causally uses the distinction.

A minimal timing-focused candidate feature vector is:

`PWD(r,t;Delta) = [Delta phi_r(t), Delta m_r(t), Delta tau_r(t)]`

Here `r` identifies the receiver and its declared reference; `Delta phi_r` is receiver-relative phase
or timing change; `Delta m_r` is a declared magnitude measure, such as integrated absolute signal
over the event window; and `Delta tau_r` is event duration. This is a deliberately reduced subtype,
not the complete PWD definition. A complete candidate can additionally include frequency or event
rate, transmitted quantity, variability, and spatial or network state, as specified in
[[phase-wave-differential-calculus|Phase-Wave Differential Calculus]]. The vector is a proposed
operational model, not a new physical unit. It is not automatically a derivative, error signal,
semantic symbol, or conscious bit. Each application must name the two compared states or signals,
time window, measurement units, anatomical route, receiver use, omitted dimensions, and comparison
model it is expected to outperform.

## Source-faithful SAN operator

PWD is not merely the proposition that phase carries information. The recovered SAN mechanism is a
candidate receiver-relative update chain:

```text
ongoing tonic receiver context
-> comparatively uncommon excitation, inhibition, timing, waveform, or release event
-> propagation through a declared axonal, synaptic, or recurrent route
-> receiver-relative change in timing, tuning, recruitment, or suppression
-> resynchronization, structured dispersion, or persistence in a changed state
-> altered rendering, memory readiness, report, or action
```

A PWD can begin with unexpected excitation or unexpected inhibition. One event does not have to
instantiate every link in the chain, and a population-scale pattern cannot be assigned to a single
cell without a separate scale argument. Phase-compatible convergence can support coincidence and
effective summation at a named receiver; the longer proposed route from convergence to motor output
is developed in [[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential]] and
[[electrical-valence-receiver-relative-drive]].

The information claim is conditional. If an event class is less probable under a declared tonic
reference and probability model, its Shannon surprise is `I(e|R) = -log2 p(e|R)`. This makes a rare
departure potentially more informative than a common synchronized state to a receiver that can use
the distinction. It does not make synchrony intrinsically information-free. See
[[information-rarity]].

SAN further hypothesizes that recurrent activity can gradually absorb or dissipate a PWD into an
updated tonic context while the recurring change remains available long enough to influence named
receiver, recall, and plasticity processes. This is an experimentally separable persistence-and-decay
proposal, not a claim that every PWD writes a memory or that diffusion-model mathematics has already
been demonstrated in neural tissue.

## Biological variables that must remain separate

| Variable | What is measured | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Spike phase | Event timing relative to a reference cycle | Semantic content or causal direction |
| Phase locking | Consistency of event timing across trials or cycles | Communication, reconstruction, or consciousness |
| Coherence | Statistical phase relation between signals | Direct interaction without common-input and leakage controls |
| Traveling wave | Propagating spatial activity or phase pattern | Tomography or a rendered percept |
| Extracellular electric potential or electric field | Voltage (V) or electric field (V/m), measured or estimated relative to a declared reference | A globally causal content field |
| Magnetic field | Magnetic flux density (T) associated with neural currents, measured with a declared magnetometry or MEG method | A semantic channel or the complete SAN rendering mechanism |
| Phase reset | Change in the estimated phase of an ongoing signal | A broadcast, memory write, or conscious update by itself |

Oscillatory timing can be biologically consequential in named preparations and circuits. The
following primary studies establish component observables, not the complete PWD synthesis:

- [Montemurro et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2008.02.023) found that spike timing
  relative to low-frequency local-field-potential phase carried information beyond spike count in
  anesthetized macaque V1 during naturalistic movies.
- [Slezia et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3375-10.2011) measured
  nucleus-specific thalamocortical timing rather than one universal thalamocortical phase relation.
- [Thorne et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.6176-10.2011) found that visual input
  reset auditory-cortical phase and that the prestimulus relation predicted response speed in a
  bounded human task.
- [Aggarwal et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32378-x) measured interacting visual
  feedforward and feedback traveling waves in awake mice.
- [Xiao et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2012.08132.x) used voltage-sensitive dye
  imaging and pharmacological manipulation in rat occipital-neocortical slices. Reducing GABA-A or
  GABA-B inhibition increased network synchrony and simplified the spatial dynamics of oscillation.
  This is bounded component evidence that inhibitory organization changes oscillatory wave
  structure, not validation of the complete PWD operator.
- [Chemla et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2792-18.2019) measured a suppressive
  traveling wave in V1 of awake monkeys during apparent-motion trials. Suppression here is a
  measured population-response relation; the study does not establish that every suppressive wave
  is a GABAergic inhibitory wave or a PWD.
- [Frohlich and McCormick (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.06.005) and
  [Anastassiou et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2727) show that weak endogenous-range fields
  can influence or entrain nearby neural activity in bounded slice preparations. They do not
  establish a global content field or the SAN consciousness mechanism.
- [Barry et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1601513113) measured action-potential-associated
  magnetic fields with nitrogen-vacancy diamond magnetometry in excised marine-worm and squid
  neurons and from outside intact marine worms. This establishes a distinct magnetic observable in
  specialized preparations, not semantic content or the complete PWD mechanism.

Measurement also needs explicit controls. [Lachaux et al. (1999)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10619414/)
formalized phase-locking statistics; [Nolte et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2004.04.029)
showed how volume conduction can create spurious EEG/MEG connectivity; and
[Cole and Voytek (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2016.12.008) explain why nonsinusoidal
waveform shape can confound spectral interpretations. These are design constraints for a PWD test,
not reasons to discard the receiver-relative hypothesis.

In the SAN operator, waves of excitation and inhibition are typed components of the departure from
tonic reference. In an experiment, each component still requires scale-appropriate evidence, such
as identified-cell spiking, synaptic-current or conductance measurements, pharmacological or
cell-type-specific manipulation, or a declared population-response measure. A negative field
deflection or reduced population response alone cannot establish a GABAergic inhibitory wave.

## Tonic context and phasic update

SAN treats a PWD as a candidate phasic update relative to a tonic context. Both are receiver- and
timescale-relative. The reference may include membrane, synaptic, population, behavioral, and field
state. It is not one global clock, and the PWD is not one fixed fast or low-amplitude signal.

## Scale ladder

- **Micro:** a timing or waveform difference affects a named dendritic compartment, soma/AIS,
  terminal, or postsynaptic receiver.
- **Meso:** a population phase relation, reset, or wave changes a named local or downstream circuit.
- **Macro:** coordinated activity changes a distributed body-world model, report, or action through
  declared pathways.

The same term should not move across these scales without a new biological denominator.

## When a PWD becomes a code claim

A PWD supports content only if it:

1. predicts held-out content or behavior beyond rate, power, waveform, movement, arousal, and history;
2. is available to a named receiver;
3. survives volume-conduction, common-input, filtering, and reference controls;
4. changes the receiver or behavior under selective perturbation;
5. shows the predicted loss or rescue when the relation is disrupted or restored.

Decoding alone establishes recoverable structure under a measurement pipeline. It does not reveal
a native neural alphabet or phenomenal content.

## Relation to Neural Rendering and NAPOT

PWD is a candidate variable within [[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]]. [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] adds
the stronger claim that multiple partial projections support an identifiable reconstruction and
redistributed update. One PWD, wave, or coherent pair cannot establish the entire tomography cycle.

The separate bridge from recurrent PWD consequences through transient readiness and conditional
plasticity to later cue-driven reconstruction is developed in
[[tonic-absorption-generative-reexpression|Tonic Absorption and Generative Re-expression]].

The further claim that a dominant PWD constitutes conscious experience remains an unresolved SAN
hypothesis and must be separated from representation, access, report, and action.
