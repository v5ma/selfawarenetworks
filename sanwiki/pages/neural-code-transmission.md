**Neural code transmission** is the fourth of four key [[neural-code-functions|neural code functions]] identified by Perkel and Bullock (1968): stimulus representation, interpretation, transformation, and **transmission**. It describes how encoded signals propagate between specialized brain regions. Despite being critical to distributed brain computation, transmission is the least studied of the four.

## Why Transmission Matters for SAN

In SAN theory, the brain operates as a [[self-aware-network]] — a network of networks where meaning arises from the routing and relay of phase-encoded signals across cortical and subcortical layers. Without transmission fidelity, local computations in one region cannot influence or be integrated with computations elsewhere. [[napot]] depends implicitly on transmission: oscillating [[attractor]] states must propagate outward to create the global phase patterns that constitute conscious experience.

Transmission failures or degradation map onto disconnection syndromes and altered states of consciousness, providing empirical grounding for the SAN claim that awareness is network-relational, not localized.

## Brain-as-Query-Protocol

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s 2022 audio note ([[gh-b0144y|b0144y]]) proposes a complementary framing: the brain's data structures can be **queried** rather than physically copied. Rather than needing a perfect scan for mind uploading, one could use network-protocol-style queries — analogous to HTTP or FTP requests — to characterize threshold states at different nodes. This reframes mind uploading as an information-retrieval problem rather than a hardware-duplication problem.

Supporting this: TMS-based rapid estimation of cortical neuron activation thresholds (via convolutional neural networks) demonstrates that threshold landscapes can be measured non-invasively and computationally — a step toward brain querying at scale.
