# Crossmodal Pattern Invariance and Re-Expression

## The problem before the name

A printed word, a spoken word, and a Braille word begin as different physical events and activate
different receptors. Yet a person can learn that they preserve the same order, meaning, or
instruction. The brain therefore needs a way to carry useful relations across different sensory
formats without copying one identical code into every system.

```text
different sensory carrier
  -> local feature and timing pattern
  -> learned object, order, or action relation
  -> receiver-specific transformation
  -> recognition, prediction, report, or action
```

SAN calls this **Crossmodal Pattern Invariance and Re-Expression**. The invariant is the declared
relation that survives the transformation; re-expression is the new, receiver-specific neural
form that makes the relation usable again.

## What is established

Neuroscience supports a mixed architecture. Visual and haptic object processing can recruit
overlapping object-selective regions; visual, auditory, and tactile changes can recruit multimodal
networks; and crossmodal binding depends on timing and task. At the same time, sensory cortices
retain distinguishable modality-specific activity. The evidence does not require one universal
code shared by every sense.

## What SAN adds

The SAN proposal joins three operations:

1. preserve a task-relevant relation across a change of carrier;
2. transform that relation according to the receiving population's anatomy and state; and
3. re-express it as a prediction, recognition, experience, or action.

`a0132z.md` states this idea through Braille, visual language, sound, and imagined heat-based Morse
code, then connects it to receiving, transforming, and projecting neural arrays. `a0225z.md`
supplies an earlier symbol-grounding ancestor, while `a0306z.md` develops the broccoli example and
the BCI question of reconstructing a multimodal object experience.

The stronger frequency-to-modality, universal cortical hierarchy, quantum-soliton, and
orthogonal-field claims in `a0132z.md` are not required for this proposal.

## Why the term is not a relabeling

Multisensory integration says that several senses jointly affect processing. Supramodal
representation says some structure is shared. Sensory substitution shows that information can be
delivered through another channel. The SAN term adds the joined, testable sequence of **relation
preservation through receiver-specific transformation and later re-expression**.

PWD is a possible stronger account of the timing and waveform variables used by that
transformation. It enters only after the crossmodal operation is understood.

## Test

Train matched patterns through two senses, test transfer to held-out examples and actions, then
perturb the proposed transformation node or timing relation. A SAN-specific account must predict
crossmodal transfer and selective disruption better than standard shared-representation or
recurrent models while unimodal discrimination remains intact.
