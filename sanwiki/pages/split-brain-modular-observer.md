Two lines of neuroscientific evidence converge to dismantle the unified observer:

1. **Commissurotomy** (surgical severing of the corpus callosum) produces two independent
   personalities in one skull — the brain becomes, functionally, two souls.
2. **Hemispherectomy** (removal of an entire hemisphere) leaves a person cognitively
   near-normal — half a brain is sufficient for a normal life.

Together these demonstrate that "the observer" is a convenient [[gh-b0049y|summary]], not a biological
reality. The unified [[gh-a0206z|self]] is a [[distributed-observer-vs-consciousness-module|distributed
fiction]] — a label we apply to what is actually billions of concurrent expectations.

## Billions of observers, not one

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] (2012): "There is no biological truth to 'one see-er.' It's a fiction, a
social contract, an idea, not real." Every neuron is expecting, predicting, modelling. With
~84 billion neurons each running its own predictive update, "the observer" is a compressed
[[gh-b0049y|summary]] of this concurrency — the same compression that turns a symphony concert into a
single piece of music.

This is the phenomenological version of [[napot]]'s mechanistic claim: each neural array
is an observer at its own scale; no level in the hierarchy holds a monopoly on observation.

## Individuality as useful map

The individual / observer is a **map**, not the territory. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]:

> "Individuality is a concept, like the observer, its a distinction... Without the mind,
> there would be no such artificial concept as an individual... but these concepts do not
> have to have an objectively real reality to be useful."

The map is genuine enough to coordinate behavior; it is not genuine enough to locate a
singular, indivisible [[gh-a0206z|self]] inside the brain. The split-brain patient's two personalities
reveal that the map can be split too — and both halves continue to function.
