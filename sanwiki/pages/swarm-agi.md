Swarm AGI is Blumberg's 2011 [[gh-formulation|formulation]] of [[artificial-general-intelligence|artificial general intelligence]] organized as a **decentralized swarm** — an ant colony or slime mold — rather than a centralized processor.

## Core Model

The architecture has three features:

1. **TCP-like packet protocol**: agents (ants) act as packets. If an agent does not return, the swarm stops dispatching more. Throughput is [[gh-a0206z|self]]-regulated without a central controller.
2. **Environment as memory**: like a slime mold, the system uses the physical or virtual environment as a component of its memory — traces are left, strengthened by re-use, and decay without reinforcement.
3. **Emergent concept formation**: individual agents have no pre-specified concept representations; concepts emerge from the interaction patterns between activated nodes, analogous to the AN Model's vector tree where "the concept is the memory of the tree's reaction."

## Relation to SAN

Swarm AGI prefigures the [[fractal-agency]] principle: agent-level behavior at one scale (individual swarm member) gives rise to agency at a higher scale (swarm mind) through the same oscillatory synchrony that underlies [[self-aware-networks]]. The TCP-ping mechanism also maps onto [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]]'s phase-differential signaling: neural arrays send probe signals; response patterns determine which phase wavefronts reinforce.

## Extended Brain Corollary

The same conversation introduces the "Extended Brain" vision: a virtual cyborg attachment that gives a person access to an AI layer ~80× the size of their biological brain — not replacing cognition but extending its connection density. This anticipates [[bci-expanded-sensory-range]] and the [[agentic-brain]] architecture.

## History

Formulated in conversation ca. 2012, predating the formal NAPOT / SAN framework. Influenced by Jeff Hawkins (*On Intelligence*), Douglas Hofstadter (*Gödel Escher Bach*), and the Google Cat neural network experiments (2012).
