[[neural-lace|Neural Lace]] refers to a vision of delivering [[augmented-reality]] and [[virtual-reality]] experiences directly through neural interfaces — eliminating the need for wearable headsets or glasses entirely.

## Core Idea

Where conventional AR/VR encodes imagery into photons directed at the retina, [[neural-lace|Neural Lace]] proposes encoding experience at the level of neural signals. The human mind is reframed as readable and writable data storage: perceptions, memories, and spatial overlays become addressable via direct brain-computer coupling.

## Information as the Bridge

The framework treats a "bit of information" as simultaneously a physical particle and a neural pattern. [[Concepts]] are defined as maps of associations between bits — a [[gh-formulation|formulation]] that bridges [[information-theory]] and cognitive neuroscience, and underpins the argument that mind-states can be represented in computable form.

This framing connects directly to the broader [[self-aware-networks]] thesis: if neural patterns encode meaning, then sufficiently precise interfaces could read and write experience without any external display surface.

## History

Introduced in a 2017 Silicon Valley Global News (SVGN.io) article announcing *The [[neural-lace|Neural Lace]] Talks* podcast. The podcast reached audiences in India, Germany, and Japan who requested follow-on resources. The framing of consciousness as data predates the formalized SAN theoretical apparatus but anticipates its core vocabulary.
