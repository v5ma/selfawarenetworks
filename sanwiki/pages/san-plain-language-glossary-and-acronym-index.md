# SAN Plain-Language Glossary and Acronym Index

Self-Aware Networks (SAN) uses new terms only when an existing label does not capture the complete operation being proposed. This glossary therefore begins with the problem each term addresses, explains the operation in ordinary language, and only then gives the SAN name.

The definitions below are reading aids. They do not turn a hypothesis into an established biological fact. Follow each concept link for its sources, claim status, alternatives, and tests.

## Core signal and rendering terms

### Tonic baseline

A nervous system needs a continuing context against which a new event can count as a change. SAN calls the relatively persistent, receiver-specific activity that supplies that context the **tonic baseline**. Tonic is a functional role at a declared scale and timescale, not a synonym for broadband local field potential activity and not a frequency permanently assigned to one band.

### Phasic update

A system also needs brief changes that revise the continuing context. SAN calls a transient, consequential departure from the relevant tonic baseline a **phasic update**. High-frequency bursts can play this role, but phasic does not mean that every update must be gamma-band activity.

### Phase-Wave Differential (PWD)

Established neuroscience separately measures timing, phase, frequency, amplitude, duration, synchrony, propagation, and receiver response. SAN needs a term for the structured, receiver-relative difference produced when those properties jointly change and the difference has a traceable source, route, and consequence. That proposed unit is a **Phase-Wave Differential (PWD)**.

PWD is not a replacement name for every oscillation, traveling wave, phase code, or action potential. It is the proposed update relation between an active pattern and the tonic context against which a receiving system can distinguish it.

See [[tonic-phasic-phase-wave-differential|Tonic Baseline, Phasic Update, and Phase-Wave Differential]].

### Coherence

**Coherence** means that signals maintain a sufficiently stable relationship, such as phase alignment or a consistent nonzero phase lag, for a declared interval. In SAN, coherence can support coupling, recruitment, persistence, and accumulated effective drive. It does not mean that all participating cells fire identically.

### Structured phase dispersion or splay

A network must distinguish routes and contents as well as bind them. **Structured phase dispersion**, sometimes called a splay relation in the SAN sources, means an organized separation among phases rather than random failure. It can support distinction, inhibition, routing, and decoupling. It must not be equated automatically with quantum decoherence.

### Interference

When signals meet, their timing and polarity can reinforce, reduce, or reshape the resulting activity. **Interference** names that interaction. SAN proposes that interactions among coherent and dispersed activity help sculpt the changing differential pattern; the exact biological contribution remains an empirical question.

See [[coherence-dispersion-sculpting-hypothesis|Coherence-Dispersion Sculpting Hypothesis]].

### Neural rendering

Perception requires a system to transform partial sensory signals, memory, body state, expectation, and action feedback into a usable current model. SAN calls this distributed construction and re-expression **neural rendering**.

There is no little observer watching the render. Cells and circuits are the receivers, transformers, and transmitters; the network's distributed use of the resulting state is the observing-and-acting process.

### Perceptual screen

The **perceptual screen** is an analogy for a distributed neural state whose changes are available to the same system that produces them. It is not a literal screen in the brain and does not move perception into a hidden viewer.

See [[perceptual-screen-neural-rendering|Perceptual Screen and Neural Rendering]] and [[entification-observer-problem|Entification and the No-Inner-Observer Problem]].

### Neural Array Projection Oscillation Tomography (NAPOT)

No single neuron or measurement sees the entire modeled object. A system must combine partial projections, transform them at successive arrays, and distribute an updated result. SAN calls the proposed receive-transform-project architecture **Neural Array Projection Oscillation Tomography (NAPOT)**.

Tomography here is a mechanistic analogy and research program: partial, differently situated signals contribute to a distributed reconstruction. It does not claim that the brain literally runs a medical CT algorithm or that the neural inverse problem has already been solved.

See [[napot-revision-genealogy|NAPOT Revision Genealogy]] and [[receptive-inceptive-dendritic-projection|Receptive Fields, Inceptive Fields, and Dendritic Projection]].

### Receptive and inceptive fields

A **receptive field** describes the inputs capable of changing a cell or circuit. SAN uses **inceptive field** for the downstream population and routes that a cell or assembly can influence through its output. The pairing makes reception and consequential projection explicit without claiming that dendrites are literal cameras or axon terminals are literal screens.

### Neural tuning

A signal matters only relative to what a receiver can detect and what state the receiver is already in. **Neural tuning** is SAN's receiver-relative account of how morphology, receptors, channels, synapses, learned thresholds, oscillatory state, and circuit context shape sensitivity and output.

See [[neural-tuning|Neural Tuning in SAN]].

### Gamma Consideration Sandwich (GCS)

Sensory evidence, ongoing thought or expectation, body state, and action do not operate as isolated stages. The **Gamma Consideration Sandwich (GCS)** is SAN's organizing model for their recurrent coordination across faster and slower activity, including descending preparation and returned sensorimotor consequence.

It is not a claim that one frequency band has one universal psychological meaning. See [[embodiment-vision-gamma-consideration-sandwich|Embodiment, Vision, and the Gamma Consideration Sandwich]].

## Agency, self, and consciousness terms

### Choice operator

Brains must select one route or action while suppressing alternatives. SAN's **Choice** proposal describes circuit-level route selection through excitation, inhibition, timing, accumulated evidence, learned constraints, and feedback. It is a mechanistic candidate for how choices are implemented, not by itself a proof of metaphysical free will.

See [[neural-path-selection-interneurons-choice|Neural Path Selection, Interneurons, and Choice Across Scales]] and [[agency-valuation-action-feedback|Agency, Valuation, Action Selection, and Returned Consequence]].

### Entification

A distributed system can temporarily organize many differences into one behaviorally consequential whole. SAN calls this proposed formation of a bounded, integrated information-using entity **entification**. The term identifies a problem and candidate operation; it does not establish that every integrated network is conscious.

### Field-cell self

The self must persist across changing neural events without being placed in one cell or an immaterial observer. The **field-cell self** is SAN's hypothesis that cellular structure, bioelectric state, recurrent field interactions, memory, body regulation, and action form one history-bearing system. A conscious episode is shorter-lived than the continuing personal system that can learn from it.

See [[field-cell-self-bioelectric-information-entity|The Field-Cell Self]].

### Conscious-State Perturbation Tomography (CSPT)

Correlations alone cannot establish which measured features participate causally in a conscious state. **Conscious-State Perturbation Tomography (CSPT)** is a proposed measurement program that combines multimodal observation, controlled perturbation, behavior, report, and recovery across scale. It is a test framework, not a currently validated consciousness meter.

See [[conscious-state-perturbation-tomography|Conscious-State Perturbation Tomography]] and [[consciousness-claim-and-evidence-ladder|Consciousness Claim and Evidence Ladder]].

## Cellular and engineering extensions

### Cellular Oscillating Tomography (COT)

Cells outside the nervous system also receive many signals, transform them through receptor and intracellular state, and project consequential outputs. SAN calls its proposed generalization of this receive-transform-project logic **Cellular Oscillating Tomography (COT)**. Established cellular sensing and coordination motivate the question; full neural equivalence, learning, representation, and consciousness require separate evidence.

See [[cellular-oscillating-tomography|Cellular Oscillating Tomography and the Route to SET]].

### Protein Oscillation Tomography (POT)

At a smaller scale, proteins participate in modification cycles, binding, localization, and signal integration. **Protein Oscillation Tomography (POT)** is SAN's molecular-scale extension of the same question. It is a hypothesis about organized signal transformation, not evidence that an individual protein is a neuron or conscious observer.

See [[protein-oscillation-tomography|Protein Oscillation Tomography]].

### Alternating Transmission Protocol (ATP)

The brain needs both feedback-rich exchange and rapid transmission that does not wait for a full handshake. In the 2017 Neural Lace work, Micah Blumberg proposed **Alternating Transmission Protocol (ATP)** for a biological network architecture that can shift between those communication roles. The acronym deliberately echoes adenosine triphosphate, connecting communication cost with cellular energetics; the proposal does not claim literal TCP or UDP packets in the brain.

See [[alternating-transmission-protocol-cellular-handshakes|Alternating Transmission Protocol and Cellular Handshakes]].

### Neo Mind Cycle

**Neo Mind Cycle** names the research program that grew from Micah Blumberg's 2011-2014 neurofeedback and BCI work: NerveGear or neural lace, an external cortex that expands human thought, and autonomous sentient artificial brains. It is also a genealogy route into later SAN work.

See [[neo-mind-cycle-neurofeedback-bci|Neo Mind Cycle, Neurofeedback Salon, and the BCI Bridge]].

### NerveGear

**NerveGear** is the prospective full read/write BCI objective: measure and influence neural activity with enough spatial, temporal, molecular, and functional specificity to reproduce or alter experience safely. Current devices do not provide general-purpose thought reading, experience download, or complete neural write access.

See [[bci-instrument-atlas|BCI Instrument Atlas and the NerveGear Measurement Problem]] and [[world-body-brain-semantic-coregistration|World-Body-Brain Co-Registration]].

### Artificial Neurology

Artificial systems intended to develop mind-like capabilities need more than a classifier or language interface. **Artificial Neurology** is SAN's program for developmental state, embodiment, memory, valuation, choice, self-modeling, sensorimotor consequence, consciousness testing, and governance in synthetic systems.

See [[artificial-neurology-states-stages-development|Artificial Neurology: States, Stages, and Development]].

## Neighboring theories

**Super Evolution Theory (SET)** and **Super Information Theory (SIT)** are later, distinct branches of Micah Blumberg's research program. Their appearance in a SAN genealogy does not make every SET or SIT claim part of SAN, and SAN evidence does not automatically validate their biological or physical extensions.
