# Perceptual Screen

The **perceptual screen** is a SAN metaphor for the organized, changing body-world state maintained
and used by sparse distributed neural and bodily systems. It is not a literal anatomical display,
five-millisecond frame buffer, thalamic screen, or volumetric image watched by an inner observer.

## Positive mechanism

1. Sensory receptors and recurrent pathways provide partial, transformed evidence.
2. Dendritic, cellular, circuit, and population dynamics combine it with ongoing state.
3. Visual, auditory, somatosensory, thalamic, hippocampal, association, motor, cerebellar,
   subcortical, and body loops revise the working model.
4. The same distributed system is changed by that state and uses it for recognition, prediction,
   autonomic control, sampling, report, and action.
5. Returning consequences update the next cycle.

The screen and observer are two metaphors for this one operation, not two objects.

## Timing boundary

Perceptual processing unfolds across many timescales. Wavefront interactions, phase alignment,
attractor dynamics, recurrence, and inhibition can matter in named preparations. There is no
established universal five-millisecond screen refresh, one phasic-detail channel, one tonic-organizing
channel, or single moment when a complete complex shape crystallizes across the brain.

PWD and NDCA are SAN hypotheses that require measured variables, receivers, held-out prediction,
alternatives, and causal perturbation. They cannot be used as definitions that make geometry or
semantics appear automatically.

## Physics boundary

Neural electrical activity follows physical law, but SIT, quantum coherence, free-energy language,
and coherence/decoherence analogies do not supply medical evidence for a perceptual screen. Physics
and comparison claims must be tested and cited separately from the neural mechanism.
