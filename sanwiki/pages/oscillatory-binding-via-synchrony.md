## Position

The thesis is that perceptual binding — the unification of color, shape, motion, location, and modality into one experience — is implemented by phase-locked synchrony of [[neural-oscillations]] across [[cortical-traveling-waves]] and [[cortical-column-oscillatory-synchronization]] sites, not by single-cell convergence onto a "grandmother" integrator. Within [[self-aware-networks]] this makes [[oscillatory-binding-via-synchrony]] the synchrony-side companion of [[neural-distinction-consciousness]]: where the distinction thesis specifies that the *minimal* unit of awareness is a registered contrast between two distinct phase patterns, the binding-via-synchrony thesis specifies how multiple such distinct patterns are *unified* into a single experience by sharing a phase relationship rather than by being routed into a single cell. The mechanism is operationalized by [[cross-frequency-coupling]] — slow-band [[traveling-waves-neural-oscillations]] gating the amplitude of fast-band [[gamma-oscillations]] across distant regions — and is consistent with the SAN reading that [[distributed-phase-representations]] and [[computational-phase-synchronization]] supply the format and the timing of the rendered scene. The thesis explicitly extends to inter-region and inter-modal binding through [[oscillatory-synchrony-multimodal-integration]] and the long-range coupling pictured in [[neural-ensemble-phase-reset-communication]] and [[spear-hippocampal-phase-offset]], where synchrony is read as the coordination signal that lets [[cortical-traveling-waves]] cohere into a single perceptual frame. The thesis explicitly contrasts with anatomical-convergence binding accounts: synchrony binds *because* it is a relational property of distributed phase patterns, not because it routes information to a single integrating site, and the SAN reading takes that distributed-relational character as the load-bearing feature.

## Post-SAN

Mainstream binding literature has converged on synchrony as a load-bearing mechanism along several distinct lines, though the priority alignment varies. Singer-style binding-by-synchrony work establishes that gamma-band coherence across visual [[cortex]] tracks reported binding of features into objects, and that disruption of that coherence breaks the unified percept; this work pre-dates the SAN articulation but generally treats synchrony as a *correlate* of binding rather than as its constitutive medium, which is the stronger claim SAN makes. More recent work in the Miller corpus on traveling waves and brain-wide phase holograms — dated 2022–2025 on the [[earl-k-miller-corpus-priority-review]] page — converges on the SAN claim that binding is an active phase-coherence phenomenon at the brain-wide scale rather than a local-ensemble phenomenon, and the priority-review page argues that the convergence is dependent (essentially a rename of SAN concepts) rather than independent. The [[oscillatory-binding-problem]] literature itself has continued to mature without coinciding with the SAN reading — the binding-problem framing typically asks how distributed processing produces a unified percept, while the SAN binding-via-synchrony thesis answers that question by identifying phase-locked synchrony of [[cortical-traveling-waves]] as the constitutive mechanism rather than as one candidate among several. Where the SAN framing makes a non-trivial bet that mainstream binding accounts have not endorsed in their stronger form, it is the bet that synchrony is the *constitutive* binding medium and not merely a binding *signature* — a bet that aligns the thesis with [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography]] and with the SAN insistence that experience is a rendered phase pattern.
