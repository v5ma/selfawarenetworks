**Spontaneous Harmonic Synchronization** is the empirical phenomenon — and SAN theoretical anchor — whereby independent oscillators sharing a physical medium spontaneously phase-lock without central coordination. The canonical demonstration: 32 out-of-sync metronomes placed on a movable surface eventually all tick in unison, driven only by the subtle mechanical feedback through the shared platform.

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] invokes this as a physical existence proof that [[criterial-causation]] can operate without a "homunculus" coordinator. The physics of the shared medium — noise, vibration, microscopic coupling — conjures harmony from disorder.

## Neural implication

If neurons are oscillators sharing a physical substrate (the extracellular fluid, glial scaffold, and electromagnetic field), the same spontaneous synchronization principle applies. Noisy, independent neural firing can [[gh-a0206z|self]]-organize into coherent phase-locked ensembles without any master clock issuing commands. This is the substrate for [[cortical-column-oscillatory-transmission]] and [[oscillatory-neural-coupling]].

The key phrase in [[gh-a0116z|a0116z]]: "it's about noise, and how the physics seems to conjure spontaneous harmonies." Noise is not the enemy of coherence — it is the medium through which coherence finds itself.
