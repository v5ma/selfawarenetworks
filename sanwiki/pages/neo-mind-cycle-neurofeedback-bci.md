# Neo Mind Cycle, Neurofeedback Salon, and the BCI Bridge

## The problem before SAN

Can a person learn a relation between an internal neural measurement and an external sensory
signal when a computer continuously closes the loop between them?

Neo Mind Cycle explored that question with scalp EEG, brainwave graphs, adaptive light and sound,
audio-visual entrainment, and repeated participant sessions. The program was broader than one
device: its sources also discuss nutrition, exercise, coaching, sleep, and brain health.

## Durable operation

The core operation is measurable without accepting every later interpretation:

1. sample electrical activity at the scalp;
2. transform selected signal features in software;
3. render a changing visual or auditory consequence;
4. let the participant perceive that consequence;
5. observe how the measured state changes; and
6. repeat the loop so the participant can learn some relation between state and feedback.

This is a closed-loop human-machine system. It supplied practical experience with the limits of
EEG, artifacts, fixed classifiers, participant variability, and sensory feedback before SAN and
NAPOT were published.

The same loop also supplies a disciplined way to turn a personal observation into a research
question. Record a baseline, declare the feedback rule or other intervention, predict which
measured feature and behavior should change, repeat the observation, and test whether the result
survives sham feedback, ordinary sensory learning, expectation, movement, and artifact. A
prospective N-of-1 design can establish a bounded effect for one participant when its controls and
analysis are valid; an informal time series can only nominate the hypothesis.

This distinction preserves the value of hands-on neurofeedback without converting it into a
general treatment claim. The broader multiscale method is developed in
[[multimodal-biosensor-fusion]].

## Relation to later SAN

- The loop gives a practical ancestor for senses -> changing neural state -> action -> new senses.
- Mirrored feedback anticipates the distinction between decoding a command and returning a
  structured difference that the brain itself can interpret.
- The session apparatus grounds the later Neural Lace and NerveGear mission in real BCI work.
- The participant learning their own changing signal is an early self-model and observer-loop
  problem, not proof of the later entification mechanism.
- The use of light and sound as coupled feedback helped motivate later questions about multisensory
  rendering, phase, and plasticity.

## Command decoding versus brain-self-decoding

The internally dated May 18, 2012 note `a0083z.md` states the distinction unusually clearly.
One neurofeedback mode maps EEG features onto rigid software controls. The Neo Mind Cycle mode
instead converts measured variation into light and sound and returns it to the participant, letting
the living brain learn the relation in a repeated loop. This is not evidence that the system
decoded thought content. It is an early engineering distinction between:

```text
machine-decoded command: EEG feature -> fixed software action
brain-self-decoded feedback: EEG feature -> transformed sensory signal -> participant learns loop
```

`a0085z.md` later records Micah's 2022 retrospective interpretation of the 2012 experience as a
"mirror of the brain to itself." That first-person interpretation explains the research direction;
it is not independent evidence that consciousness literally extended into the computer.

## Outward route to the Extended Brain

`a0086z.md` and `a0087z.md` carry the feedback problem into an artificial-cortex proposal: a
reciprocal human-machine system that could expand accessible thought, add new sensory relations,
and alter subjective temporal resolution. The separate [[extended-brain-thought-density-hypertime]]
page keeps that proposal distinct from ordinary tools, autonomous artificial minds, physical time
dilation, and biological lifespan extension.

## Neural Lace expansion into registered world and body models

`a0210z.md` preserves the public October 10, 2017 Neural Lace compilation. It expands the earlier
EEG feedback loop into a proposal to study the person and environment together, including eye
movement, heart activity, movement, sensory channels, and later write-back. `a0214z.md` preserves a
public March 7, 2021 expansion through 3D semantic segmentation, body/world tracking, XR, and a
larger sensor atlas. This is the development bridge from Neo Mind Cycle toward NerveGear and later
NAPOT experiments.

The proposed measurement contract is now governed in
[[world-body-brain-semantic-coregistration|World-Body-Brain Co-Registration]]. Historical forecasts
about reading thoughts, wireless thalamic access, or medical diagnosis remain goals or hypotheses,
not completed Neo Mind Cycle capabilities.

`a0340z.md`, internally dated July 15, 2012, adds a predictive BCI route: compare learned brain
models with EEG/fMRI, use model error to improve control, and eventually test reciprocal brain-
computer coupling. Its modern form is a closed measurement, prediction, feedback, and update loop.
It is a program ancestor rather than proof of thought decoding or mind-to-mind communication.

## Claim boundary

Historical sources can establish apparatus, procedure, public description, business planning,
participant reports, and Micah's interpretation. Clinical efficacy, therapeutic claims, mechanism,
and comparative superiority require independent evidence. The program should be described as
hands-on amateur and community neuroscience, not dismissed as art and not presented as a clinical
trial.

## Book route

Introduce this history before NerveGear and before the mature SAN terminology. It explains why the
later theory treats feedback, oscillation, perception, and write-back as engineering questions
rather than only philosophical abstractions.

## 2012 feedback practice and the later measurement problem

[[gh-a0569z|a0569z]] preserves an early Neo Mind Cycle observation: when Micah's internal state
changed, the EEG-driven feedback changed with it, and repeated use helped him recognize aspects of
that relationship. [[gh-a0562z|a0562z]] and [[gh-a0578z|a0578z]] make the instrumentation boundary
clearer. EEG, MEG, and optical systems observe different projections of population activity; none
is the complete state of a thought, emotion, or self.

That history belongs before the mature SAN vocabulary because it explains the engineering problem
that motivated it: how can a system measure a changing brain-body state, return a useful
representation to the person, and determine whether learning transfers beyond the feedback
session?
