# Discarded Particle-Wave Analogy (Historical Source Note)

This page contextualizes `a0647z`, a speculative source fragment publicly fixed in June 2022. It is retained for genealogy, not presented as current Self-Aware Networks neuroscience or established particle physics.

## What the note was trying to ask

The note provisionally combines four ideas:

```text
oscillatory process
-> geometry of interaction
-> compatible or opposed states
-> relative temporal scale
-> apparent persistence or binding
```

The scientifically recoverable question is whether a moving or oscillating process can have a direction-dependent **interaction geometry**. The word "cone" is most defensibly read as a provisional picture of influence or radiation, not as literal electron anatomy. Physics does contain cone-shaped interaction patterns, such as the [dead-cone suppression of gluon radiation around a moving heavy quark](https://home.cern/alice-makes-first-direct-observation-fundamental-effect-particle-physics/), but that is not evidence that an electron itself has a cone shape.

The source already marks its own uncertainty. Its original text ends by calling the idea interesting "whether correct or incorrect." On June 13, 2022, Micah added a retrospective note asking what a cone-shaped electron would even mean and saying the fragment was being kept because it remained interesting.

## Scientific boundary

The fragment does **not** establish that:

- electrons have a literal cone shape;
- neural delta, theta, alpha, beta, or gamma bands are ingredients of subatomic matter;
- the strong nuclear interaction is simply curvature of space;
- an atom is an event frozen in time.

In the Standard Model, electrons are elementary leptons; the electromagnetic interaction binds electrons to nuclei, while gluons carry the strong interaction that binds quarks and contributes to nuclear binding. Gravity is not part of that Standard Model account. See CERN's [Standard Model overview](https://home.cern/science/physics/standard-model/) and [LHC guide to the fundamental forces](https://home.cern/sites/default/files/2018-07/CERN-Brochure-2017-002-Eng_0.pdf).
