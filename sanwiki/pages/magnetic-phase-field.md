# Magnetic Phase Field

**Magnetic phase field** is a SAN term introduced in the dated source record for the time-varying
magnetic coordinate that accompanies neural ionic current, together with the stronger proposal that
a biological receiver might use some part of that coordinate. The term remains part of SAN's current
multimodal event model. It names a research question; it does not make the magnetic field a second
action potential, a copied semantic message, or an established substrate of consciousness.

> **Scientific boundary:** neural currents generate measurable magnetic fields. An external sensor
> can recover action-potential timing and propagation from those fields. No experiment cited here
> has shown that ordinary mammalian cortical tissue detects a neighboring action potential's own
> magnetic field and uses it for computation.

> **Image status:** the earlier plate failed medical review because it depicted field reset at nodes,
> division of a magnetic pattern at terminals, and direct projection into cortical-column variants.
> It has been removed from the reader and retained in the do-not-use medical-review archive. A
> replacement requires independent medical review before installation.

## The source-faithful operation

The May 2012 through August 2022 source family joins three statements:

1. an action potential changes electrical current and therefore its surrounding magnetic field;
2. the same event also has ionic, chemical, mechanical, thermal, and metabolic consequences with
   different paths and timescales;
3. one or more named receivers might retain or respond to part of that changing state and thereby
   influence a later neural response.

The bounded field-specific causal chain is:

`neural current -> local B(r,t) -> candidate transducer -> receiver-specific state change -> altered later response`

Inside the larger SAN proposal, that field-specific route is one candidate branch of a joined event:

`one firing event -> coupled physical coordinates -> different transfer and decay paths -> receiver-selective consequences -> updated tonic context`

This larger operation is the source's medically recoverable proposal. It does not require every
coordinate to carry an identical copy of semantic content, and it does not assume that every
coordinate has a demonstrated endogenous receiver.

The word **phase** refers here to the timing and relational organization of a changing field. It
does not imply a separate magnetic substance. At nodes of Ranvier, action potentials are regenerated
by membrane and cable biophysics; nodes are not magnetic reset devices. At an axon branch, current
and field geometry change with the anatomy, but semantic content is not divided into magnetic
fractions.

## Measurement is not reception

Diamond quantum sensors have measured action-potential magnetic fields in large invertebrate axons
and compound-action-potential fields in mouse corpus-callosum slices
([Barry et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1601513113);
[Hansen et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-39539-y)). The mammalian study describes
neural biomagnetic fields as nano- to femtotesla and required close sensing plus repeated-trial
averaging. These measurements establish a passive readout channel for instruments, not a cellular
receiver in the brain.

By contrast, weak **electric** fields can alter membrane voltage and entrain active cortical networks
under bounded preparations. That is evidence for local ephaptic electric-field feedback, not proof
of magnetic reception
([Frohlich and McCormick, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20624597/);
[Anastassiou et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21240273/)). The electric and magnetic
coordinates arise from related current, but they have different coupling mechanisms and cannot be
interchanged in an explanation.

## Temporary state and the meaning of memory

SAN's stronger 2022 proposal asks whether the initiating event could leave a short-lived or more
persistent local state that changes a later response:

`event -> retained local state -> later probe -> changed receiver response`

This is an operational use of **memory**. A candidate state must outlast the event, be measurable,
and alter a later matched response. It is not automatically a semantic memory, image, engram, or
conscious percept. Ordinary voltage, ions, transmitter, calcium, temperature, deformation, and
metabolic history must be controlled before assigning the retained component to magnetism.

## Candidate receiver families

- **Flavin or radical-pair chemistry:** Drosophila experiments report light- and FAD-dependent
  magnetic effects in declared cellular and whole-animal preparations, including some
  geomagnetic-range conditions. They do not establish an endogenous mammalian cortical
  action-potential receiver
  ([Bradlaugh et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05735-z)).
- **Membrane or ion-channel effects under applied fields:** strong static magnetic stimulation can
  temporarily alter excitability in mouse cortical pyramidal neurons, but the applied field is not
  comparable to a nearby action-potential field
  ([Sinha et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38302440/)).
- **Magnetomechanical transduction:** externally driven magnetic nanodiscs can mechanically activate
  intrinsic channels, but the introduced particles are the transducer
  ([Su et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36323817/)).
- **Mitochondrial consequence:** ATP or redox change is a possible downstream readout, not a magnetic
  sensor by definition. A declared flavin-to-mitochondria route must outperform matched electrical,
  calcium, and metabolic explanations.

The whole-animal Drosophila evidence is disputed and must remain separate from cellular evidence.
A large multi-assay replication reported no magnetic effect on the fly behaviours it tested
([Bassetto et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06397-7)). Two published Matters Arising
articles challenged that interpretation, followed by a reply defending the negative replication
([Reppert, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07319-x);
[Kyriacou, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07320-4);
[Bassetto et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07321-3)). This dispute neither supplies
nor rules out the SAN mechanism, because none of those behavioural assays tests whether a mammalian
neural receiver responds to the nanotesla-scale field generated by a nearby action potential.

## Multimodal and multiscale context

The magnetic coordinate belongs inside the larger [[action-potential-magnetic-phase-field|multimodal
event model]]. Electrical, ionic, magnetic, mechanical, thermal, and chemical/metabolic consequences
are coupled observables of partly shared activity, not interchangeable copies of one message.

At the **micro scale**, the question is whether a named molecule, membrane, organelle, or cell detects
a field-specific component. At the **meso scale**, it is whether that route changes a declared local
circuit. At the **macro scale**, it is whether a distributed magnetic measurement adds information
beyond ordinary electrophysiology. Evidence at one scale does not automatically establish another.

## Scientific layers

**Established science:** neural ionic currents generate measurable magnetic fields; externally
applied electric and magnetic interventions can alter neural preparations through mechanisms that
depend on strength, geometry, waveform, tissue, and transducer.

**Evidence-supported SAN interpretation:** one neural event can be analyzed as several coupled
physical coordinates with different receivers, paths, and persistence.

**Open SAN hypothesis:** a named endogenous receiver detects an action-potential-scale magnetic
component or a retained magnetic/material state and changes later cellular or circuit behavior.

**Not asserted as established:** magnetic reset at nodes of Ranvier, semantic vector division at
axon terminals, identical multimodal copies of a memory, universal cortical-layer decoding, or a
magnetic field acting alone as NAPOT or the observer.
