Coincidence patterns are the fundamental unit of neural transmission in SAN theory — the "canvas of concepts." A neuron transmits and receives burst-coincidence patterns; these patterns are the only thing the brain sends and the only thing it receives.

## Core claims

When a neuron fires in response to an incoming pattern, that firing **is** the memory. The firing event triggers new protein synthesis at the synapse, increasing the synapse's electromagnetic density and effective rank. A higher-ranked synapse fires more readily in future — encoding that pattern as an expectation.

Patterns do not stop at one synapse. They cascade across the brain in temporal stages: each cortical layer re-represents the same pattern at a shifted temporal phase, enabling the brain to track objects through time (e.g., a moving object appears at different positions in successive cortical representations).

## Neurotransmitter substrate

[[Synaptic-Vesicle-Release]] releases neurotransmitters — [[dopamine]], [[GABA]], [[acetylcholine]] — from microvesicle sacks in the axon terminal into the synaptic cleft. Downstream neurons fire based on their own coincidence threshold, not uniformly. [[Temporal-Oscillation-Phase]] variance determines which downstream neurons respond.
