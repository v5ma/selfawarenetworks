# Thalamus as Display Screen

**Thalamus as display screen** is a historical SAN metaphor, not an anatomical description. The
thalamus is a heterogeneous collection of nuclei with different sensory, motor, attentional,
associational, arousal, and cortico-thalamo-cortical roles. It is not one bottleneck on which every
percept is drawn.

## Anatomical correction

- First-order and higher-order thalamic nuclei have different inputs and cortical relationships.
- Thalamic reticular, intralaminar, pulvinar, geniculate, motor-associated, and other systems cannot
  be collapsed into one screen.
- Olfactory pathways do not follow the same initial thalamic relay pattern as vision, audition, or
  somatosensation.
- Cortico-thalamo-cortical loops participate in distributed processing; signals do not simply travel
  cortex -> screen -> cortex as a completed frame.
- Gamma coincidences, coherence, or phase locking in the thalamus are not pixels or qualia by default.

## What the metaphor can preserve

Thalamic systems can gate, coordinate, relay, and transform activity that changes what cortical and
subcortical receivers do next. In SAN terms, they can participate in the distributed rendering and
observer-action loop. The useful translation is **state-dependent recurrent coordination**, not a
place where a finished picture is shown.

## Stimulation boundary

Electrical or other stimulation of a thalamic target can alter sensation, arousal, movement, or
network state depending on nucleus, parameters, spread, and patient condition. Such effects do not
show that the thalamus is a writable display. An artificial-perception claim must identify the
target, pathway, evoked content, alternatives, reproducibility, and downstream causal use.

## Evidence status

The recovered packet for the literal phrase contained zero primary source quotations. This page
therefore preserves and corrects a metaphor; it does not claim a recovered canonical thalamic
mechanism.
