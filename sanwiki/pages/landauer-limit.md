# Landauer Limit

**Landauer Limit** is the lower bound, established by Rolf Landauer in 1961, on the energy that must be dissipated as heat whenever a single bit of information is logically erased — *kT* ln 2 per bit at temperature *T* (≈ 2.85 × 10⁻²¹ J at 300 K). It is the thermodynamic floor that any *irreversible* computation must respect, regardless of substrate, and it is the bridge concept that connects [[information-theory]]-style accounts of [[biological-computation|biological computation]] to the physical-substrate-and-heat-budget side of the [[brain-as-dissipative-system|brain-as-dissipative-system]] picture. ## Post-SAN

Mainstream literature treats the Landauer limit as a *bound* and rarely as a *design constraint*: most computation is run many orders of magnitude above the limit, so the limit is interesting only in low-temperature, high-density, or biology-comparison contexts. SAN-side commitments push past description in two specific places. First, the [[micahs-new-law-of-thermodynamics|new-law-of-thermodynamics]] / [[new-law-of-thermodynamics]] line proposes that *coherent* information dynamics in [[cortex]] (and in [[coherence-gated-yang-mills-gap|coherence-gated-Yang-Mills]]-style physical substrates) operate under a distinct accounting in which the *coherence* across many degrees of freedom modifies the per-bit cost — a stronger claim than Landauer alone, and one that has to be reconciled with the limit rather than simply quoting it. Second, the [[coincidence-as-a-bit|coincidence-as-a-bit]] family redefines what counts as "a bit" for the purposes of the limit: if the elementary cortical operation is a coincidence event whose meaning is its phase relationship to neighbouring events ([[phase-wave-differentials]], [[computational-phase-synchronization]], [[neural-coincidence-patterns]]), then the right thermodynamic accounting is not "*kT* ln 2 per memory-erase" but "the energy cost of resolving one coincidence" — a quantity that has to be derived from cortical biophysics, not asserted from the Landauer line. Neither commitment denies the limit; both reframe what the limit *applies to* in a brain-style substrate.
