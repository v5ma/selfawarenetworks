It depicts inhibitory feedback reducing phase error until independent cortical oscillators become one computational unit for binding, gating, routing, and memory. Observed phase locking does not by itself establish that mechanism or those functions.

**Computational phase synchronization** is the SAN hypothesis that a phase relation can alter what a receiving neural population computes. Synchronization is a measurement; it becomes a computational claim only when the relation changes a defined input-output transformation.

## What must be ruled out

Apparent synchrony can reflect:

- common input;
- volume conduction or field spread;
- reference choice;
- evoked transients;
- spike contamination;
- changes in power or firing rate;
- waveform asymmetry; or
- a third population driving both signals.

Phase locking, coherence, amplitude correlation, cross-frequency coupling, and traveling waves are different statistics. They should not be presented as stages of one synchronization process.

## Receiver-defined operation

A computational claim should specify:

1. the source populations and their measured rhythms;
2. the anatomical route;
3. the target population and its excitability cycle;
4. the candidate phase relation;
5. the input-output function changed at the target; and
6. the behavioral or control consequence.

Bosman et al. found attention-related selective gamma synchronization between macaque visual areas, supporting pathway-specific tests of phase-sensitive communication ([Neuron, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.06.037)). It does not show that synchrony is a cortical arithmetic primitive or a universal binding operation.

## SAN boundary

SAN can treat phase-sensitive routing as one mechanism within a larger receive-transform-re-express-use architecture. Synchrony neither creates a new observer nor merges populations into one subject. The participating receivers, recurrent paths, memory, body state, and action consequences perform the larger operation.

## Tests

Hold firing rate and power as constant as possible while perturbing relative timing at the proposed receiver. Predict a specific change in effective communication or behavior, then restore the timing relation for rescue. Compare the model with common-input, rate, and connectivity alternatives.
