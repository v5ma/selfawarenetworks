# Intention Decoding

**Intention Decoding** is the [[bci|brain-computer interface]] task of reading a user's *intended* action — typically a planned movement, planned word, or planned target selection — from neural activity, before or during the motor execution that would normally make the intention observable. Within [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] it is the BCI-side application that the [[predictive-motor-self-model|predictive motor self-model]], the [[consciousness-as-high-bandwidth-choice-mechanism|high-bandwidth choice mechanism]], and the [[agentic-brain|agentic brain]] commitments are taken to underwrite — i.e. the SAN reading is that intentions are decodable *in principle* because they exist as [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differentials]] in the rendering loop *before* they reach motor [[cortex]] output.

## Post-SAN

Mainstream intention-decoding work treats the readout problem as a *signal-processing* and *machine-learning* problem on top of a fixed neural substrate — extracting decodable structure from spike-trains, ECoG, EEG, or fNIRS. SAN's reformulation makes two specific commitments worth flagging against the published BCI literature. First, it predicts that *phase-wave-differential* readouts via [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]] should *outperform* rate-based readouts at the same recording density for the *intention* (predictive) layer of motor planning — because intentions live in the predictive substrate before they are encoded in motor-[[cortex]] spike rates.
