# Theory of Redness

The **Theory of Redness** uses a familiar color experience to ask a bounded mechanistic question: how does a distributed nervous system construct, stabilize, use, and later recognize a state that a person reports as red, without requiring a second observer inside the brain?

## Begin with color physiology

There is no single red photoreceptor, red neuron, cortical layer, or brainwave frequency.

Long-, middle-, and short-wavelength-sensitive cones contribute overlapping signals. Retinal circuitry transforms those signals into several opponent and non-opponent channels whose responses depend on receptive-field geometry, adaptation, luminance, and spatial context. The lateral geniculate nucleus relays and transforms parts of this retinal output, while multiple cortical regions carry further color-selective population structure.

Primary results establish several important boundaries:

- [Crook et al. (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21289186/) measured L-versus-M cone opponency in primate midget ganglion-cell circuitry. Red-green opponency arose through outer-retinal horizontal-cell feedback rather than through a simple red channel or one generic inhibitory gate.
- [Godat et al. (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548340/) found more diverse cone-opponent retinal ganglion-cell responses than the classical cardinal-channel picture alone predicts.
- [Brouwer and Heeger (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19890009/) decoded stimulus color from distributed human visual-cortical activity. V4 and VO1 population geometry more closely followed perceptual color similarity than V1 population geometry did.
- [Conway, Moeller, and Tsao (2007)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17988638/) found luminance-invariant color tuning in clustered macaque extrastriate populations, distributed across several retinotopic color-biased regions.

Together these findings support transformed, distributed, and context-sensitive color representation. They do not establish why a represented color is consciously experienced, identify a complete mechanism of redness, or demonstrate SAN's phase-wave differential proposal.

## The recovered SAN operation

The 2022 SAN sources propose that redness is not an isolated label. It is a learned neural difference that is meaningful relative to the receiver's ongoing state, prior tuning, available contrasts, inhibition, and recurrent context.

The source-faithful operation is:

```text
spectral and spatial input relations
+ retinal, thalamic, cortical, bodily, and task state
+ learned receiver readiness and contrasting alternatives
-> distributed color-selective population change
-> recurrent use in discrimination, memory, report, and action
-> changed input to later receivers and later cycles
```

SAN calls the changing event relative to an active reference state a [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] (PWD). In this proposal, timing, phase, frequency, magnitude, duration, inhibition, transmitted quantity, and receiver state may jointly help distinguish and redistribute the update.

This does not mean that one phase angle, oscillation frequency, or waveform is red. The empirical question is whether a receiver-relative PWD description explains later neural and behavioral consequences beyond stimulus, firing rate, population geometry, connectivity, recurrence, attention, and ordinary state history.

## Rendering and observation without an inner viewer

[[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]] names the repeated construction and revision of a usable neural state. The terms **render** and **detect** describe roles in a distributed recurrent operation; they do not name known neuron classes.

A population renders in the limited sense that its output changes the state available to anatomically connected receivers. A receiver detects in the limited sense that its existing structure and current state transform that input. The same cells may participate in different roles at different moments and scales.

No single receiver contains the completed percept. The network's color-guided discrimination, memory, report, autonomic response, and action are consequences of the distributed state. SAN locates the proposed observer operation in those coordinated consequences, not in a hidden viewer watching a cortical screen.

## Tonic context and phasic difference

The historical sources distinguish an ongoing **tonic** context from less common **phasic** departures. The medically bounded interpretation is operational:

- tonic activity is the receiver's evolving background state and readiness;
- a phasic event is a change relative to that state;
- a PWD is SAN's proposed multidimensional description of the receiver-relative change; and
- later recurrence can preserve, transform, or end the state.

The tonic context need not be globally synchronized, identical across cells, or devoid of color information. A phasic update need not be high frequency, and persistence does not require one fixed oscillatory loop. Coherence and structured dispersion may both contribute to recruitment and differentiation.

## Scale ladder

| Scale | Established or testable operation | SAN extension and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Receptor and retinal circuit | Cone responses, adaptation, center-surround structure, and opponent transformations | Not redness by itself |
| Thalamic and cortical population | Distributed, transformed, context-sensitive color-selective activity | Decodability is not proof of experience |
| Recurrent network | Color state can influence discrimination, memory, report, attention, and action | Recurrence alone does not prove PWD or qualia |
| Whole embodied system | Perception guides sampling, naming, valuation, and behavior | SAN proposes that these coordinated consequences constitute the distributed observer operation |

The sources sometimes speak of arrays that render and detect a pattern. The recovered claim is a distributed recurrent role relation. It is not evidence for anatomical render cells, detect cells, a universal thalamocortical loop, or fixed laminar phase offsets.

## What the theory does not yet establish

- A stable neural correlate is not automatically sufficient for experience.
- A decoder that predicts color does not identify the conscious mechanism.
- One thalamic, cortical, or oscillatory route does not universally sustain color experience.
- Synchrony, phase, recurrence, firing rate, population geometry, and causal efficacy are related but non-identical variables.
- Naming a physical pattern does not solve qualia by definition.

## A discriminating experiment

A useful test should hold the viewed chromatic stimulus, luminance, adaptation, task, eye movements, report demands, and mean population response as closely matched as possible while perturbing a preregistered receiver-relative timing variable.

The study should compare at least:

1. stimulus and retinal-opponency models;
2. rate and population-geometry models;
3. connectivity and recurrent-state models;
4. synchrony-only and phase-only models; and
5. the full receiver-relative PWD model.

The PWD account gains support only if its specified variables predict and selectively alter color discrimination, similarity judgment, report, or downstream action beyond the simpler alternatives. It is weakened if the full PWD description adds no reproducible prediction or causal leverage.

## Image status

The existing `theory-of-redness-20260701-v1.png` is preserved as a historical generated asset but does not pass medical review for public use. It depicts invented render-cell and detect-cell classes, arbitrary fixed phase offsets, an oversimplified universal six-layer circuit, a single thalamocortical loop as the sustainer of red qualia, and unsupported claims about tonic synchrony and percept persistence. The public projection suppresses this image. A future replacement must show established color processing first and place the SAN PWD and distributed-observer interpretation in a visibly separate proposed layer.
