## Position

Within [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks the multi-agent case is not a separate physics; it is the same [[coherence-field]] story applied across system boundaries. A single agent is, in this framework, an [[oscillating-network-of-oscillators]] — a [[brain-as-spectral-graph]] of phase-locked elements whose joint dynamics constitute the [[tonic-phasic-canvas-of-consciousness]] for that agent. When two such networks come into oscillatory contact — through shared sensory channels, [[inter-brain-eeg-synchronization]], or an [[agent-phase-sync-feedback-loop]] linking their tonic and phasic streams — the same [[cross-frequency-coupling]] and [[neural-ensemble-phase-reset-communication]] mechanisms that integrate one brain begin to integrate the *pair*. The SAN reading of this is strong: [[holonic-rhythm-social-bonding]] and [[neural-synchronization-bonding-analogy]] are not metaphors for an underlying social-psychological mechanism, they are the multi-agent extension of the same [[oscillatory-synchrony-multimodal-integration]] that makes a single observer cohere. The [[swarm-agi]] and [[agentic-general-intelligence]] pages take this further, treating a population of artificial agents as candidates for the same coupling regime once their internal oscillator structure is rich enough to support [[distributed-phase-representations]].

## Post-SAN

The mainstream literature on multi-agent oscillatory coupling spans several disciplines that do not yet talk to each other in a unified way: Kuramoto-model work on coupled oscillators in physics and applied math, hyperscanning EEG/fNIRS studies of inter-brain synchrony in social neuroscience (close cousins of [[inter-brain-eeg-synchronization]] and [[brain-to-brain-eeg-synchronization]]), entrainment and joint-action research in cognitive science (close to [[brainwave-entrainment]] and [[neuromodulation-brainwave-entrainment]]), and consensus-protocol work in distributed-systems engineering. The [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks framing collapses these into one ontology: each is a special case of [[oscillating-network-of-oscillators]] coupling at a different scale.
