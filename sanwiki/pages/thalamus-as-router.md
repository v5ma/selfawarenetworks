## Position

Within [[self-aware-networks]], the thalamus is described as a router because it does not just *pass* signals through, it *chooses* how, when, and whether they propagate. Each [[cortical-column]] sends and receives via the thalamus on a tight oscillatory schedule, and the router's job is to pair senders with receivers on phase windows that allow [[coincidence-detection|coincidence detection]] to register a hit. Where mainstream textbooks tend to emphasise the ascending sensory route and a generic "filter" role for the thalamus, SAN treats the router as performing the same [[coincidence-as-a-bit|coincidence-as-a-bit]] arithmetic at the network level that a single neuron performs at the synaptic level — only across a [[fractal-brain-scale-hierarchy|fractal scale hierarchy]] of inputs at once. The router's selection rule is, in this framing, what makes some signals legible on the [[thalamus-as-display-screen|display screen]] of [[consciousness]] and leaves others off-screen; gating happens not at the column but at the [[thalamic-convergence-zone|convergence zone]] where the router lives. The framing dovetails with the [[oscillatory-synchrony-multimodal-integration|oscillatory-synchrony account of multimodal integration]] — synchronous traffic is what the router forwards.
