The substrate-independence question asks whether [[self-aware-network]] properties — intelligence, consciousness, [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modification — can emerge from non-biological systems that replicate functional neural properties rather than biological chemistry.

## The Core Debate

The discussion centers on a concrete case: could fiber-optic neurons using light beams instead of electrons support genuine intelligence? Three positions emerge:

1. **Substrate-permissive**: any system exhibiting "similar properties to neurons" (Peter Rothman) could support mind — photonic computing and quantum photonic chips already demonstrate light-based computation.
2. **Substrate-critical**: synaptic function requires specific electrochemical properties; swapping electrons for photons breaks synaptic transmission (Łukasz Stafiniak's objection).
3. **Proton bridge**: [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] proposes redesigning synapses to respond to protons rather than electrons — a targeted workaround that preserves the electrochemical interface while changing the carrier.

## SAN Position

From a [[self-aware-network]] perspective, the key property is not the carrier (electron, photon, proton) but the pattern of [[neural-oscillations]] and coincidence detection the substrate enables. A substrate that preserves phase-locking, inhibitory-excitatory balance, and the capacity for [[self-modeling]] could in principle support intelligence even if the underlying physics differs.

The proton-synapse proposal is notable: it suggests substrate changes need not be wholesale — targeted substitutions at the synaptic interface may be sufficient if the oscillatory dynamics are preserved.

## Self-Modification Requirement

Mark Waser's question about [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modification is significant: genuine intelligence may require not just pattern processing but the ability to alter one's own processing rules. This is a higher bar than mere neural mimicry and connects to the [[neomindcycle]] concept of recursive [[gh-a0206z|self]]-awareness.
