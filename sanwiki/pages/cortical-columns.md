# Cortical Columns: From Local Populations to Distributed Coordination

**Cortical columns** are useful descriptions of vertically organized cortical populations where that organization has been demonstrated.

SAN uses the plural term for a different question from the singular [[cortical-column|Cortical Column]] page: how can multiple local populations contribute changing, partly overlapping information to distributed perception and action without one central observer? This does not require every cortical area to be tiled by identical modules, every population to share one phase, or synchrony to be the only useful state.

## What is established

Columnar organization is real in bounded preparations, but its form and functional significance depend on cortical area, species, scale, and measurement:

- [De Martino et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1507552112) found depth-stable frequency preferences consistent with columnar organization in human primary auditory cortex. This supports particular functional columns, not one universal cortical module.
- [Constantinople and Bruno (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1236425) showed that thalamus can activate deep layers directly in rat barrel cortex. The familiar `thalamus -> L4 -> L2/3 -> L5/6` sequence is therefore not an obligatory route.
- [Lee and Imaizumi (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11062-013-9385-2) found thalamic input to both layers 4 and 6 in sensory-cortex preparations, with different intracortical inputs contributing to their different receptive-field properties.

Primary studies also establish dynamic coordination across local and distant populations:

- [Gray et al. (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1038/338334a0) observed stimulus-dependent oscillatory synchronization between spatially separated columns in cat visual cortex.
- [Bosman et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2012.06.037) found selective gamma synchronization between behaviorally relevant V1 and V4 sites in monkeys.
- [Saalmann et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1223082) found that pulvinar activity regulated synchrony and information transmission among interconnected visual cortical areas during attention.
- [Bastos et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.018) found different frequency profiles for feedforward and feedback influences across macaque visual areas.
- [Semedo et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.01.026) found that interactions between cortical areas occupied a low-dimensional communication subspace rather than transmitting every dimension of local population activity.
- [Muller et al. (2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35963850/) found visual-evoked feedforward and feedback traveling waves across cortex in awake mice, with the feedback-wave phase modulating feedforward amplitude and coordinating firing.

These findings support population coordination, selective routing, recurrent influence, and spatiotemporal organization. They do not establish that anatomically discrete columns always send one phase-coded message, that coherence is always the cause of communication, that phase alignment is sufficient for perceptual binding, or that synchronized columns constitute consciousness.

Two results are especially important for avoiding a one-way "synchrony is success" story. [Esghaei et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-018-0551-2) found that attention reduced low-frequency spike-field coupling in macaque area MT while improving the neural representation of the attended stimulus. [Schneider et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.09.037) showed that inter-areal coherence can arise as a consequence of communication, oscillatory power, and anatomical connectivity rather than serving only as its gate. Depending on the declared receiver, task, and measurement, coupling and structured dispersion can both be informative.

## Source recovery: the SAN plural-column proposition

The SAN proposition developed in stages that should not be collapsed into one 2022 claim:

1. **June 8, 2022:** `a0005z` proposed that partial whole-brain patterns include the horizontal activity of local cortical populations, described three population roles as inhibition, excitation, and tonic activity, and asked whether one oscillating population could magnify a smaller pattern for another population. The compact phrase was "columns are reading columns."
2. **August 21, 2022:** a later `a0005z` addition described synchronization as a phasic change being absorbed into an ongoing tonic population state. This was an exploratory mechanism, not a claim that all useful communication ends in synchrony.
3. **August 26, 2022:** [[gh-a0001z|a0001z]] proposed that cortical reference frames *may* relate slower ongoing activity to faster incoming phasic events. The source explicitly noted contradictory papers and the need for more research.
4. **December 8, 2022:** `a0005z` placed cortical columns, minicolumns, hippocampus, nuclei, and any jointly oscillating or collectively functioning group at a mesoscopic level between cells and whole-brain corticocortical and thalamocortical coordination.
5. **May 2025:** `12san` introduced the mature "tonic canvas / phasic ink / NDCA" formulation. That later synthesis may clarify the earlier mechanism, but it must not be backdated to 2022.

The recovered operation is:

`ongoing states in multiple local populations -> transient receiver-relative differences -> selective coupling, dispersion, and routing -> recurrent cortical and thalamic updating -> overlapping ensemble response -> distributed perceptual or action consequence`.

This is a candidate composition rule. "Columns reading columns" means that neurons in one declared population receive and transform activity arriving through real anatomical projections from other populations. No column literally views another column, and no localized module contains the observer.

## Translating the historical language

| Historical SAN expression | Source-faithful biological interpretation |
|---|---|
| "Columns reading columns" | Neurons in one bounded population receive state-dependent synaptic input from neurons in other populations and change their own activity. |
| "Tonic canvas" | The ongoing distribution of membrane, synaptic, spiking, inhibitory, neuromodulatory, and extracellular-field states against which later events differ. |
| "Phasic ink" | A transient, receiver-relative departure in declared variables such as timing, phase, rate, waveform, conductance, or population state. |
| "Phase match" | A measured relation among declared signals that may alter effective interaction; it is not automatically binding or agreement among entire columns. |
| "Absorption into the tonic state" | A transient input changes the receiving population and may be followed by settling, adaptation, recurrence, plasticity, or a new ongoing state. |
| "Column as agent" | A modeling stance justified only when the population has a defined boundary, state, inputs, outputs, adaptive rule, and measurable consequence. It does not imply a separately conscious unit. |
| "NDCA canvas" | The 2025 SAN hypothesis that evolving population states can be modeled as a nonlinear continuous approximation. It is not an established cortical structure or named physiological process. |

Tonic and phasic are roles relative to a receiver and observation window, not permanent frequency bands. A slow event can be phasic relative to a still slower context, and a fast rhythm can be part of an ongoing tonic state. Likewise, synchrony, phase offsets, splay-like dispersion, rate changes, and communication subspaces are different measurements and must not be substituted for one another.

## Cellular, circuit, and network scales

- **Cellular:** synapses, dendritic compartments, somata, axons, terminals, and identified interneurons transform input according to their current state.
- **Circuit:** a declared layer, barrel, column-related population, minicolumn, or local ensemble expresses recurrent excitation, inhibition, oscillation, adaptation, and learned re-expression.
- **Network:** corticocortical, thalamocortical, corticothalamic, hippocampal, subcortical, and motor pathways coordinate selected population variables across regions and behavior.

The same abstract receive-transform-project relation may recur at all three scales, but the receiver, signal, reference state, boundary, and output must be redefined at each scale. SAN's [[distributed-observer-vs-consciousness-module|distributed observer-action]] claim concerns the aggregate causal organization of these receivers, recurrent pathways, bodily feedback, and actions; it does not place a viewer inside one cell, column, rhythm, thalamus, or field.

## The SAN residual

The established literature already contains columnar organization, inter-column synchronization, frequency-specific directed influence, traveling waves, thalamic regulation, attention-related desynchronization, and low-dimensional communication subspaces.

SAN's remaining proposal is their composition with a receiver-relative distinction:

`ongoing local context + typed transient difference + selective coupling or dispersion + learned recurrent transformation + anatomically constrained projection = one changing contribution to a distributed render-and-act cycle`.

Calling a local population a "canvas" or "agent" becomes scientifically useful only if this joined model predicts later population state, downstream recruitment, perception, or action better than standard firing-rate, phase, power, connectivity, latent-state, and communication-subspace models.

## Discriminating tests

A strong test should:

1. declare the species, cortical areas, task, recording scale, candidate column boundaries, cell classes, thalamic nuclei, and anatomical projections;
2. measure identified cellular activity together with laminar population activity in at least two local populations and a relevant downstream or thalamic site;
3. compare rate, timing, phase, power, coherence, structured dispersion, traveling-wave, latent-state, and communication-subspace explanations on held-out trials;
4. test whether anatomical column boundaries improve prediction over matched unconstrained local populations;
5. perturb the proposed sending population, receiving population, inhibitory route, or thalamic route separately and require mechanism-specific loss and rescue; and
6. test whether the receiver-relative tonic-plus-difference model adds reproducible predictive value for perception or action beyond conventional state and connectivity variables.

Failure of column boundaries or SAN variables to add predictive value would weaken the stronger plural-column account. Success would support the bounded operation tested; it would not establish a universal cortical algorithm, a complete percept in one column, or consciousness from synchrony alone.
