The Electromagnetic Theory of Feeling proposes that subjective emotions are **electromagnetic moments** — transient dipole fields generated by synchronized electric and magnetic dipole activities across neural assemblies — rather than purely synaptic or neurochemical states.

## Core Claim

Feelings are generated at the intersection of electric dipole oscillations (charge separation across membranes) and magnetic dipole moments (current loops in neural tissue). The conjunction of these fields, when coherent across a cortical assembly, produces a detectable electromagnetic signature that the brain reads as a qualitative "feel." This maps onto [[cellular-oscillating-tomography|COT]] at the affective layer: if sensory qualia arise from phase-based tomographic rendering, emotional qualia arise from the same mechanism applied to interoceptive and visceral inputs.

The theory is explicitly materialist: consciousness and feeling consist entirely of physical electromagnetic patterns. Information "can be in the material but not of the material" — meaning the pattern is substrate-encoded but not reducible to any single substrate instance.
