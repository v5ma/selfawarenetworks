**CaMKII Activity Transfer** is, in SAN's terminology, the molecular mechanism by which the kinase CaMKII propagates and stabilises a frequency-specific synaptic state — explicitly framed as "**CaMKII Activity Transfer: A [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Sustaining Memory Trace**" in both the rexnote01 and rexnote12 ledgers (rexnote01--md, line 93; rexnote12--md, line 292). It is the kinetic side of the wiki's molecular-memory pair: paired with [[kibra-pkm-zeta-memory-anchor|KIBRA]]–[[kibra-pkm-zeta-memory-anchor|PKMζ]] anchoring, it forms what `rexnote01` calls a dual mechanism — "**The dual mechanisms of [[kibra-pkm-zeta-memory-anchor|KIBRA]]-[[kibra-pkm-zeta-memory-anchor|PKMζ]] anchoring and CaMKII activity transfer provide molecular underpinnings for these theoretical constructs**" (rexnote01--md, line 140).

In the SAN account the *transfer* word is doing real work. CaMKII is not just persistently active at a tagged synapse — it transfers its activity state, and therefore the frequency-specific tuning it encodes, between coupled synapses, so that "the specific firing frequencies at those synapses (through CaMKII activity transfer)" become preserved features of the network rather than per-synapse facts ([[gh-rexnote09|rexnote09]]--md, line 195). The same source amplifies that "CaMKII activity transfer preserving frequency-specific responses" is the load-bearing claim. Together with KIBRA–PKMζ anchoring, this is what `rexnote01` calls the molecular underpinning of "the multi-frequency processing, dynamic frequency transitions, and phase sensitivity" the SAN theory requires (rexnote01--md, line 288), and what is "[building] upon the molecular insights from the studies on KIBRA-PKMζ anchoring and CaMKII activity transfer, we now delve into how these mechanisms facilitate the complex [[multi-frequency-processing|multi-frequency processing]]" (rexnote01--md, line 201).

The mechanism therefore sits at the seam between molecular biology and SAN-level theoretical constructs. Upstream, CaMKII activity transfer rides on [[nmda-receptor|NMDA-receptor]] activation and Ca²⁺ dynamics, intersecting [[gaba|GABAergic]] inhibition that times the windows in which transfer can occur. Downstream, it provides the *[[gh-a0206z|self]]-sustaining* leg of [[hebbian-learning|Hebbian learning]] that pure correlation-counting models lack — explaining why a pattern survives once written rather than decaying with each subsequent firing. Architecturally, this is what lets [[cortical-columns|cortical columns]] hold [[synaptic-frequency|synaptic-frequency]] patterns as durable [[3d-semantic-segmentation|3D semantic-segmentation]]-style features that [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]] tomography can read out, with behavioral-time-scale plasticity supplying the trigger event that initiates a transfer episode. The SAN page therefore treats CaMKII activity transfer as one half of the molecular substrate of [[consciousness|consciousness]]-supporting [[san|SAN]] dynamics, with KIBRA–PKMζ anchoring as the spatial-stability half.

## Medical evidence boundary

- **Established in bounded preparations:** NMDAR-dependent Ca2+ activates CaMKII in individual dendritic spines. Imaging studies show rapid activation and decay, with activity largely restricted to the stimulated spine rather than propagating as a stable kinase wave.
- **Measured local coordination:** Ras activity can spread about 10 micrometers along a dendrite and enter nearby spines. Dendritic electrical and Ca2+ signals, synaptic tagging/capture, and branch-local protein synthesis provide additional mechanisms for clustered plasticity.
- **Supported but separate:** KIBRA-PKMzeta coupling has experimental support as a maintenance mechanism for late LTP at activated synapses. That does not establish that KIBRA-PKMzeta carries a CaMKII-coded firing frequency between synapses.
- **Not demonstrated:** A traveling CaMKII activity state that copies or preserves frequency-specific tuning from one synapse to another. The SAN claim requires a direct experiment that excludes shared input, dendritic voltage/Ca2+, Ras-pathway signaling, and protein-synthesis effects.

Primary evidence:

- [Lee et al. (2009), single-spine CaMKII activation and spatial restriction](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07842)
- [Chang et al. (2019), CaMKII as a leaky Ca2+ integrator in single spines](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-10694-z)
- [Harvey et al. (2008), approximately 10-micrometer spread of Ras activity](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1159675)
- [Govindarajan et al. (2011), dendritic branches and protein-synthesis-dependent LTP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.008)
- [Tsokas et al. (2024), KIBRA-PKMzeta coupling in late-LTP and memory maintenance](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adl0030)
