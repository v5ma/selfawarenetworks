# Dendritic Pattern Recognition

**Dendritic pattern recognition** is a bounded name for differential cellular responses to defined spatial-temporal input configurations. A dendritic branch does not recognize an object in the human sense. Under controlled conditions, location, timing, order, inhibition, membrane state, and learned synaptic organization can make one input relation produce a different local or cellular transition from another.

## Define the pattern and the output

A pattern-recognition claim is incomplete unless it specifies:

- the presynaptic sources and activated synapses;
- their locations, order, timing, and strength;
- the cell type, dendritic compartment, and membrane state;
- inhibitory and neuromodulatory context;
- the measured endpoint, such as local voltage, calcium, somatic voltage, spike timing, burst structure, release, plasticity, or behavior;
- a matched counterfactual pattern.

The word *recognition* describes reproducible discrimination by this declared physical system. It does not introduce a small observer inside the neuron.

## Established examples

[Polsky, Mel, and Schiller (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1253) showed that nearby inputs on the same thin basal dendrite of rat neocortical pyramidal neurons could combine sigmoidally, while more separated arrangements combined differently. [Branco, Clark, and Hausser (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1189664) showed direction and speed sensitivity for sequential dendritic input. [Smith et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12600) linked local dendritic spikes to enhanced orientation selectivity in mouse visual cortex.

These findings establish branch- and cell-level selectivity. They do not establish that every neuron is a universal classifier, that every branch performs the same operation, or that one neuron's activity contains a complete percept.

## Recognition is graded and state dependent

A local event need not be all-or-none. Subthreshold voltage, calcium, plateau duration, spike latency, burst probability, and plasticity can all carry consequential differences. A single strong input can matter; coincidence is not always required. Different input patterns can converge on the same spike count, while nominally identical input can produce different output under different tonic states.

Basal and apical compartments both receive heterogeneous excitation, inhibition, and modulation. Apical does not universally mean prediction or top-down context, and basal does not universally mean sensory or bottom-up evidence. Those assignments require a specified pathway and preparation.

## Selective capture and distributed re-expression

SAN's camera and microphone analogy names **selective capture**: learned dendritic structure and state make one incoming relation more consequential than another. It is a functional analogy. Dendrites have no lens, diaphragm, or private display.

SAN's screen, pixel, television, and speaker analogy names **distributed re-expression**: the transformed state changes spikes, bursts, axonal projection, target-specific release, local fields, and downstream receiver readiness. An output is not a lossless semantic copy. Each receiver transforms it according to its receptors, morphology, inhibition, membrane state, and history.

The tonic canvas is the relatively maintained network condition against which a consequential departure is registered. Phasic ink is the differentiated change that alters receivers and what they can transmit next. These are proposed operational roles, not merely decorative metaphors and not universal frequency-band labels.

## Scale ladder

- **Micro:** a dendritic compartment or neuron discriminates declared input relations.
- **Meso:** recurrent local circuits select, amplify, suppress, and stabilize population states.
- **Macro:** distributed systems integrate perception, memory, body state, prediction, and action.

Meaning belongs to the changed receiver and downstream consequence across this ladder. The active distributed network is the observer process; neither one branch nor one soma is an inner viewer.

## SAN/NAPOT hypothesis

SAN proposes that learned cellular selectivity supplies structured components for NAPOT reconstruction. The stronger hypothesis is that dendritic organization can store a compressed spatial-relational projection whose activation helps reinstate a larger network state. Established dendritic selectivity supports the feasibility of local structured storage, but it does not yet prove three-dimensional image storage or NAPOT reconstruction.
