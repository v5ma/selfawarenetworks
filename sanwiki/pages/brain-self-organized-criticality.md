**Brain [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Organized Criticality** is the observation — discussed extensively in György Buzsáki's *Rhythms of the Brain* and echoed in SAN notes — that the brain perpetually operates near a dynamic tipping point: not settled into a fixed oscillatory [[attractor]], but poised at the threshold where a single additional input can cascade into a large-scale response.

Buzsáki's sand-pile analogy: add grains one at a time and the pile stays stable — until one more grain triggers an avalanche. The brain similarly maintains irregular, labile oscillatory states ready to react quickly to any incoming stimulus. Attempting to force one neuron's firing rate alters the entire oscillatory pool, which pulls the outlier back into alignment.

## Scale-free fractal EEG signature

*Rhythms of the Brain* reports: "a collective pattern recorded from a small portion of the cortex looks like a pattern recorded from the whole." This scale invariance — identical statistical structure at every spatial resolution — is the defining feature of [[gh-map-intro-fractal|fractals]]. Scalp EEG recordings across extended durations show scale-free, spatial fractal structure, confirming that criticality is a global, not local, property of the brain.

This directly supports [[fractal-brain-scale-hierarchy]] and the SAN claim that the same oscillatory program runs at every level of the hierarchy (cell → column → region → whole brain). See also [[fractal-neural-scale-hierarchy]].

## Oscillations as the substrate

Criticality requires an oscillatory substrate: without ongoing temporal structure, there is no "edge" to balance on. The brain's tonic [[neural-oscillations]] across 0–200 Hz provide this substrate. Slow oscillations span large populations and maintain macro context; fast oscillations serve local integration within brief temporal windows. The two co-exist because they occupy complementary temporal niches, not because they synchronize.

*Computation always means information is moving from one place to another.* Criticality ensures this movement is always possible: the brain is never "frozen" into a single [[attractor]], so new patterns can propagate at any moment.

## Relevance to SAN

In SAN theory, the brain's criticality is what makes it a high-bandwidth [[consciousness-as-high-bandwidth-choice-mechanism]]. A supercritical brain would flood with noise; a subcritical brain would be too rigid to learn. Criticality is the regime where [[napot-theory]] operates: phase patterns can propagate laterally across the cortex, encode novel stimuli, and return to baseline — all within a single [[tonic-phasic-neural-oscillation]] cycle.

The scale-free EEG fractal structure is one of the empirical pillars [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] uses to argue that [[fractal-pattern]] organization is not metaphorical but measurable — supporting the broader SAN/[[self-aware-networks]] framework.
