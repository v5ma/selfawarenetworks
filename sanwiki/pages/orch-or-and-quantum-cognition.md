# Orch OR and Quantum Cognition

**Orch OR** and **quantum cognition** are not the same proposal. [[orch-or|Orch OR]] is a physical theory involving quantum states in neuronal microtubules and objective reduction. Quantum-cognition models often use quantum probability or Hilbert-space mathematics to describe contextual decisions and order effects without claiming that the brain is a quantum computer.

## Keep the levels separate

| Label | Main commitment | Does it require quantum brain physics? |
|---|---|---|
| Quantum probability model | Human judgments can follow nonclassical probability structure | No |
| Quantum biology in microtubules | A defined quantum or quantum-optical effect occurs in tubulin or microtubules | Yes, for that physical effect |
| Orch OR | Orchestrated microtubule quantum states undergo objective reduction and contribute to consciousness | Yes |
| SAN | Distributed cellular and network dynamics implement observer-action | No; a quantum contribution would need an explicit causal bridge |

A successful quantum-probability fit does not identify a microtubule mechanism. A measured microtubule optical effect does not show that cognition uses it. An anesthesia-associated effect does not by itself demonstrate a conscious-content mechanism.

## SAN position

SAN can compare its contextual, phase-dependent, and receiver-dependent dynamics with quantum-cognition mathematics without claiming a quantum physical substrate. It can also evaluate microtubule quantum biology empirically. In both cases the required bridge is causal: identify the physical state, biological receiver, transformation, circuit consequence, and effect on a declared behavior or conscious target.

The distributed network remains the observer-action system. Neither mathematical formalism nor a microscopic event becomes a hidden viewer.

Micah's 2021-2022 SAN notes add an explicit causal criterion: a microscopic state must be read by a
cellular mechanism, change membrane or plasticity variables, survive amplification, and alter a
declared circuit or behavioral target. This [[microtubule-to-circuit-scale-translation|scale-
translation test]] is compatible with investigating quantum biology while keeping SAN independent
of Orch OR.
