The fundamental unit of information in the brain is a **spatiotemporal coincidence pattern** — the set of neurons that fire together within a window of time or physical proximity. This holds regardless of whether quantum effects operate inside microtubules. Even if microtubules store or process quantum states, the output to the neural circuit is still a coincidence event: a fluctuation in ionic charge, a shift in [[action-potential|action potential]] timing. The pattern is what the network encodes, not the substrate.

## The Argument

Quantum theories of consciousness (e.g., Penrose-Hameroff) suggest that microtubules may store quantum information. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s counter: even granting quantum storage, the read-out mechanism is a neural firing event — and neural firing is still governed by **coincidence detection**. An [[action-potential|action potential]] sometimes fires, sometimes doesn't; when and whether it fires encodes the bit. The quantum hypothesis moves the computation inward but does not change the outer format: spike timing + spatial co-activation.

This is consistent with [[napot]]: the brain computes via oscillatory patterns, and the patterns are coincidence relationships across many neurons simultaneously.

## Hebbian Learning and Pattern Encoding

Neurons that fire together wire together; neurons that fire apart wire apart ([[hebbian-learning]]). What is stored is not a single neuron's state but the entire oscillation's pattern — every neuron that participates in a synchronized firing event encodes an **invariant representation** of that spatiotemporal pattern. This makes the pattern robust to individual neuron loss.

A cortical column functions as a **network node**: a mini-orchestra whose instruments (neurons) learn shared pattern signatures. When the physical structure of synaptic connections changes (new protein synthesis, LTP), the column is literally reshaped toward the patterns it expects to process in the future.

## Microtubules and Memory

Microtubules provide structural scaffolding for long-term potentiation (LTP) — new protein synthesis requires intact microtubules. If microtubules are damaged, LTP fails and new memories cannot form. This associates microtubule integrity with memory, but the causal chain is structural (protein synthesis scaffolding), not necessarily quantum information storage.

The more parsimonious account: microtubules are load-bearing structure for synaptic remodeling, not quantum memory registers.
