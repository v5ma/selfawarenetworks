Memory in the SAN framework is not a stored pattern but a **probability distribution** — a weighting of connection strengths and [[dendrite]] configurations that increases the likelihood of a particular pattern being regenerated when the right context arises.

The pattern does not exist between experiences; it is re-created in the moment of recall. What is stored is only the conditional probability that a similar pattern will re-emerge under similar conditions — "a chance of you," not you.

## Key claims

- Memories are **location-invariant**: each recall may recruit different neurons yet produce functionally equivalent output, because what is stored is the probability, not the specific cell ensemble.
- [[Neural spikes]] are downstream of conscious experience, not the carriers of qualia. Sub-threshold chemical activity does the heavy lifting; spikes are a dissipative by-product — a discharge removing excess, analogous to heat.
- Brain "computation" differs from digital computation: in the brain, every analogy affects everything else; there are no isolated data silos. Patterns form spontaneously across active ideas without explicit links.
- Changing the brain's chemical ecosystem (e.g., nootropics) can alter the resolution and fidelity of probability distributions, not just recall speed — formatting is biochemically plastic, not hard-coded.

## History

This concept emerged in a 2014 Facebook discussion responding to Ray Kurzweil's mind-upload thesis. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] argued that uploading a mind copies a probability distribution, not an actual subjective [[gh-a0206z|self]] — leaving open the "hard problem" of whether the copy would have qualia.
