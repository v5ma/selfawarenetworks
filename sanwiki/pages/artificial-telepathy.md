# Artificial Telepathy

**Artificial telepathy** is a historical SAN engineering label for technologically mediated
communication in which a measured signal from one nervous system is transformed into information
delivered to another. Existing brain-to-brain interfaces transmit highly constrained choices,
commands, or task variables. They do not copy unrestricted thoughts, complete percepts, qualia,
consciousness, or personal identity.

The source record asks a stronger and still open question: could a future system reconstruct a
functionally corresponding perceptual or cognitive state in a receiver? That is not the same as
copying the sender's voltage trace. Different nervous systems have different anatomy, learning
histories, baseline states, and response functions. A serious version of the proposal therefore
requires a receiver-specific transformation and an outcome test.

## The Recoverable Operation

The medically and technically bounded operator is:

**declared sender task or state -> bounded measurement -> uncertainty-aware decoding -> ordinary
message transport -> receiver-specific display or stimulation -> reported or behavioral
consequence -> feedback and recalibration**

This operator preserves the early SAN aim without assuming a portable mental-state waveform. It
also exposes where stronger claims enter:

- Current evidence supports transmission of a small, explicitly trained task variable.
- Artificial perception would require a calibrated sensory consequence in the receiver.
- Cross-person neural reconstruction would require evidence that a richer relation or content was
  reinstated, not merely that stimulation occurred.
- Shared experience would additionally require a defensible criterion for experiential
  correspondence. No established method supplies that criterion.

## What Existing Experiments Establish

The component literature already demonstrates several bounded forms of mediated transfer:

- [Pais-Vieira and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep01319) decoded a rat's
  sensorimotor choice and converted it into intracortical stimulation for another rat.
- [Grau and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0105225) encoded binary words through
  motor-imagery EEG and delivered the bits as TMS-induced phosphenes.
- [Rao and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0111332) decoded motor imagery from one
  person and used TMS to elicit a task-relevant motor response in another.
- [Stocco and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0137303) used an interactive
  yes-or-no protocol in which EEG-decoded answers were delivered as the presence or absence of a
  visual phosphene.
- [Jiang and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-41895-7) linked two senders and one
  receiver in a repeated collaborative task, including feedback and learned weighting of a less
  reliable sender.

These are genuine brain-computer-brain systems, but their codebooks were explicit and their
messages were very small. The receiver did not inherit the sender's neural state. A decoded
decision was digitized, transported, and converted into a different receiver-side event.

## Capability and Evidence Ladder

| Level | Example | What would have to be shown |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Binary event or command | Above-chance detection with sham and artifact controls |
| Choice estimate | One option from a trained set | Generalization, calibrated uncertainty, and resistance to cue leakage |
| Structured message | Multiple symbols through an explicit code | Reproducible information rate and error correction |
| Task feature | A bounded sensory, motor, or relational variable | Feature-specific transfer beyond a generic alert |
| Artificial perception | A calibrated receiver-side sensory consequence | Content and pathway controls, not stimulation alone |
| Cross-person reconstruction | A richer relation or working representation | Outcome equivalence across tasks despite different biological implementations |
| Shared subjective state | A corresponding experience | No validated transfer criterion currently exists |

Progress at one level does not establish the next. In particular, a phosphene, movement, or correct
choice is not evidence that the sender's complete percept was copied.

## Receiver-Specific Reconstruction

The June 2022 SAN sources asked whether a neural *tomogram* could be extracted and implanted through
a brain-to-brain interface. The source-faithful, testable interpretation is not a literal image
packet moving between brains. It is a reconstruction problem:

1. Declare the sender content or task relation.
2. Identify which measured features predict that content under held-out conditions.
3. Learn a receiver-specific intervention that produces a corresponding usable relation.
4. Test whether correspondence survives new examples, delays, distractors, and altered context.
5. Compare the result with simpler symbolic, sensory-cue, and state-matched controls.

SAN adds the hypothesis that receiver timing, ongoing tonic context, phasic departures, recurrent
state, inhibition, and learned structure may help determine whether an intervention becomes
usable. Phase-aware delivery can be compared with location-, rate-, power-, waveform-, delay-,
expectation-, and state-matched controls. A phase effect would support a timing-sensitive receiver
model; it would not by itself prove qualia transfer or a shared mind.

## A Separate 2022 Idea: Apparent Telepathy from Shared Clocks

The recording titled *Towards artificial telepathy?* develops a different operation. Two people
with a shared history may learn similar temporal regularities and expectations. When an expected
interaction is delayed or changed, one person may infer that something happened to the other. The
experience can feel telepathic even though no new signal crossed between them at the test moment.

The bounded chain is:

**prior interaction -> learned temporal regularity in each person -> later deviation from
expectation -> memory retrieval and inference -> convergent report or action**

Common sensory and task structure can synchronize neural and physiological measurements across
people, and studies of joint action warn that apparent interbrain synchrony can arise from shared
sensorimotor information rather than direct brain-to-brain causation. This supports testing the
shared-history account. It does not validate field-mediated or psychic telepathy.

This source must not be cited as evidence that oscillatory synchrony is a proven carrier of content
between separate brains. It is a predictive-memory explanation for why coordinated people can
appear to anticipate one another.

## Two Distributed Observers

Sender and receiver remain separate organisms with separate nervous systems, bodies, histories,
and observer-action loops. A channel can coordinate them without making one observer, one
conscious field, or one identity. Even a high-bandwidth future interface would need explicit
causal-boundary, persistence, agency, and welfare tests before any stronger entification claim.

## What the Quarantined Figure Was Trying to Say

The failed figure was trying to preserve three useful ideas: a read-transform-write pipeline,
receiver compatibility, and a capability ladder. It failed medical and scientific review because
it portrayed a live sender "phase pattern," a machine "phase codec," receiver-cortex acceptance of
an injected pattern, and "full qualia" as if they were one demonstrated progression.

A future figure must show:

1. a declared sender task and measured signal, not a picture of a thought inside a brain;
2. decoding with uncertainty and an explicit bounded message;
3. ordinary network transport;
4. a separately calibrated receiver-side display or stimulation route;
5. measured receiver perception, report, or action;
6. a feedback loop for recalibration;
7. separate organisms and separate observer-action loops; and
8. a visibly discontinuous boundary between demonstrated task transfer and hypothetical
   cross-person reconstruction.

No replacement art is generated while image work remains paused.

## Safety and Rights

Neural communication systems raise consent, cognitive privacy, coercion, authentication, model
drift, false inference, stimulation risk, agency, data custody, and withdrawal concerns. The
receiver must know what is being delivered and retain an immediate interruption path. A device that
changes perception or action needs independent safety review; this Wiki is not an intervention
protocol.
