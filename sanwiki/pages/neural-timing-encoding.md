Neural timing encoding is the principle that neurons encode information in the precise timing of action potentials, not merely in their firing rate — a finding that challenges rate-coding models and aligns with SAN's oscillation-based account of cognition.

## Core Claim

Researchers found that "neurons unexpectedly encode information in the timing of their fire," meaning the *when* of a spike carries meaning independent of *how often* the neuron fires. This reframes neural activity from simple rate signals to a rich temporal code.

## Phase Precession

[[Phase-precession]] — the progressive temporal shift in neural activation across oscillatory cycles — is the best-documented example of timing encoding. Established in rodent studies, it remains understudied in humans yet is central to [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography): phase differentials across arrays are the carrier of perceptual information.

## Brain Oscillations as Carrier Waves

Both local field oscillations and global brain rhythms (see [[rhythms-of-the-brain]]) act as carrier waves for timing-encoded signals. The bidirectional feedback between microscale neuronal timing and macroscale oscillatory patterns creates the hierarchical structure [[napot]] predicts.

## Emotions as 4D Spatiotemporal Patterns

An extension of timing encoding: emotions may be representable as four-dimensional spatiotemporal firing patterns — varying in frequency, phase, spatial location, and time. This bridges subjective emotional experience with measurable neural geometry, relevant to [[consciousness]] and [[fractal-consciousness]].

## Cellular Communication Substrate

The broader principle from *The Secret Language of Cells*: intercellular signaling determines health, disease, and cognition — timing encoding is one expression of this at the neural level.
