# Neuroimaging Reproducibility

## Position

Within [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks the reproducibility question is not free-floating methodology; it is downstream of what the framework asserts the instrument is actually measuring. If the [[brain-as-rendering-engine]] view is taken seriously, an [[fmri]] BOLD signal is a coarse hemodynamic shadow of the underlying [[tonic-phasic-neural-oscillation]] and [[neuronal-global-resonation]] that constitute a [[tonic-phasic-canvas-of-consciousness]] — and a coarse shadow of a high-dimensional [[brain-as-spectral-graph]] is not the same object across two scans, two subjects, or two analysis pipelines. The [[neuroimaging-localism-critique]] formalises one half of that gap (treating a voxel cluster as a "region for X" mistakes a [[holographic-brain-distributed-memory]] for a filing cabinet), while the [[neuroimaging-reliability-critique]] formalises the other half (small-sample inference, flexible analysis chains, and circular ROI selection inflate apparent effects). Reproducibility, in the SAN reading, is what is left over after both critiques have done their work — and it points toward the instrument program sketched under [[neuroimaging-next-generation]], [[neural-tomography]], [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography]], and [[fnirs-brain-imaging]] rather than toward a defence of the existing pipeline.

## Post-SAN

The mainstream literature on the reproducibility of [[fmri]] inference — large-scale analytic-flexibility studies, cluster-thresholding revisits, prediction-vs-explanation distinctions, and registered-report reforms — converges on the descriptive claim that headline effects are smaller and noisier than first reported. [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks accepts that descriptive claim and reads it through a stronger frame: under a [[brain-as-memory-prediction-machine]] account in which the [[observer-mechanism]] is an [[observer-as-aggregate-expectation]] rather than a localised module, low replication is the *expected* output of asking a [[brain-as-spectral-graph]] question with a tool that resolves only its slowest, lowest-frequency shadow. Where mainstream neuroscience reads the reproducibility crisis as a statistics-and-incentives problem, the SAN reading adds an instrument-and-ontology problem on top: the [[subjective-objective-distinction]] is being measured through a transducer whose bandwidth is mismatched to the [[tonic-oscillation-as-predictive-reference-frame]] it is trying to image.
