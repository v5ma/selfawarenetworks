The sense of [[gh-a0206z|self]] in the SAN framework emerges from the brain's **inversion of its own neural prediction patterns** — specifically, the predictive model of when neurons will fire, combined with sensory data about the consequences of motor outputs.

The [[gh-a0206z|self]] is not a static object stored in a region; it is a dynamic process: the brain predicts motor effects, those effects produce sensory feedback, and the coordinated modeling of cause-and-effect across that loop instantiates what is experienced as an embodied "I".

## Key claims

- **[[gh-a0206z|Self]] as inverted prediction**: The [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model is "the inversion of neurons predicting when they're going to fire." Where ordinary prediction points forward in time, the [[gh-a0206z|self]] arises from modeling the gap between predicted and actual firing.
- **Motor-sensory integration**: Predicted motor control outcomes are combined with incoming sensory data to model the causes of movements. This causal modeling is the functional substrate of embodiment.
- **No homunculus**: Because the [[gh-a0206z|self]] is a process (continuous loop of prediction + inversion + sensory comparison), there is no need for a separate observer — the loop is the observer.

## Relation to SAN theory

This concept connects to [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography) where oscillatory patterns across neural arrays encode predictions about future states. The [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model in [[gh-b0152y|b0152y]] is a special case of NAPOT: the arrays whose predictions are being inverted are the motor arrays.

It also anticipates [[memory-as-probability-distribution]]: the predicted motor state is never an exact stored pattern — it is a probability distribution over likely motor outcomes, inverted in real time.
