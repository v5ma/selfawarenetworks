**Cognitive privacy** is the principle that neural, emotional, and intention data generated inside XR and BCI systems should be protected by law and consent frameworks — not merely by platform policy. As XR headsets incorporate [[eeg-xr-neurofeedback-bci|EEG + eye tracking]] and environmental cameras, the risk of non-consensual cognitive profiling becomes concrete.

## The sensor surface problem

Modern XR devices (Valve Index with Tobii eye tracking, OpenBCI EEG overlays, Project Aria glasses) simultaneously capture:
- Gaze direction and blink patterns (inferring [[attention]], attraction, arousal)
- Facial micro-expressions (emotion inference)
- Environmental geometry (semantic mapping of private spaces)

This sensor fusion enables [[affective-computing]] — predicting emotions, intentions, and medical states from continuous biometric streams — without the user's awareness or explicit consent.

## SAN relevance

[[self-aware-networks]] theory predicts that [[phase-wave-differential|phase patterns]] at the neural scale encode the content of experience. If XR sensors can read proxies for those patterns (EEG, gaze, expression), they constitute a form of shallow neural readout. The ethical question SAN raises: who owns the [[neural-array]] signal?

[[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]]'s position (2021): cognitive integrity must be protected by **legislation**, not platform promises. Voluntary data-governance commitments lack enforcement teeth.

## Affective computing applications

Positive applications include emotion-adaptive interfaces, ADHD/Alzheimer's early detection, and meditation feedback (see [[eeg-xr-neurofeedback-bci]]). The same pipeline, unregulated, enables continuous psychological profiling.
