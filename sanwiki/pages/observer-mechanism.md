The **observer mechanism** is SAN's proposed account of how a neural system can register a change
without sending a completed internal picture to a second viewer. The participating network is
itself changed by the event. That change alters later recognition, prediction, autonomic response,
sampling, report, or action, and the consequences return as new input.

This is a positive distributed mechanism, not merely a denial of a homunculus:

1. receptors, synapses, and dendritic compartments receive partial differences;
2. cells and local circuits transform those differences relative to their current state;
3. axons and presynaptic terminals re-express consequential changes to downstream receivers;
4. recurrent circuits, thalamic and subcortical loops, and body feedback alter the next network state;
5. the resulting sparse, distributed process supplies the observer-action operation in aggregate.

No single neuron, rhythm, field maximum, cortical area, or bright convergence point contains the
complete observer.

## Tonic context and phasic difference

SAN calls the relatively maintained comparison conditions of a receiving system its **tonic
context** or **tonic action field**. A **phasic difference** is an event that changes those conditions
and thereby changes what the system can do next.

Both terms are receiver-relative. The relevant baseline, frequency, phase, magnitude, and time
window depend on the cells, circuit, behavior, measurement, and preparation. SAN therefore does
not require every neuron to share one rhythm, nor does it assign awareness to a universal
three-millisecond deviation or to a shift such as 20 Hz to 19.997 Hz.

Oscillatory phase and spike timing can matter in named circuits. For example, causal timing
perturbation has shown a role for theta organization during hippocampal memory encoding. Such a
result establishes a circuit- and task-specific timing contribution, not a general consciousness
alphabet:

- [Quirk et al. (2021), causal timing perturbation during memory encoding](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00919-0)

## What synchrony can and cannot mean

Synchrony, desynchronization, traveling waves, cross-frequency relations, and population geometry
can each influence communication and coordination. Their roles vary by area, state, task, and
timescale. A movement toward synchrony is not automatically perceptual integration, and a movement
away from synchrony is not automatically information or entropy dissipation.

The earlier SAN wording compared resynchronization to magnetic attraction. That is not an adequate
medical mechanism. Synaptic excitation and inhibition, intrinsic membrane dynamics, gap junctions,
neuromodulation, common drive, and network recurrence are major coordination routes.
Extracellular electric fields can also influence nearby neurons under measured conditions, but the
observed subthreshold effects in cortical slices were small and preparation-dependent. Ephaptic
coupling should therefore be named as a bounded physical contribution, not as a universal magnetic
force that restores conscious equilibrium:

- [Anastassiou et al. (2011), ephaptic coupling of cortical neurons](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2727)

## Scale declaration

| Scale | Biological denominator | Bounded SAN interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | Synapse, dendritic compartment, soma, axon initial segment, axon, terminal, receiver | A cell participates by receiving, transforming, and re-expressing differences |
| Meso | Local circuit, cortical or subcortical population, recurrent loop | Population state supplies a local comparison context and routes consequential updates |
| Macro | Distributed brain, body, and environment loops | Sparse participating systems jointly maintain a revisable body-world model and observer-action |

The same receive-transform-update motif can recur across scales, but similarity of form does not
show that every scale implements the same operator or possesses its own consciousness.

## Established physiology and SAN interpretation

| Status | Claim |
|---|---|
| Established | Neurons integrate synaptic input; dendrites can be nonlinear; spikes and synaptic release affect downstream cells; recurrent circuits and oscillatory timing can alter processing |
| Bounded empirical support | Endogenous extracellular fields can modulate nearby neural activity under some preparations; timing perturbations can causally affect named tasks and circuits |
| SAN interpretation | The ongoing field-cell state is an active comparison context and consequential phasic differences are registered by the coupled network |
| Unresolved SAN claim | This distributed field-cell registration is constitutive of observation or phenomenal consciousness |

## What would test the observer mechanism

A discriminating experiment must specify:

1. the participating cells, circuit, body loop, and timescale;
2. the candidate tonic state and phasic variable;
3. the biological receiver of that variable;
4. what the variable predicts beyond firing rate, power, waveform, movement, arousal, and history;
5. a selective perturbation and a rescue or reversal;
6. separate outcomes for representation, access, report, action, and phenomenal interpretation.

Evidence that a decoder can recover stimulus information is not enough. The test must show that the
named receiver causally uses the distinction in the proposed observer-action loop.
