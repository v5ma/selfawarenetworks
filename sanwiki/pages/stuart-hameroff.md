# Stuart Hameroff

**Stuart Hameroff** is an anesthesiologist and consciousness researcher who developed [[orch-or|Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR)]] with Roger Penrose. Their theory proposes that biologically orchestrated quantum processes in neuronal microtubules regulate neural activity and terminate through objective reduction, producing moments of conscious awareness or choice.

## What Orch OR claims

The published Orch OR account combines several propositions:

1. microtubules support relevant quantum states;
2. neuronal biology orchestrates those states;
3. the states influence synaptic or membrane activity;
4. objective reduction terminates the process;
5. the reduction event contributes to consciousness or choice.

Hameroff and Penrose present this conjunction in their 2014 review ([Physics of Life Reviews](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2013.08.002)). Evidence for microtubule biology or in-vitro photophysics does not by itself establish objective reduction or consciousness.

## Micah's source position

Micah discussed Hameroff and Orch OR in an April 2013 note. He described Hameroff as locating possible choice at the connection between neuronal microtubules and the quantum scale, asked whether available evidence ruled the proposal out, and resisted treating scale alone as decisive. He also considered whether field-scale effects could connect microscopic and macroscopic behavior.

That note records an open comparative question, not endorsement of a proven mechanism. It should not be rewritten as either "SAN accepts Orch OR" or "SAN showed Hameroff is wrong." The source-faithful boundary is that SAN can engage Orch OR as a neighboring scale proposal while requiring evidence for each link in its causal chain.

## Evidence boundaries

Several findings motivate continued study without proving the whole theory:

- prepared microtubules show nontrivial electronic energy migration under ultraviolet excitation, altered by anesthetics ([Kalra et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114));
- prepared biological tryptophan networks can show collective ultraviolet-emission behavior ([Babcock et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c07936));
- microtubule-modulating drugs can shift isoflurane righting-reflex endpoints in rodents, with effects depending on drug and protocol ([Khan et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024); [Li et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-025-02956-9); [Huang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2026.110834)).

The in-vitro studies did not measure intact neural computation. The animal studies did not isolate a quantum mechanism or directly measure conscious content. Tegmark's decoherence calculation remains a model-specific challenge that a defined long-lived quantum state must answer ([Physical Review E, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)).

## Relation to SAN

SAN and Orch OR can be compared without collapsing them:

- Orch OR places a proposed consciousness-relevant operation at the microtubule and quantum-gravity scale.
- SAN places the observer-action relation in distributed field-cell-network activity across sensory input, recurrent transformation, memory, body state, and action.
- SAN can include cytoskeletal effects on cellular readiness without requiring objective reduction.
- Detecting a quantum microtubule effect would add a molecular causal term; it would not automatically relocate the observer into tubulin.

The theories therefore differ in required evidence. Orch OR must establish a specified quantum state, biological orchestration, neural readout, objective reduction, and consciousness contribution. SAN's classical mechanism must establish receiver-relative tonic/phasic relations, recurrent integration, cross-scale redistribution, and action-linked observer behavior. Neither theory is validated merely because the other remains incomplete.

## Fair comparison rule

A public comparison should evaluate Hameroff's strongest published mechanism, not a caricature. It should also avoid treating credentials, popularity, or generalized skepticism as evidence. The decisive questions are experimental:

- What state is measured?
- At what scale and in what preparation?
- What biological receiver uses it?
- What changes after selective perturbation?
- Which classical and quantum alternatives make different predictions?
- Which conscious capacity or content is measured directly?
