# Burstlet Information Hypothesis

## The idea in plain language

Breathing circuits sometimes produce a small, partly synchronized event without producing a full
breath-related motor output. Researchers call this smaller event a **burstlet**. Under other
conditions, recruitment grows into a full burst that reaches downstream motor systems.

That creates a useful question for SAN: can a small event carry timing or preparation information,
while the larger event expresses the pattern to the next system?

## What the experiments show

The established work concerns the preBotzinger complex, a specialized respiratory network.
Burstlets can appear rhythmically without full hypoglossal motor bursts. Changes in excitability,
inhibition, opioids, and the `I_h` current can affect rhythm stability and the transition to full
bursts.

This does not mean that every brain region uses burstlets or that a burstlet is the universal unit
of thought.

## What SAN proposed in 2022

In [[gh-a0127z|a0127z]], Micah Blumberg connected burstlets to SAN's tonic/phasic model. A maintained
network state supplies the context. A small timing difference perturbs that context. If enough of
the right cells are recruited, the event becomes a larger pattern that a downstream circuit can
use.

The safe hypothesis is therefore:

```text
maintained context
-> partial timing event
-> state-dependent recruitment
-> expressed pattern
-> downstream consequence
```

The event becomes information-bearing relative to a receiver and consequence, rather than simply
because it has a particular amplitude.

## What would test it

Researchers would need to find burstlet-like events in another circuit, show that they predict
timing or state, identify the transition to full expression, perturb that transition selectively,
and show that timing, phase, duration, waveform, magnitude, and spatial recruitment outperform
simple firing-rate or power measures.
