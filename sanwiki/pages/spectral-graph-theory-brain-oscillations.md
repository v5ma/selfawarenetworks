Spectral graph theory of brain oscillations models how neural rhythms arise from and are shaped by the topological structure of the brain's [[connectome]]. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the brain's structural connectivity matrix predict the spatial patterns and frequencies of endogenous oscillations.

## Core Idea

The brain's white-matter connectivity can be encoded as a Laplacian matrix. Its spectrum — the set of eigenvalues — maps directly to resonant oscillatory modes: low eigenvalues correspond to slow, spatially broad oscillations (delta, theta), while high eigenvalues correspond to fast, spatially focal ones (gamma). This is the "spectral fingerprint" of the connectome.

This framing is central to [[self-aware-network]] theory: if every region's oscillatory mode is partly determined by the global graph structure, then local neural computation is always in dialogue with the network's geometry. Awareness is not localized — it is a spectral property.

## Relation to SAN Concepts

- [[napot]] ([[neural-array-projection|Neural Array Projection]] Oscillation Tomography) depends on the premise that oscillations propagate and interfere across long-range networks — spectral graph theory provides the structural underpinning for why they can.
- [[gravity-of-attention]] posits that [[attention]] is attracted to high-energy oscillatory sites; the spectral modes determine where those sites form.
- [[dopamine-associative-memory]] links associative memory encoding to dopamine-gated synaptic changes, which alter the local connectivity and therefore shift the spectral modes.

## Dynamic Emergence

A 2022 biorxiv paper (Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2022) extends the static spectral model to dynamic relational structure networks — showing that the effective connectivity graph changes moment-to-moment as the brain switches cognitive states. The resonant modes are not fixed; they reorganize around the task.

## Retinal Connection

The spectral graph framework generalizes to the retina: oscillatory eigenmodes in the retinal network shape which patterns of scattered light from deep retinal layers are integrated into a signal. See [[retinal-oscillations]].
