A published argument by [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] (SVGN.io) rebutting Faizal, Krauss, Shabir, and Marino's claim that undecidability in physics rules out algorithmic universes and simulations.

The central critique is a **category error**: the challenged paper conflates limits on formal provability with limits on physical generation. A simulator need not prove all global truths about itself — it only needs to execute local update rules. [[simulation-and-consciousness]] and information-theoretic positions remain unaffected by Gödelian incompleteness.

**Core distinctions Blumberg enforces:**

- *Execution vs. Decision*: processes can run while their global properties remain undecidable.
- *Provability vs. Generation*: Gödel/Tarski restrict what formal systems can derive, not what physical dynamics can instantiate.
- *Inaccessibility vs. Incomputability*: hidden information may still have been computationally generated even if it cannot be reconstructed.

The challenged paper proposes an external "truth predicate oracle" — a device that abandons operational physics for formal semantics. Blumberg isolates the key empirical question: does quantum measurement provide decision power over undecidable problems? Without such evidence, the oracle framing is metaphor, not mechanism.

Connects to [[quantum-super-time-position]] and [[oscillating-spacetime-theory]] by grounding the simulation question in executable physical dynamics rather than formal proof systems.

## Key terms introduced

- **Category error** — mistaking semantic/logical limits for physical/dynamical ones
- **Dynamical generation** — physical instantiation through local-rule evolution
- **Oracle** — technical term for a device that decides undecidable languages
