# Pyramidal Cells in SAN

Pyramidal cells are a major excitatory projection-neuron family through which cortical activity is integrated locally and transmitted to other cortical and subcortical targets. SAN uses their polarized anatomy as part of a receive-integrate-project motif, but the interpretation must preserve the measured direction of signaling and the diversity of pyramidal-cell classes.

For baseline anatomy and physiology, see [[pyramidal-cell]].

## Established findings

- Basal, oblique, and apical dendrites receive synaptic input and can perform passive and active integration.
- Many cortical pyramidal-cell action potentials initiate in the axon initial segment and travel along the axon; some also backpropagate into dendrites.
- Projection target and physiological role vary across layer, area, and cell class.
- In defined mouse layer 5 somatosensory populations, apical dendritic activity can interact with sensory, motor-related, contextual, and behavioral variables. This does not establish the same role for every pyramidal cell.

Primary examples: [Xu et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11601) and [Takahashi et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-0677-8).

## Corrections to the older SAN vocabulary

The apical dendrite should not be called an **exit terminal**. It is a dendritic input and integration compartment. Cellular output leaves mainly through the axon.

Layer 2/3 pyramidal cells should not be labeled universally as "mismatch cells," and layer 5/6 pyramidal cells should not be labeled universally as "render broadcasters." Oddball, prediction-related, or movement-related activity is task-, area-, and cell-population-specific. Layer 6 corticothalamic neurons and layer 5 corticofugal neurons also have different projection targets and functions.

CA1 theta phase progression is a hippocampal population phenomenon. It can motivate a phase-sensitive SAN hypothesis, but it does not prove that phase waves travel along every pyramidal dendrite to encode a rendered prediction.

## Bounded SAN/NAPOT interpretation

At the **micro scale**, a pyramidal neuron gathers spatially distributed synaptic input, transforms it through dendritic and somatic dynamics, and projects action potentials through its axon.

At the **meso scale**, recurrent populations of pyramidal neurons and interneurons exchange updates within and across local circuits. A cortical column diagram is a useful canonical motif only when labeled as such; actual laminar connectivity varies by area and species.

At the **macro scale**, cortical, thalamic, hippocampal, sensory, and motor networks exchange partial state. SAN/NAPOT proposes that this recurrent activity contributes to a coherent, continuously updated working model. The model is distributed: no pyramidal cell, layer, column, or central point is the observer.

Within this interpretation, tonic activity is the changing population context or canvas against which phasic synaptic and spiking events make differences. The claim becomes scientific only when tonic and phasic variables, anatomical pathways, decoded content, motor consequence, and falsifying perturbation are specified.
