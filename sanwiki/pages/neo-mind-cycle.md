**Neo Mind Cycle** was an EEG-based [[neurofeedback]] and [[brainwave-entrainment]] system that [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] operated from 2012 to 2014. It provided real-time audio-visual feedback from EEG sensors, allowing the user to intentionally modulate their own [[neural-oscillations|neural oscillations]].

## Audio-cognitive parameter map

The system exposed six audio parameters — volume, pitch, modulation frequency, LFO speed, LFO depth, wave shape, and reverb — each mappable to a cognitive or emotional state. Users could customize which mental commands drove which parameters. The default binding:

- **Pitch**: controlled by thinking "up" / "down"
- **Other parameters**: controlled by thinking "rotate clockwise" / "rotate counterclockwise"

A waveform visualization and sensor-connectivity indicator provided live visual feedback.

## Significance for SAN theory

Operating Neo Mind Cycle gave [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] first-hand experience with [[intentional-oscillation-control]]: the idea that directed thought can selectively alter the frequency and amplitude of neural waveforms. This practical grounding supports SAN claims about [[criterial-tempo-spatial-waves]] — that oscillatory patterns are not passive background activity but can be subject to top-down criterial selection.

The system also illustrated early that [[tonic-oscillation]] (baseline state) and phasic modulation (event-driven change) are jointly necessary for closed-loop BCI control, a distinction that reappears throughout SAN theory.
