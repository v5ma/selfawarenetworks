# Mitral Cells

**Mitral Cells** are the principal output neurons of the [olfactory neural pathway](olfactory-neural-pathway.md) in vertebrates: second-order olfactory neurons that receive primary input at glomeruli from olfactory receptor neurons, exchange signals through [dendro-dendritic](dendritic-architecture.md) synapses with granule cells and juxtaglomerular neurons including periglomerular cells, and route the result to piriform [cortex](cortex.md) and beyond. Within [Self](gh-a0206z.md)-Aware Networks they enter the wiki as a worked example of how the SAN [oscillation-hierarchy](neural-oscillation-hierarchy.md) / [brain-rhythm](brain-rhythms.md) account makes contact with mainstream sensory neuroscience: mitral cells are *strongly modulated by theta oscillations* in the olfactory bulb, paralleling [pyramidal cells](pyramidal-cell.md) in primary visual [cortex](cortex.md), and so they instantiate the SAN reading that across modalities the same theta / [gamma](gamma-wave-consideration-sandwich.md) grid organises sensory output.

## Post-SAN

Compared with mainstream olfactory-bulb literature - which treats mitral-cell modulation as a within-modality phenomenon (theta-coupled odour sampling, breath-locked oscillation) - the SAN reading reframes the mitral-cell theta modulation as one instance of a *cross-modal* oscillation grid that also organises [pyramidal-cell](pyramidal-cell.md) output in primary visual [cortex](cortex.md), in the *same* high-theta-low-alpha band (`vecA--md`, line 2579, [on github](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/d1c26a6fbf273695185a4036411215f53209eb71/vecA.md)). The structural prediction this generates is that mitral-cell-style theta modulation should generalise across other principal-cell populations in early sensory pathways, and that perturbing the high-theta-low-alpha band should produce parallel signatures in olfaction and vision rather than modality-specific ones - see [neural-oscillation-hierarchy](neural-oscillation-hierarchy.md), [brain-rhythms](brain-rhythms.md), [brain-rhythms-in-cognition](brain-rhythms-in-cognition.md), and [napot-theory](napot-theory.md).
