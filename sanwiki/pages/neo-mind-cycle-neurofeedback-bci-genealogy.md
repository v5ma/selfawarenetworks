# Neo Mind Cycle Source Genealogy: Neurofeedback Salon to BCI

Neo Mind Cycle was a real San Francisco neurofeedback program and research practice, not a later
metaphor invented to decorate Self Aware Networks. The recovered sources describe EEG use,
brainwave graphs, audio-visual feedback, isochronic stimulation, client sessions, business plans,
equipment limitations, and an effort to help participants notice relations between internal state
and changing light and sound.

The later SAN importance is not that every early efficacy claim was established. It is that Micah
spent years confronting a practical question that became central to NerveGear and Neural
Rendering: how can a machine measure a changing neural signal, transform it, return a structured
signal through the senses, and let a living brain learn the loop?

## What the apparatus did

The program described in the sources used an EEG headset to sample scalp electrical activity,
displayed brainwave graphs, and transformed some measured variation into changing light and sound.
The participant could then see and hear a signal related to their own current measurement. Some
sessions added isochronic audio-visual stimulation. This produced a closed sensor-machine-sensor
loop:

> measured neural activity -> software transformation -> light and sound -> sensory response ->
> changed neural activity

That loop is the durable engineering idea. It is also why the early work belongs in the BCI and SAN
history even though scalp EEG has low spatial specificity and the software did not decode detailed
mental content.

## Two neurofeedback modes

`a0031z` makes a useful distinction:

1. **Command decoding:** software tries to classify EEG features and map them onto a fixed external
   control, such as moving an object on a screen.
2. **Mirrored feedback:** the system transforms measured activity into light or sound and lets the
   participant's brain learn relations within the returned signal.

Micah favored the second mode for the salon because it avoided pretending that the available EEG
and software could reliably decode arbitrary intent. Later NerveGear work keeps both problems in
view: read a useful state, then determine what structured change can safely be written back.

`a0083z` makes the deeper design principle explicit. In a rigid command decoder, software must
decide what the measured feature means. In mirrored feedback, software preserves a changing
relation and returns it through the senses, while the participant learns what the relation means
inside the closed loop. That does not turn low-resolution scalp EEG into a thought reader. It does
explain why later SAN emphasizes receiver-relative transformation and recursive sensory return.

## A September 2013 choice bridge

`a0092z.md` connects learned criteria, noisy neural transitions, oscillator synchronization,
neurofeedback, and plasticity inside the Neo Mind Cycle program. This is an early ancestor of the
later Choice model: current state and learned criteria constrain a transition, feedback changes the
next state, and repeated outcomes can alter later routing. The metronome analogy is an intuition,
not proof of a universal synchronization mechanism. The multi-speaker record preserves Matthew
Bendyna's contributions separately.

`a0116z.md` preserves an expanded version of the `a0092z.md` conversation family. It repeats the
Neo Mind Cycle exchange and later asks whether added hardware becomes integrated with, suppresses,
or displaces an earlier active self-process. Matthew Bendyna's black-hole-brain proposal and
Micah's self/hardware replies remain separately attributed. The source turns external-cortex
engineering into an identity and ethics question.

## Historical evidence versus efficacy evidence

The raw notes establish that Micah designed and described the program, used EEG, specified session
apparatus and prices, and connected the work to a longer BCI mission. They do not by themselves
establish clinical effectiveness, superiority to standard neurofeedback, treatment of autism,
PTSD, addiction, depression, or other conditions, or the proposed mechanism of broad cross-region
plasticity.

Book 2 should preserve the hands-on amateur-neuroscience history while clearly separating:

- apparatus and procedure;
- participant reports and Micah's personal observations;
- historical marketing or benefit claims;
- independent neurofeedback and entrainment evidence;
- present-day safety and clinical standards; and
- the later SAN interpretation of feedback, self-modeling, and neural rendering.

The additional `a0435z.md` reflection shows why the history matters: direct work with EEG led to a
demand for explanations that connect cellular and synaptic dynamics to loops, regions, and the
changing person. `a0438z.md` and `a0442z.md` then place NerveGear, SAN biology, and artificial
intelligence in one program while explicitly anticipating three separable books. The Wiki records
that architecture while Book 2 remains paused.
