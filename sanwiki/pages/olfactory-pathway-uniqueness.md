# Olfaction and the First-Order Thalamic Relay

> **Scientific boundary:** Olfaction is unusual because receptor input can reach primary olfactory cortex without an obligatory first-order thalamic relay. It is not thalamus-free, it does not prove that smell has a privileged relation to consciousness, and it should not be contrasted with every other sense as though each followed one identical serial route.

## What is anatomically distinctive

Olfactory sensory neurons in the nasal epithelium send axons through the cribriform plate to the olfactory bulb. Neurons expressing a specified receptor converge on a small number of glomerular targets in mouse bulb ([Mombaerts et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0092-8674%2800%2981387-2)). Bulb output then branches to piriform cortex and other olfactory forebrain targets.

This differs from the obligatory first-order thalamic relay that precedes primary cortical processing in vision, audition, and somatosensation. The bounded comparison is about **relay order**, not the absence of thalamic participation and not one universal architecture for all non-olfactory senses.

## The thalamus still participates

Later olfactory processing includes mediodorsal-thalamic and orbitofrontal relations. Attention to odor altered coupling along an indirect piriform-mediodorsal-thalamus-orbitofrontal route in human imaging ([Plailly et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5607-07.2008)). Focal thalamic lesions could spare detection while impairing identification or hedonic judgment ([Tham et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2114-09.2009)).

These findings do not license a simple split in which the thalamus detects and localizes while orbitofrontal cortex understands. Detection, identification, memory, valuation, attention, and report depend on overlapping but distinguishable network relations.

Orbitofrontal cortex also should not be treated as anatomically synonymous with ventromedial prefrontal cortex. The labels overlap in some usages and parcellations but are not interchangeable names for one structure.

## Active sampling is the more useful distinction

Sniffing controls when and how odorants reach the sensory epithelium. Human imaging distinguished sniff-related from odor-related activity ([Sobel et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/32654)), and experimentally shifting olfactory activation within the sniff cycle changed mouse discrimination behavior ([Smear et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10521)).

Olfaction therefore demonstrates that a sensory measurement includes the organism's sampling action. A receptor pattern alone is not a finished odor identity, value, memory, or conscious experience.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can use olfaction as a counterexample to camera-like sensory models. Receptor convergence, dispersed cortical ensembles, recurrent routing, respiration-locked sampling, memory, body state, and action provide different receiver contexts at different scales.

The SAN proposal is that maintained tonic population activity and phasic departures may coordinate those partial measurements within a distributed observer-action loop. The established anatomy supports the component architecture; it does not establish a universal phase code, a special olfactory consciousness channel, or NAPOT tomography.
