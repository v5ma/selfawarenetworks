A published dispute by [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] (SVGN.io) asserting that MIT's Picower Institute improperly claimed originality for "Spatial Computing" theory in 2023, when Blumberg had published foundational concepts through the [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks framework between 2017 and 2022.

Blumberg's priority claim rests on timestamped GitHub releases, podcast episodes, and archived materials predating Miller Lab's 2023–2024 publications by five to six years.

**The core analogy:**

- **Blumberg's "Canvas and Ink"** (2022): beta-band wave as structural canvas; gamma-band wave as content ink — an oscillatory binding mechanism (→ [[tonic-phasic-canvas]]).
- **Miller Lab's "Stencil"** (2024): the same oscillatory distinction under different terminology.

Both converge on [[criterial-tempo-spatial-waves]] as a neural scanning mechanism, on phase-based [[cortical-column-oscillatory-transmission]] for binding distributed activity, and on space-time equivalence in cortical dynamics.

The article applies the phrase **academic credit laundering** to institutional erasure of prior art through rebranding. Core SAN concepts named in the dispute: [[napot]], [[super-information-theory-v76]], [[phase-differential-rendering]], [[tonic-phasic-canvas]].

The priority argument applies equally to NAPOT's earlier articulation of the "tomography" mechanism (converting oscillatory phase into volumetric phenomenal experience) and to the broader [[multi-scale-causality]] framework Blumberg published on GitHub from 2017 onward.

## History

Blumberg contacted the Picower Institute directly; the "first proposed in 2023" claim was not retracted.
