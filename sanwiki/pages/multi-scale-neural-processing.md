# Multi Scale Neural Processing

## Post-SAN

Mainstream literature treats multi-scale neural processing primarily as a *modelling* and *measurement* problem — what scales are computationally relevant, how they couple, how multi-scale models should be parameterised. SAN-side commitments push past this in two specific places. First, the [[fractal-brain-scale-hierarchy|fractal-brain-scale-hierarchy]] / [[fractal-neural-scale-hierarchy|fractal-neural-scale-hierarchy]] / [[brain-as-fractal-of-modules|brain-as-fractal-of-modules]] line treats spatial multi-scale as *[[gh-a0206z|self]]-similar* — the same column-and-array motif repeats at every scale — so that the [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]] readout machinery is *recursively applicable* across scales rather than scale-specific. Second, the [[scale-invariant-causation|scale-invariant causation]] / [[multi-scale-causality|multi-scale causality]] pair treats causal structure as *preserved* across scales, so that the [[criterial-causation|criterial-causation]] mechanism that operates at one scale has direct analogues at every other. Both commitments yield testable predictions: the first about cross-scale phase-locking signatures during cognitive tasks, the second about whether disrupting one scale's coordination should preferentially disrupt cross-scale-causal patterns rather than within-scale activity. None of this denies the published multi-scale-neuroscience literature; it relocates multi-scale neural processing as a *constitutive* primitive of the SAN architecture rather than as an emergent property to be measured. The SAN line is therefore distinguishable from a flat "the brain is multi-scale" gloss by being a specific architectural commitment about how the multi-scale structure is coordinated.
