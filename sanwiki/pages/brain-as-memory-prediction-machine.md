# Brain as a Memory-Prediction Machine

> **Image status:** The previous concept plate failed medical and scientific review and is preserved
> in the do-not-use medical-review archive. No replacement image is accepted. A source-bounded
> replacement prompt exists, and image generation remains paused.

The phrase **brain as a memory-prediction machine** names an early SAN functional proposition:
learned regularities help a living system form a best available expectation, action tests that
expectation in the world, and returned consequences can change later memory and prediction.

It does not mean that the whole brain implements one prediction algorithm, that every neural signal
is a prediction error, or that each cortical column has one universal memory-prediction circuit.
Different memory, sensory, motor, autonomic, and valuation systems contribute under different tasks
and timescales.

## The recovered operator

```text
past experience and learned structure
-> present cue, body state, and recurrent context
-> partial retrieval or reconstructed state
-> expectation about a possible sensory or action consequence
-> selection and action
-> actual bodily and environmental consequence
-> match, mismatch, value, and salience signals
-> selective updating of state, routing, and memory
```

The word `machine` is functional. The biological system is living tissue with metabolism,
neuromodulation, plasticity, many interacting cell types, and embodied consequences. Prediction is
one operation within that system, not its only purpose.

## What the 2011 and 2012 sources actually say

The September 2011 source [[gh-a0051z|a0051z]] describes experience being sorted into statistical
memory, memory being used to predict what happens next, those predictions influencing movement,
and sensory consequences returning through a learning loop that can learn aspects of itself.

The December 2012 dialogue [[gh-a0563z|a0563z]] contains the exact phrase that the brain is "in large
part a memory-becomes-prediction machine." Its joined argument is that an organism does not require
certainty before acting. It uses a best available model to coordinate movement, encounters the
consequences, and continues as a metabolically maintained living process.

That source also argues that thought is physical and that stimuli can affect neural tissue without
being consciously understood. The medically defensible recovery is narrower than some of its
wording: an unfamiliar spoken word can still have acoustic, orienting, arousal, and learning effects,
but semantic effects depend on learned meaning and context. Brain imaging can associate tasks or
representational variables with distributed activity; it does not assign every thought type to one
distinct cortical location.

## What established neuroscience supports

Several component operations have direct empirical support:

- Sparse recurrent connectivity and disynaptic motifs in hippocampal CA3 can support pattern
  completion in experiment-constrained network models
  ([Guzman et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27609885/)).
- In a human high-resolution fMRI task, cue-plus-action trials related hippocampal pattern
  completion to memory-based expectation in early visual cortex
  ([Hindy, Ng, and Turk-Browne, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4948994/)).
- Human behavior and intracranial responses during recognition of heavily occluded objects support
  a role for recurrent computation in inference from partial information
  ([Tang et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30104363/)).
- Congruence between an action and its visual outcome can sharpen decodable sensory
  representations rather than simply cancel them
  ([Yon et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30327503/)).
- During motor adaptation, perception of one's own action can depend on adaptable internal
  predictions about its sensory outcome
  ([Synofzik, Thier, and Lindner, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16738220/)).

These findings support memory-based expectation, pattern completion, recurrence, predicted action
consequences, and updating in bounded tasks. They do not prove that the brain is exhausted by one
predictive objective, that prediction error automatically rewrites memory, or that SAN's complete
rendering and observer account is correct.

## Memory is not a stored movie

`Memory` can refer to synaptic and intrinsic changes, structural plasticity, active engram
ensembles, short-lived working state, hippocampal-neocortical interaction, learned policies, or
reconstructed content. A later percept is not necessarily a bit-for-bit replay of an earlier neural
state.

[[pattern-completion|Pattern Completion]] describes recovery of a larger learned state from partial
input under specific network constraints. [[engram|Engram]] describes physical memory-bearing
changes and participating cell ensembles.
[[memory-state-drift-virtual-mind|Memory, Engrams, Representational Drift, and the Virtual Mind]]
explains why persistence of function does not require an anatomically frozen representation.

## Prediction is not one universal cortical message

Prediction can concern the next sensory feature, action consequence, location, reward, body state,
task transition, or longer learned sequence. Its implementation can involve different circuits and
variables. A predictive representation, a generative model, a motor command, corollary discharge,
attention, and mismatch response are related but not interchangeable.

The earlier figure's labels `L2/3 association`, `L4 sensory evidence`, and `L5 motor/output` resemble
one simplified laminar predictive-coding diagram. They cannot stand for cortex universally. Laminar
cell types, inputs, outputs, and recurrence vary by area, species, developmental state, task, and
measurement method.

## The later SAN extension

The 2011-2012 operator is an ancestor. NAPOT and PWD were named and developed later.

The June 2022 source [[gh-a0215z|a0215z]] joins temporospatial neural patterns, prediction of future
input, decision, and motor response. The August 2022 Whitepaper then develops memory scaling:
learned cellular and synaptic structure can contribute partial states that are re-expressed through
larger neural groups. The November 2022 sources [[gh-a0310z|a0310z]] and [[gh-a0319z|a0319z]] join
memory, prediction, recurrent rendering, tonic context, differentiated phasic activity, and motor
consequence.

SAN's mature extension is therefore:

```text
learned cellular and circuit constraints
-> cue-dependent partial recruitment
-> recurrent multiscale reconstruction
-> receiver-relative tonic and phasic change
-> distributed action-usable rendering
-> selection, movement, and returned consequence
-> state and plasticity update
```

[[napot-overview|NAPOT]] proposes the multiscale redistribution and reconstruction architecture.
[[tonic-absorption-generative-reexpression|Tonic Absorption and Generative Re-expression]] develops
the transition between fading current differences and later cue-driven reconstruction.
[[agency-valuation-action-feedback|Agency, Valuation, Action Selection, and Returned Consequence]]
develops the action side of the loop.

## Four scales that must not be collapsed

| Scale | Candidate operation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Molecular and cellular | Receptors, synapses, dendrites, membrane conductances, plasticity, and recent state alter response. | One neuron does not contain or predict the complete world model. |
| Local circuit | Recurrent excitation, inhibition, neuromodulation, and cell assemblies support state selection or completion. | One canonical cortical microcircuit cannot be assumed in every area. |
| Distributed systems | Hippocampal, cortical, thalamic, striatal, cerebellar, brainstem, autonomic, and body systems contribute different variables. | Their participation is task-dependent and does not imply direct all-to-all connection. |
| Organism-world | Action changes the body and environment; consequences return as new sensory and bodily input. | A passive prediction-only model omits the causal loop that gives the prediction practical meaning. |

## A bounded formal model

Let `c_t` be a present cue, `x_t` current distributed state, `b_t` body state, and `W_t` learned
constraints. A retrieval operator `R` produces a partial reconstructed state `h_t`:

```text
h_t = R(c_t, x_t, b_t; W_t)                                   [1]
```

An expectation operator `G` and action policy `Pi` generate a predicted consequence `p_t` and an
action `a_t`:

```text
p_t = G(h_t, x_t, b_t);  a_t = Pi(h_t, p_t, g_t)               [2]
```

The body and environment produce an observed consequence `y_(t+1)`. A comparison `D` need not be a
literal subtraction performed in one brain region:

```text
y_(t+1) = E(a_t, body_t, world_t);  d_t = D(y_(t+1), p_t)      [3]
```

Learning depends on mismatch, value, salience, neuromodulation, eligibility, and current state:

```text
W_(t+1) = U(W_t, h_t, a_t, d_t, value_t, state_t)              [4]
```

A SAN model must identify which variables are measured, decoded, perturbed, or merely inferred. It
must not label every neural change a prediction error or every later change a memory rewrite.

## Discriminating experiments

1. **Memory contribution:** Compare a learned predictive cue with matched novel and familiar but
   nonpredictive cues. Test whether hippocampal and cortical state predicts the specific expected
   outcome rather than familiarity or arousal alone.
2. **Action consequence:** Separate predictions caused by an intended action from passive temporal
   expectation while holding the sensory sequence constant.
3. **Partial reconstruction:** Perturb recurrence at a declared time and circuit. Test whether the
   intervention selectively impairs completion from partial information more than recognition from
   complete information.
4. **Selective updating:** Measure whether mismatch changes later behavior or representation only
   when value, salience, neuromodulatory state, and plasticity conditions permit learning.
5. **SAN increment:** Test whether receiver-relative PWD, tonic-context, pathway, and embodied-state
   variables improve held-out prediction beyond rate, spectral power, ordinary recurrent state,
   predictive-coding, and reinforcement-learning baselines.
6. **Observer boundary:** Keep successful prediction, report, action, learning, and phenomenal
   experience as different outcomes. None should be treated as proof of the others.

## Claim ladder

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Memory and learned structure can influence expectation and action. | Established broadly, with circuit and task limits. |
| Partial cues can recruit larger learned states through recurrent pattern completion. | Supported in named preparations and models; not universal. |
| Action predictions can alter sensory representations and motor adaptation. | Supported in bounded human and animal tasks. |
| The complete memory-prediction-action-return loop is a useful SAN organizing architecture. | Source-defined synthesis; its complete biological implementation remains to be tested. |
| NAPOT and PWD explain how reconstructed states become distributed rendered content. | Open SAN hypothesis. |
| The same recurrent process constitutes the observer or phenomenal self. | Strong SAN hypothesis; unresolved. |

## Thermodynamic boundary

Living brains are open, metabolically active, dissipative systems: they consume energy and material,
perform work, and release heat and waste. The 2012 `vortex` language preserves that organism-level
intuition. It does not by itself specify a neural code, a learning rule, or a consciousness
mechanism. Entropy accounting must be tied to declared physical variables and system boundaries.
