A published open letter by [[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]] (SVGN.io) addressed to AI video generation platforms — Seedance, Kling, Runway, Pika, Luma, Sora, Veo, and Firefly — arguing they should resist legal pressure from major entertainment companies to over-restrict user-generated creative content.

The article grounds its argument in existing U.S. copyright law: fair use doctrine (17 U.S.C. §107) and DMCA safe harbors (17 U.S.C. §512) already protect both creators and platforms, making aggressive pre-emptive content blocking legally unnecessary and culturally harmful. The Sony Betamax precedent is cited — general-purpose creative tools bear no inherent liability for how users employ them.

**Core legal distinctions the article enforces:**

- *Transformative use*: parody, commentary, criticism, and satire retain protection even when extensive copying occurs.
- *Substantial noninfringing uses*: a tool's dual-use character provides safe harbor unless the platform intentionally induces infringement.
- *Safe harbor mechanics*: notice-and-takedown procedures, not prior restraint, are the appropriate response to infringement claims.

**Recommended platform strategy**: clear terms of service, optional technical warnings (not blanket blocks), efficient takedown systems, content metadata transparency, and selective licensing — not pre-emptive prompt-level censorship.

This article complements Blumberg's broader thesis on [[consciousness-as-high-bandwidth-choice-mechanism]] — that intelligent agents (human and AI) must preserve freedom of generative [[choice]] to express creativity.
