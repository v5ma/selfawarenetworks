# Attention Schema Theory and the SAN Comparison

> **Image status:** The earlier plate is preserved in the failed-medical-review archive and is not approved for publication. It incorrectly drew a one-way progression from attention to a TPJ-centered schema and then to a SAN oscillatory internal screen. A replacement must compare two causal models without localizing awareness to one region, depicting a homuncular display, or treating a phase pattern as consciousness.

## What Attention Schema Theory proposes

Attention Schema Theory (AST) is principally associated with Michael Graziano and colleagues. Its core proposal is that a nervous system constructs a simplified, continuously updated model of its own selective attention. Like a body schema used in motor control, an attention schema need not reproduce every mechanism. It represents enough of the current and likely state of attention to help predict and control it.

AST distinguishes:

- **attention:** selective changes in how information influences processing and behavior;
- **attention schema:** a proposed descriptive and predictive model of that selection;
- **awareness attribution:** information by which a system attributes awareness to itself or another;
- **awareness report:** a judgment or communication based partly on those internal models;
- **phenomenal consciousness:** the existence and character of experience, which remains a stronger explanatory target.

Graziano and Kastner introduced awareness as a perceptual model of attention in 2011. Webb and Graziano developed the mechanistic control-model account in 2015. AST does not require a nonphysical spectator, a literal screen, or one consciousness center.

## What the evidence establishes

Behavioral and computational results support narrower parts of the control-model proposal:

- In six human experiments, awareness of a cue was required for one form of endogenous attentional control, while exogenous capture could persist without cue awareness.
- In one fMRI study using that task, right temporoparietal-junction activity tracked the condition in which cue awareness supported control of attention.
- In a trained neural-network agent, access to a descriptive attention schema improved learning and control of a simple visual-attention task.
- People can combine gaze and affective cues when attributing awareness to themselves or others.

These results support attention-model and control questions. They do not establish that the right TPJ contains awareness, that an attention schema is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, or that a successful artificial controller is conscious. The TPJ is a heterogeneous nexus involved in attention, social cognition, memory, and other task-dependent operations.

## Historical SAN source recovery

Micah recorded a sustained analysis of Graziano on October 11, 2021; a hand-edited version was [publicly fixed on November 28, 2022](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/4e0cf1ee0975c01dc57182368497d3f0cd864030/b0146yhand.md). The source contains an important self-correction: it retracts the earlier reading that Graziano had reduced consciousness to a TPJ module.

The source was not merely proposing a larger screen. It developed five operations:

1. **selection at several levels:** neurons and populations can be selected into, and help select, learned play-back or play-forward patterns;
2. **partial-pattern completion:** a partial cue can recruit a learned ensemble and bias which predicted pattern is completed;
3. **context and consequence:** excitation, inhibition, readiness, prior learning, and expected consequences alter which pattern gains influence;
4. **tracking versus report:** sensory systems can continue to track content that is not currently attended, consciously accessed, or reported;
5. **observer-action closure:** recurrent transmission, multimodal convergence, listening-and-sending turns, movement, and returned sensory consequences update the network.

Some anatomical language in the recording was exploratory. Multimodal information does not literally collide at one TPJ screen, V1 and V2 are not complete sender and listener populations, and cortical traffic is not exhausted by one six-direction Cartesian hierarchy. The recoverable operation is distributed convergence, transformation, recurrent return, and action.

## AST and SAN: overlap and difference

Both accounts reject an extra inner observer and distinguish attention from a model or representation of attention. Both also connect internal modeling to prediction and control.

AST asks whether a simplified model of attention helps a system control attention, attribute awareness, and generate awareness reports. SAN asks an additional implementation question: how do distributed receivers, learned assemblies, ongoing context, event-related changes, inhibition, recurrence, body state, and action jointly instantiate and revise the model?

[[latent-pattern-conscious-rendering-transition|From Latent Pattern to Conscious Rendering]] makes
that implementation difference explicit. Early AST supplies an attention-model and control
problem; SAN's current bridge asks how a latent virtual engram is reconstructed, selectively joins
an active distributed state, gains flexible consequence, and later leaves without erasing the
underlying memory.

The source-faithful SAN comparison is:

**current sensory and bodily state + ongoing context -> candidate learned patterns -> selective recruitment or suppression -> model and prediction of the attended relation -> partial completion or revision -> memory, report, or action -> returned consequences update the next cycle**

SAN calls the ongoing receiver-relative context **tonic** and the differentiated updates **phasic**. These are proposed operational roles, not fixed frequency bands. Phase relations may contribute to selection or communication, but phase alignment alone neither constructs an attention schema nor establishes consciousness.

SAN's use of **rendering** means distributed transformation and action-ready re-expression. It does not mean that a volumetric picture is projected onto an oscillatory screen for another entity to inspect. The network performing detection, selection, transformation, report, and action is the observer-action system.

## Established, proposed, and open

- **Established:** attention is selective and controllable; attention and awareness can dissociate; distributed neural systems contribute to attention, social inference, report, and action.
- **AST proposal:** a simplified descriptive and predictive model of attention contributes to endogenous control and awareness attribution.
- **SAN proposal:** receiver-relative tonic context, phasic updates, learned pattern completion, recurrent routes, bodily state, and consequences jointly implement a distributed attention-to-observer-action loop.
- **Open:** whether either architecture explains or is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness.
- **Rejected shortcut:** TPJ activation, phase coherence, successful report, or improved controller performance is not a consciousness certificate.

## Discriminating tests

A useful comparison should independently manipulate sensory evidence, distractor strength, task relevance, cue awareness, the subject's belief about attentional state, report demand, action mapping, and consequence. It should measure:

1. attention itself;
2. the model's prediction of attention;
3. learned pattern recruitment and completion;
4. receiver-specific timing, gain, and connectivity;
5. flexible downstream use, movement, and returned feedback.

AST gains support when an explicit attention model improves control beyond attention alone. SAN gains separate support only when its receiver-relative context-and-update variables predict and causally alter pattern completion, route recruitment, and action-feedback dynamics beyond rate, recurrent-state, connectivity, and schema-only models.

## Replacement figure brief

A medically accurate comparison should use two overlapping feedback loops. The AST loop should show selective attention, a descriptive/predictive attention model, control, and updated consequences. The SAN loop should show distributed sensory and bodily state, learned candidate assemblies, receiver-relative tonic context and phasic updates, selective recruitment, action or report, and returned feedback. Right TPJ may appear only as one task-dependent node supported by a specific experiment. The figure must not contain a localized awareness center, volumetric inner screen, universal phase code, or linear claim that SAN simply generalizes AST.
