[[neural-lace|Neural Lace]] Podcast Season 2 Episode 2 (Sept 2018), hosted by [[gh-resnote04|Micah Blumberg]] with Jules Urbach (CEO, Otoy). The interview covers Nvidia RTX ray tracing, volumetric capture, AI-accelerated rendering, and the SAN question of whether rendered systems can develop [[self-aware-network]] properties.

## Core themes

**Real-time ray tracing (RTX)** — Urbach argues that path-traced rendering is foundational for the next decade: 8-10× over Pascal, solving anti-aliasing and depth-of-field without hacks, enabling 90fps 4K VR. The implication for SAN: photorealistic fidelity approaches the threshold where simulated environments become indistinguishable sensory substrates.

**AI in the render pipeline** — AI denoising finishes renders by inferring unsampled rays; AI upscaling fills resolution gaps. This models a [[fractal-pattern]] found in neural prediction: the brain also "finishes" perception by inferring from sparse sensory samples. See [[brain-as-memory-prediction-machine]].

**Volumetric capture and neural feedback** — Lightstage capture records reflective fields beyond RGB/depth. When combined with EIT (Electrical Impedance Tomography), brain activity can be rendered volumetrically in VR — a closed-loop system where neural state generates visual output which loops back to influence neural state. This directly mirrors the [[gh-a0206z|self]]-referential architecture in [[self-as-learning-loop]].

**Consciousness and simulation** — Urbach draws on Peter Tse's framing: consciousness as a higher-level pipeline solving problems the unconscious cannot. This is compatible with the SAN model of [[oscillator-consciousness-threshold]] — awareness as emergent from sufficient oscillatory integration, not from substrate alone.

## SAN connections

- [[agentic-viewpoints]] — Urbach's "philosophical zombie" discussion maps to SAN's distinction between functional intelligence and genuine agency.
- [[phase-differential-rendering]] — foveated rendering via ray tracing is a real-world instance of selective phase-resolution allocation, a pattern SAN identifies at the cortical level.
- [[neural-tomography]] — EIT + volumetric capture is the hardware analogue of SAN's [[neural-tomography|neural tomography]] concept.
