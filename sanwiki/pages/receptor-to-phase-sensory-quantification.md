# Receptor-to-Phase Sensory Quantification

## The idea in plain language

A taste receptor does not send a finished taste into the brain, and a hair cell does not send a
finished sound. Each cell transforms what reaches it. Receptors, second messengers, ion channels,
membrane voltage, calcium, and synaptic release all help determine the pattern passed onward.

SAN calls attention to the complete route:

```text
outside difference -> receptor response -> cellular change -> neural output -> array pattern
```

The final array pattern is interpreted relative to the activity already present in its receiver.
That receiver-relative difference is where this idea joins Phase Wave Differential (PWD).

## Why taste and hearing belong together

Umami can involve multiple receptor pathways and combinations of chemical inputs. Hearing begins
with mechanical movement of hair bundles and gating of mechanotransduction channels. cAMP can alter
the sensitivity of that auditory machinery. The details differ, but both senses demonstrate that a
cell converts an input through its own morphology and current state.

## What is established and what SAN adds

Established biology supports selective receptors, intracellular signaling, channel modulation,
mechanotransduction, action potentials, and state-dependent release.

SAN adds the hypothesis that these transformations should be followed all the way into a
receiver-relative spatial-temporal phase pattern, and that this richer pattern may predict sensory
distinctions better than firing rate alone.

That hypothesis is testable; it is not yet a demonstrated universal sensory code.

The [[synaptic-unreliability-mvr-2021|ionotropic information-channel]] continues this transformation
chain through presynaptic waveform, calcium, release probability, MVR, receptors, and dendritic
state.

`a0448z.md`, a multi-speaker discussion internally dated September 3, 2013, adds an important
boundary: a transmitter's name is not an experience. Effects depend on receptor subtype and
location, cellular state, network context, and downstream consequence. It also recovers ATP's
extracellular transmitter or cotransmitter role. That role is distinct from intracellular ATP's
metabolic-energy role. A drug that activates a receptor does not thereby become an endogenous
neurotransmitter.
