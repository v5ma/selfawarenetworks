# EEG, XR, Neurofeedback, and BCI

Combining EEG with an interactive XR scene can form a closed loop:

```text
measured physiological activity
    -> feature extraction
    -> declared mapping or trained model
    -> visual, auditory, or haptic scene change
    -> participant perception and action
    -> new physiological activity
```

This is a useful experimental and artistic architecture. Its interpretation depends on what was measured, how the feature was validated, and what changed in the participant.

## What the sensors measure

Scalp EEG measures voltage differences generated by spatially and temporally summed transmembrane currents, filtered by tissue, skull, electrodes, reference choice, and artifact. Eye tracking measures gaze and eye events. An inertial measurement unit measures headset motion. Pulse or photoplethysmographic sensors measure cardiovascular proxies. Combining them can improve a task-specific model, but it does not turn them into interchangeable measurements of intention, emotion, diagnosis, or conscious content.

Eye blinks, facial muscle activity, head motion, and eye movement can contaminate EEG while also carrying behavioral information of their own. A system can distinguish intentional from reflexive events only after those classes are operationally defined and tested with appropriate labels, held-out participants or sessions, artifact controls, and uncertainty estimates.

## Historical SAN route

Neo Mind Cycle used EEG-derived activity to change lights and sounds in a feedback setting. The later 2018 collaborator-built route passed processed EEG values through Python, Go, WebSocket, Three.js, and A-Frame so browser-based VR geometry changed in real time. That demonstrates state-responsive media and a closed sensory feedback path.

It does not demonstrate semantic thought decoding, reliable intentional control, diagnosis, reconstruction of a viewed or imagined object, or direct percept writing. Missing original code, configuration, complete contributor ledger, and session data remain provenance limits for reconstructing the exact 2018 implementation.

## Neurofeedback boundary

Neurofeedback provides a participant with information derived from a measured physiological signal so that learning or state change can be studied in a closed loop. Effects depend on the signal, feedback rule, controls, participant strategy, learning, and outcome. A responsive display alone does not establish therapeutic efficacy. Clinical claims require condition-specific controlled evidence and should not be inferred from the existence of the EEG-XR loop.

The positive measurement program is:

```text
baseline and declared task
-> measured feature
-> specified visual, auditory, or haptic transformation
-> participant perception and strategy
-> next measurement and behavior
-> sham, transfer, and retention comparison
```

That loop can be compared with a drug, stimulation, cueing, or other perturbation by asking whether
the registered response trajectories converge for a named variable and time window. A shared
endpoint does not imply a shared mechanism, equal safety, or clinical interchangeability. SAN
preserves the testable extension as an individualized intervention-response atlas rather than a
blanket efficacy claim. See [[multimodal-biosensor-fusion]].

## SAN interpretation

At the macro scale, the EEG-XR system is an engineering analogy for recurrent receive-transform-re-express-use organization: an external system samples a coarse physiological state, transforms it, changes the sensory environment, and receives the participant's returned response. It is not a direct scale-up of one cortical column or neuron, and it does not prove that the same algorithm operates at cellular, circuit, and whole-person scales.

The useful SAN question is whether a declared feedback variable changes an identified internal or behavioral state beyond sham feedback, ordinary sensory learning, movement, expectation, and artifact. That requires preregistered targets, controls, held-out evaluation, and causal intervention.

## From responsive display to external cortex

The 2018 EEG-in-XR route supplies a real closed-loop engineering ancestor:

```text
person acts or changes state
-> system measures a declared feature
-> XR scene changes
-> person perceives and responds
-> next measurement
```

That operation does more than a passive recording, but it is not yet an external cortex. The
stronger external-cortex proposal requires person-specific learning, reciprocal adaptation,
improvement beyond static and behavior-only controls, retention or transfer tests, and safe
withdrawal. Co-adaptive brain-machine-interface research shows that user and decoder can learn
together ([Shenoy and Carmena, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.038)); cognitive-
offloading experiments show that external support can improve immediate performance while also
changing unaided memory
([Grinschgl et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218211008060)).

Those established operations do not show that EEG reads arbitrary thoughts or that an adaptive
display has become cortical tissue. SAN preserves the testable extension: a multimodal,
person-specific, reversible loop may become functionally cortex-like when it repeatedly expands
the person's available memory, discrimination, planning, or action while remaining causally
integrated with that person's learning.
