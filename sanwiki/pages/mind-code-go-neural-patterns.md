The ancient game of Go is proposed as a metaphor for neural coding: black stones (excitation) and white stones (inhibition) together define the [[spatiotemporal-pattern]] of a thought, just as a Go position is defined by both placed and empty intersections.

## The Core Analogy

Consciousness is not only what is excited in the brain but equally what is inhibited. The identity of a mind — what it is attending to — emerges from the *inverse*: the inhibited surround that frames the excited figure. This maps directly to [[napot]] (Neuroacoustic Phase Offset Theory), which grounds perception in wave interference patterns where cancellation is as meaningful as amplitude.

## Multiple Concurrent Minds

A provocative hypothesis follows: the brain's multilayered systems (neocortical columns, glial networks, mitochondrial signaling, EM fields) may host several coexistent consciousnesses operating at different frequency bands — delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma. These "alternate beings" could:

- Operate at different timescales within the same skull
- Communicate across individuals below conscious awareness
- Emerge only under specific conditions (altered states, stress, disease)

This extends [[self-aware-networks]] theory: a [[gh-a0206z|self]]-aware network]] need not be singular. Multiple phase-offset patterns could coexist in the same substrate.

## AlphaGo and the Threshold

AlphaGo's defeat of human Go champions is read as evidence that artificial systems can discover spatiotemporal pattern spaces humans cannot navigate consciously — a threshold moment for [[brain-computer-interface]] and eventually [[neural-lace-ar-vr-no-glasses]] verification of these hypotheses.
