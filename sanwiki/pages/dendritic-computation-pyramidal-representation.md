# Dendritic Computation and Pyramidal-Cell Representation

**Dendritic computation and pyramidal-cell representation** asks how the output of one
[[pyramidal-cell]] influences a larger circuit and how a downstream population could infer which
distributed input pattern changed. The cellular sequence and the SAN interpretation must be kept
separate.

> **Boundary note (2026-07-18):** This page is already medically bounded; keep it as the model for separating established dendritic integration, AIS spike initiation, axonal transmission, synaptic effect, and structural plasticity from SAN network-readout hypotheses. Do not turn population phase readout into a universal neuron-identity code or literal tomography by one pyramidal cell. See [[receptive-inceptive-dendritic-projection]], [[san-scale-ladder-and-claim-boundaries]], and [[phase-language-firewall]].

## Established cellular and circuit sequence

1. **Distributed synaptic input** reaches spines, dendritic shafts, the soma, and other compartments.
   Branch location, input timing, excitation, inhibition, and membrane conductances shape the
   resulting dendritic and somatic voltage.
2. **Action-potential output** is normally initiated in the axon initial segment of a cortical
   pyramidal neuron and propagates along the axon. The exact morphology and channel distribution
   vary by pyramidal-cell subtype and preparation.
3. **Axonal synaptic transmission** affects selected postsynaptic targets. Pyramidal-cell boutons
   are typically glutamatergic; local inhibitory interneurons provide GABAergic inhibition to
   compartment- and cell-type-specific targets.
4. **Population recruitment** depends on the circuit. Excitation and inhibition can alter spike
   timing, gain, coincidence windows, and which neurons are recruited. These effects do not by
   themselves encode a unique identity for the neuron that fired.

This sequence is consistent with branch-dependent integration in cortical pyramidal neurons,
axon-initial-segment spike generation, and cell-type-specific inhibitory microcircuits. See
[Branco and Hausser (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.02.006),
[Katz et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29991598/), and
[Obermayer et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30291244/).

## Structural plasticity is a separate timescale

Activity-dependent plasticity can change synaptic efficacy and dendritic-spine structure. A
stimulated spine can enlarge selectively and recruit additional AMPA-receptor-mediated current,
while nearby unstimulated spines change less. This is **not** an immediate process in which a
dendrite grows toward whichever neuron just fired. Structural remodeling changes the conditions
for future responses and must not be drawn as one step in the millisecond firing sequence. See
[Matsuzaki et al. (2004)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15190253/).

## SAN network-readout hypothesis

SAN proposes that downstream populations may use receiver-relative phase relationships, together
with spike timing, rate, power, and connectivity, to infer which distributed input pattern changed.
This is the proposed connection to [[napot-theory|NAPOT]] and [[oscillatory-phase-coding]]. A valid test must ask
whether phase features improve held-out prediction after matched rate, power, connectivity, common
input, and flexible-history controls.

The proposal does **not** imply that one phase value identifies one neuron, that every neuron shares
one clock, or that a single pyramidal cell performs network-level tomography. The image's electrode
traces are explanatory schematics, not recorded results.

## Biological analogy: thymic self-tolerance

The earlier source compares neural discrimination with T-cell training. This can remain only as a
bounded analogy. In the thymus, cortical and medullary thymic epithelial cells and antigen-presenting
cells participate in positive and negative selection. Medullary thymic epithelial cells express
diverse tissue-restricted self antigens through AIRE-dependent and AIRE-independent programs; this
is not simultaneous expression of an entire gene repertoire by a single generic "teacher cell."
See [Anderson et al. (2002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12376594/) and
[Derbinski et al. (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15983066/).

The immune analogy does not provide evidence for the proposed neural phase-readout mechanism.

## Medical accuracy boundary

The plate shows an example mammalian cortical microcircuit, not a universal cortical-column wiring
diagram. Pyramidal-cell morphology, myelination, receptor expression, interneuron class, synaptic
placement, and response timing vary by region, layer, species, age, and preparation. Established
cell biology ends at dendritic integration, axonal spike propagation, synaptic transmission, and
measurable circuit effects. Network-level phase tomography remains the SAN hypothesis.
