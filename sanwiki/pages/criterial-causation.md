# Criterial Causation: Future Response Criteria and the SAN Extension

> **Scientific boundary.** Peter Tse's criterial-causation model asks how physically realized neural processing can change the conditions governing later neural transitions. It does not show that a neuron deliberates, that one mechanism explains organism-level choice, or that information acts as an additional nonphysical force. SAN adopts the future-response idea and connects it to coincidence-sensitive, recurrent, embodied selection as an explicit extension.

## The central distinction

Neural responses depend on current physical state. That state can also change, making the same later input more or less effective. Criterial causation gives a higher-level name to this future-directed relationship:

**current physically realized processing -> changed response conditions -> different eligibility for a later transition**

Five levels must not be collapsed:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Present response condition | Membrane potential, conductances, synaptic input, dendritic state, inhibition, and recent history determine a current response. |
| Plastic change | Synaptic efficacy, receptor state, intrinsic excitability, inhibition, and other cellular or circuit variables can change. |
| Future criterion | A model-level description of the conditions under which a later input will produce a declared transition. |
| Circuit selection | Distributed excitation, inhibition, recurrence, and neuromodulation make some routes or assemblies more likely than others. |
| Organism choice | An embodied system evaluates context, commits motor output, receives consequences, and updates future behavior. |

Calling a neuron's current transfer conditions "criteria" can be useful, but the word must not turn a cell into a tiny person. A cell undergoes state-dependent physical transitions. Deliberation, responsibility, and organism-level choice require distributed neural and bodily operations across longer timescales.

## What Tse contributed

In *The Neural Basis of Free Will: Criterial Causation* (2013), Peter Ulric Tse proposed that presently realized information can alter the physical criteria governing what later neural information will be realized. The official book description emphasizes NMDA receptors, synapses, dendrites, neurons, circuits, and rapid synaptic plasticity.

That proposal is stronger than the familiar observation that thresholds and synapses vary. It is a theory of mental causation: processing at one time changes the physical basis of possible processing at a later time. Tse argues that this future-directed organization helps explain how informational and mental descriptions can be causally relevant without escaping physical implementation.

This is a donor theory, not an established medical fact. Established plasticity mechanisms support the possibility of changing future responses; they do not by themselves prove Tse's account of strong free will or downward mental causation.

## What established neuroscience supports

| Mechanism | Supported statement | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Synaptic plasticity | Activity, neuromodulation, eligibility traces, and instructive signals can alter connection strength. | No single plasticity rule governs every synapse, cell type, task, or timescale. |
| Metaplasticity and homeostasis | Prior activity can change the threshold, direction, or magnitude of later plasticity while stabilizing network function. | A sliding plasticity threshold is not identical to a neuron's moment-to-moment firing criterion. |
| Intrinsic excitability | Learning and recent history can alter a neuron's propensity to generate repeated spikes. | Changes are preparation-, cell-type-, and timescale-dependent. |
| Inhibitory plasticity | Excitatory inputs to interneurons and inhibitory synapses can change, shaping timing, gain, selectivity, and stability. | Inhibition is diverse; it is not one nested gate architecture. |
| Dendritic integration and receptor state | Location, timing, conductances, and receptor configuration affect how inputs influence somatic and local dendritic events. | A dendritic branch does not by itself select an organismal action. |

The medically responsible claim is therefore that neural systems possess multiple mechanisms for changing future response conditions. Whether those changes realize Tse's philosophical account or SAN's larger observer-action account remains an experimental question.

## The SAN extension

SAN adds a joined operator:

**current embodied context and prior learning -> distributed sensory, interoceptive, and recalled signals -> coincidence- and state-sensitive neural transitions -> recurrent excitation, inhibition, and route selection -> changed future response conditions -> motor or physiological commitment -> sensory and proprioceptive feedback**

Within this proposal:

- **The tonic state** is the receiving population's ongoing context, not one fixed frequency band.
- **A phasic event** is a consequential departure from that receiver-relative state, not a universal upper-gamma burst.
- **Criteria-setting** includes multiple physical changes, not just a scalar threshold.
- **Selection** can involve recruitment, suppression, delay, redirection, or changed gain.
- **Choice** belongs to the embodied distributed system, even though cellular and circuit events are necessary parts of it.
- **The observer** is the recurrent network operation that receives, changes, redistributes, acts, and is changed by the consequences. It is not a hidden viewer or one choosing neuron.

This interpretation preserves Micah's early claim that thought is active within feedback and that knowledge can matter to later action. It also corrects the historical phrase "information is non-physical." An abstract pattern is distinguishable from any one material instance and does not add a separate mass or energy substance. In a brain, however, every causal use of that pattern requires a physical realization and an energy-dependent transition.

## Choice is not randomness

The recovered SAN record explicitly resists the claim that random neural or quantum fluctuations alone produce meaningful choice. Noise may affect a trajectory, but unconstrained randomness does not supply reasons, goals, memory, evaluation, or responsibility.

The stronger question is whether prior processing changes future neural eligibility in a way that is:

- sensitive to reasons and context;
- implemented by identifiable physical mechanisms;
- distributed across the relevant neural and bodily system;
- able to alter action under controlled conditions; and
- updated by the consequences of action.

That is a testable architecture question even when the larger metaphysical debate over free will remains unresolved.

## Scale ladder

- **Molecular and synaptic:** receptor state, transmitter release, synaptic efficacy, and biochemical history alter local response.
- **Cellular:** dendritic integration, membrane state, intrinsic excitability, and inhibition alter the probability and form of output.
- **Local circuit:** recurrent excitation, inhibition, neuromodulation, and plasticity alter which population trajectory is expressed.
- **Distributed network:** association, memory, sensory, subcortical, and motor systems constrain one another over time.
- **Organism:** bodily state, environmental context, action, and feedback make the selection meaningful and consequential.

A valid explanation must state which transition is being selected at which scale. It cannot simply repeat "criteria" from receptor to person as though the same mechanism passed unchanged through every level.

## How to test the added SAN claim

A bounded experiment should identify a specific future response and compare nested models:

1. current input, membrane state, firing rate, and known anatomy;
2. those variables plus synaptic, receptor, intrinsic-excitability, inhibitory, and neuromodulatory state;
3. those variables plus distributed timing and receiver-relative tonic/phasic or typed PWD features;
4. those variables plus recurrent routing, motor commitment, and feedback.

The intervention should change one proposed criteria-setting mechanism after an initial event and test the response to a matched later input. Circuit tests should perturb the predicted recurrent or inhibitory route. Organism tests should measure the action and its sensory or proprioceptive consequence rather than treating a cellular spike as the choice.

The SAN extension is weakened if future responses are fully explained by conventional state and plasticity variables, if PWD or oscillatory features add no held-out prediction or causal leverage, or if the proposed circuit update fails to alter the declared embodied action under matched conditions.
