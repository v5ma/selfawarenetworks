The hypothesis that "awareness" is not a fundamental feature of mind but a prediction — a story the brain generates — rather than a transparent window on reality or an ultimate ground of being.

## Core position

[[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]] posed this as a challenge to [[Advaita Vedanta]], which treats pure awareness (Brahman/consciousness) as the one undivided reality. The SAN counter-position: what we call awareness is itself a construct, another layer of the mind's predictive machinery — a high-level representation that the neural system generates about its own processes. The word "awareness" labels a model, not a ground.

This aligns with [[predictive-coding]] accounts of perception and [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modeling: the brain continuously generates predictions; "awareness" is the label we attach to the current winning prediction about internal state.

## Distinction from eastern philosophy

[[Advaita Vedanta]] locates ultimate reality in pure, undivided awareness prior to all mental content. The SAN view inverts this: even the sense of awareness is produced by neural computation. There is no "awareness" standing behind the computation — the computation is all there is. Awareness is a representation, not a ground.

## Neurological roots

The position draws on research into [[brain-plasticity]] and cognitive neuroscience. If the brain can rewire its own representations (plasticity), then the sense of "being aware" is no less plastic — it too is a learned, malleable pattern, not an immutable substrate.
