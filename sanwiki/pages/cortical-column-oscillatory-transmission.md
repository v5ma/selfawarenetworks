This SAN plate shows cortical columns as active oscillatory transmitters: lower-layer sensory input is relayed through L4, layers 5-6 broadcast and coordinate long-range column activity, apical modulation gates the coupling, and many column measurements converge into one NAPOT-style coherent model.

A foundational observation in SAN theory: cortical columns in layers 5-6 do not merely
synchronize with each other - they actively transmit **sensory and motor information** across
regions via long-range apical and perimeter cell connections.

The thalamocortical bridge pathways are majority-modulatory: most traffic between cortical areas
modulates sensorimotor activity rather than routing raw input. This is distinct from the narrow
columnar traffic rising from the senses through lower layers.

## Core claim

Each neuron functions as a **pattern recognition and pattern creation node**. Working collectively,
neurons form patterns that vary temporally, spatially, and in firing rate. The brain encodes a
sensory snapshot - "bottles" it - and then transmits it across regions like a projector signal
over fiber optics. Different cortical areas receive and coordinate responses to this
internally-broadcast representation.

This mechanism is an early-form statement of [Neural Array Projection](neural-array-projection.md)
Oscillation Tomography (NAPOT): the brain performs a standing wave / oscillatory tomographic reconstruction of
reality, not a passive relay of raw sense data.

## Cross-scale imaging analogy

The speaker draws a parallel to tomographic imaging (electrical impedance tomography, Fourier
slice projection theorem, LiDAR + RGB fusion, deep-learning 3D reconstruction). The unifying
idea: **many measurements from many angles, combined into one coherent model**. The brain does
the same thing - many columns, many firing patterns, one unified percept. This cross-scale analogy
is a recurring [fractal pattern](fractal-pattern.md) in SAN theory.
