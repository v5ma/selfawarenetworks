# Penrose and Hameroff

**Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff** developed the theory now called [[orch-or|Orchestrated Objective Reduction]]. Penrose contributed an objective-reduction proposal tied to space-time geometry; Hameroff connected that proposal to organized processes in neuronal microtubules. Their collaboration should be represented as a specific theory, not as a synonym for every quantum-biological effect in neurons.

## The theory in bounded form

The mature account proposes that orchestrated quantum states in microtubules undergo objective reduction and that the resulting events participate in conscious moments ([Hameroff and Penrose, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2013.08.002)). This is stronger than any one of the following claims:

- microtubules affect neuronal physiology;
- anesthetics interact with microtubules;
- tubulin assemblies exhibit quantum-optical behavior;
- quantum mechanics is relevant to biological matter; or
- consciousness depends on intact cellular organization.

The complete Orch OR claim requires those physical events to occur in the relevant living neural context, be orchestrated and causally available to the nervous system, satisfy the proposed reduction dynamics, and explain a declared consciousness target.

## Relation to SAN

SAN is not a reformulation of Orch OR. SAN locates observer-action in the distributed operation of receivers, cells, recurrent circuits, body feedback, memory, and action. It can include ordinary microtubule biology as one contributor to cellular readiness without assigning conscious observation to a tubulin lattice or collapse event.

This difference should remain experimentally legible. A SAN experiment asks how a measured difference is received, transformed, redistributed, and used across micro, meso, and macro scales. An Orch OR experiment must additionally isolate the proposed quantum state and objective-reduction mechanism. Evidence for shared concern with physical consciousness is not evidence that the mechanisms are identical.
