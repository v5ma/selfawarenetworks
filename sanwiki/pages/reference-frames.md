# Reference Frames in an Embodied Nervous System

> **Scientific boundary:** A reference frame specifies what a measured or represented variable is relative to. Neural systems use eye-, head-, body-, limb-, object-, landmark-, and environment-relative relations. Established evidence does not show that every signal is converted into one master map, that every cortical column implements one grid algorithm, or that one oscillatory band is the brain's universal coordinate frame.

## Reference frames are task-relative

Retinal location changes when the eye moves. A sound initially depends on its relation to the head. Touch occurs on a moving body surface. Proprioceptive and vestibular measurements constrain limb and head state. A reach may therefore require selected transformations without requiring every population to contain a completed world coordinate.

Recordings in macaque intraparietal cortex found eye-centered, head-centered, and intermediate or complex visual and auditory codes ([Mullette-Gillman, Cohen, and Groh, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00021.2005)). Mixed codes are not failed representations. Across a population they can preserve information another population uses for a bounded judgment or action.

## Several spatial variables coexist

Place, grid, head-direction, boundary, and object-related responses describe different measured relations. Place-related firing does not contain a miniature room, and grid-cell firing does not prove graph paper or one complete path-integration algorithm.

Movement also changes the coordinates of sensory input. Gaze-dependent gain fields can make eye position available to later populations ([Andersen and Mountcastle, 1983](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.03-03-00532.1983)), while predictive remapping updates selected visual relations before a saccade finishes ([Duhamel, Colby, and Goldberg, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1553535)). Neither result identifies a central scene map.

## Common coordinates are not enough

Two cues can occupy nearby coordinates without sharing a cause. Human audiovisual localization behavior supports models that jointly estimate location and whether cues arise from one event ([Kording et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000943)). Reliability can then change each cue's influence without erasing its sensory identity.

Body coordinates also are not synonyms for body ownership, self-location, agency, or interoception. Those variables can covary or dissociate and must be measured separately.

## SAN interpretation

SAN generalizes the reference-frame problem into a recurrent observer-action problem:

1. cellular and local populations receive partial measurements;
2. each receiving population interprets an update relative to its current state and available pose, task, and landmark information;
3. recurrent routes maintain and transform only the relations needed by downstream receivers;
4. resulting motor, autonomic, attentional, and sampling changes alter the next measurements.

The ongoing tonic action field is proposed as part of the receiver context or **canvas**, while phasic departures are proposed as differentiated **ink**. This is an operational hypothesis, not a claim that theta, alpha, or another named band is always the coordinate system. A SAN experiment must name the two compared signals or states, receiver population, anatomical route, time window, represented variable, and behavioral consequence.

No final coordinate reader or inner viewer is required. The distributed network's changing relations and actions are the observer operation.
