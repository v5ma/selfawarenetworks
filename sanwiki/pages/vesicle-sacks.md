# Vesicle Sacks

This SAN plate maps vesicle sacks as a calcium-gated quantization device: action-potential phase controls calcium-channel open time, which sets the 0/1/2/3 vesicle-release count written into the tonic-phasic canvas for dendritic pattern detection and cortical rendering.

**Vesicle Sacks** are, in [[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]]'s [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] writings, the small membrane-bound containers full of [[neurotransmitters]] that a neuron releases — zero, one, two, or three at a time — across each [[synapse]] during each cycle of its [[tonic-phasic-neural-oscillation|tonic-phasic oscillation]]. The count of sacks released per interval is the unit of "paint" in the SAN canvas-and-paint metaphor for [[consciousness]]: changes to a [[tonic-phasic-canvas-of-consciousness|tonic-phasic canvas]] are written in vesicle-sack release counts, and those counts carry the bit-rate that supports [[brain-as-rendering-engine|cortical rendering]] and [[dendritic-pattern-detection|dendritic pattern detection]] across [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography|neural array projection tomography]].

## Position

Within [[gh-a0206z|Self]]-Aware Networks the vesicle sack is not anatomy for its own sake; it is a *quantization device* that turns a graded action-potential phase into a small integer release count, and that integer is treated as the elementary symbol of cortical computation. The position is summarized in the canvas-and-paint passage that recurs across the manuscript: a "group of cells is the canvas of [[consciousness]], and the changes to it, via the release of 0 1 2 or 3 vesicle sacks at each interval relative to the group oscillation is the paint of the [[mind|mind's]] internal representations" — a framing repeated in the longer passage `a0288zslow--md`, line 192, [[gh-a0288zslow|on github]], which calls "0, 1, 2, or 3 Vesicle sacks" "the essence of computational rendering & pattern detection between arrays" of cortical units. The micro-anatomy is given in `a0262z--md`, line 8, [[gh-a0262z|on github]]: "connections between dendrites and the synapses of other cells, [[inhibitory-interneuron-system|interneuron]] gates and synapses for micro vesicle sacks and [[neurotransmitters]] to fly through" — placing the sack inside the [[chemical-synapse|chemical synapse]] proper and tying it to [[inhibitory-interneurons-plasticity-gating|interneuron gating]]. The result is a tight coupling: [[multi-vesicle-release|multi-vesicle release]] is *how* the [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|dominant phase-wave differential]] is written into a target [[neural-array-projection|neural array projection]], and the integer sack count is the unit in which [[multivesicular-release-spatial-information|spatial information is encoded]] across [[cortical-column|cortical columns]].

## Post-SAN

Mainstream synaptic-physiology literature has long acknowledged that some synapses release more than one vesicle per spike, especially under high-frequency input, and treats the variance in release count as part of [[multivesicular-release-synaptic-unreliability|"synaptic unreliability"]]. The SAN framing differs in two testable directions. First, it predicts that the integer release count (0, 1, 2, or 3) carries *more* information than the spike timing alone — a claim adjacent to but stronger than published "multivesicular release" papers, and one that should be testable with paired-recording techniques that can resolve quantal events at single synapses. Second, it predicts a phase-locked relationship between sack count and the local [[dominant-phase-wave-differential-rhythm|phase-wave differential]] of the postsynaptic [[neural-array|array]] — a relationship that would not be expected under a pure noise model and that aligns the sack count with [[neural-oscillation-rendering|neural-oscillation rendering]] more generally. Where the published literature treats vesicle release as a stochastic transmission mechanism, SAN treats it as the inscription tool by which a [[brain-as-rendering-engine|rendering engine]] writes [[mental-pixels|mental pixels]] onto a tonic-phasic substrate.
