This SAN plate maps distributed memory as reconstruction from many neural-array microperspectives: phasic signals illuminate perspective-shifted fragments through a tonic oscillation substrate, and the combined interference pattern yields a reconstructed memory whose fidelity degrades with partial tissue loss.

Memories are not stored at a single locus (an [[engram]]) but are distributed holographically across neural arrays — each neuron stores a slightly different perspective or variation of the pattern, and reconstruction requires combining many such microperspectives.

## From engrams to distributed representation

Early neuroscience (Penfield's temporal-lobe stimulation studies) suggested memories had fixed loci. Lashley's ablation experiments overturned this: removing partial cortex degraded memory *fidelity* rather than erasing specific memories, implying the record was spread across tissue. This led to [[sparse-distributed-memory]] and, later, [[holonomic-brain-theory]].

Jeff Hawkins ([[thousand-brains-theory]]) extends the idea: different cortical regions store *variations* of the same pattern, altered by their intersections with other signals — not identical copies but perspective-shifted versions.

## Holographic analogy

A hologram is produced by splitting a laser beam, reflecting one beam off the subject, then recording the interference pattern of the two beams on film. Every region of the film contains the whole scene at reduced resolution — no single patch "owns" any feature. The brain's distributed encoding mirrors this: every neuron stores a "viewpoint reduction in 4 dimensions," a microperspective. Neurons at larger scales (cortical columns, whole brain) combine microperspectives into meso- and macroscale views.

## Phasic signals as the illuminating beam

In [[napot]] terms, [[phasic-signals]] play the role of the laser beam passing through holographic film: routing through [[tonic-oscillation]] arrays, they activate rendered microperspectives. These perspectives are amplified and propagated across oscillating arrays in temporal-sequence patterns. The result is [[holographic-attention-schema-theory]] — [[attention]] selects and magnifies certain interference patterns.
