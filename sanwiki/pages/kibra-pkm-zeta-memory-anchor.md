# KIBRA–PKMζ and Memory Persistence

KIBRA and PKMζ form a postsynaptic molecular interaction implicated in maintaining some forms of long-term potentiation and long-term memory. This is important evidence about molecular persistence, but it is not a complete or universal explanation of memory.

## Experimental finding

PKMζ is an autonomously active atypical protein kinase C isoform. KIBRA is a postsynaptic scaffolding protein that can bind and localize PKMζ. In mice, disrupting the KIBRA–PKMζ interaction after learning impaired maintenance of late LTP and established long-term memories in the studied paradigms. The 2024 work included molecular interaction assays, hippocampal physiology, and behavioral interventions.

The result supports a mechanism by which a potentiated synaptic state can be actively maintained despite turnover of individual molecules. It does not show that:

- KIBRA–PKMζ is required for every memory, brain region, species, or timescale;
- one molecular complex contains remembered meaning;
- synaptic potentiation alone is sufficient for retrieval or conscious recollection;
- CaMKII and KIBRA–PKMζ form a proven universal redundant memory system;
- enhancing the interaction is currently a validated treatment for memory disorders.

Earlier knockout and inhibitor findings made PKMζ's role controversial and revealed compensatory mechanisms. The newer KIBRA-interaction evidence narrows and strengthens the maintenance claim in particular experimental settings; it does not erase that history.

## SAN interpretation

For SAN, KIBRA–PKMζ is a candidate mechanism of **changed synaptic readiness**. It may help preserve how a synapse responds when a later network state re-engages it. The molecular interaction does not itself specify the represented object, episode, or meaning. Those depend on the synapse's place in a distributed, embodied, recurrent system.

SAN further proposes that retrieval includes receiver-relative population dynamics, including tonic context and phasic updates. That systems-level interpretation is not established by the molecular experiments. It becomes testable by asking whether selective disruption of KIBRA–PKMζ changes later pathway-specific transmission, population phase relationships, behavioral recall, and rescue together.
