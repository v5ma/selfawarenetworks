A neuron has been compared across scientific literature to a **telegraph** (discrete signal transmission), a **clock** (rhythmic timing), and a **Geiger counter** (event detection / threshold firing). Sources include Stephen Strogatz's *Sync* and Jon Lieff's *The Secret Language of Cells*.

These analogies map onto SAN theory's account of the neuron as a threshold-firing oscillator that both detects coincident signals and transmits phase-encoded information to downstream arrays.

- The **telegraph** analogy captures the all-or-nothing [[action-potential|action potential]] — digital-like pulse transmission.
- The **clock** analogy captures the neuron's intrinsic rhythmicity and contribution to population oscillations (see [[neural-oscillatory-tomography]]).
- The **Geiger counter** analogy captures stochastic threshold detection — a neuron fires when accumulated input crosses a detection boundary, analogous to radiation particle counting.

In SAN theory, none of these analogies is individually sufficient. The neuron functions as all three simultaneously: it is an oscillating clock that detects coincidences like a Geiger counter and encodes the result as a telegraph signal to neighboring arrays. This tri-function is the basis for [[napot]] (Neural Array-Projection Oscillatory Tomography).
