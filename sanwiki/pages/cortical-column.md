# Cortical Column: A Mesoscopic Study Unit in SAN

A **cortical column** is a useful mesoscopic study unit when a particular cortical area has a
demonstrable vertical anatomical or functional organization.

It is not one universally bounded organ, one wiring diagram repeated unchanged everywhere, or a
proven elementary unit of consciousness. SAN uses the column more specifically as a candidate
local population in which ongoing activity supplies a receiver context, differentiated cellular
events alter that context, and recurrent excitation and inhibition shape what the population
transmits next.

## What established cortical anatomy permits

[Mountcastle's 1957 recordings](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1957.20.4.408) showed that
neurons encountered along near-vertical penetrations in cat somatosensory cortex could share
modality and receptive-field properties. This established an important form of columnar
organization. It did not establish one sharply bounded module, one computation, or one circuit
diagram for every cortical area and species.

Primary studies show both reusable motifs and consequential variation:

- In mouse barrel cortex, layer 4 barrels provide an anatomically identifiable column-related
  organization, but even this preparation contains parallel and pathway-specific thalamic routes.
- [Petreanu et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07709) mapped distinct local and
  long-range inputs to separate dendritic domains of layer 3 and layer 5 pyramidal neurons.
- [Constantinople and Bruno (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1236425) found that
  thalamus directly activated deep neurons in rat barrel cortex, so the familiar
  `thalamus -> L4 -> L2/3 -> L5/6` sequence was not obligatory.
- [Shai et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004090) found basal-apical
  coincidence-sensitive bursting in layer 5 pyramidal neurons from mouse V1. This supports
  compartment-sensitive cellular integration; it does not turn one pyramidal cell into a complete
  column readout.
- [Kondo et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13210) found mixed functional
  microarchitectures in mouse V1: some local groups showed weak vertical clustering and others
  contained diverse orientation preferences.

The medically accurate conclusion is therefore **motif without universality**. Layer 4 is often a
major thalamorecipient layer in primary sensory cortex; superficial and deep pyramidal neurons have
important cortical and subcortical projections; apical and basal dendritic domains can receive
different input streams; and inhibitory circuits shape local timing. The exact cell classes,
laminar routes, dendritic targets, projections, and functional grouping must be identified for the
area, species, developmental state, and task being discussed.

## Source recovery: the SAN column proposition

The current SAN proposition developed through several distinguishable stages:

1. **June 8, 2022:** [[gh-a0050z|a0050z]] proposed that an ongoing tonic population state supplies
   a background against which phasic departures become differentiated. It then asked whether an
   oscillating local assembly could learn and re-express a pattern even when the participating
   neurons changed.
2. **June 8, 2022:** the early portion of `a0056z` proposed alternating receiving and firing stages
   among cortical populations and distributed storage of different low-level contributions to one
   higher-level pattern.
3. **August-September 2022:** [[gh-a0007z|a0007z]] joined layer 5 basal-apical coincidence,
   dendritic electrogenesis, inhibition, burst output, and corticothalamic recurrence to the
   column-scale question. The quoted claim that layer 5 pyramidal dendrites can span cortical layers
   came from Shai et al.; Micah's addition was the learned receive-integrate-return interpretation.
4. **August 2022:** [[gh-a0018z|a0018z]] and [[gh-a0001z|a0001z]] used "cortical column #1" for a
   functional hippocampal analogy. That phrase joined two external donors discussed below; it was
   not a claim that hippocampus has ordinary six-layer neocortical anatomy.
5. **June 18, 2023:** a later addition to `a0056z` described the local assembly as "reading" streams
   of wavelets or [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differentials]]. This later wording should
   not be backdated into the June 2022 source.

The recoverable operation is:

`ongoing local population context -> input-specific cellular and dendritic events -> recurrent
excitation, inhibition, and state change -> learned ensemble response or re-expression -> output
through anatomically available projections`.

This is more precise than saying that a column is an "oscillating sack" that literally sees a
brainwave. It also preserves more of SAN than replacing the source with a generic description of
cortical layers.

## What "the column reads" means

SAN's visual language can be translated into measurable biological referents:

| Historical expression | Source-faithful biological interpretation |
|---|---|
| "Oscillating sack" | A declared local population with time-varying membrane, synaptic, spiking, inhibitory, and extracellular-field states. It need not share one frequency or phase. |
| "Reads wavelets" | Incoming synaptic and field-associated events change the states and outputs of actual cellular receivers. No separate viewer reads a waveform. |
| "Tonic canvas" | The ongoing distribution of receiver and population states against which a later event is a difference. It is not necessarily one sinusoid or one EEG band. |
| "Phasic ink" | A transient, receiver-relative departure measured in declared cellular or population variables. |
| "Reproduces an invariant" | A learned ensemble relation can recur or be partially completed across different trials and overlapping cell memberships. |
| "Column dipole" | Population transmembrane currents produce spatial current-source and current-sink patterns whose measured field depends on geometry, synchrony, conductivity, distance, and recording method. |
| "Cortical column #1" | A functional analogy among hippocampal recurrence, hierarchy, and neocortical population processing, not anatomical identity. |

[Carrillo-Reid et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf7560) showed that selected
neurons in awake mouse visual cortex could be optogenetically imprinted into recurring ensembles
and later recalled under the study conditions. [Carrillo-Reid et al.
(2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.045) further demonstrated behaviorally relevant
recall of identified cortical ensembles. These experiments support bounded ensemble recurrence and
pattern completion. They do not show that a complete percept is stored by every cell, that the
ensemble is a cortical column, or that all columns implement one invariant algorithm.

## The field and dipole correction

The 2022 source phrase "electrical diffusion of potential differences from action potentials"
compressed several scales. Action potentials, dendritic spikes, synaptic currents, active membrane
currents, extracellular potentials, local field potentials, ECoG, EEG, and MEG are related but are
not interchangeable signals.

At the cellular and local-circuit scales, transmembrane currents create extracellular current
sources and sinks. Their spatial summation depends strongly on neuronal morphology, alignment,
input location, timing, tissue conductivity, and cancellation. At larger scales, volume conduction
and the inverse problem further constrain what can be inferred. In rat barrel cortex,
[Riera et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00098.2011) found that neocortical EEG
generators were not adequately represented as one simple mesoscopic dipole.

The source-faithful SAN hypothesis is not that action potentials diffuse horizontally as a picture.
It is that the evolving current-source and current-sink geometry of a local population may be one
measurable component of its ongoing context and differentiated response. That proposal requires
simultaneous intracellular or laminar measurements and explicit field modeling; an EEG or LFP
trace alone cannot identify the complete column state.

## The hippocampus analogy and its donors

The phrase "cortical column #1" has a recoverable two-source ancestry:

- [Hawkins and Blakeslee's *On Intelligence*](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780805074567/onintelligence/)
  was first published in 2004; Micah recalls the 2005 edition as his source. It treated the cortical
  column as a candidate basic computational unit and placed the hippocampus at the top of the
  cortical hierarchy.
- [Buzsaki's *Rhythms of the Brain* (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195301069.001.0001)
  described the hippocampus as a one-layer cortex and used the functional analogy of one giant
  cortical column while emphasizing its recurrent and inhibitory organization.
- Micah's August 2022 notes joined the hierarchical and oscillatory analogies into "cortical column
  #1" and proposed that hippocampal-entorhinal organization might provide a spatial scaffold whose
  contents are supplied by distributed cortical populations.

Anatomically, hippocampus is allocortex with circuitry and lamination distinct from six-layer
neocortex. Functionally, both hippocampal and neocortical circuits can exhibit recurrence,
inhibition, oscillatory organization, ensemble sequences, completion, and learned state-dependent
output. The scientifically useful question is not whether the structures are literally identical.
It is which input-transform-output operations are shared, which are different, and whether SAN's
common description predicts data better than region-specific models.

## Micro, meso, and macro receivers

- **Micro:** identified synapses, dendritic branches, somata, axons, and terminals transform
  receiver-relative inputs into cellular state and output changes.
- **Meso:** a declared layer, barrel, column-related population, or other local circuit expresses
  recurrent, inhibitory, oscillatory, and ensemble dynamics.
- **Macro:** thalamic, hippocampal, cortical, subcortical, bodily, and motor systems exchange
  population updates through actual anatomical pathways.

The same abstract receive-transform-project relation may recur across these scales, but the
receiver, signal, reference state, and measurement must be redefined at every scale. A column does
not contain a localized observer. In SAN, local receivers, recurrent interactions, distributed
pathways, bodily feedback, and motor consequences collectively constitute the
[[distributed-observer-vs-consciousness-module|observer-action system]].

## SAN residual and discriminating tests

The distinctive SAN residual is a mesoscopic composition:

`tonic population context + typed phasic departures + learned recurrent transformation +
receiver-specific projection = one local contribution to distributed rendering and action`.

This remains a hypothesis. A strong test should:

1. declare one species, cortical area, behavioral task, column or alternative local boundary, cell
   classes, and anatomical projections;
2. measure layer- and cell-specific synaptic input, dendritic state, spiking, inhibition, local
   field/current-source geometry, and downstream population response;
3. compare rate, count, timing, phase, power, latent-state, recurrent-ensemble, and SAN typed-PWD
   models on held-out trials;
4. test whether a tonic reference plus typed departures predicts later ensemble re-expression and
   downstream recruitment beyond conventional state and connectivity variables;
5. perturb the proposed cellular, recurrent, inhibitory, and projection stages separately and
   require stage-specific loss, mediation, and mechanism-matched rescue; and
6. test the hippocampal analogy as an operation-level comparison, not by forcing both structures
   into one anatomical template.

Failure of column boundaries to improve prediction over unconstrained local populations, failure
of receiver-relative phase variables to add value, or failure of targeted perturbation to alter the
proposed re-expression route would weaken the stronger SAN column account. Conversely, finding a
shared operator does not establish that every cortical area uses it, that it produces a complete
percept, or that oscillatory coordination alone is consciousness.
