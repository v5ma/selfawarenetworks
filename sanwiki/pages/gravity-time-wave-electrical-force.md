A SAN theoretical claim ([[gh-a0113z|a0113z]]) that gravity is not an independent fundamental force
but a **time wave propagating within the electromagnetic force**, with mass standing as
its inverse in a unified field description.

## Core Claim

The standard view separates gravity and electromagnetism as distinct forces. In this
SAN [[gh-formulation|formulation]], they are not separate: gravity is a time-dimensional oscillation mode
**embedded in the electrical force**. Where the electrical force acts across space, its
time-wave component — gravity — acts across the temporal dimension. Mass is the local
condensation (high temporal density) and gravity is the reciprocal propagation (the
outward time wave) that moves away from that condensation.

The "inverse of mass" language means: wherever mass concentrates time locally (see
[[particle-gravity-time-density]]), gravity is the complementary gradient — time
expanding and propagating outward through the electromagnetic substrate.

## Relationship to QGTCD

[[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation|QGTCD]] provides the scale-resolved version of
this idea: at the quantum scale, time frames dilate near mass, altering particle
trajectories. The [[gh-a0113z|a0113z]] [[gh-formulation|formulation]] adds a carrier: the electrical field is the medium
through which these time-frame gradients propagate. [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] describes the *effect*;
this note identifies the *mechanism's carrier medium*.

## Electromagnetism as Time Carrier

In standard physics, gravitational and electromagnetic waves propagate through spacetime
independently. Here the claim is that the temporal component of the electromagnetic wave
**is** gravity — the same oscillation seen in two projections. This would make
gravitational redshift ([[gh-a0070z|a0070z]]) a special case: as electromagnetic radiation climbs out
of a gravitational well, it is literally climbing against the time wave embedded in the
field it propagates through.

## Connection to Oscillator Framework

[[oscillating-spacetime-theory]] holds that gravity arises from synchronized oscillators
bending the surrounding field. The [[gh-a0113z|a0113z]] time-wave view is compatible: the
synchronization produces a shared oscillatory phase in the electrical medium, and that
phase gradient is what registers as a gravitational time wave for neighboring matter.
