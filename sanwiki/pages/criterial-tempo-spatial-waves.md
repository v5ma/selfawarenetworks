A **criterial tempo-spatial wave** is an oscillatory pattern in neural activity that satisfies specific selection criteria across both time and space simultaneously. In SAN theory, the [[gh-a0206z|self]] or conscious agent is understood as the *distributed [[gh-b0049y|summary]]* of many such waves propagating through a neural vector.

The phrase "I am the distributed [[gh-b0049y|summary]] of criterial tempo-spatial waves in some vector" frames consciousness not as a fixed location but as an emergent aggregate — an ongoing integration of wave activity that meets [[criteria]] at each spatiotemporal step.

## Key properties

- **Criterial**: waves are not arbitrary; they must satisfy threshold conditions to contribute to the [[gh-b0049y|summary]]. This links to [[criterial causation]] and [[predictive coding]].
- **Tempo-spatial**: the criteria apply jointly in time and space — phase, frequency, and spatial distribution all matter.
- **Distributed [[gh-b0049y|summary]]**: no single locus holds the pattern. Identity arises from cross-regional integration, consistent with [[global workspace theory]] and [[tonic oscillation]].
- **Vector**: the underlying substrate is a high-dimensional state space — a neural or phase-space vector — not a scalar or a single channel.

## Relation to SAN core theory

This [[gh-formulation|formulation]] anticipates [[self-aware networks]] core claims: that [[oscillatory phase patterns]] carry bound information, that [[attention]] selects which patterns rise to the [[gh-b0049y|summary]] level, and that the distributed nature of the [[gh-b0049y|summary]] explains why damage to any single region rarely eliminates consciousness entirely.

Related concepts: [[phase wave differential]], [[non-linear differential continuous field]], [[neural array projection oscillation tomography]].
