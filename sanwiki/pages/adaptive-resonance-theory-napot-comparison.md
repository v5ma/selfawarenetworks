**[[adaptive-resonance-theory|Adaptive Resonance Theory]] (ART)** is a neuroscience/AI framework developed by Steve Grossberg positing that perception arises from interaction between top-down expectation templates and bottom-up sensory signals, governed by a *vigilance parameter* that sets the threshold for category membership. See [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] for the SAN framework being compared here.

## ART Core Mechanism

ART's primary claim: object recognition results from matching a stored "prototype" against incoming sensory data. As long as the mismatch is below the vigilance threshold, the input is classified into the expected category. This solves the **plasticity-stability problem** — new learning does not catastrophically overwrite existing knowledge.

## Overlap with NAPOT

Both ART and [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] share the principle that the brain performs comparisons between internal (top-down) representations and incoming (bottom-up) sensory signals. Key shared intuitions:

- Top-down expectations actively shape perception.
- Bottom-up signals perturb, not simply overwrite, prior state.
- Lateral processing occurs at multiple scales.

## Where NAPOT Diverges

[[napot-overview|NAPOT]] goes beyond ART's computational abstraction by specifying **biological neural mechanisms**:

- The baseline is the **[[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]]** — the brain's pre-existing multi-dimensional brainwave pattern, not an abstract prototype.
- Comparisons occur as [[phase-wave-differentials]] propagating through phase-matched, functionally connected regions (e.g., Theta oscillations in hippocampus).
- [[inceptive-field]] (apical [[dendrite]] / exit terminal) sends rendered output to [[receptive-field]] (basal dendrites / input side) — structurally richer than ART's two-layer comparison.

## Buzsáki Bridge

György Buzsáki's *The Brain from Inside Out* is a near-match to ART at the biological level: the brain maintains pre-existing instinctual rhythms and inherent connectivity biases against which incoming sensory events must interact. Buzsáki frames cognition as incoming stimuli *perturbing* and *shaping* existing dynamics — consistent with ART's vigilance mechanism, but rooted in neural oscillation science rather than ANN design.

## SAN Assessment

ART is described in the source as an exercise in making intuitive psychological observations work in artificial neural networks — plausible as a computational metaphor but not biologically specified. [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] is positioned as the biologically grounded successor, inheriting ART's top-down/bottom-up comparison logic while replacing its abstract vigilance parameter with the concrete machinery of oscillatory phase matching.
