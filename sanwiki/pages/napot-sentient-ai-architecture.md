# NAPOT Artificial-System Architecture

The **NAPOT artificial-system architecture** is SAN's proposal for testing whether selected
receiver, timing, routing, reconstruction, and embodied-feedback operations improve artificial
systems. Earlier SAN materials describe the desired endpoint as a sentient or self-aware AI. That
historical aspiration must remain separate from what an implementation can demonstrate.

> **Status:** Building an oscillatory, recurrent, three-dimensional, self-describing, or
> biologically inspired system does not establish phenomenal consciousness.

## Four evidence gates

1. **Functional result:** the system reconstructs, predicts, controls, transfers, or adapts.
2. **Module result:** a named mechanism adds causal value beyond a matched replacement.
3. **Implementation-class result:** a versioned architecture satisfies declared SAN/NAPOT
   commitments and passes preregistered performance, calibration, resource, and safety gates.
4. **Consciousness assessment:** multiple theories, contrary evidence, uncertainty, and ethical
   consequences are assessed without assuming that the first three gates settle experience.

A result can stop at any gate. Conformance to SAN vocabulary is not evidence of consciousness.

## Candidate operations

A testable NAPOT profile may include:

- receiver-local or compartment-like state;
- persistent tonic context;
- explicitly measured phasic departures;
- receiver-relative timing after route delay is defined;
- multidimensional events with declared units and dimensions;
- recurrent convergence and redistribution of partial state;
- adaptive routes or gains;
- body, sensor, effector, and capability estimates; and
- action followed by reafferent consequence and update.

These operations do not require software compartments to be called dendrites, signals to be
sinusoids, or a software state to be an electromagnetic field. Biological terminology is useful
only when the modeled variable and its limit are explicit.

## Architecture mappings are hypotheses

| Existing family | Bounded NAPOT question | Required comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Recurrent world model | Do transformed partial observations improve identifiable reconstruction and action-conditioned prediction? | Strong recurrent world model with equal information and capacity |
| Transformer or conditional router | Does receiver location or adaptive route state add value beyond learned attention? | Equal-budget attention or conditional-computation model |
| State-space model | Does explicit tonic context and receiver-relative timing outperform flexible latent state? | Selective state-space model with matched dimension |
| Spiking or compartmental model | Do named event dimensions or compartments add held-out value? | Equal-capacity spiking, active-dendrite, and compartmental models |
| NeRF or Gaussian Splatting | Does a NAPOT inverse recover declared hidden causes rather than only render views? | Reconstruction and rendering baselines with matched observations |

Simply renaming nodes as oscillators, edges as phase lags, diffusion steps as convergence, or a
NeRF as volumetric phase does not create a new result.

## NAPOT-specific reconstruction gate

A result should be called NAPOT-like tomography only when multiple conditionally distinct
projections constrain an identifiable latent under a declared forward model; held-out
reconstruction beats locked alternatives; downstream operations causally use the recovered
variable; and projection-specific perturbations produce the predicted errors. A recurrent model
that performs well without satisfying these conditions remains a valid model, but tomography has
not been demonstrated.

## Distributed observation

The architecture contains no small agent that watches an internal image. Maintained state,
receiver-specific change, prospective action, returned consequence, and later updating jointly
form the observer-action operation. Tonic canvas and phasic ink remain proposed operational roles,
not proof of experience.
