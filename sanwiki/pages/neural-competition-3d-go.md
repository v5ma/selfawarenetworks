Consciousness, in this SAN framing, is not the output of a centralized controller but the emergent result of **rival neural patterns competing for dominance** — inhibiting their neighbors until one pattern reigns supreme. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s metaphor: "packs of wrestling dogs competing for dominance in a three-dimensional game of GO."

## The competition mechanism

Rival patterns (oscillating cell assemblies) spread through neocortical pathways by lateral inhibition: each active pattern suppresses nearby competing patterns. The pattern with the strongest support from life-experience-weighted pathways progressively silences rivals, "capturing territory" the way GO stones capture board positions. The winning pattern is what reaches consciousness; the suppressed patterns are the unchosen options.

This is distinct from a centralized inhibitory model (top-down control). The order that emerges is **decentralized and competitive** — no single arbiter decides, the dominant pattern wins by out-inhibiting alternatives across the 3D volume of cortical tissue.

## Neocortex as primary arena

Most of what it means to be human plays out in the neocortex: "competition between energy surging through rival pathways strengthened or weakened by life experience." The lower brain structures (thalamus, limbic system) contribute, but the neocortical pathway-competition is where the character, memory, reasoning, and personality reside.

Life experience shapes which pathways are strong (high-weight attractors) and which are weak. Consciousness at any moment is a snapshot of which [[attractor]] won the current competition.

## Relation to inhibitory logic gates and NAPOT

Each inhibitory event is a [[inhibitory-logic-gate-pattern-cascade|logic gate]] that bifurcates the pattern space. In [[napot-overview]] terms, the competing patterns each project their phase-wave differentials; the one that propagates most successfully through the array architecture becomes the rendered percept. The "wrestling" is the interaction of competing [[phase-wave-differential]] signals.

## Connection to bottom-up vs. centralized network distinction

Ben Thomas's contrast in this same discussion is useful: fungal tree-communication networks are purely bottom-up with no centralized inhibitory order. Vertebrate brains add top-down inhibitory connections that can impose global constraints on local competition. SAN theory treats these as complementary levels of a fractal architecture — local wrestling + global thalamic modulation.
