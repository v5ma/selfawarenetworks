**Consciousness** is the central explanatory target of Self-Aware Networks (SAN), but the word covers several distinct questions.

## Separate the targets

- **State consciousness:** whether an organism is awake, anesthetized, asleep, or clinically unresponsive.
- **Conscious content:** what is experienced, such as a color, sound, body location, object, or thought.
- **Conscious access:** whether content is available for flexible report, memory, reasoning, and control.
- **Metacognition:** whether the organism can evaluate its own performance or access.
- **Phenomenal consciousness:** why and how physical activity has a subjective character at all.

Evidence for one target does not automatically establish the others. Neural decoding can identify stimulus information without proving access. Report can reflect decision and motor preparation. A recurrent or synchronized pattern can correlate with experience without being sufficient for it.

## Established neuroscience

Conscious states and contents depend on distributed brain activity. Sensory cortex, association cortex, thalamic and subcortical systems, memory, arousal, body-state regulation, and action systems contribute in task- and state-dependent ways. Feedforward processing, local recurrence, long-range interaction, attention, and report can be experimentally dissociated.

No single frequency band, cortical column, magnetic field, phase maximum, prefrontal ignition, posterior hot zone, or information metric is currently accepted as a complete neural explanation. A 2025 preregistered adversarial study found results that supported some predictions and substantially challenged key predictions of both Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory.

## The SAN proposal

SAN proposes that consciousness depends on a distributed observer-action architecture. Receptors and cells register differences; learned structure selects consequential combinations; recurrent populations transform and preserve relations; and sensory, memory, body, and action systems maintain a revisable working model. The network itself performs the observing operation. It does not send a picture to an inner viewer.

[[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography|NAPOT]] is the proposed many-to-one-to-many reconstruction process, [[tonic-phasic-canvas|tonic/phasic]] language describes reference and update roles, [[gamma-wave-consideration-sandwich|GCS]] proposes circuit-specific timing relations, and [[nonlinear-differential-continuous-approximation|NDCA]] proposes continuity-preserving approximation. These are theory components whose biological implementations and necessity remain open.

## Evidence ladder

SAN should distinguish four increasingly strong claims:

1. a neural state represents a distinction;
2. an external model can decode that distinction;
3. the organism causally uses the distinction; and
4. the state is proposed as constitutive of phenomenal experience.

The first three can constrain the fourth but do not prove it. A successful SAN program must state unique predictions, matched alternatives, selective perturbations, expected impairments, and rescue conditions for each step.

## Primary anchor

- The COGITATE Consortium directly tested preregistered predictions from IIT and GNWT and found mixed support plus major challenges for both ([Nature, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1)).
