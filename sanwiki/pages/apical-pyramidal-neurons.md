# Apical Pyramidal Neurons

**Apical pyramidal neurons** is a descriptive SAN phrase for cortical pyramidal neurons considered
through their apical dendritic compartment. It is not one uniform medical cell class. Pyramidal
neurons vary across cortical area, layer, projection target, morphology, channel expression,
development, and state.

> **Scientific boundary:** Apical dendrites receive and integrate input. Pyramidal-cell output is carried by axons. Basal and apical domains may preferentially sample different pathways in a named circuit, but they are not universal receptive and output poles.

## The source-faithful cell-class question

The earlier page treated one generic layer 5/6 pyramidal cell as the central NAPOT unit. The
medically viable question is more precise:

**Can different pyramidal-cell classes contribute complementary receive-transform-project roles,
while recurrent pathways coordinate their distinct outputs into a distributed neural rendering and
action cycle?**

The recovered operator is:

**cell class and dendritic morphology + pathway-specific input + learned synaptic and intrinsic
state -> compartment-sensitive dendritic and somatic transformation -> AIS output decision ->
projection-class-specific axonal route -> downstream and recurrent consequences -> changed
population, body, and environmental state**

The same abstract operation can recur across classes without requiring one canonical morphology,
one frequency, one laminar algorithm, or one direct route from dendrite to consciousness.

## Layer and projection diversity

Examples of cortical excitatory projection classes include:

- layer 2/3 intratelencephalic neurons with local and corticocortical projections;
- layer 5 intratelencephalic neurons with targets inside the telencephalon;
- layer 5 extratelencephalic or pyramidal-tract neurons with subcortical targets;
- layer 6 corticothalamic neurons;
- layer 6 corticocortical, intratelencephalic, near-projecting, and other classes.

These are families, not interchangeable cells. A combined transcriptomic and projectional study in
adult mouse visual and motor cortex found many glutamatergic cell types with area- and
projection-related differences. See
[Tasic et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0654-5).

Even corticothalamic organization varies by cortical region. In mice, a frontal layer 5 pathway to
the thalamic reticular nucleus differed qualitatively from sensory-cortex organization. See
[Hádinger et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01217-z).

The same cell can receive synapses on its apical tuft, apical trunk, oblique branches, basal
dendrites, soma, and AIS. The functional effect depends on source, location, receptor, inhibition,
timing, and current state.

## What the layer 6 evidence establishes

Ledergerber and Larkum studied apical dendrites of layer 6 pyramidal neurons in young-adult rat
somatosensory slices. They found active backpropagation, local dendritic electrogenesis,
timing-dependent interaction, and inhibitory control. Their sampled cells usually had apical
dendrites ending in upper layer 5 or layer 4 rather than layer 1. The preparation does not establish
that every layer 6 pyramidal neuron in every area has that morphology or function. See
[Ledergerber and Larkum (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2254-10.2010).

Layer 6 corticothalamic axons can also have both excitatory and indirect inhibitory effects on
thalamic circuits. In a mouse somatosensory preparation, the net effect shifted with stimulation
frequency because the relevant synapses had different short-term dynamics. See
[Crandall et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25913856/).

This supports state-dependent, cell-class-specific loop control. It does not show that a layer 6
apical dendrite directly sends a prediction to the thalamus or universally sets an attention
frequency for a whole cortical column.

## Cellular transformation

In a named cortical pyramidal class, the ordinary sequence is:

1. distributed synaptic input changes dendritic and somatic voltage;
2. local dendritic events may amplify, suppress, or gate selected combinations;
3. the AIS normally initiates axonal action potentials;
4. the axon routes output to class-specific cortical or subcortical targets;
5. downstream synapses transform the signal again;
6. recurrent circuit and sensorimotor consequences contribute to later input.

See [Kole et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn2040) for AIS initiation. In mouse
somatosensory cortex, apical activity and descending projection class had different causal
relations to tactile detection, demonstrating why cell identity and target cannot be omitted. See
[Takahashi et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-0677-8).

## The 2017 donor model and the 2022 SAN extension

Kasevich and LaBerge proposed in 2017 that apical-dendrite loop circuits could supply sustained
oscillatory timing, that layer 5 bursting could amplify selected network processing, and that
layer 5/6 corticothalamic loops could participate in attention and circuit selection. This is an
external precursor, not a Micah-authored SAN claim. See
[Kasevich and LaBerge (2017)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2017.00037).

Micah's 2022 SAN notes used that donor model to develop a different joined hypothesis:

- learned dendritic and cellular state changes how a cell responds;
- current evidence and prior context can interact across cellular compartments;
- pyramidal classes re-express different consequences through different axonal routes;
- phase differences between recurrent loops can change temporary assembly recruitment;
- tonic population state supplies receiver-relative context for phasic departures;
- many cellular and circuit transformations participate in distributed rendering and action.

The distinction matters. SAN did not originate apical amplification, corticothalamic loops, or the
2017 loop-clock proposal. Its candidate contribution is the way these components were integrated
with NAPOT, PWD, learned response prediction, distributed observation, and observer-action closure.

## Coincidence, selection, and consciousness boundary

Coincidence between backpropagating spikes and distal apical input can recruit dendritic calcium
events and bursts in some pyramidal neurons. This supplies a candidate association or amplification
mechanism. It is not a universal behavioral-timescale comparator and not tomography by one cell.

Oscillatory phase and burst output may correlate with or influence communication under specified
conditions. They do not create an automatic frequency gate in which every matching neuron joins a
task and every mismatching neuron is blocked. Tests must distinguish shared input, connectivity,
sender power, inhibition, phase relation, receiver gain, and behavioral effect.

Pyramidal populations contribute to measured cortical fields, but no one pyramidal class is the
complete biophysical basis of consciousness. A consciousness claim must specify state, content,
access, report, metacognition, or phenomenal identity and report the evidence level separately.

## Bounded SAN interpretation

SAN can treat identified pyramidal classes as complementary micro-scale
receive-transform-project units:

- dendritic compartments receive and transform partial, pathway-specific state;
- somatic and AIS dynamics select axonal events;
- projection-specific axons and synapses re-express different consequences;
- recurrent cortical, thalamic, subcortical, bodily, and environmental routes update the next
  cycle.

NAPOT is a meso- and macro-scale reconstruction hypothesis built from populations and pathways. It
should not be assigned to one generic pyramidal cell. The observer is the distributed network whose
cellular and field state is changed and used, not a miniature observer inside an apical compartment.

## Claim boundaries

- **Established neuroscience:** pyramidal cells are diverse; dendritic compartments integrate
  input; the AIS and axon produce and route output; corticothalamic and corticocortical pathways
  are cell- and region-specific.
- **Supported interpretation:** compartment-sensitive input and projection-class-specific output
  provide a biological basis for complementary transformations across recurrent circuits.
- **SAN-specific hypothesis:** learned cellular response, receiver-relative phase differences, and
  recurrent class-specific projection jointly support NAPOT reconstruction, temporary assembly
  selection, and distributed observer-action.
- **Stronger experimental claim:** a specified pyramidal class, loop frequency, burst mode, or
  apical event carries a defined representation or is necessary for conscious content. This
  requires cell-class-specific causal tests and competing explanations.
