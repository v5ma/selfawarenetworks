**Consciousness as frames** is a historical SAN metaphor for temporal updating. It should not be read as a claim that the cortex produces a sequence of complete still images like a film or display.

## Why the literal frame model fails

Neural events unfold across milliseconds to seconds and beyond. Spikes, synaptic currents, oscillations, eye movements, recurrent processing, working memory, and action each have different and overlapping timescales. A percept can remain stable while some features change, and different systems can update asynchronously.

Gamma, beta, alpha, theta, and slower activity do not combine into one display refresh rate. Their functional roles vary by circuit, task, state, measurement, and receiver. Brainwave entrainment also does not directly turn the rate of conscious frames up or down.

## A better use of the metaphor

A **frame** can mean a bounded analysis window or a temporary state estimate. Under that definition, the model asks:

- which relations are preserved during the window;
- which evidence updates them;
- which populations can use the state;
- how overlapping windows maintain continuity; and
- what changes when a route or timing relation is perturbed.

SAN's tonic canvas and phasic ink language then describes relative persistence and update, not a film strip. NAPOT proposes reconstruction across partial projections, while NDCA proposes that nonlinear recurrent dynamics can maintain usable continuity despite discrete cellular events.

## Distinguish temporal claims

Temporal integration, perceptual sampling, working-memory maintenance, event segmentation, and subjective duration are different phenomena. A result in one cannot be promoted to a universal frame theory. Quantum-gradient or spacetime language also requires its own independent physical derivation and cannot be inferred from changing subjective duration.

## Tests

A frame-like SAN model should predict performance at a declared temporal task better than continuous-state and recurrent alternatives, identify the responsible receiver and route, and predict how selective timing perturbation changes integration without merely changing arousal, attention, or motor timing.
