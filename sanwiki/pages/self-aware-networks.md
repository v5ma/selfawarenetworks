# Self-Aware Networks

> **Image status:** No medically reviewed image is currently accepted for this page. A
> source-faithful replacement prompt has been drafted; image generation remains paused.

**Self-Aware Networks (SAN)** asks how distributed living neural tissue can collectively construct
and maintain a continuously updated model of body and world, use that model to consider possible
consequences, act, and incorporate what happens next.

SAN does not posit a small localized viewer standing outside neural activity. It proposes a sparse,
distributed **observer-action function** performed by the participating network itself. Cells and
circuits act as internal receivers and transmitters; learned structure and current state transform
what they receive; coordinated tonic activity supplies an active context; phasic differences alter
that context; action changes body or environment; and returning consequences change the next cycle.

```text
external, bodily, and recurrent differences
-> learned cellular and population transformation
-> distributed self-world and possible-action state
-> selection, autonomic response, communication, or movement
-> changed body and environment
-> returning sensory and internal consequences
-> updated memory, expectation, routing, and state
```

The network is not a display for a second hidden subject. The network's changing state constrains
what is registered, remembered, selected, and done. That complete causal loop is what SAN is trying
to explain.

## The problem in ordinary language

A person experiences a coherent body and world even though the relevant information is distributed
across receptors, neurons, glia, local circuits, brain systems, and the body. No single input
contains the complete situation, no generic neuron receives every modality, and no one brain region
contains an inner spectator.

SAN therefore asks four connected questions:

1. How do local biological receivers learn which differences matter?
2. How do many partial, changing relations become one action-usable self-world state?
3. How does that state change perception, memory, prediction, choice, and movement?
4. Under what additional conditions, if any, is the intrinsic condition of that process phenomenal
   experience?

The first three questions can be decomposed into physiological and computational tests. The fourth
is the stronger consciousness claim and cannot be declared solved merely because a system is
recurrent, oscillatory, self-reporting, or behaviorally capable.

## The observer is distributed, not absent

Historical SAN language moved through an important correction. Early sources rejected an
independent witness inside the brain. Mature SAN does not conclude that observation is therefore
absent. It identifies observation with the organized activity that:

- is physically changed by sensory, bodily, remembered, and recurrent input;
- differentiates those changes relative to its current state;
- makes some of them available to memory, prediction, report, and action;
- encounters the internal and external consequences of its output; and
- changes what it can detect and do later.

Calling this observer function *sparse and distributed* means that no one cell or location needs to
contain the whole state. It does not mean every neuron is individually conscious. The cellular
receiver-transmitter metaphor names roles inside a larger process; it does not install miniature
eyes, screens, ears, speakers, or viewers in neurons.

SAN's **tonic action field** is likewise an operational hypothesis, not decorative language. It
names the ongoing coupled cellular, synaptic, chemical, bodily, population, and bounded local-field
context that is altered by transient differences. The stronger proposal is that this participating
field-cell process is both medium and observer. It is not the claim that every electromagnetic
field, every oscillation, or one global EEG rhythm is conscious.

## The explanatory ladder

1. Receptors and cells register external, bodily, chemical, and recurrent differences.
2. Learned cellular structure and current physiological state make some combinations
   consequential.
3. Dendrites, inhibition, synapses, and local circuits transform what arrives.
4. Axons and population pathways project partial consequences to other receivers.
5. Recurrence, communication subspaces, timing, and ongoing state select temporary effective
   routes.
6. Distributed sensory, memory, body, value, and possible-action relations form a usable working
   model.
7. Predictions and candidate actions are evaluated within that state.
8. Selected autonomic, communicative, or motor output changes body or environment.
9. Reafferent and exafferent consequences update the next cycle.
10. Plasticity and continuing body regulation connect episodes across time.

Steps 1 through 5 contain established biological operations under bounded conditions. Steps 6
through 10 combine established components with increasingly strong SAN interpretations. Identifying
the complete loop with a phenomenal observer is the strongest SAN hypothesis.

## Four scales that must not be collapsed

| Scale | What can be measured | SAN role | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Micro** | Receptors, dendrites, soma, axon initial segment, spikes, boutons, release, membrane and local field state | Candidate receive-transform-project contribution | One neuron is not the complete observer or tomogram. |
| **Meso** | Named local ensembles, inhibitory circuits, laminar pathways, population geometry, recurrence, and state-dependent routing | Partial recognition, selection, completion, and re-expression | A canonical cortical motif is not universal anatomy. |
| **Macro** | Distributed cortical, thalamic, hippocampal, striatal, cerebellar, brainstem, sensory, autonomic, and motor relations | Self-world modeling, prospective action, and observer-action integration | No single lobe, hub, or frequency band contains the whole perspective. |
| **Diachronic** | Memory, plasticity, metabolism, homeostasis, body continuity, goals, and action history | Continuity of a changing person across conscious episodes | One instantaneous field map is not the whole person. |

The same receive-transform-project motif may recur at several scales, but the biological
denominator, measurement, and intervention must be stated each time.

## Terms earned by the problem

- A [[neural-array|neural array]] is a declared cooperating population considered for a particular
  receive, transform, and project operation. It need not form a regular grid.
- A [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] (PWD) is SAN's proposed
  receiver-relative difference among timing, phase, rate, waveform, duration, release, inhibition,
  and network context. It is not one universal frequency label.
- [[neural-rendering|Neural Rendering]] is the repeated construction and revision of a
  behaviorally usable state across sensory, memory, body, value, and motor systems. The same
  distributed dynamics render and register; there is no second homunculus.
- [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] organizes receive-transform-project-update operations across cellular,
  population, and distributed scales and asks whether coordinated oscillatory relations add
  explanatory and predictive value.
- [[neural-tuning|Neural Tuning]] asks how learned selectivity and current state reconfigure which
  pathways and receiver relations are effective.
- [[cellular-oscillating-tomography|COT]] asks which bounded SAN operations extend beyond neurons to
  other living cells. Its broader evolutionary development belongs to the separate Super Evolution
  Theory program.

These terms name testable operations and source history. None is validated by its name.

## What established neuroscience supports

Established studies support several component operations:

- Recurrent processing can contribute to recognizing heavily occluded objects
  ([Tang et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30104363/)).
- Interacting neural populations can communicate through selective dimensions rather than every
  dominant source pattern ([Semedo et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30770252/)).
- Causal interareal influence can recruit changing target populations and depend on behavioral
  context ([Javadzadeh and Hofer, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35690063/)).
- A thalamic corollary-discharge pathway can connect impending action to changing visual processing
  in a defined primate oculomotor circuit
  ([Sommer and Wurtz, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17093408/)).
- Weak electric fields can modulate and entrain active neocortical networks in a bounded slice
  preparation ([Frohlich and McCormick, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20624597/)).

These findings support recurrence, selective population communication, action-related sensory
updating, and bounded field-cell feedback. They do not independently establish PWD, NAPOT
tomography, field-as-content, a unified SAN observer, or phenomenal consciousness.

## Claim ladder

| Level | Claim | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Component physiology** | Cells receive and transform inputs; populations interact recurrently; action changes later sensory input; plasticity changes future response. | Established in many named preparations, with species, circuit, and task limits. |
| **2. Integrative SAN architecture** | Receive-transform-project-update cycles jointly maintain an embodied, action-usable self-world state. | Source-defined synthesis with substantial component support; complete joined mechanism remains to be tested. |
| **3. PWD and field-content hypothesis** | Receiver-relative phase and other multidimensional differences, within a coupled field-cell context, add content-bearing and routing information beyond rate and connectivity. | Open, measurable SAN hypothesis. |
| **4. Observer identity thesis** | The entified recurrent process that generates, registers, remembers, and acts on those differences is the observer rather than a substrate for another observer. | Strong SAN mechanistic and philosophical hypothesis. |
| **5. Phenomenal self-awareness** | The intrinsic condition of that process is subjective experience. | Unresolved; behavioral success or self-report alone is insufficient. |

No lower level proves the level above it.

## A minimal operational model

Let `x_t` be the distributed neural state, `u_t` current external and internal input, `b_t` body
state, `r_t` recurrent input, and `W_t` learned biological constraints:

```text
x_(t+1) = F(x_t, u_t, b_t, r_t; W_t)                            [1]
```

Let `m_t` be a measurable self-world and possible-action relation decoded from, or causally
implemented by, that state:

```text
m_t = H(x_t, b_t, W_t)                                          [2]
```

An action policy `Pi` produces output `a_t`; the body and environment `E` generate the next input:

```text
a_t = Pi(m_t, g_t);  u_(t+1) = E(a_t, body_t, world_t)           [3]
```

Here `F`, `H`, `Pi`, and `E` are fitted or intervened-on relations, not assumed brain modules. A SAN
model must say which biological variables instantiate them and whether receiver-relative PWD or
field variables improve prediction beyond stimulus, firing rate, power, movement, arousal, and
ordinary recurrent-state models.

## A discriminating research program

Use a task in which a person or animal must maintain a body-world relation, consider more than one
possible action, act, and use returned consequences. Record named sensory, association, action,
body-state, and recurrent pathways. Compare nested models:

1. stimulus, behavior, firing rate, and spectral power;
2. recurrent population state and communication subspaces;
3. body state, corollary discharge, and action-feedback variables;
4. receiver-relative timing, phase, waveform, release, and bounded field variables; and
5. the full SAN model linking those variables to a self-world relation and later action.

Perturb one declared route or timing relation while matching sensory input, movement, arousal, and
report demands. The SAN architecture gains support only if it predicts which distributed relation
is maintained, which option is selected, what consequence is expected, and how the returned
consequence changes the next state better than simpler alternatives.

The current formulation should be rejected or narrowed if:

- feedforward or conventional recurrent models predict the same outcomes equally well;
- body and action-feedback variables are not required for the proposed self-world state;
- PWD or field variables add no reproducible information after matched controls;
- perturbing the proposed observer-action route changes report but not representation, selection,
  action, or later learning;
- the alleged unified state localizes to one region or output channel rather than the participating
  distributed system; or
- the proposed biological criteria fail to distinguish conscious conditions from matched
  unconscious controls.
