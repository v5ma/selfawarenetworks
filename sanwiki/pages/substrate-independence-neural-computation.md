**Substrate independence of neural computation** is the question of whether intelligence and consciousness depend on *what* neurons are made of (carbon, silicon, photons) or only on *how* they compute — their pattern-matching, coincidence detection, and temporal coordination properties.

## Micah's 2012 formulation

In a 2012 discussion ([[gh-a0409z|a0409z]]), [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] posed: *"What if the neuron was fiber optic and the [[action-potential|action potential]] was a beam of light instead of an electron? Could you still have intelligence?"* This was an early exploration of whether the electrochemical substrate of biological neurons is necessary or merely one implementation of a more abstract computational process.

The responses raised key constraints: synapses rely on chemical signaling (would they work with proton-based triggering?), and any substrate must support [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modification to enable learning.

## Relation to SAN theory

SAN's [[coincidence-pattern-neural-bit|coincidence pattern]] framework implies substrate is irrelevant *if* the candidate substrate can:

1. Detect coincident activation across inputs (coincidence detection).
2. Adjust connection weights based on firing history (a form of [[neuron-rank-protein-synthesis|plasticity]]).
3. Sustain [[oscillatory-phase-patterns|oscillatory phase synchrony]] across arrays.

Photonic or silicon implementations that satisfy these three properties would, under SAN, be capable of [[self-aware-networks-theory|self-aware computation]].
