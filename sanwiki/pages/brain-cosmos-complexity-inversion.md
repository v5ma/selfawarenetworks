The **brain-cosmos complexity inversion** is the conjecture that the number of synaptic connections in a single human brain may rival or exceed the number of stars in the visible cosmos, inverting the usual assumption that the cosmos is the larger, more complex system from which brains emerged.

If brain-scale organization exceeds cosmic-scale organization in raw complexity, then the standard evolutionary story ("cosmos to stars to planets to life to brains") may understate the significance of mind. The universe produces something more intricate than itself.

## Connection to retrocausality

The inversion connects to [[quantum-retrocausality|quantum retrocausality]]: the 2012 source note frames future-to-past influence as a way to interpret entanglement. If future conditions can constrain earlier states, and if brains are the most complex ordered systems the universe produces, then the existence of future minds becomes a candidate cause for why the universe began at all: a cosmological anthropic argument grounded in complexity rather than fine-tuning.
