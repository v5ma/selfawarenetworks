# Cytoskeletal Signal Routing

**Cytoskeletal signal routing** is a proposed SAN term for the way intracellular structure and transport can change which molecular resources reach a neuronal compartment, when they arrive, and how that compartment responds later. The phrase should not be read as evidence that microtubules are electrical wires carrying the same signals as membranes and synapses.

## Established intracellular organization

Neurons use several interacting cytoskeletal systems:

- **Microtubules** are prominent in axons and dendritic shafts. Motor proteins move vesicles, organelles, RNA-protein complexes, and other cargo along them.
- **Actin filaments** are especially important in growth cones, the cell cortex, presynaptic terminals, and dendritic spines. Actin remodeling changes spine shape and postsynaptic organization.
- **Intermediate filaments**, including neurofilaments, contribute to axonal structure and caliber.

These systems interact, but they do not perform one uniform operation. Transport, local anchoring, structural remodeling, mechanotransduction, and biochemical signaling occur through different molecules and on different timescales.

Growing EB3-positive microtubule ends can transiently enter a subset of actin-rich dendritic spines. Jaworski and colleagues linked these entries to actin-associated changes in spine morphology ([Neuron, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2008.11.013)). Hu and colleagues found that BDNF prolonged microtubule dwell time in cultured mouse hippocampal spines and that blocking microtubule dynamics prevented the measured BDNF-related increase in PSD-95 ([Journal of Neuroscience, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2445-11.2011)).

These findings support a route from activity and trophic signaling through cytoskeletal dynamics to later postsynaptic organization. They do not show that every spine contains a stable microtubule bundle or that the cytoskeleton carries a complete percept.

## Routing is not one fast signal channel

Dendritic voltage, synaptic current, calcium events, receptor signaling, cargo transport, local translation, organelle positioning, and structural remodeling can influence one another. Calling all of them "routing" is useful only if the transported object, path, timescale, and receiver are stated.

For example:

- ion channels and receptors shape membrane voltage on fast electrophysiological timescales;
- calcium and kinase cascades can change local biochemical state;
- motors transport cargo along microtubules over longer distances and times;
- actin and microtubule remodeling can change future synaptic efficacy and cellular morphology.

A medically accurate account should not depict microtubules as carrying action potentials from dendrite to axon hillock. Action potentials propagate through excitable membrane. Cytoskeletal processes can modulate the conditions under which membrane and synaptic events occur.

## SAN interpretation

SAN can use **changed intracellular readiness** as the bounded bridge. Cytoskeletal dynamics may change receptor or scaffold availability, mitochondrial placement, local translation, cargo delivery, morphology, and plasticity. Those changes can alter how a neuron receives, integrates, and projects later activity.

This contribution sits below cell and network computation:

1. molecular and cytoskeletal processes alter a compartment's future state;
2. dendritic and somatic compartments transform synaptic input;
3. axonal and synaptic output changes downstream populations;
4. recurrent population activity changes perception, memory, and action.

The cytoskeleton is one causal layer inside the distributed observer-action process. It is not a hidden observer, a second nervous system inside the neuron, or proof of [[orch-or|Orch OR]]. SAN does not require quantum microtubule coherence for cytoskeletal state to matter.

## What would establish a stronger routing claim

A stronger claim requires a declared causal chain:

1. identify the molecular signal or cargo;
2. measure its cytoskeletal path and timing;
3. perturb that path selectively;
4. measure the resulting compartmental electrical or biochemical change;
5. measure the downstream circuit and behavioral consequence;
6. rule out matched effects on general cell health, transport, and excitability.

Evidence at one step should not silently establish the later steps.
