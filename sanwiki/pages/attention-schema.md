# Attention Schema

![Attention Schema foreground inhibition and tomographic map plate](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/attention-schema/attention-schema-foreground-inhibition-tomographic-map-20260629-v1.png)

This plate renders the page's central SAN mechanism: a dominant phase-wave differential becomes foreground through inhibitory surround suppression, while apical schema input, basal sensory evidence, thalamic coordination, and phase-aligned oscillator slices bind into a tomographic attention map.

**[[attention]] Schema** is the brain's internal model of its own [[attention]] process — the [[gh-a0206z|self]]-referential representation that allows neural circuits to monitor, control, and deploy attentional resources across the cortical hierarchy. In the [[Self-Aware Networks]] framework the term carries a specific mechanistic meaning derived from Michael Graziano's [[attention]] Schema Theory but substantially extended: the schema is not a localized module but a distributed tomographic and [[attention-schema-holographic|holographic]] map defined by [[inhibitory-circuits]] and [[phase-wave-differential|phase differentials]] across the entire oscillatory network, and its structure is inseparable from the structure of [[Consciousness]] itself.

At the cellular level the [[NAPOT]] notes describe the [[attention]] schema mechanism with concrete clarity: neurons that fire at one frequency inhibit neighbors oscillating at a different frequency, so "[[attention]] (reference: [[attention]] schema theory) becomes focused on the neuron that fired, and its signal is amplified via the inhibition of nearby neurons" (a0007z--md, line 585). This is the tectum-level illustration of the schema: "the tectum illustration of [[attention]] schema allows the neuron that fired, that inhibits its neighbors to set the sensory trajectory of movement" ([[gh-00abstractbump|00abstractbump]]--md, line 23), enabling [[action-potential]] events to propagate their influence by suppressing the surrounding [[Cortical-columns|cortical column]] background. The same dynamic recurs at every spatial scale — a single [[phasic-burst]] in a [[micro-columns|microcolumn]] inhibits its local neighborhood, while large-scale [[cortical-traveling-waves]] implement the same schema logic across brain regions, with the [[thalamus]] coordinating which columns enter attentive foreground versus recede into background oscillation. The [[inhibitory-circuits]] doing this work are predominantly [[GABA]]-ergic interneurons whose firing tightly constrains the spatial envelope of the attended representation; the [[apical-dendrite|apical dendrites]] of pyramidal cells serve as the integration sites where top-down schema signals from [[prefrontal-cortex]] meet ascending sensory evidence.

The [[Self-Aware Networks]] book contains an explicit chapter called "Holographic or Tomographic [[attention]] Schema, which builds on the existing [[attention]] Schema theory" ([[gh-04san|04san]]--md, line 475). The key insight of this extension is that the rendering medium for [[Consciousness]] is tomographic rather than holographic in the strict optical sense: "consciousness are not creating a holographic rendering but a tomographic rendering in the minds eye, [[attention]] schema helps define a tomographic map in each oscillator that makes up part of the whole experience of consciousness" ([[gh-00abstractbump|00abstractbump]]--md, line 21). The [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography]] (NAPOT) framework operationalizes this: each [[oscillator]] in the network contributes a slice of the overall tomographic reconstruction, and the [[attention]] schema is the dynamically updated map specifying which slices are currently foregrounded. The rendering "is also accomplished by the inhibition of parts of the brain, in alignment with [[attention]]-schema theory but specifically in the context of a 4 dimensional brain grid & electro-magnetic [[phase-wave-differential|phase change graph]]" ([[gh-a0027z|a0027z]]--md, line 11), meaning the schema is inseparable from [[phase-pattern|phase patterns]] distributed across a four-dimensional cortical geometry — not a static diagram but a continuously updated [[attractor]] in [[phase-field]] space. The [[gh-a0158z|a0158z]] notes add that "phasic signals are activating microperspectives, which result in holographic [[attention]]-schema-theory" ([[gh-a0158z|a0158z]]--md, line 61), linking the microscale [[phasic-burst|phasic bursts]] that define each microperspective to the emergent holographic schema at the macroscale.

The schema does more than select; it binds. As the a0039z notes argue, "the [[attention]] schema both defines which areas of your mental map you are seeing, but it also binds those areas together in sequences that your [experience unfolds through]" (a0039z--md, line 63), and specifically the schema "of circuit activity binds in temporal & spatial firing patterns of [[phase-wave-differential|phase differentials]]" (a0039z--md, line 66). This binding function connects the [[attention]] schema directly to the [[binding-problem]]: the schema is the mechanism by which distributed cortical representations cohered into a unified perceptual object. The SAN notes position the [[attention]] schema alongside — and in dialogue with — [[global-workspace-theory]], [[integrated-information-theory]], and [[predictive-coding]] as candidate theories of consciousness, with the SAN author noting explicitly that while "Michael Graziano thinks that the next step to [[global-workspace-theory]] is going to be the [[attention]] schema," the SAN reinterpretation argues that the schema as he describes it "actually isn't the next step" because it does not yet account for the full distributed oscillatory structure ([[gh-b0151ywhisper|b0151ywhisper]]--md, lines 244, 256). The SAN extension — elaborated further in [[attention-schema-reinterpretation]] and [[attention-schema-holographic]] — is that the schema must be understood as a multi-level, multidimensional entity: "the brain's tomographic representation of reality has its own multi level or multi scale or multidimensional [[attention]] schema. You are attending the [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|dominant phase wave differentials]] in your internal rendering that represent whatever external thing" ([[gh-veca|vecA]]--md, line 7660). This multi-scale architecture also confers computational efficiency: the brain "emphasizes computational and energy efficiency through a user-driven [[attention]] schema. By selectively activating only relevant subsets of a fractal [[oscillator|oscillatory]] network based on user focus" (vecB--md, line 5874), the cortex avoids broadcasting across the entire [[Cortical-columns]] network, instead sampling the [[phase-wave-differential]] tree selectively — a form of adaptive sparse coding that the SAN notes link to the broader principle of [[sparse-distributed-representation]].

The SAN notes survey "Global Workspace Theory, Integrated Information Theory, [[attention]] Schema Theory, Higher Order Thought Theory, & Recurrent Processing Theory" as the competing theoretical landscape ([[gh-00todolist|00todolist]]--md, line 32), and the SAN framework's claim is that the [[attention]] schema, properly reinterpreted through [[oscillatory-tomography]] and [[phase-precession]], provides the missing mechanistic bridge between these frameworks: "Building on other theories of consciousness like [[attention]] Schema theory & others, we could propose that conscious perception arises when predictive oscillations in prefrontal and sensory [areas converge]" ([[gh-04san|04san]]--md, line 475). The [[temporal-parietal-junction]] (TPJ) identified by Graziano as the neuroanatomical correlate of the schema maps, in the SAN account, onto the [[Cortical-columns|columnar]] population whose phase relationships most strongly encode the foregrounded attentional focus — consistent with [[fmri]] studies showing TPJ activation when [[attention]] shifts to a novel object, as documented in the b0047y notes (b0047y--md, line 93). [[Neurofeedback]] and [[brain-computer-interfaces]] that target [[attention]] regulation must therefore intervene at the level of the distributed phase pattern rather than simply reading TPJ activation, and [[Neuralink]]-class implants probing this structure would need to sample from populations spanning multiple cortical regions to capture the full tomographic map.
