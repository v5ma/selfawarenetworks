Blumberg's position holds that what appears as *emergence* — irreducible complexity arising from simpler components — is fully reducible to iterative wave-based computational processes in which property differences progressively diminish toward equilibrium.

## Core Claim

Phenomena such as gas expansion, neural firing, and conscious experience do not exhibit genuinely novel properties that cannot be derived from their parts. All such phenomena are **phase-differential resolution processes**: the apparent irreducibility dissolves once the oscillatory substrate is made explicit. Equilibrium is not a statistical outcome but the state at which iterative signal exchanges cease to produce net phase differences.

[[oscillatory-tomography]] (or [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography]]) provides the neural mechanism: cortical arrays construct sensory renderings through phase wave interactions that progressively eliminate differentials. What looks like irreducible sensory binding is, under this view, a convergence trajectory in oscillator phase space.

## Three-Theory Unification

Blumberg explicitly connects three frameworks under this anti-emergence thesis:

1. **[[new-law-of-thermodynamics]]** — entropy as the physical instance of reducible computational dissipation.
2. **[[dark-time-theory]]** — time-density fields resolving spacetime phase differentials, unifying gravity and quantum mechanics.
3. **[[self-aware-networks]]** — wave synchronization in neural oscillators yielding consciousness as phase-coherent convergence.

All three are instances of the same reducible process: iterative wave interactions eliminating property differences across sensing components.

## Relation to Standard Emergence Accounts

Standard emergence accounts (Chalmers' hard problem, Anderson's "More is Different") claim emergent properties cannot be predicted or derived from lower-level descriptions. Blumberg disputes the irreducibility claim: apparent unpredictability dissolves when [[wave-synchronization]] and phase dynamics are taken as the explanatory primitive. Influences include Stephen Strogatz (synchronization) and György Buzsáki ([[neural-oscillations|neural oscillations]]).
