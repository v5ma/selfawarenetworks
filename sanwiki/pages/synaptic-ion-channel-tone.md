The **synaptic ion channel tone** is the unique amplitude-frequency-phase signature an [[action-potential|action potential]] acquires from the specific mix of sodium, potassium, and calcium channel activations at a synapse. Different receptor ratios produce distinguishably different "tones," and the brain creates and detects a multi-scale temporal-spatial phase space from the aggregate of these tones.

## The Ionic Mechanism

Sodium channels open first, driving depolarization and triggering the [[action-potential|action potential]] at the axon hillock. Potassium channels open second, expelling positive charge and repolarizing the cell so it can fire again. Calcium mediates termination. The key insight: every synapse has a different *number and type* of available sodium, potassium, and calcium receptors. Clogging, inhibiting, or adding channels alters the shape — amplitude, frequency, and phase — of the resulting spike.

This is the mechanism behind short-term memory (transient channel opening/closing/connecting/disconnecting) versus long-term memory (protein synthesis that structurally alters channel availability).

## Action Potential as Tone

Each unique channel composition produces a spike with distinct:
- **Amplitude** — how much the inside of the cell depolarizes relative to outside
- **Frequency** — how fast the cell can fire again (set by potassium repolarization speed)
- **Phase characteristics** — the timing relationship to oscillatory neighbors

Amplitude and frequency are inversely proportional at multiple scales: greater amplitude → lower frequency; greater frequency → lower amplitude. This inverse relationship recurs fractally from single synapses to cortical columns.

## Phase Space the Brain Creates

The "phase space" a neuron contributes is not just its spike shape but the combination of:
1. The **ionic gradient** (inside vs. outside cell electrode voltage ratio)
2. **Tonic oscillations** of local neural circuits and cortical columns
3. **Phasic interrupts** — novel firing patterns that modulate the tonic baseline

Because the brain is a densely connected graph (only a few hops between any two neurons via interneurons and cortical columns), these local phase signatures propagate and synchronize into global coordinated oscillatory patterns. Distant brain regions can oscillate coherently without direct wiring.

## Multi-Sensory Integration

The temporal-parietal junction integrates across modalities — visual, auditory, tactile — by coordinating oscillatory patterns from distributed cortical areas. Consciousness arises when multiple brain areas engage in overlapping oscillations simultaneously over time. The phase space is the substrate; the "content" of experience is encoded in which regions take their turn in a temporal sequence (like pixels lighting up in a sequence that *appears* to be a traveling brain wave).
