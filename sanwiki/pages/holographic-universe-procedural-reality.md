The holographic universe hypothesis holds that the information content of a 3D region of space can be fully encoded on a 2D boundary surface — analogous to how a hologram encodes a 3D image on a 2D plate. The black hole horizon is the canonical case: its surface area encodes all interior information.

The **Fourier slice theorem** provides a mathematical bridge: in the same way that a 2D slice of a 3D Fourier transform reconstructs a 1D projection, a 2D cosmic surface could in principle reconstruct the full 3D volume. This is not just analogy — it is the mathematical basis of the AdS/CFT correspondence in string theory.

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] connects this to an even stronger claim: that reality may be **procedurally generated at the point of observation**, resembling the double-slit experiment at cosmic scale. Every interaction instantiates the portion of reality required for that interaction — the rest is latent. Sky Nelson-Isaacs' proposal that "spacetime paths as a whole" determine local outcomes is cited as a step toward formalizing this.

The procedural generation analogy (No Man's Sky) illustrates how a deterministic algorithm can produce an apparently infinite, richly detailed universe without pre-computing every piece — detail is rendered on demand. If applied to physical reality, this would mean the universe is maximally compressed, generated in full only where observation demands it.

This intersects with [[napot]] via the projection model: just as the holographic surface projects 3D space, neural arrays project information across cortical columns to create the "space" of conscious experience. The observer — in physics and in consciousness — is the projection mechanism.
