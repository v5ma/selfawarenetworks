# The Canvas Is Also the Receiver

In SAN, the **tonic canvas** is not a passive screen viewed by a hidden observer. It is the ongoing state of the participating cells and populations. A phasic event changes that state, and the changed network influences what is recognized, transmitted, predicted, or done next. In this operational sense, the canvas receives the ink by being physically changed by it.

> **Scientific boundary:** Experiments show that ongoing neural state can shape evoked responses, that spike timing relative to an oscillatory phase can carry information in some systems, and that weak extracellular electric fields can affect neuronal timing under bounded conditions. These findings support components of the SAN proposal. They do not establish that one global field decodes all neural content, that fields alone produce consciousness, or that every phasic event is consciously observed.

## What receiver means

A receiver must be named at the scale being tested. It may be a dendritic compartment, a neuron, a local population, or a distributed recurrent system. Calling a state a receiver means that:

1. it exists before the candidate update;
2. the update measurably changes it;
3. the changed state alters a later neural or behavioral consequence; and
4. perturbing the state changes how the same update is used.

This definition does not require a separate viewer. The participating system performs the discrimination through its own state transition and subsequent action.

## Established component science

### Ongoing activity changes the response context

Arieli and colleagues found that ongoing cortical activity accounted for a large part of trial-to-trial variability in visually evoked population responses. The result supports the narrower claim that an input is not processed against a blank baseline: the state already present in the receiving tissue matters.

- [Arieli et al. (1996), ongoing activity and evoked cortical responses](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.273.5283.1868)

### Timing relative to population activity can add information

Montemurro and colleagues reported that the phase of low-frequency local field activity at which spikes occurred carried visual information beyond spike counts in macaque primary visual cortex. Belitski and colleagues separately found partly independent visual information in low-frequency local field potentials and spikes. These results establish preparation-specific timing and population-context effects, not a universal phase code.

- [Montemurro et al. (2008), phase-of-firing coding in primary visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2008.02.023)
- [Belitski et al. (2008), low-frequency field potentials and spikes](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0009-08.2008)

### Fields can feed back on active neural tissue

Frohlich and McCormick showed in an in-vitro neocortical slow-oscillation preparation that weak applied fields within the reported range of endogenous fields could enhance and entrain network activity. Closed-loop positive and negative feedback manipulations provided causal evidence that field-like feedback can alter the activity producing it. Anastassiou and colleagues found small subthreshold membrane-potential changes and spike entrainment from extracellular fields in cortical slices, with the strongest effects for slow fluctuations.

- [Frohlich and McCormick (2010), endogenous electric fields and neocortical activity](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.06.005)
- [Anastassiou et al. (2011), ephaptic coupling of cortical neurons](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2727)

These experiments justify treating field-cell feedback as a biological variable. They do not show that field effects dominate synaptic transmission, operate identically across regions or states, or contain a complete perceptual scene.

## The SAN synthesis

SAN proposes that the ongoing **field-cell state** supplies a distributed receiver context:

```text
ongoing cellular and population state
-> differentiated synaptic, dendritic, and recurrent update
-> changed cellular and extracellular state
-> altered recognition, transmission, prediction, or action
-> next ongoing state
```

The tonic canvas is therefore both context and participant. The phasic ink is not observed from elsewhere; its biological effect consists in changing the network that will generate the next response. SAN further proposes that coordinated changes across these receivers contribute to distributed observer-action. That final observer claim remains a theory-level interpretation requiring discriminating evidence.

## Micro, meso, and macro versions

| Scale | Candidate canvas/receiver | Candidate ink/update | Consequence to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | Membrane, dendritic, synaptic, and excitability state | Synaptic event, dendritic spike, burst, or waveform change | Changed spike probability, timing, plasticity, or transmitter release |
| Meso | Local recurrent population, inhibitory balance, and structured extracellular activity | Ensemble transition, phase reset, or traveling-wave passage | Changed routing, selection, decoding, or local state transition |
| Macro | Distributed recurrent brain-body state | Sensory evidence, recalled pattern, action consequence, or state transition | Changed report, sampling, decision, movement, or later reconstruction |

Evidence at one scale does not automatically validate the same claim at another. In particular, a field effect in a slice is not evidence that one brain-wide field reads a complete percept.

## Alternatives that must be controlled

A successful SAN test must outperform explanations based on firing rate, membrane potential, synaptic conductance, adaptation, arousal, movement, recurrent connectivity, common input, and volume conduction. Decoding a condition from phase or field measurements is insufficient unless the proposed receiver causally uses the information.

## Discriminating experiment

Hold anatomy and mean firing rate approximately constant while manipulating the phase or spatial structure of a candidate tonic context. Present the same phasic input under matched conditions and measure a prespecified downstream reconstruction or action. Compare four models on held-out trials:

1. firing rate and spike count;
2. recurrent state without an explicit receiver-relative phase term;
3. field or phase correlation without causal feedback; and
4. the SAN receiver-relative tonic/phasic model.

The SAN account gains support only if selective perturbation of the proposed canvas changes how the same update is used, the receiver-relative model adds held-out predictive value, and restoring the relevant state rescues the effect. It is weakened if ordinary recurrent state and movement variables explain the result, if field removal has no selective consequence, or if the proposed relation fails to generalize across trials and preparations.

## What this page does not claim

- No localized inner observer watches a render.
- No frequency band has one fixed semantic meaning.
- No universal phase alphabet has been demonstrated.
- No single neuron or field contains the whole percept.
- Ephaptic coupling is not assumed to replace synaptic and chemical signaling.
- Component support is not proof of the complete SAN consciousness account.
