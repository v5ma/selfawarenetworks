Neurophysics is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s [[gh-a0206z|self]]-defined discipline that synthesizes neuroscience, theoretical
physics, and computation to explain brain function at classical and quantum scales.

## Discipline Boundaries

| Field | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Neuroscience** | Neuroanatomy — connectome mapping, brain regions |
| **Computational (neuro)biology** | Functional connectivity, information flow |
| **Neurophysics** | Theoretical physics of neuron function at all scales |
| **Neurology** | Treatments and causes of neural dysfunction |

Neurophysics subsumes the others as prerequisites: biology → computation (math) →
theoretical physics. Adding cognitive neuroscience yields **cognitive neurophysics** —
the study of how physical neural dynamics give rise to cognition.

## Why the Distinction Matters

Mainstream neuroscience maps *structure*; neurophysics asks how the physics of ion
gradients, oscillating phase fields, and quantum-scale events produces *meaning*.
In [[san|SAN theory]], the brain is modeled as an **oscillating phase field** — a
multi-frequency holographic soliton where [[phase-wave-differential|phase changes at synapses]]
encode and transmit information across [[tonic-oscillation|tonic baselines]].

The size and type of ions in a neuron matter more in this framework than in standard
neuroscience, because ion mass and electronegativity directly affect the phase dynamics
of the field — an aspect [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] notes is under-discussed in the literature.
