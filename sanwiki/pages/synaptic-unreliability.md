# Synaptic Unreliability and Multivesicular Release

![Medically corrected multivesicular-release plate separating preparation-specific mouse neocortical evidence, probabilistic quantal output, Kvbeta1-dependent presynaptic facilitation, and an open SAN network test](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/synaptic-unreliability-mvr/synaptic-unreliability-mvr-medically-corrected-20260713-v6.png)

*The plate separates established cellular physiology from the proposed SAN interpretation. Multivesicular release and activity-dependent presynaptic waveform changes can produce graded, history-dependent synaptic output; they do not by themselves establish a phase-wave code or consciousness.*

**Synaptic unreliability** describes the probabilistic nature of chemical synaptic transmission. It should not be reduced to a universal one-site, one-vesicle relay model. Depending on the synapse, pathway, preparation, extracellular conditions, and recent activity, one presynaptic action potential can produce release failure, fusion of one vesicle, or fusion of multiple vesicles.

## The Simplified Quantal Model

A common starting model describes mean synaptic strength using the number of functional release sites (`N`), vesicle-release probability (`Pr`), and quantal postsynaptic response (`q`). This model remains useful, but a one-site or strictly univesicular implementation is not a universal property of cortical synapses.

The postsynaptic response is also not a linear vesicle counter. Receptor occupancy, saturation, desensitization, glutamate spillover, cleft geometry, and quantal variability can all change the response produced by a given number of fusion events.

## Preparation-Specific MVR Evidence

Holler and colleagues combined paired recordings from connected layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons in acute mouse somatosensory-cortex slices with correlated light and electron microscopy. Their quantal analysis estimated **at least 2.7 neurotransmitter-release sites per synapse on average** and supported [[multi-vesicular-release|multivesicular release]] in that preparation.

This result challenges simple one-site or strictly univesicular estimates of neocortical synaptic capacity. It does not show that every cortical pathway uses the same release mode, nor does it eliminate stochastic release failure.

## Presynaptic Waveform Control

The [[action-potential-waveform|presynaptic action-potential waveform]] (`APsyn`) is one control variable. In primary cultured mouse hippocampal neurons, Cho and colleagues found that rapid inactivation of presynaptic Kv1 channels mediated by the auxiliary Kvbeta1 subunit supports activity-dependent APsyn broadening and short-term facilitation. Depleting Kvbeta1 blocked APsyn broadening during high-frequency stimulation and eliminated facilitation without changing the initial vesicle-release probability.

This is a preparation-specific channel mechanism upstream of exocytosis, not a universal fixed mapping from potassium concentration to vesicle number.

## What the Evidence Establishes

- Chemical synaptic output can remain stochastic while also being graded and history-dependent.
- Univesicular and multivesicular release modes vary across synapses, pathways, preparations, and conditions.
- Presynaptic ion-channel state and recent activity can alter release dynamics.
- MVR expands the possible local outcomes relative to a one-site Bernoulli model, but it does not imply that a conventional bouton freely selects the *type* of neurotransmitter released on each spike.

## SAN Interpretation: An Open Network Test

SAN treats release statistics as a candidate contributor to phase-conditioned ensemble dynamics. That interpretation remains a hypothesis. A relevant same-preparation experiment would jointly measure APsyn, presynaptic calcium, glutamate release, postsynaptic currents, spiking, and network phase before and after a Kvbeta1 or release-site perturbation.

The prediction is that a controlled presynaptic change will produce a defined change in ensemble timing statistics. A dissociation between the synaptic-output change and the predicted network-phase change would falsify that specific bridge. MVR and APsyn plasticity alone do not demonstrate a phase-wave code, [[napot|NAPOT]], or consciousness.
