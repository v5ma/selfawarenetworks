# Neuroaffective Self-Registration

**Neuroaffective self-registration** is SAN's proposal that a distributed brain-body system can
register part of its own emotional transition because the transition changes the system and some of
those changes return through neural, autonomic, interoceptive, and behavioral pathways.

It does not require a second observer watching the body from inside the brain.

## Plain-language mechanism

```text
event, memory, thought, or appraisal
-> distributed neural and bodily transition
-> electrophysiological, autonomic, and behavioral change
-> intrinsic and recurrent reception of part of that change
-> receiver-relative difference against maintained context
-> felt state, valuation, regulation, choice, or action
-> returned consequences and learning
```

The public research paper
[How a Brain-Body Network Detects Its Own Emotional State Changes](https://zenodo.org/records/21693970)
develops this mechanism in full. The related
[[multimodal-neuroaffective-xr-closed-loop|multimodal closed-loop page]] explains how a scientific
instrument can estimate selected components without pretending to read a complete emotion or mind.

## Established scientific ground

Emotionally relevant events can alter distributed neural activity, cardiac and respiratory state,
skin conductance, pupil behavior, muscle activity, movement, and report. Interoceptive processing
can make some bodily changes available to discrimination and regulation. Brain-body influence is
bidirectional, heterogeneous, and dependent on context.

These observations do not imply that one signal is an emotion, that every physiological change is
felt, or that a classifier has measured consciousness. For example, stimulation can produce bodily
responses without a corresponding conscious emotional report. Physiology, subjective experience,
and behavior must therefore be measured separately before their relationship is inferred.

## SAN-specific interpretation

SAN joins those observations into a distributed self-registration hypothesis:

- ongoing tonic activity supplies part of the maintained receiver context;
- phasic changes alter neural and bodily state;
- recurrent and interoceptive pathways expose parts of the alteration to later processing;
- the network itself is changed by and responsive to the transition; and
- valuation and action feed consequences back into the next cycle.

In this account, the "self" is not a localized reader. It is the changing topology of participating
receivers, transmitters, body systems, learned models, and action routes. This connects
[[tonic-phasic-canvas|Tonic Canvas and Phasic Ink]],
[[phase-wave-differentials|Phase-Wave Differentials]], and
[[agency-valuation-action-feedback|Agency, Valuation, Action, and Feedback]].

## What would make the proposal distinctive

To outperform an ordinary arousal or interoception model, neuroaffective self-registration should
predict later report, regulation, route selection, or action from the **pattern of returned
brain-body change** better than any single sensor, firing-rate summary, stimulus label, or generic
autonomic index. The prediction should generalize within a person across repeated trials and state
which parts are person-specific.

## Falsification boundary

The stronger SAN mechanism would be weakened if its proposed recurrent and receiver-relative
variables add no predictive information after controlling for stimulus, report, behavior,
physiology, and standard neural features. A successful state classifier alone does not confirm the
mechanism.
