## Position

Entanglement phase lock specifies what "entangled" *means* in the SAN/QGTCD ontology: two particles whose internal phase oscillations remain in a fixed relation against the shared reference oscillator are *phase-locked*, and that lock is what produces the correlations a Bell-type experiment registers. The lock is not action-at-a-distance; it is the persistence of a relation each particle separately maintains against the background. In [[supertimeposition]] this is consistent with the picture in which apparent quantum randomness is an artifact of undersampled deterministic [[wave-dissipation-universality|wave dissipation]] — the lock survives because the underlying [[quantum-supertimeposition|deterministic phase-attractor cycles]] of the two particles are running against the same time crystal.

The lock is *modulable* and *breakable*. [[gravitational-modulation-of-entanglement]] makes gravity a tunable parameter that shifts the time-crystal tick rate, which is why entanglement is sensitive to gravitational potential. Decoherence in this reading is *unlocking* — the loss of a stable phase relation as one or both particles couple to environmental oscillators that are *not* sharing the original reference. [[wave-interference-attractive-repulsive-forces|Constructive and destructive interference]] then become the local manifestations of the lock, and the breaking of the lock is the moment at which a measurement or a noisy environment substitutes a different reference. The framework also handles non-locality: the lock is a *relation* over a global reference, so its presence at a distance is not a signal but a shared phase relationship — exactly the SAN reading of how [[oscillatory-neural-coupling|oscillatory neural coupling]] would generalize to a particle-scale substrate.
