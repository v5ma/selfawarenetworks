## Post-SAN

The mainstream cognate is Frank Wilczek's *time crystal* proposal (2012, "Quantum Time Crystals", Phys. Rev. Lett. 109, 160401) and the experimental discrete-time-crystal work that followed in driven many-body systems (Choi et al., Zhang et al., 2017). Where the Wilczek line treats time crystals as a *condensed-matter* phase — a system that breaks time-translation symmetry while remaining in a stable ground state — SAN/QGTCD takes the same word in a much wider sense: every oscillating mass is treated as a time crystal whose centre compresses temporal interval and whose surroundings dilate it, with the dilation gradient identified with what general relativity calls a gravitational field. The testable difference is structural — SAN/QGTCD predicts that the time-dilation gradient around a localised [[oscillator]] should follow the [[oscillator]]'s frequency profile in a measurable way that pure general relativity does not predict, while Wilczek-style time crystals make no claim about gravity at all. The framing also gives SAN a particular relationship to the [[oscillating-spacetime-theory]], [[fractal-oscillation-cosmos]], and [[black-hole-temporal-well]] cosmology pages, which all read directly off the same primitive.
