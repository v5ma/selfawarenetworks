Memory-prediction is the foundational claim of early SAN theory: **all intelligence is memory-prediction**. Cells, hearts, and brains are all intelligent because they all perform memory-prediction — they store patterns and bet on what comes next.

## Core claim (2012 formulation)

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s 2012 [[gh-formulation|formulation]]: "intelligence is memory-prediction, a betting memory… all life is intelligent, and all intelligence is memory-prediction." A memory is not merely a recording but a standing prediction — the organism bets that a remembered pattern will recur and prepares accordingly.

This makes the brain a "betting machine" — a phrase [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] also applies to any system that stores patterns and wagers on their future occurrence. Cells, organs, and social minds all satisfy this definition.

## Extension beyond the brain

The same memory-prediction logic applies at every biological scale: neurons store patterns via [[Synaptic-Weight-Update]] (protein synthesis), cardiac neurons store autonomic responses, and intercellular signaling embeds cellular memory. Heart transplant anecdotes prompted early exploration of extra-cranial memory storage, though [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] noted this evidence as "on shaky ground" — the more parsimonious explanation invokes drug effects and severed vagal nerves.

## Identity and self as prediction

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] extends the theory to personal identity: "I am a memory-prediction." The [[gh-a0206z|self]] is not a fixed entity but a running predictive model that persists in other minds after the biological substrate ends. Information loss at death is real but bounded — the prediction that others hold of you continues to develop.
