# Are Grid-Like Representations a Component of All Perception and Cognition?

Grid cells are best established in the hippocampal-entorhinal navigation system, where population activity relates an animal's position and movement to a spatial map. The broader question is whether grid-like codes also help organize sensory features, abstract concepts, and action-relevant state spaces elsewhere in the brain. A 2022 *Frontiers in Neural Circuits* Hypothesis and Theory article reviews evidence for that possibility and proposes experiments; it does not establish that every perceptual or cognitive process uses a universal grid.

## What the 2022 paper argues

[Chen, Zhang, Long, and Zhang's 2022 article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2022.924016/full) begins with classical grid cells in medial entorhinal cortex and then examines reports of grid-like responses beyond that system. The evidence levels differ. Peer-reviewed single-unit work reported spatially tuned cells in rat primary somatosensory cortex (S1), whereas the V2 result cited in the review was preliminary preprint evidence. Human studies include intracranial recordings during virtual navigation and fMRI analyses of sixfold modulation in learned spatial or conceptual task spaces; an fMRI modulation pattern is not a direct recording of grid cells.

The paper proposes that these findings may reflect a more general coordinate mechanism. On that account, perception and cognition can be treated as movement through structured spaces:

- physical navigation moves through locations;
- touch moves across an object's surface;
- vision relates features to objects and viewpoints;
- audition relates sound sources to head-centered and world-centered coordinates;
- conceptual reasoning moves through a learned relational or feature space.

The authors discuss recurrent computation, attractor dynamics, predictive representations, reinforcement learning, and successor representations as candidate mechanisms. Their conclusion is deliberately prospective: generalized grid codes are a hypothesis that requires additional recordings, causal manipulation, and computational testing.

## Why Micah connected it to SAN

Micah Blumberg's `a0645z` note linked this paper to an earlier SAN problem: how activity distributed across cortical and subcortical pathways can maintain a volumetric, action-relevant model of body and world.

The note pictured neural processing as intersecting flows through sensory pathways, thalamus, neocortex, hippocampal-entorhinal circuits, and recurrent cortical projections. It then asked whether a local neural population could register its changing relation to nearby populations along three spatial axes while participating in a larger, continuously updated network state. The original language described six directions - up, down, left, right, forward, and back - as an intuitive coordinate metaphor, not as a claim that every cortical column has six literal anatomical channels.

This is where grid-like representations become relevant to SAN. A grid or reference-frame code could provide structured coordinates for relationships among features, objects, body position, remembered states, and possible actions. SAN then asks how those coordinate-bearing signals might be received, transformed, synchronized, and redistributed by neural arrays.

## Relationship to NAPOT and NDCA

Within the proposed SAN architecture, the connection can be expressed in three stages:

1. **Structured sampling:** sensory and motor systems generate partial, viewpoint-dependent samples of body and environment.
2. **Coordinate integration:** grid-like, place-like, head-direction, and other reference-frame signals may help relate those samples across position, direction, object structure, and task state.
3. **Continuous reconstruction:** [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] and [[ndca-nonlinear-differential-continuous-approximation|NDCA]] propose that recurrent neural populations combine and redistribute those partial relations to maintain a stable but revisable working model.

The Frontiers paper supports the plausibility of generalized coordinate-like coding. It does not test NAPOT, NDCA, phase-wave differentials, or SAN's tonic-field interpretation. Those are additional SAN hypotheses.

## Oscillations and distributed state

The source note also considered electrical activity, magnetic fields, mechanical waves, blood flow, pressure, and temperature. These should not be collapsed into one signal channel. Neurons and glia respond to electrical, chemical, mechanical, metabolic, and thermal conditions through different mechanisms and at different spatial and temporal scales.

Likewise, TMS-EEG experiments show that a controlled cortical perturbation can produce measurable responses that propagate through connected networks. They do not show electromagnetic activity freely ricocheting through the brain like water in a bowl.

The water-flow image is therefore an analogy, not a measured propagation law. It nevertheless points to a source-faithful SAN hypothesis that should not be discarded: spatially distributed electrical, chemical, mechanical, metabolic, vascular, and thermal changes may form part of the physical state sampled by cells and populations. SAN proposes that receiver-relative phase and magnitude changes within that constrained, anatomically embedded state contribute to volumetric neural rendering and feeling.

Existing work shows that endogenous extracellular electric fields can feed back locally on cortical activity and that physiologically weak fields can affect nearby neurons. This establishes local causal plausibility for one component of the SAN proposal. It does not establish the global range, relative contribution of each physical modality, content mapping, decoder mechanism, or necessity or sufficiency for consciousness. Those remain discriminating experimental questions.

Other component studies establish similarly narrow links for metabolic and thermal state. Lactate reversibly reduced spontaneous calcium-spiking frequency in cultured primary mouse cortical neurons through a pertussis-toxin-sensitive pathway. Focal cortical temperature manipulation changed frontal-cortical evoked potentials in anesthetized rats, with pharmacological evidence that inhibitory contribution shaped the measured relation. These results show that metabolic and thermal variables can alter neural operation in those preparations; they do not show that either variable carries semantic content, forms one unified field code, implements volumetric rendering, or explains consciousness.

The source note also proposed an artificial-neurology analogue. Instead of assuming that a small computer must reproduce the brain's material composition, Micah asked whether a spatial simulation could map changing artificial-neuron states to positions in a point cloud or fluid-like model and allow the artificial network to use those relations. The source presented WebXR and game engines as candidate engineering media for that experiment. This is a testable implementation direction, not evidence that present simulations reproduce emotion or consciousness.

## What is established and what remains open

**Established or directly reported:**

- medial entorhinal grid cells participate in spatial navigation and path integration;
- place, head-direction, boundary, and grid-like signals contribute to spatial and reference-frame coding;
- grid-like responses have been reported outside classical entorhinal tasks and regions;
- recurrent networks and attractor-like dynamics can generate structured spatial codes;
- perception and action use multiple egocentric and allocentric coordinate systems.

**Still unresolved:**

- whether grid-like coding is universal across perception and cognition;
- whether reported fMRI hexagonal modulation and single-cell grid firing reflect one common mechanism;
- how sensory-cortical grid-like responses are generated and what causal role they play;
- whether SAN's oscillatory and field-level mechanisms explain how coordinate relations become one distributed observer-action;
- whether an artificial spatial simulation reproduces any biologically relevant component of emotion or conscious perception.
