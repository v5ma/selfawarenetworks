**Dark Time Theory** (DT theory) is [[micah-blumberg|Micah Blumberg]]'s cosmological proposal that the observed deviations in galactic redshifts arise from local variations in **time density** rather than from accelerating spatial expansion (dark energy) or a global cosmic time slowdown.

## Core claim

The observed Hubble tension and anomalous redshifts do not require the universe to be accelerating, nor do they require time to be universally slowing or speeding across cosmic history. Instead, different regions of space-time have different **dark time** densities. Light traversing regions of varying time density experiences wavelength stretching without any change in the global expansion rate or global flow of time.

## Contrast with competing theories

| Theory | Redshift explanation | Time claim |
|---|---|---|
| ΛCDM (standard) | Accelerating spatial expansion driven by dark energy | Time was slower in the past |
| Cosmic Time Slowdown (Periodic Physics) | Variant-rate time: universal slowdown of time | Time faster in past, slower now |
| **Dark Time Theory** | Local time-density variations | No global time change required |

Dark Time theory shares with [[periodic-physics]] the idea that time itself is a dynamic participant in cosmic evolution, but drops the requirement for a universal monotonic trend. Time density varies locally, not globally.

## Hubble tension

DT theory offers a natural explanation for the [[hubble-tension]]: local measurements of the Hubble constant (supernovae) probe a region with one time density, while CMB-derived values reflect early-universe conditions with different time density. The two measurements disagree because they sample different temporal environments, not because any parameter in ΛCDM is wrong.

## Replacing dark matter

The same time-density gradient mechanism that replaces dark energy also addresses dark matter anomalies. Galaxy rotation curves appear to require additional unseen mass — but if time density is higher within a galaxy (closer to its aggregate mass), the apparent velocities of stars in the outer regions are influenced by the gradient, not hidden mass. Similarly, gravitational lensing that overshoots visible mass predictions could reflect time-density compression near mass concentrations rather than an invisible matter halo.

DT theory thus unifies the dark-energy and dark-matter problems under a single variable: the local density of time near mass, which is already demonstrated to vary by general relativity (gravitational time dilation).

## Naming: Dark Time vs QGTCD

"Dark Time Theory" was chosen as the public-facing name over the technical "Quantum Gradient Time Crystal Dilation Theory" ([[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]]) because it immediately parallels the familiar terms "dark energy" and "dark matter" — signaling that what was called dark energy/matter may actually be dark *time*. [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] remains the precise name for academic contexts.

## Quantum wave-interference mechanism

At the micro scale ([[gh-raynote09|raynote09]]), DT theory proposes that the local time-density variations arise from **wave interference**:

- **Constructive interference** → increased time density → attractive force (gravity).
- **Destructive interference** → decreased time density → repulsive force (electromagnetism, explosive energy release).

Gravity at the macro scale is recast as **statistically aligned electromagnetic fields** across chaotic particle distributions — greater alignment strengthens observed gravitational effect; disorder dilutes coherent EM into the weaker long-range force we measure as gravity.

This quantum mechanism complements the cosmological observations above by providing an underlying physical process for why time density varies near mass concentrations.
