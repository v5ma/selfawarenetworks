# Potassium

# The Potassium K+ Channel might be the most important part of the human brain in the context to what it adds.
I think of the original artificial neuron, the perceptron, and the idea of the all-or-nothing [[action-potential|action potential]], which is sort of like summing up everything the neuron could sense as an accumulation of charges that would result in an all-or-nothing spike, essentially an on-or-off switch.

That model of a neuron, which might be similar to the Hodgkin-Huxley model of a neuron, has sodium channels as the star of the show. The neuron fills up with charge, it spikes, and then [[neurotransmitters|neurotransmitters]] flow through the synaptic cleft to the next neuron. In that model, the [[potassium-channel-modulation|potassium channel]] is not more important than the sodium channel; it is just another pump helping to lower the neuron's positive charge so the neuron can be reset again.

We know now, and I am presenting more of this research today, that the all-or-nothing [[action-potential|action potential]] is not an all-or-nothing event in the computational sense. The reason is that [[potassium-channel-modulation|potassium channels]] are doing more than repolarization and hyperpolarization. Potassium channels are manipulated by metabotropic receptors that affect [[camp-neural-phase-modulation|cAMP cascades]], and this manipulation can change the [[action-potential-duration|duration]] of the [[action-potential|action potential]], changing the magnitude of the [[action-potential-waveform|AP wave shape]], causing [[gh-a0138zcalcium|calcium channels]] to open longer, and releasing more exosomes or [[synaptic-vesicle-release|synaptic vesicles]]. There is a lot more happening with potassium channels, and it affects the [[phasic-signals|signals]] that the cell is sending to other cells.

In other words, [[potassium-channel-modulation|potassium channels]] are key to [[computational-phase-synchronization|phase variations]] in signal transmission between nerve cells, and phase variations are how the rendering of the mind is tomographically computed and perceived by [[neural-oscillations|oscillating networks]] of neurons. Each neuron is computing and perceiving a small part of the conscious mind, a [[neural-tomography|volumetric tomographic pattern]], that is being computationally rendered by the brain or, alternatively, by an artificial neural network.

# "Cardiac Delayed Rectifier Potassium Channels in Health and Disease"
Paraphrasing the article: delayed rectifier [[potassium-channel-modulation|potassium channels]] conduct outward potassium currents during the plateau phase of [[action-potential|action potentials]] and play pivotal roles in repolarization.

"During the plateau phase, calcium entry via L-type [[gh-a0138zcalcium|calcium channels]] triggers contraction. Counterbalancing the calcium influx, [[potassium-channel-modulation|potassium ions]] pass through the membrane in the outward direction. The plateau phase is a balance of inward and outward currents."

Paraphrasing the article: the plateau phase is after the initial depolarization of the [[action-potential|action potential]]. It is a prolongation of the duration of the depolarization phase before the repolarization phase begins.

Paraphrasing: sodium channels terminate quickly, initiating the repolarization phase, but slower [[potassium-channel-modulation|potassium channel]] currents persist during the plateau phase, the repolarization phase, and the hyperpolarization phase.
