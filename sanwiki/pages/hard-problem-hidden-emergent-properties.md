The **hard problem of hidden emergent properties** is [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s generalization of Chalmers' hard problem of consciousness. The claim: consciousness may be merely *one* interesting emergent property that arises from particular configurations of matter; others may exist — in stars, rivers, or arbitrary matter — yet remain entirely hidden from any external observer or measurement.

## The Argument

Standard hard-problem framing asks why physical processes give rise to *this one* non-physical phenomenon: subjective experience. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s generalization asks: why assume consciousness is the *only* such phenomenon? The assumption that complex non-brain systems have no hidden emergent properties is "nothing more than a human prejudice, a bald assertion with no evidence" ([[gh-a0649z|a0649z]], 2013).

Implication: the universe may be richer than both physics and consciousness-centric views assume. Physics measures state transitions; consciousness-centric futurism asks only "what varieties of consciousness are possible?" — both framings may miss large swaths of what can exist.

## Anti-Panpsychism Stance

This is NOT a panpsychist claim. [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] explicitly rejects Penrose–Hameroff quantum consciousness and pan-psychist views that consciousness is distributed everywhere. Consciousness is held to be "a very narrow phenomenon that depends upon a particular type of classical Turing machine like the brain." The hidden emergent property conjecture is *compatible* with consciousness being rare and substrate-specific.

## Relation to SAN

SAN explains consciousness via [[napot]] and [[fractal-oscillation-cosmos]] — oscillatory phase tomography in biological neural arrays. The emergent-properties framework situates consciousness as one output of a broader oscillatory pattern-formation process, not a unique cosmological exception. [[consciousness-energy-wave-differences]] are the specific phenomenal instance of a more general class of pattern-emergence.

Penrose's "three worlds, three mysteries" (Mind, Matter, Math) is cited as a useful scaffold; SAN seeks the physical mechanism that bridges the three rather than leaving the relationship unexplained.
