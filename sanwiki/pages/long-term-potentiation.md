# Long Term Potentiation

**Long Term Potentiation** (LTP) is a persistent, activity-dependent increase in synaptic efficacy and a major experimental model for studying cellular mechanisms of learning and memory. It can be induced by several stimulation or pairing protocols; its expression, duration, protein-synthesis dependence, and structural correlates vary by pathway and preparation. Within the [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] graph LTP sits as a slower [[synaptic-plasticity|synaptic-plasticity]] substrate beneath the proposed [[napot-neural-array-phase-tomography|NAPOT]] / [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave-differential]] readout machinery.

> **Medical-status note:** LTP is not one universal pathway, is not automatically permanent memory, and is not NAPOT itself. The historical SAN source language below is theory provenance.

## Medical evidence boundary

- **Established phenomenon:** Repetitive or paired activity can produce a persistent increase in synaptic efficacy. The original Bliss-Loemo preparation measured perforant-path potentiation in rabbit dentate gyrus; later LTP forms differ across pathways and species.
- **Bounded CA1 mechanism:** In common Schaffer-collateral CA1 preparations, postsynaptic depolarization relieves the NMDAR Mg2+ block, Ca2+ enters the spine, and CaMKII, Ras-pathway signaling, AMPAR trafficking, and actin remodeling contribute to induction and expression.
- **Late consolidation:** Protein synthesis, synaptic tagging/capture, local dendritic translation, gene expression, and postsynaptic remodeling contribute in protocol-dependent combinations. Functional potentiation and visible spine enlargement are related but not identical.
- **SAN interpretation:** Calling LTP an "information write" or a slow substrate is a systems interpretation. A timing or phase variable is informative only if it improves held-out plasticity prediction beyond spike count, firing rate, spectral power, and common input.

Primary evidence:

- [Bliss and Loemo (1973), long-lasting potentiation in rabbit dentate gyrus](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1973.sp010273)
- [Lee et al. (2009), CaMKII activation in single spines during LTP](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07842)
- [Park et al. (2006), recycling-endosome exocytosis and spine growth](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2006.09.040)
- [Bosch et al. (2014), structural and molecular spine remodeling during LTP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.03.021)
- [Govindarajan et al. (2011), branch-local protein-synthesis-dependent LTP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.008)

## Post-SAN

The SAN treatment of LTP makes three commitments that go beyond the standard plasticity literature. First, LTP is read as the *substrate* of the [[ltp-structural-memory-encoding|structural-memory-encoding]] story, in which spine remodelling is what gives a memory its days-to-years persistence — closer to the Yang/Pak/Lan two-photon spine-imaging line and the Frankland/Josselyn [[engram]]-cell line than to the older "weight change" formulations. Second, the [[synaptic-computation-ltp|synaptic-computation-LTP]] page treats an LTP event as a *bit-level* event — consistent with the [[coincidence-as-a-bit|coincidence-as-a-bit]] family — meaning the SAN reading frames each LTP event as an information-write into the synapse, not just as an analogue weight increment; the calcium-based-plasticity reference ("A calcium-based plasticity model predicts long term potentiation and depression in the neo [[cortex]]", `dl-oct6-2025--part_1`, line 1, paper-[[gh-resnote04|micah]], 2023) is the model [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] uses to bridge [[gh-a0138zcalcium|calcium dynamics]] to the SAN bit-event reading.
