A mathematical framework applying category theory to model cellular and neural state transformations. Markings in an extended Petri net become the categories; transitions between markings are the morphisms. The framework generalizes beyond classical Petri nets by allowing non-linear, multidirectional transformations across organizational scales.

## Core claim

Different biological pathways (protein synthesis, receptor growth, [[atp]] production, inter-cellular signaling) that converge on identical outcomes exhibit **isomorphism** — they are the same morphism expressed through distinct substrate pathways. This lets the model abstract away mechanism and focus on state-change structure.

The author critiques classical Petri nets for tying functions to *place movements* unnecessarily. Natural transforms (extended to multidimensional variants) are proposed instead, capturing the full space of non-linear state transitions in biological systems.

## Neural instantiation

The framework applies specifically to:
- **[[apical-dendrites|Apical dendrites]] of L6 pyramidal cells** — where thalamic-cortical feedback modulates soma firing
- **Burst activation** — rapid sequential firing treated as a distinct morphism category
- **Nuclear deformation** triggering gene activation — a morphism that crosses from electrical to biochemical domains

This maps onto [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography]] (NAPOT): each oscillatory cycle is a morphism that transforms the array's representational state.

## Relationship to SAN

Within [[self-aware-networks]] theory, this framework underpins the claim that consciousness is substrate-independent: if the morphism structure is preserved, the substrate (biological vs. silicon) does not change the functional category.
