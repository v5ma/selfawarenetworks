A 2015 *Nature Photonics* paper provides empirical grounding for a core claim in [[qgtcd]] (Quantum Gradient Time Crystal Dilation): that the tick rate of a crystal can be influenced by gravitational time dilation effects analogous to those in special relativity.

The study probes a nuclear crystal with X-rays and shows that both gravitational redshift and special-relativistic time dilation produce a time-dependent deflection of X-rays through the crystal. A rotating crystal subjects interacting X-rays to inhomogeneous clock tick rates within it — demonstrating millimetre-scale sensitivity to gravitational and relativistic effects.

## Relevance to QGTCD

[[qgtcd]] proposes that quantum processes at neural (and cosmic) scales are shaped by local gravitational gradients modulating the effective "tick rate" of quantum oscillators. This 2015 result shows that:

1. Crystal oscillators (time crystals in the broad sense) are not isolated from gravity — their internal timing is gravity-sensitive.
2. X-ray superradiance can transduce these relativistic timing differences into measurable deflection — a compact, table-top detection scheme.

This bridges from abstract [[quantum-time-crystal]] theory to observable physics, supporting the [[gh-qgtcd|QGTCD]] framework's claim that time is not uniform across spatial gradients, even at small scales.
