This plate renders a SAN hypothesis: an action potential can contribute to a receiver-relative
change whose consequence depends on timing, waveform, pathway, receiver state, and concurrent
population activity. Coordinated changes may then contribute to oscillatory synchronization,
phase-wave differentials, neural-array propagation, and proposed NAPOT operations.

**Phase Change**, in [[self-aware-networks]], is not a replacement for spikes, spike rate, or spike
timing. It is a proposed description of the difference a contextualized neural event makes to
specified receivers and later population states.

## Source genealogy

SAN sources use "phase change" at several levels: a burst process, a changed timing relation, an
event reaching learned receivers, a population-pattern update, and later Neural Rendering and

PWD, NAPOT, and qualia. Those are later theory layers, not consequences established by the
earlier cellular operation alone.

## Position in SAN

Phase change has four separable roles:

1. a measurable change in event timing or relation to a declared reference;
2. a receiver-relative change in synaptic or population consequence;
3. a candidate typed component inside PWD;
4. a later SAN hypothesis about distributed rendering, qualia, and consciousness.

The first role is not automatically the second, and neither establishes the third or fourth.
Every scale transition requires a declared route, measurement, receiver, and causal test.

## Post-SAN

External neuroscience supports phase coding, phase precession, state-dependent spike timing,
oscillatory coordination, and waveform-sensitive release in named preparations. Those findings
validate components, not the joined SAN architecture.

SAN adds three hypotheses: **(a)** neural events should be modeled as typed transformations rather
than context-free scalar activations; **(b)** practical meaning is receiver-relative and must be
demonstrated by learned use and downstream consequence; and **(c)** distributed phase-change
patterns may contribute to Neural Rendering and qualia. The third claim carries the largest
burden. Readout of a phase variable is not sufficient to establish a reconstructed percept.
Competing models must include rate, timing, waveform, connectivity, receiver state, movement, and
history.
