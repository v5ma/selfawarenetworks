# Telescoping Network-Neighborhood Reinstatement

**Telescoping network-neighborhood reinstatement** is a SAN/NAPOT hypothesis about how a dendritic arbor can store a learned three-dimensional image or projection of a prior functional network neighborhood, then help regenerate that larger state. The proposed image is encoded in the spatially distributed organization of synapses, branches, receptor states, local nonlinearities, and their learned input relationships. It is not a flat bitmap or a separate picture watched by an inner observer. When later input activates the learned dendritic configuration, the neuron and its recurrently connected populations can expand that local projection into an active, distributed approximation of the earlier three-dimensional scene or network state.

## The contraction and expansion

The proposed motif has two directions:

1. **Contraction during learning:** a distributed event changes selected synapses, dendritic compartments, intrinsic excitability, and ensemble relationships. In the stronger SAN/NAPOT proposal, their three-dimensional arrangement stores a compressed spatial-relational projection of the event and of the functional network neighborhood that produced it.
2. **Expansion during retrieval:** a partial cue engages some of those changed dispositions. Their outputs propagate through recurrent, hippocampal, cortical, thalamic, and embodied pathways, producing pattern completion and context-sensitive reinstatement.
3. **Revision:** the reinstated state meets the present tonic context, sensory input, body state, and action consequences. Retrieval can therefore reshape the trace rather than replaying an invariant recording.

The “timestamp” in this account need not be a clock value stored on a dendrite. Temporal context can be carried by ensemble overlap, changing excitability, sequential state, neuromodulation, oscillatory phase relationships, and the connectivity produced during the event.

## Micro, meso, and macro scales

- **Micro:** nonlinear dendritic integration detects a learned conjunction and changes the probability, timing, or pattern of neuronal output.
- **Meso:** recurrent local and hippocampal circuitry uses partial activity to separate or complete population patterns.
- **Macro:** distributed cortical and subcortical systems reinstate sensory, relational, affective, bodily, and action-relevant components. No single cell or region is the inner viewer.

The remembered state is therefore **regenerated by the network**. A neuron can be an addressable participant whose changed receiver and transmitter properties help call a larger configuration back into activity.

## The Inversphere analogy

Sphere inversion maps a point at radius (r) to a point at reciprocal radius (R^2/r). It is conformal: local angles are preserved while scale changes. The visual effect can fold an environment around a sphere without preserving ordinary distance.

This supplies a useful design analogy for SAN:

- a large external or network neighborhood can be represented through a much smaller local coordinate system;
- relational organization may matter more than absolute scale;
- a compact state can unfold into a larger relational configuration when interpreted by the surrounding system.

The analogy is not biological evidence. The brain is not known to implement a literal Möbius transformation, sphere inversion, or Gaussian-splat renderer. Retinal projection, neural coding, memory reinstatement, and motor updating are learned biological transformations with losses, ambiguities, and pathway-specific constraints.

The related lucid image of a 3D scene folding into a compact representation and unfolding again expresses the proposed **analysis-and-synthesis mechanism**: NAPOT contracts distributed spatial relations into learned dendritic projections, while recurrent inference and action expand selected projections into a usable three-dimensional model. This is a substantive SAN hypothesis, not merely a decorative metaphor. The reconstructed model can be partial, viewpoint-bound, and context-sensitive rather than a metric duplicate of the original scene.

## Excitatory and inhibitory expansion

A reactivated local disposition can affect more than the neuron that carries it. An excitatory pyramidal neuron can distribute action potentials to multiple targets. Those targets can include other principal neurons and inhibitory interneurons, so the resulting cascade can contain direct excitation, disynaptic feedforward inhibition, recurrent inhibition, and later disinhibition. An inhibitory neuron can directly suppress its postsynaptic targets. The expanding state is therefore a patterned redistribution of excitation and inhibition, not a uniform wave of activation.

This gives the telescoping proposal causal force. A dendritic conjunction that changes one neuron's output may bias which attractor, action policy, or interpretation the larger network can reach. Experiments show that stimulation of a single somatosensory cortical neuron can influence detection behavior under controlled conditions, demonstrating that single-cell effects need not always average to zero. That finding does not imply that any one dendrite contains a decision or that one neuron ordinarily controls behavior. Amplification depends on cell type, projection pattern, recurrent gain, inhibition, neuromodulation, current tonic state, and downstream convergence.

In the SAN model, a **candidate dendritic 3D memory** is a learned local receiver structure whose branch-specific organization stores a spatial-relational projection and can seed a branching excitatory-inhibitory transition. The dendrite supplies a structured component of the image; the surrounding network interprets, combines, and amplifies that component into the active reconstruction that can reshape perception, available choices, and action.
## Race-to-inhibition within an oscillating array

One candidate selection mechanism is a shared oscillatory opportunity window. Several neurons approach threshold during the same cycle. A more strongly prepared neuron fires first, excites fast-spiking interneurons, and recruits feedback inhibition before weaker competitors fire. As inhibition decays, the circuit can open another opportunity window. In that bounded circuit, relative spike timing converts small differences in dendritic readiness into sparse population selection.

This is close to a winner-take-most process, not necessarily one permanent winner. Recurrent excitation can preserve a co-active group; surround inhibition can suppress alternatives; disinhibition can release another group; and inhibitory timing can synchronize cells rather than only silence them. Gamma-frequency PING and ING mechanisms are examples of excitation-inhibition interactions that create repeating spike windows, but SAN should not identify every oscillating neural array with one gamma mechanism.

For telescoping reinstatement, the implication is that a previously modified dendritic branch may give its neuron a small timing advantage. If that neuron fires early enough to recruit the local inhibitory circuit, the learned difference can alter which ensemble wins the cycle and therefore which larger network configuration, interpretation, or action becomes reachable. This is a testable candidate mechanism for turning local changed readiness into network-scale choice.
## Established evidence and open SAN claim

Established work supports nonlinear dendritic integration, branch-specific plasticity, pattern separation and completion, causal engram participation, and distributed memory reinstatement. It does not yet establish the stronger SAN/NAPOT claim that dendritic organization stores a decodable three-dimensional image of a prior functional neighborhood, that one dendritic activation can initiate its reconstruction, or that phase-wave differentials are the specific expansion signal.

The SAN claim becomes experimentally useful if it predicts that:

1. a learned dendritic or cellular state identifies a reproducible network neighborhood more accurately than firing rate alone;
2. selective reactivation produces ordered expansion from local cellular state to meso- and macro-scale reinstatement;
3. disrupting the proposed output timing or phase relation impairs reinstatement while preserving some local excitability;
4. restoring that relation rescues both downstream configuration and behavior;
5. the regenerated pattern is conditioned by current tonic context rather than being an exact replay.
