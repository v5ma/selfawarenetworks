**Predictive Processing** is the mainstream neurocomputational research tradition — closely tied to [[predictive-coding|predictive coding]], the [[free-energy-principle|free energy principle]], and active inference — that treats the brain as a hierarchical inference engine which constantly generates and updates a model of its environment by minimizing prediction error. Within [[self-aware-networks|Self-Aware Networks]] this tradition is explicitly engaged as a *cousin theory*: SAN incorporates its core commitments while reformulating the substrate in terms of [[cortical-traveling-waves]], [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|dominant-phase-wave-differential]] dynamics, and [[phase-differential-rendering]] across [[neural-oscillations|nested neural oscillations]].

## Position

SAN's position is that predictive processing identifies the right *function* but stops short of the right *substrate*. The `a0324z` comparison states the relationship plainly: "[[gh-a0001z|Self Aware Networks]] theory is similar to Predictive Processing but also different from Predictive Processing in we can argue that inferences are rendered to internally distributed neural arrays and are made conscious through the … persistent tempo-spatial rendering of phase wave differential patterns is a key part of what makes a predictive processing machine a phenomenally conscious machine" (`a0324z--md`, line 72, [[gh-a0324z|on github]]). The substrate-level claim is that the [[cortical-traveling-waves|cortical traveling-wave]] machinery is what *implements* prediction in flesh: chapter 07 of SAN reads alpha/beta-band feedback as the carrier of predictions and "aligns cortical layers and oscillatory rhythms with predictive processing" (`07san--md`, line 256, [[gh-07san|on github]]), and the [[gh-napotconcepts|NAPOTConcepts]] gathering of citations records that "because traveling waves (TWs) propagate across the [[cortex]] shaping neural excitability, they can carry information to serve predictive processing" (`NAPOTConcepts--md`, line 79, [[gh-napotconcepts|on github]]; restated at `NAPOTConcepts--md`, line 133, [[gh-napotconcepts|on github]]). Chapter 04 ties the same claim to clinical and developmental phenomena: "disorders involving disrupted predictive processing (e.g., schizophrenia, autism) might be characterizable by specific alterations in hierarchical oscillatory dynamics and phase-coupling patterns" (`04san--md`, line 355, [[gh-04san|on github]]), and "this provides a framework for understanding how predictive processing abilities evolve with brain development" (`04san--md`, line 511, [[gh-04san|on github]]). The `b0304y` interview transcript closes the loop in plain language: when [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] lays out the SAN hierarchy, the interlocutor observes "that starts to look a lot like the predictive processing and the hierarchical predictive processing that you were talking about" (`b0304y--md`, line 648, [[gh-b0304y|on github]]). ## Post-SAN

The external evidence in this packet places SAN against a still-active research front. The 2026 *[[brain-rhythms-in-cognition|Brain rhythms in cognition]]* review explicitly opens a section titled "Predictive processing" and surveys models that "give centre stage to priors and predictions as 'predictive processing'", reporting a "link between oscillatory activity and predictive processing" (`dl-oct6-2025--Brain_rhythms_in_cognition`, lines 546, 552 and 557, note, 2026). Where that review treats oscillatory activity as evidence *for* predictive-processing models, SAN inverts the priority: oscillatory activity, specifically [[dominant-phase-wave-differential|DPWD]] across [[cortical-traveling-waves]], is the substrate that makes the inference machinery possible at all. The Barrett-and-Miller 2025 *Categorization* paper makes the same priors-and-error reading central to perception — "predictive processing has emerged as a powerful neurocomputational research tradition" — and treats categorization as one of its operating principles (`dl-oct6-2025--Categorization_Barrett_and_Miller_2025_08_13_2025`, lines 452 and 465, note, 2025); SAN absorbs the categorization claim and ties it to [[concepts]] formed inside [[cortical-columns]] under a [[neural-oscillation-hierarchy|hierarchy of oscillations]]. The [[gh-laukkonen|Laukkonen]]-and-Chandaria *DEPTH* preprint frames [[consciousness]] science as building on "active inference, predictive processing, or the [[free-energy-principle|free energy principle]] (Friston, 2010; Clark, 2013)" (`dl-oct6-2025--DEPTH_LaukkonenChandaria`, line 10, note, 2026) and notes that this pairs well with hierarchical theories of [[conscious-access|conscious access]] (`dl-oct6-2025--DEPTH_LaukkonenChandaria`, line 140, note, 2026); SAN extends this pairing by demanding that the rendered model be *phenomenally* conscious only when the prediction is sustained as a [[consciousness-as-persistent-rendering|persistent rendering]] — a stronger commitment than active inference makes on its own.
