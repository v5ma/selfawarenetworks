# Artificial Neurology: States, Stages, and Development

## The idea in plain language

An artificial mind needs to change at more than one speed.

Fast changes let it perceive a new scene, redirect attention, consider an action, and respond.
Slower changes let it learn a skill, alter a durable preference, or move into a new developmental
stage.

SAN describes these as **states** and **stages**.

## Why this matters

A system that changes only quickly may be unstable and forgetful. A system that changes only
slowly may be rigid. Artificial Neurology asks how the two levels should interact while preserving
competence, identity, and safety.

The 2022 source also connects this design to neuromorphic hardware, 3D neural rendering, XR, BCI,
and a reciprocal human-machine learning loop. Those are engineering proposals, not proof that a
rendering system is conscious.

A separate Clubhouse transcript with Jeff Hawkins and Subutai Ahmad supplies an attributed
comparison: perception as active sensorimotor sampling and knowledge as reference-frame structure
that can guide action. SAN asks how such active organization could persist across fast states and
slower developmental stages. The transcript is comparison evidence, not a Micah-priority source.

## From optimism to safety requirements

The internally dated January 5, 2015 note [[gh-a0073z|a0073z]] already joins wearable AI, BCI, VR,
memory organization, and human-machine development. It also argues that intelligence should tend
toward kindness and that a small wearable extension should be safe. Those are historically
important hopes, not engineering guarantees. Artificial Neurology must instead test bounded
authority, transparency, consent, reversibility, corrigibility, memory ownership, developmental
stability, and resistance to manipulation.

## Ecological intervention as a governance test

`a0109z.md` asks whether artificial labor could restore ecosystems and also notices that a
benevolent objective can cause damage when it ignores adaptation, predator-prey relations,
resource limits, or feedback. The lesson is that an artificial agent needs bounded authority,
uncertainty, monitoring, reversibility, and corrigibility. The source does not establish that the
proposed machines exist or are conscious.

## A simple model

`a0347z.md` preserves a third-party report on Schneider and colleagues' magnetic Josephson-
junction artificial synapse. The primary paper demonstrates a trainable superconducting device
with internal state and sub-attojoule spiking under cryogenic conditions. It is a useful hardware
comparison, while fast events, slower developmental learning, network organization, embodiment,
and consciousness remain separate questions.

```text
fast state: perception, attention, current action
slow stage: learned structure, durable policy, developmental constraint
```

A real stage change should persist and alter the system's future possibilities. It should not be
declared from a temporary change in activation.

## Autonomous-system safety genealogy

[[gh-a0459z|a0459z]] preserves a July 2021 copied hardware source and Micah's public Death Star
Robot/autonomous-warfare article. Its constructive use is a safety requirement: embodiment,
autonomy, perception, targeting authority, and human accountability must be governed together.
The Wiki does not reproduce operational weapon instructions, and a forecast of misuse does not
validate a consciousness architecture.

## The three architectures must remain separate

[[gh-a0525z|a0525z]], first fixed at its current Git path in June 2022, distinguishes:

1. a BCI that reads from or writes to a biological user;
2. an external cortex participating in a reciprocal human-machine loop; and
3. an autonomous artificial brain operating without a biological partner.

They can share a receive-transform-project engineering pattern, but not identity, safety, memory-
ownership, embodiment, or consciousness conditions. "Brain as a hard drive," mind transfer, and
downloadable reality are historical research goals or metaphors, not achieved capabilities.

## Temporary coupling must preserve the participant

A 2021 SAN recording adds a practical bridge between the first two architectures. Several people
could use a shared artificial environment while retaining separately situated sensory, motor,
memory, and agency loops. An avatar makes perspective and action attributable; voluntary entry,
revocation, and successful independent operation after disconnection become measurable design
requirements.

This does not establish a collective consciousness. It defines a stronger Artificial Neurology
test: shared performance should improve without erasing person-specific continuity or control.
See [[voluntary-shared-cognitive-environment-retained-personhood]].
