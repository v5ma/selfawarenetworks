# Conscious-State Perturbation Tomography

## The idea in plain language

A single brain scan is one view of a moving system. A stronger experiment would measure the same
person many times: awake, asleep, performing tasks, recovering, and under separately approved
perturbations such as anesthesia, medication, or stimulation. Each condition reveals different
parts of the system's organization.

The aim is to build a person-specific map of neural state and behavior. The word *tomography* is
being used for reconstruction from multiple views. It does not mean that EEG is a direct CT-like
image of consciousness.

## Why this matters to SAN

One 2020 experiment found that general anesthesia disrupted communication between distal apical
dendrites and the cell bodies of Layer 5 pyramidal neurons. A 2022 study found something that
prevents an easy slogan: anesthesia also increased widespread synchrony in Layer 5 cells.

Together, these results show why "more coherence" or "less coherence" is too simple. We need to
know which cells and compartments are coordinated, in what direction, for how long, and whether
their activity can still influence memory, report, or action.

## The 2022 SAN research route

An earlier source, [[gh-a0384z|a0384z]], is internally dated April 3, 2013 and asks how consciousness
could be recognized in a machine during a multi-speaker Orch OR debate. It poses the problem but
does not yet provide an operational criterion.

Two SAN notes preserve the route. [[gh-b0232y|b0232y]] says the apical-dendrite anesthesia study
helped crystallize the oscillatory part of NAPOT. [[gh-a0291z|a0291z]] records a broader
collaborative proposal using repeated EEG, sleep, tasks, brain stimulation, and pharmacological
conditions to map a person's changing brain dynamics. Speaker attribution in that collaborative
note remains incomplete.

## What a strong experiment must separate

- wakefulness from conscious content;
- reportability from experience;
- movement from neural state;
- memory failure from loss of experience;
- drug effects from consciousness-specific effects;
- global synchrony from pathway-specific coupling;
- condition classification from causal explanation.

## Relationship to machine consciousness

The method could help design controls for NP73's machine-consciousness measurement program, but a
human anesthesia signature is not automatically a
universal test for an artificial system.
