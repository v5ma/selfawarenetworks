The minimum electrical charge (current × time) that must arrive at a neuron's soma to trigger an [[action-potential]]. Below this threshold, the neuron does not fire; at or above it, it fires.

## The Hodgkin-Huxley context

The classic Hodgkin-Huxley model provides differential equations relating membrane voltage to ion channel conductances for Na⁺ and K⁺. The threshold is not a fixed number — it depends on:

- **Resting membrane potential** (typically −70 mV)
- **Rate of depolarization** (slow ramps require more total charge than fast pulses)
- **Ion channel state** (recently fired neurons are in a refractory period and have elevated thresholds)

A practical reference: *Models of the Mind* (Grace Lindsay) provides accessible treatments of these equations for non-specialists.

## SAN relevance

In [[napot]], the firing threshold is the decision boundary of a single neural unit. SAN's theory extends this: because the threshold is dynamic (modulated by [[dendritic-computation]] and inhibitory inputs), any given neuron is not a simple threshold gate but a *tunable filter* whose pass condition is continuously shaped by context. This makes individual neurons loci of computation, not just transmission.

The threshold concept also links directly to [[tonic-phasic-canvas-of-consciousness]]: tonic oscillations keep large populations near (but below) threshold, primed for rapid phasic response when a pattern match occurs. The canvas is "threshold readiness at scale."
