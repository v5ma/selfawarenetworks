Cells function as chemical neural networks with threshold detection mechanisms. According to Jon Lieff's framework (drawing on Strogatz's synchronization work), 3D protein structures in cellular receptors encode learned information through **coincident detection** — the same principle underlying Hebbian synaptic plasticity operates at the molecular level.

A receptor fires (changes conformation) only when multiple input signals arrive simultaneously within a narrow time window. This is the cellular equivalent of the integrate-and-fire neuron, pushed one level down the biological hierarchy.

## Connection to SAN

SAN theory extends this principle upward: what cells do with chemical signals, neurons do with [[phase-wave-differentials]], and neural arrays do with oscillatory population codes. [[Neural-distinction-consciousness]] names the higher-order version of coincident detection: consciousness arises when an observer circuit detects the *coincidence* of two distinct patterns.

Cells also operate on **cellular clocks** — timed feedback loops of genes, proteins, and RNA — that produce synchronous oscillations. These mirror the [[neural-oscillations]] SAN posits as the substrate of perception.
