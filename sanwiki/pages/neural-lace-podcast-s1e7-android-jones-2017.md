## Three EEG-XR modes

The episode separates three different uses of EEG in immersive systems:

1. an AI model predicts a coarse state, emotion or intention;
2. a derived EEG measure controls a game mechanic; or
3. the signal changes light, sound or touch directly, making the environment a biofeedback mirror.

This is a valuable distinction. Classification, interaction control and signal visualization make
different promises, and none is equivalent to semantic thought decoding.

## A special kind of hard drive

The accompanying article says that Neural Lace research regarded the brain as a **special kind of
hard drive** that might eventually be read from and written to. The bounded meaning is not a passive
digital disk or a single memory address. It is a biological system whose learned structure and
current activity constrain what can be measured, reconstructed, changed, and learned next.

The episode introduction also states an important differential-write operation:

```text
measure the receiver's present pattern
        -> estimate a desired pattern
        -> introduce only the required difference
        -> measure the resulting state again
```

This is a public 2017 ancestor of later receiver-relative SAN language. The source does not yet use
the later terms PWD, NAPOT, or Neural Tuning, and those names must not be backdated into it.

## Why it matters

Micah says he used EEG to drive light and sound changes in 2012. The episode therefore supplies a
public bridge from Neo Mind Cycle and early neurofeedback work to the later Neural Lace and SAN
program. Microdose VR also provides a concrete low-bandwidth feedback loop: measure, transform,
render, perceive and adapt. It also connects learned biological storage, receiver-relative
write-back, distributed reconstruction, and closed-loop action without locating a complete memory
or observer in one neural structure.

## Evidence boundary

Consumer EEG is coarse and artifact-prone. A responsive visual does not prove that a selected band
has one fixed mental meaning or that the system has decoded a thought.
