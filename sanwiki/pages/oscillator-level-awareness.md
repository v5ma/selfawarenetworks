# Oscillator-Level Responsiveness and Awareness Claims

A neuron or ensemble can be sensitive to and respond to rhythmic activity in other cells. Calling
that interaction **awareness** is a SAN interpretation, not an established consequence of phase
locking, phase reset, or oscillation.

## Established mechanisms

Neural timing can change through synaptic excitation and inhibition, intrinsic membrane dynamics,
common input, neuromodulation, gap junctions, recurrent circuitry, and bounded extracellular field
effects. Population phase reset can reflect a stimulus-evoked response, a change in oscillator
timing, or both. It is not automatically a broadcast or a self-model update.

Long-range cortical output is primarily carried by projection neurons, including diverse pyramidal
cell classes. Interneurons are mostly local, although their axons can span different local distances.
The earlier phrase "long interneurons between pyramidal cells" should not be used as the general
cross-brain broadcast anatomy.

## Scale ladder

| Scale | Established description | Stronger SAN claim |
|---|---|---|
| Micro | A cell or dendritic compartment changes response with timing and state | The cell registers a meaningful difference |
| Meso | A population changes coordination, gain, routing, or output | The population performs a local observer-like operation |
| Macro | Distributed systems change model, action, and feedback | The aggregate field-cell process constitutes observer-action |

Evidence at one level does not automatically establish the next. In particular, a local circuit's
responsiveness does not show that every oscillator is conscious at its scale.

## What would justify awareness language

The page must identify:

1. the measured source, receiver, and timing relation;
2. the alternative rate, power, common-input, movement, and arousal explanations;
3. the downstream state or behavior changed by the receiver;
4. memory or self/world context beyond a transient reflex;
5. a selective perturbation and predicted loss or rescue;
6. why "awareness" adds explanatory value beyond responsiveness or control.
