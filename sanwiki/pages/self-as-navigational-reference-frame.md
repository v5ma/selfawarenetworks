# Self as a Navigational Reference Relation

> **Scientific boundary:** SAN does not identify the self with one coordinate origin, one map, one brain region, or one rhythm. Spatial self-location is one component of embodied self-modeling, alongside body ownership, agency, interoception, memory, value, and action. Coherent group motion in a flock or swarm is not by itself evidence of consciousness.

## A bounded navigational claim

An organism must maintain relations among its sensors, body parts, movements, goals, and surroundings. Eye-, head-, limb-, body-, object-, and environment-relative variables can make action possible without one master map.

In this bounded sense, **here relative to there** is an important component of self-related processing. It does not exhaust what a self is, and it should not be confused with phenomenal consciousness.

## Distinct measurable variables

- **Body coordinates:** where a part is relative to another part or the environment.
- **Body schema:** action-oriented organization of bodily relations.
- **Ownership:** whether a body or proxy is experienced as mine.
- **Self-location:** where the experiencing organism reports itself to be.
- **Agency:** whether the organism caused an action or consequence.
- **Interoception:** measurements and regulation of internal bodily conditions.

Rubber-hand studies demonstrate that ownership reports and proprioceptive drift can dissociate ([Rohde, Di Luca, and Ernst, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0021659)). These variables therefore cannot be collapsed into one directional self signal.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats the self as a distributed, revisable relation across sensory, interoceptive, mnemonic, evaluative, and motor populations. No hidden navigator inspects an internal map. Cellular receivers and transmitters, recurrent pathways, tonic receiver contexts, phasic updates, and bodily consequences collectively produce observer-action.

Navigation supplies a useful special case:

1. sensory populations register partial body-world relations;
2. movement-related signals update those relations;
3. memory and value constrain candidate goals;
4. distributed motor systems change the body;
5. resulting sensory consequences update the next cycle.

The system itself performs this process. The term **self-aware** should be reserved for a declared capacity and evidence set, not inferred from synchronization, signal propagation, static coordinates, or collective motion alone.

## Testable extension

A SAN account should predict when body coordinates, ownership, self-location, agency, and report converge and when they dissociate. It should outperform capacity-matched multisensory causal-inference, predictive, recurrent, and sensorimotor models, then survive selective perturbation and rescue.
