# Feature Detection

**Feature Detection** in [[self-aware-networks|SAN]] is the operation that high-frequency [[neural-oscillations|gamma]] activity in sensory cortices performs on incoming patterns, framed by [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] as the *biological homologue* of feature-detection layers in deep-learning networks — not as a separable cognitive function but as one band-specific component of the framework's broader [[criterial-causation|criterial-causation]] / [[pattern-neurons|pattern-neuron]] account. The SAN-distinctive move is to bind feature detection to a specific oscillatory band and modulator: gamma in sensory cortex performs the detection, and lower-frequency beta originating in PFC performs the abstraction and integration that follows.

## Position

Within SAN, feature detection is the *gamma-band specialization* of a more general operation the framework calls [[dendritic-pattern-detection|dendritic pattern detection]] / [[criteria-causation-single-neuron-pattern-detection|single-neuron pattern detection]]. The composition runs in three layers. First, at the cell level, an [[eds|EDS]] (synapses + dendrites) recognizes a particular receptor configuration as a "feature." Second, at the local-circuit level, gamma activity in sensory cortex aggregates many EDS detections into the band-coherent perception/feature-detection output `03san--md`, line 69 names. Third, at the cross-region level, beta from PFC modulates and integrates these features into the abstraction stage that the rest of the SAN pipeline ([[multi-perspective-pattern-convergence|multi-perspective convergence]], [[neural-rendering-of-qualia|rendering]]) consumes. The deep-learning analogy in `GPT2024Jan1toJan22--md`, line 3933 attaches a structural reading to this: habituation — inhibition of features by repeated non-essential stimuli — is the biological correlate of the *filtering* layer in artificial networks, while the band-specific gamma detection is the analog of the *feature* layer.

The position has structural consequences. It commits SAN to predicting that disrupting gamma in sensory cortex should specifically degrade feature detection while leaving abstraction relatively intact, and that disrupting beta in PFC should specifically degrade abstraction while leaving detection relatively intact — a dissociation visible in [[eeg|EEG]]/MEG studies of cross-frequency coupling. It also commits SAN to a particular reading of [[ltd-inhibitory-pattern-sharpening|inhibitory pattern sharpening]] as the biological *feature-filtering* mechanism, by analogy to how `GPT2024Jan1toJan22--md`, line 3933 maps habituation onto deep-learning filtering. The cross-architecture analogy lets SAN engage with the [[artificial-perception|artificial-perception]] literature directly, since feature detection is the single most-studied layer in deep-learning vision systems and any structural mapping between SAN and ANN architectures has to start there.

## Post-SAN

The published literature substantially supports the gamma-detection claim: gamma activity in sensory cortices is repeatedly associated with feature detection in vision, audition, and somatosensation across the EEG/MEG literature, and the gamma-beta cross-frequency-coupling pattern `03san--md` describes is a well-documented observable. The deep-learning analogy SAN draws on is also well-established in [[computational-neuroscience|computational neuroscience]], particularly in the work mapping convolutional layers onto ventral-stream cortical hierarchies. Where SAN parts company is in *binding the analogy to specific oscillatory bands and modulators*: standard mappings between deep-learning features and cortical processing typically remain band-agnostic, while SAN commits to gamma-detection / beta-abstraction as a structural claim. The testable difference is that SAN predicts the cortex-to-ANN feature-mapping should hold *most strongly when conditioned on gamma activity* and degrade when gamma is suppressed — a prediction the band-agnostic mainstream framing does not make.
