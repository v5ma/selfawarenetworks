**Neural phase pattern propagation** is the mechanism by which learned sensory patterns roll across the brain via phase changes transmitted through neural oscillation networks — the physical basis of [[neural-oscillatory-tomography]] (NOT) and [[neural-array-projection-oscillatory-tomography]] (NAPOT).

## Core Mechanism

Patterns flow from one neural region to another through phase changes. When a phase shift occurs in a group of oscillating neurons, it transmits to adjacent oscillating groups — analogous to how a chemical phase change propagates through molecules in water: "something is transmitted from one oscillating molecule and then the next one receives that transmission and renders it fresh for the next one after that."

The massive axon-terminal connectivity of the brain means that any pattern can, in principle, be represented by any population of neurons, with information flowing through the medium of phase variation rather than fixed labeled pathways.

## Color as Phase Field

Color perception exemplifies phase pattern propagation. Color is defined not by absolute wavelength but by **contrast within a phase field** — the difference relative to everything else in the visual scene. "The frequency is to your brain just a difference, relative to everything else." The brain renders color by comparing contrasts in the phase field, an operation identical in principle to how [[cellular-oscillatory-tomography]] computes local feature contrast.

This framing connects color science to SAN theory: the visual cortex does not decode a color label; it computes a phase-field pattern and hands it up the rendering stack.

## Neural Tomography

The tomographic quality of perception arises because multiple sensory perspectives (different viewing angles, times, modalities) contribute phase patterns that the brain combines to detect common links. Patterns that repeat across multiple perspectives get reinforced via Hebbian learning across layered neuron populations. The result is robust object representation via **phase-pattern superposition** rather than single-cell selectivity.

This is the informal early [[gh-formulation|formulation]] of what later became the full NAPOT model.
