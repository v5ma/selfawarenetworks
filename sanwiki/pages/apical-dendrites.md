# Apical Dendrites

**Apical dendrites**, considered as a population, are many distinct input and integration
compartments belonging to many pyramidal neurons. Their partly aligned geometry can contribute to
extracellular current dipoles and field measurements, while their synapses and active conductances
affect the output of individual cells. Population alignment does not turn them into one output
screen or a complete observer.

> **Scientific boundary:** Apical dendrites participate in recurrent cortical and thalamocortical processing, but their pathways, channel distributions, morphologies, and computational roles vary by area, layer, species, cell subtype, development, and state. They are not universally prediction branches, and basal dendrites are not universally sensory branches.

## The source-faithful population question

The earlier SAN page mixed an anatomically reversed output claim with a more substantial
population-level proposal. The viable question was not whether thousands of apical dendrites form
one terminal array. It was whether many compartment-sensitive neurons, recurrently linked through
cortical and thalamic pathways, can repeatedly gather, transform, redistribute, and register
partial neural states.

The recovered operator is:

**heterogeneous apical, basal, oblique, and perisomatic input + current network and body state ->
cell-specific dendritic integration -> single spike, burst, or silence -> diverse axonal projection
effects and changing population currents -> recurrent cortical, thalamic, subcortical, and
sensorimotor return -> next distributed state**

This is the population-scale extension of the one-cell
[[apical-dendrite|receive-integrate-project-return cycle]]. It preserves SAN's proposed operation
without treating the apical tree as presynaptic output.

## Population anatomy is heterogeneous

Pyramidal neurons in cortical layers 2/3, 5, and 6 have diverse apical morphologies. Some tufts
reach layer 1; others terminate deeper. Apical shafts can form anatomical bundles, but a bundle is
not automatically a functional minicolumn, and a minicolumn is not a universal cortical algorithm.

In mouse V1, Kondo and colleagues imaged apical dendritic bundles and found mixed functional
organization: some response similarities were greater than chance, while spatial-frequency tuning
was not organized by the same bundles. The experiment is evidence for constrained population
structure and heterogeneity, not one bundle-wide code. See
[Kondo et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13210).

Every population claim should therefore name its cells, area, layer, inputs, projection targets,
preparation, and measured variable.

## Integration, bursts, and projection classes

In some cortical pyramidal neurons, distal apical input interacts with backpropagating axonal
spikes and basal or perisomatic drive. Dendritic calcium or NMDA events can alter whether the cell
emits no spike, a single spike, or a burst. Inhibition can gate these events. See
[Larkum et al. (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1038/18686) and
[Larkum et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1717-07.2007).

A burst is not intrinsically "high information." Its information depends on the available
alternatives, reliability, receiver sensitivity, and context. Projection identity also matters. In
mouse somatosensory cortex, apical activation had different perceptual relevance across layer 5
projection classes. See
[Takahashi et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-020-0677-8).

## Feedback and thalamocortical return

Feedback pathways often terminate in superficial layers and can engage apical compartments, but
feedback is distributed across many circuits. In mouse visual cortex, feedback from lateromedial
visual cortex produced spatially organized, branch-specific nonlinear events in identified V1
apical tufts. See
[Fişek et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06007-6).

Layer 6 corticothalamic neurons also influence thalamic activity through both direct excitation and
indirect inhibition. In a mouse somatosensory preparation, the net effect shifted with stimulation
frequency because different synapses had different short-term dynamics. This supports
state-dependent bidirectional control, not a universal rule that layer 6 apical dendrites directly
prime every upper-layer cell. See
[Crandall et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25913856/).

The relevant return cycle therefore includes the axons of identified corticocortical or
corticothalamic cells, their downstream targets, and later feedback. The apical dendrites receive
part of that cycle; they do not themselves project it to the thalamus.

## What field measurements do and do not show

Aligned pyramidal-cell geometry can create current dipoles that contribute to local field,
electroencephalographic, and magnetoencephalographic measurements. A biophysical modeling study
found stronger current dipoles for pyramidal than stellate cells and estimated that synchronous
activity in tens of thousands of modeled pyramidal cells could reach detectable MEG/EEG scale. It
also found substantial basal-dendrite contributions. See
[Murakami and Okada (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2006.105379).

That supports a population-to-sensor bridge, but not the older claim that an electrode directly
reads one apical code or an inverted copy of a neuron's axonal message. Keep three claims separate:

1. **Field generation:** transmembrane and axial currents produce measurable extracellular fields.
2. **Circuit recurrence:** synapses and axons carry activity through recurrent pathways.
3. **Causal field coupling:** extracellular fields materially alter nearby neural processing.

The first is established. The second is established but circuit-specific. The third has bounded
experimental precedents and requires field strength, spatial range, orientation, timing, and a
demonstrated effect beyond ordinary synaptic coupling for each SAN application.

## From repeated feedback to NAPOT

SAN's population proposal can now be stated without a hidden viewer:

- many populations contribute partial, pathway-constrained state;
- dendritic and somatic interactions transform those partial inputs;
- diverse axons redistribute the resulting activity;
- recurrent cortical, thalamic, subcortical, and sensorimotor routes return altered state;
- an ongoing tonic population state supplies receiver-relative context;
- phasic departures change that state and can recruit later transformations;
- the distributed network itself both generates and registers the changing pattern.

Calling this **tomography** is a functional reconstruction hypothesis. It proposes that repeated
partial projections can help maintain a distributed working model that no single dendrite, bundle,
region, or field contains. It is not a claim that the brain performs conventional medical imaging
on itself or that one localized screen is watched by an inner observer.

## Consciousness and anesthesia boundary

Suzuki and Larkum found that several anesthetics decoupled distal apical-dendrite influence from
somatic output in mouse layer 5 pyramidal neurons. This supports a specific route by which
anesthesia can disrupt feedback integration. It does not establish that apical cycling alone is
necessary and sufficient for consciousness. See
[Suzuki and Larkum (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.01.024).

Evidence levels must remain separate:

1. apical input changes dendritic state;
2. dendritic state changes axonal output;
3. population currents or circuit routing change;
4. behavior or conscious access changes;
5. a phenomenal quality is identified with that pattern.

Results at one level do not automatically prove the next.

## Claim boundaries

- **Established neuroscience:** apical dendrites are heterogeneous input and integration
  compartments; aligned pyramidal populations contribute to field measurements; identified
  feedback and thalamocortical pathways can alter dendritic and circuit state.
- **Supported interpretation:** recurrent population activity can update distributed sensory,
  contextual, memory, and action-related representations.
- **SAN-specific hypothesis:** tonic population context and phasic departures participate in a
  repeated receive-transform-project-return process through which the distributed network
  reconstructs and registers its changing self-world model.
- **Stronger experimental claim:** a particular apical population, oscillatory relation, or field
  interaction is necessary for a content of consciousness or implements tomographic
  reconstruction. This requires intervention, content-sensitive decoding, competing-model
  comparison, and failure conditions.
