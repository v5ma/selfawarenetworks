The subjective-objective distinction holds that consciousness research operates across two irreducible categories: the **objective** (observable, third-person, mechanistic) and the **subjective** (phenomenological, first-person, experiential). Conflating these categories without a bridging framework produces what Sebastian Stark called "logical chaos."

## The SAN Problem Space

[[napot|NAPOT]] addresses this gap directly: neural phase-oscillation patterns are objectively measurable, yet their function is to produce [[self-modeling]] — a first-person representation of the organism's own states. SAN theory proposes that the bridge is information structure: the *same* oscillatory pattern encodes both an objective electrochemical event and a subjective felt quality.

## The Free-Will Case

[[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]] argues there is no observable mechanism for libertarian [[choice]] — the brain is a prediction system operating through cause-and-effect chains. Yet Stark's counterpoint holds: the *subjective experience* of choosing is phenomenologically real even if its causal story is fully mechanistic. Neither claim refutes the other because they operate in different ontological registers.

## SAN's Resolution

SAN does not dissolve the hard problem but reframes it: subjective experience is the rendering output of [[self-aware-network|self-aware network]] activity. The network's [[gh-a0206z|self]]-model IS the phenomenology — not an epiphenomenon, but the functional representation that guides behavior. This is distinct from eliminativist positions that deny subjective reality and from dualist positions that posit a separate substance.
