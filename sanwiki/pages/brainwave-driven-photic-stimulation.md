**Brainwave-Driven Photic Stimulation** is a neurofeedback technique in which a light machine delivers rhythmic photic pulses calibrated to the individual's real-time brainwave data and circadian rhythm state. Used in the [[neo-mind-cycle-neurofeedback|Neo Mind Cycle]] salon protocol (2012).

This diagram shows the page's core loop: EEG estimates the current dominant rhythm and phase, circadian calibration prevents stimulation from fighting the tonic state, calibrated photic pulses drive the visual pathway, and EEG readback checks whether the thalamocortical rhythm phase-locked toward the target alpha/theta band.

## Mechanism

EEG reads the user's current dominant brainwave frequency and circadian offset. A light machine is then set to deliver pulses at frequencies designed to entrain the brain toward a target state — typically alpha (relaxed [[attention]]) or theta (creative/hypnagogic) — while accounting for where the user is in their daily circadian cycle. The circadian calibration prevents entrainment conflicts (e.g., driving alpha at a time of day when the brain's endogenous clock is primed for alertness).

## Place in the Neo Mind Cycle protocol

This is the third element of the [[neo-mind-cycle-three-phase-protocol]], alongside EEG biofeedback visualization and audio beat entrainment. Together, the three modalities address the same oscillatory target via different sensory channels — visual, auditory, and cognitive [[gh-a0206z|self]]-monitoring.

## SAN relevance

Under SAN, the efficacy of photic stimulation depends on [[consciousness-canvas-wave-differential|wave differential perception]] — the light pulses create temporal contrast in the visual pathway that the brain's oscillatory system phase-locks to. The circadian calibration is a practical acknowledgment that the brain's [[tonic-oscillation|tonic phase field]] varies with time of day and that entrainment inputs must be synchronized to it.
