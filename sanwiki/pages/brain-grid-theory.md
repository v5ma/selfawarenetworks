# Brain Grid Theory

> **Evidence boundary:** Brain tissue is a three-dimensional biological network, but diffusion MRI does not establish one universal orthogonal wiring lattice. Anatomical fiber geometry, a connectivity graph, a grid-cell firing pattern, and a mathematical coordinate grid are different objects.

**Brain Grid Theory** began as a 2012 SAN inquiry into how learning changes routes through a three-dimensional neural network. Its durable claim is not that the brain is a cubic electronic backplane. It is that anatomy, plasticity, timing, and possibly local nonsynaptic influences jointly determine which distributed routes become effective at a given moment.

## The original question

Micah's October 17, 2012 note was prompted by Wedeen and colleagues' diffusion-imaging interpretation of primate white matter. The note asked whether observed crossings could be connectable or disconnectable through learning, whether directed neural routes could be represented as graphs and adjacency matrices, and whether organized three-dimensional flow could support association, prediction, and adaptive behavior.

The source was exploratory. It explicitly called much of the proposal theory, asked how the anatomical claim could be tested, and later acknowledged that a grid might not be a universal wiring principle. The source should therefore be read as a proposed research program, not as settled anatomy.

## Four different meanings of grid

1. **Anatomical network:** Axons, dendrites, synapses, glia, vasculature, and extracellular space form a three-dimensional substrate with region- and cell-specific geometry.
2. **Connectivity graph:** Cells, populations, or regions can be represented as nodes connected by directed, weighted, delayed, and plastic edges. This graph is a model of measured relations, not the tissue itself.
3. **Grid-cell code:** Some neurons show spatially periodic firing fields as an animal moves. This grid is defined in represented or behavioral space, not by a visible lattice of wires.
4. **Coordinate model:** A theory can describe local directions, paths, or neural states with mathematical axes. The axes must be declared and linked to measurements.

The 2012 six-direction language is most defensibly retained as a **local routing or coordinate abstraction**: up/down, left/right, and forward/back describe possible directions in three-dimensional tissue. It is not evidence that every neuron, cortical column, or tract is aligned to one global Cartesian lattice.

## What diffusion imaging can show

Diffusion MRI estimates preferred directions of water diffusion within image voxels and supports tractography models of white-matter pathways. Wedeen and colleagues reported sheet-like organization and approximately orthogonal crossings in parts of primate white matter. This did not show that every apparent crossing is a synapse or that every axon follows a whole-brain Cartesian grid.

The anatomical interpretation was disputed. Movahedian Attar and colleagues found the examined pathway geometry more consistent with lamellar, small-angle branching than a near-orthogonal grid. Tractography benchmarks also show that reconstruction methods can produce both valid and invalid bundles. The dispute constrains the anatomical claim; it does not erase the separate questions about plastic routing, relational computation, or three-dimensional coordinate models.

## The recovered SAN operations

### 1. A plastic biological graph

Learning does not generally convert arbitrary white-matter crossings into synapses. It can, however, alter synaptic weights, dendritic spines, local connectivity, excitability, and effective communication. The source-faithful operation is therefore:

**existing anatomy + activity-dependent plasticity -> changed effective graph -> changed future routing**

This is a measurable graph claim. Each proposed node and edge must specify its biological scale, direction, delay, plasticity rule, and recording method.

### 2. A graph can have a matrix representation

A directed network can be represented by an adjacency or transition matrix. This provides a formal language for paths, convergence, divergence, recurrent loops, and changing edge weights. It does not mean that neurons symbolically multiply matrices or solve written equations. The scientific question is whether a graph or matrix model predicts neural state transitions and behavior better than competing models.

### 3. Relational inference can precede explicit symbolic reasoning

The 2012 note proposed that association, causal expectation, prediction, and concept formation may arise from learned relations before a person expresses those relations as formal logic. Statistical learning and hippocampal-entorhinal research independently show that neural representations can reflect temporal community structure and abstract relational organization.

SAN's stronger interpretation remains a hypothesis: a plastic, recurrent graph may implement much of what is experienced as intuition, while explicit logic is a learned tool operating over those relational states. This does not establish that the brain performs no logical computation.

### 4. Hard routes and state-dependent local influences

The early source distinguished direct anatomical routes from activity that might affect nearby cells outside a direct synaptic path. A later 2022 source expanded this into electrical, magnetic, mechanical, metabolic, vascular, pressure, and thermal candidates within a volumetric rendering hypothesis.

A medically bounded modern formulation separates these branches:

- **Anatomical or synaptic routes** provide directed structural connections.
- **Local extracellular electric fields** can, under measured conditions, modulate membrane potential, spike timing, or network activity.
- **Magnetic, mechanical, metabolic, vascular, pressure, and thermal effects** require their own sensors, scales, coupling mechanisms, and causal tests.

Experimental work on endogenous electric fields and ephaptic coupling gives the electrical branch a real physiological basis. It does not establish arbitrary wireless edges, strong long-range triggering, or SAN's complete rendering and consciousness account.

## From grid intuition to NAPOT

The surviving bridge to [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography|NAPOT]] is an operation chain:

**distributed partial input -> receiver-specific transformation -> propagation through a plastic three-dimensional graph -> recurrent redistribution -> revised population state**

At the micro scale, dendrites and somata integrate inputs before axonal output. At the meso scale, local populations recruit and suppress routes. At the macro scale, distributed cortical, thalamic, hippocampal, sensory, and motor systems exchange partial state. No crossing, neuron, field pattern, grid, or brain region contains a complete percept or acts as an inner viewer.

Brain Grid Theory supplies a candidate geometry and routing substrate. NAPOT proposes repeated convergence and redistribution, while [[ndca-nonlinear-differential-continuous-approximation|NDCA]] proposes how changing samples might support a continuous working approximation.

## Discriminating tests

1. **Plastic graph test:** Measure whether learning changes a declared structural or effective graph and whether those changes predict later routing and behavior better than fixed-connectivity models.
2. **Coordinate test:** Compare a six-direction local-coordinate model with anatomy-specific, unconstrained graph, rate-only, and timing-sensitive models. Reject the coordinate model if it adds no reproducible predictive value.
3. **Field-coupling test:** After accounting for synaptic transmission and volume conduction, perturb a measured local extracellular field and test for receiver-specific changes in spike timing, recruitment, or behavior.
4. **Relational-learning test:** Present the same relational graph using different sensory contents. Test whether learned neural community structure generalizes before explicit rule instruction and whether graph-aware models predict that transfer.
5. **Volumetric-rendering test:** Record electrical, hemodynamic, metabolic, and mechanical variables separately. A multimodal SAN model must predict perceptual or behavioral state beyond standard neural activity measures and must identify the relevant receiver mechanism.

## Correction record

The earlier scientific rewrite correctly removed the claim that diffusion imaging proved a universal orthogonal brain lattice. It overcorrected by leaving only a generic network description. This revision restores the dated plastic-graph, graph/matrix, pre-symbolic relational, local field-coupling, and volumetric-rendering operations as bounded SAN hypotheses.

The rejected anatomical image remains quarantined. It should not return to publication unless a replacement clearly separates measured anatomy, model geometry, local field effects, and SAN interpretation.
