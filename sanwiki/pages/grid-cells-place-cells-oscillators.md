# Grid Cells, Place Cells, and Oscillatory Coding

> **Evidence boundary:** Grid and place cells are neurons classified by how firing relates to position, context, and behavior. Their firing can be modulated by theta and other network rhythms, but a firing field is not itself an electromagnetic vortex, conscious spatial field, or oscillation.

Grid cells and place cells contribute different, interacting signals to spatial behavior. Place cells often fire strongly in one or more locations and can remap when context changes. Grid cells can express multiple, approximately periodic firing fields. Both response classes depend on circuit state, sensory cues, movement, task, and experience.

## Spatial code versus oscillation

Three measurements must remain distinct:

- **Firing field:** the relationship between spikes and sampled position or task state.
- **Spike timing:** when spikes occur relative to behavior or another neural signal.
- **Network rhythm:** a population-level fluctuation measured in local field potential, membrane potential, current-source density, or another declared signal.

A place cell can show theta phase precession, meaning its spikes shift relative to a measured theta cycle as the animal traverses a field. Many rodent medial entorhinal grid cells are theta-modulated, but theta expression and coding vary by cell type, region, species, and behavioral condition. None of this makes a grid field a literal rotating electromagnetic geometry.

## Population integration

Spatial representation is distributed across place, grid, head-direction, speed, boundary, object-vector, sensory, and motor-related populations. Their relationships are recurrent and task dependent. A single cell supplies one partial signal; the useful map emerges from population activity and its downstream readout.

## SAN interpretation

SAN uses this system as a biological example of receiver-relative coding:

1. ongoing activity supplies a tonic reference state;
2. sensory, motor, and memory events change selected cells and populations;
3. timing and amplitude differences can alter what downstream receivers integrate;
4. recurrent loops update the larger body-world model.

The SAN terms **tonic canvas** and **phasic ink** are plain-language names for hypothesized receiver-relative roles. The canvas is the ongoing population state against which a receiver can register a change; the ink is a comparatively differentiated update in timing, phase, amplitude, duration, excitation, or inhibition. The imagery is metaphorical, but the proposed roles are intended to be measurable. They should not be mapped one-to-one onto grid cells, place cells, theta, or gamma. A candidate NAPOT mechanism must identify the anatomical sender, receiving population, measured signals, and causal readout.

The information-theory connection is conditional. If event `x` has probability `p(x)`, its self-information is `I(x) = -log2 p(x)`. A rarer event has more surprisal under a specified distribution, but rarity alone does not establish semantic content, causal relevance, or consciousness.

## Recovering the 2022 magnification question

The earlier SAN wording behind this page asked whether a pattern selected in one neural population
could be **magnified across scales**: expressed first in cells or local circuits, then re-expressed
through larger connected populations while remaining part of a volumetric body-world model. The
same 2022 note called grid and place cells "oscillators" and referred to electromagnetic "tunnels or
vortices." Those phrases should not be converted into a claim that a firing field is literally a
vortex. They preserve a narrower and still open dynamical question: do recurrent trajectories,
phase relationships, and structured propagation paths help selected spatial patterns persist,
spread, and become available to downstream receivers?

Micah's nearby 2022 notes help recover the intended operation. One describes a dissipating vortex
as a transient or decaying oscillator or resonator. Another proposes that hippocampal-entorhinal
grid/place interactions contribute coordinates to a volumetric representation whose contents are
filled in by other cortical systems. Read together, "vortex" is best treated here as early
dynamical language for recurrence, resonant persistence, or loop geometry, while "three-dimensional
grid" names a proposed volumetric coding problem. The exact field variable, pathway, scale, and
receiver remain to be specified rather than silently discarded.

## What would distinguish the proposal

Record spikes and field signals simultaneously in connected hippocampal-entorhinal and cortical populations while changing landmarks, movement, object-centered coordinates, and task demands. Compare models based on firing rate, spike timing, oscillatory phase, and connectivity. A SAN phase model should predict receiver-specific changes and behavior beyond what the rate and connectivity models explain.
