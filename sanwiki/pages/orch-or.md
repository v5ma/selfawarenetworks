# Orch OR

**Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch OR)** is Roger Penrose and Stuart Hameroff's proposal that orchestrated quantum processes in neuronal microtubules undergo objective reduction and that these events contribute to conscious moments. The theory joins several claims that must be evaluated separately: microtubule biology, quantum state dynamics, orchestration by cellular processes, Penrose's proposed gravitational reduction, and a consciousness interpretation.

## What the theory claims

Hameroff and Penrose describe Orch OR as a conjunction of quantum computation in microtubules, biological orchestration, and objective reduction related to space-time geometry ([Physics of Life Reviews, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2013.08.002)). Evidence for one component does not automatically establish the full conjunction.

For example, showing that an anesthetic binds tubulin would support microtubules as one molecular target. Showing an optical collective effect in purified microtubules would support a physical property of that preparation. Neither result alone identifies objective reduction, shows that the effect operates in intact neural tissue at the required scale, or demonstrates that it is necessary or sufficient for conscious content.

## Current evidence ladder

| Evidence level | What has been reported | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Cellular physiology | Microtubules support neuronal structure, transport, and plasticity-related remodeling | Whether a specific microtubule state carries conscious content |
| In-vitro biophysics | Purified or assembled tubulin systems have shown wavelength-dependent energy migration and collective ultraviolet emission effects ([Kalra et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114); [Babcock et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c07936)) | Whether the relevant state persists and is causally read in living neural circuits |
| Anesthesia-associated behavior | Microtubule-modulating drugs have shifted isoflurane loss-of-righting-reflex measures in rodents, with effects that depend on drug, regimen, species, and experiment ([Khan et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024); [Li et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-025-02956-9); [Huang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2026.110834)) | Loss of righting reflex is an arousal/behavioral endpoint, not a direct measurement of conscious content or objective reduction |
| Quantum-consciousness mechanism | Orch OR supplies a proposed explanatory model | Direct in-vivo measurement, selective perturbation, loss, and rescue of the proposed state remain required |

Tegmark's decoherence analysis posed a model-specific challenge to long-lived neural quantum superpositions ([Physical Review E, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.61.4194)). Later quantum-biological measurements make empirical investigation worthwhile, but they do not erase the need to specify the state, lifetime, biological receiver, causal route, and conscious target.

## Relation to SAN

[[self-aware-networks|SAN]] does not require Orch OR. SAN's observer-action proposal is organized at cellular, circuit, and distributed-network scales: receptors and dendrites register differences; cells and circuits transform them; recurrent pathways, memory, body feedback, and action systems re-express and use the changing state. The network itself performs this process; no reduction event is treated as a localized inner viewer.

Microtubules could modulate cellular readiness or plasticity within SAN without becoming the seat of consciousness. Conversely, Orch OR could be investigated on its own terms without validating [[napot-overview|NAPOT]], tonic/phasic coding, or SAN's distributed observer interpretation.

## SAN source genealogy and scale-translation test

SAN's engagement predates the 2023 chronology previously assigned by an automated Wiki draft. A
public Google Recorder source dated October 21, 2021 discusses multiscale pattern amplification and
microtubules as one possible memory mechanism. June 2022 Git sources then state two explicit
boundaries: SAN works with or without quantum effects, and a microtubule signal matters only if it
can alter a biological receiver such as a receptor, ion channel, membrane current, firing
threshold, burst, vesicle-release process, or plasticity mechanism strongly enough to affect the
next scale.

That requirement is formalized on [[microtubule-to-circuit-scale-translation|Microtubule-to-Circuit
Scale Translation]]. It preserves Micah's original argument without treating the dated source as
experimental validation.
