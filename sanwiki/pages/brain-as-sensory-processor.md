# Brain As Sensory Processor

![Distinct sensory routes and SAN's distributed recurrent interpretation, with conventional superficial-to-deep neocortical layer order](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/brain-as-sensory-processor/brain-sensory-routes-reviewed-20260926-v3.png)

SAN treats sensory evidence, learned context and action feedback as interacting parts of a distributed process. The proposal is not that cognition belongs to one isolated prefrontal controller. Incoming activity changes an already active, historically structured system; that system's responses alter both later neural activity and the evidence obtained through action.

The figure separates a selected anatomical route for each modality from the SAN systems interpretation. It is a simplified primate-oriented overview, not a complete tract map. Intermediate structures, parallel pathways, bilateral projections and feedback connections are omitted from the route rows. The brain rendering is a gross anatomical illustration, not a localization map.

## Distinct sensory routes

- **Vision:** the selected retinogeniculate route reaches visual cortex through the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN). Primate tracer studies distinguish multiple geniculocortical channels and laminar targets, rather than one generic input to layer 1. See [Diamond et al. (1985)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2418082/).
- **Hearing:** the row compresses cochlear, brainstem and midbrain processing before the medial geniculate nucleus (MGN) and auditory cortex. [Scott et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28685822/) traced distinct MGN connections with macaque auditory areas, including reciprocal cortical projections.
- **Body touch example:** skin input in the selected dorsal-column route ascends to brainstem dorsal-column nuclei, then travels through the medial lemniscus to the ventral posterolateral thalamus (VPL, part of VP) and somatosensory cortex. The VP label in the image is a family-level abbreviation, not a claim that every skin sensation follows this route. [Kalil (1981)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7204651/) traced dorsal-column projections in rhesus monkeys; other somatosensory pathways are not shown.
- **Taste:** the selected route uses a brainstem relay and the parvicellular ventroposteromedial thalamus (VPMpc) before insular-opercular cortex. [Pritchard et al. (1986)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3950095/) mapped these cortical projections in macaques.
- **Smell:** olfactory-bulb output reaches piriform and other primary olfactory areas without an obligatory first thalamic relay. [Carmichael et al. (1994)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7527806/) directly traced these connections in macaques, including subsequent connections with orbital cortex. Smell and taste are related but distinct pathways.

Hippocampal-entorhinal systems participate in memory and association; the diagram does not insert them as a compulsory relay between every sensory organ and primary cortex. Likewise, orbital/prefrontal participation in sensory processing does not make the whole prefrontal cortex a fourth primary sensory quadrant. The distributed-comparison argument does not require either anatomical simplification.

## Layers and recurrent comparison

**Layer 1 is superficial; layer 6 is deep, next to white matter.** The inset shows conventional neocortical order, not histology or a one-layer/one-function rule. It does not describe piriform cortex. Thalamic input varies by pathway, layer and cell type; [Sermet et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.52665) mapped this specificity in mouse barrel cortex. That preparation is a bounded example, not a universal map of all primate areas.

Micah's [[gh-a0206z|self and phase-field source]] explicitly connects distributed neural activity, sensory updating, learned expectations and action. Its decision account joins bottom-up receiver criteria with top-down learned patterns; it does not confine all choice to PFC. SAN's further proposal is that these interactions construct a continuing experienced world and support selection among anticipated actions. See [[deterministic-decision-making]], [[napot]] and [[phase-wave-differentials]]. Anatomical connectivity alone does not establish the complete rendering interpretation.

## Learned Spatial Self

In SAN, the represented self belongs to the modeled world and its ongoing sensory, memory and action relations. The source also explicitly rejects a blank-slate starting point, so a learned or revisable self-model must not be rewritten as a claim that humans have no innate organization.

[Ehrsson's 2007 experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1142175) used cameras behind seated participants, head-mounted displays and correlated visual/tactile stimulation to alter experienced self-location. It did not transfer a mind into a robot or establish SAN's proposed phase-field mechanism. It supplies a concrete example of malleable bodily perspective relevant to [[non-local-self]] and [[self-aware-network]].

## Historical analogy and attribution

An earlier version of this Wiki page used an elephant's legs/body/trunk as an analogy for sensory channels and distributed processing. The linked [[gh-resnote04]] discusses thalamic/prefrontal interactions but does not substantiate that particular metaphor. Until its original source is recovered, the analogy remains earlier Wiki wording, not a verified quotation from Micah. Its useful many-inputs/whole-system intuition must not be mistaken for literal anatomy or used to compress the original SAN argument.
