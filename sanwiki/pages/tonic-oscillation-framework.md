## Position

Read as a definitional consequence of its title in SAN, the **[[tonic-oscillation|tonic oscillation]] framework** is the cross-cutting set of claims about what slow rhythms do for cortical computation. Three claims hold the framework together. First, *carrier-and-modulator structure*: tonic rhythms (low-frequency, sustained) act as the carrier on top of which faster phasic events ride, which is the structural commitment that lets [[neural-array-projection|NAPOT]]'s "brain oscillations (both tonic and phasic) project sensory data across [[neural-arrays|neural arrays]]" make sense — the tonic rhythm sets the projection geometry, the phasic rhythm sets the content. Third, *bridging across scales*: tonic rhythms supply the cross-scale coupling element ([[tonic-oscillation-bridging|tonic oscillation bridging]]) that lets small assemblies tip larger ones, so the framework also names the mechanism by which the [[functional-fractal-isomorphism|fractal isomorphism]] across cortical scales is dynamically maintained. Together these three claims position the framework as the SAN-side analogue of (but stronger than) standard *neural mass model* accounts of low-frequency rhythm.
