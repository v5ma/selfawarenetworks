# Receptor-to-Phase Sensory Quantification

## The idea in plain language

A taste receptor does not send a finished taste into the brain, and a hair cell does not send a
finished sound. Each cell transforms what reaches it. Receptors, second messengers, ion channels,
membrane voltage, calcium, and synaptic release can all help determine the pattern passed onward.

SAN calls attention to the complete route:

```text
outside difference -> receptor response -> cellular change -> modality-specific output -> array pattern
```

The final array pattern is interpreted relative to activity already present in its receiver. That
receiver-relative difference is where this idea joins Phase Wave Differential (PWD). This is not a
claim that every receptor or cell uses one common phase code.

## Why taste and hearing belong together

Umami can involve multiple receptor pathways and combinations of chemical inputs. Hearing begins
with mechanical movement of hair bundles and gating of mechanotransduction channels. In rat outer
hair cells, cAMP up-regulation was reported to reduce mechanotransduction sensitivity in a manner
consistent with reduced gating-spring stiffness. The details differ, but both senses demonstrate
that a cell converts an input through its own morphology and current state.

The downstream output must remain modality-specific. Type II taste cells can use ATP release through
CALHM-family channels; mature cochlear inner hair cells signal through graded receptor potentials and
calcium-dependent glutamate release at ribbon synapses; auditory-nerve fibers then generate action
potentials. A central presynaptic neuron's spike waveform and terminal calcium dynamics are another,
different case. The common SAN operation is receive -> transform -> project, not one universal
receptor-to-spike cascade.

## What is established and what SAN adds

Established biology supports selective receptors, intracellular signaling, channel modulation,
mechanotransduction, graded sensory-cell output, action potentials in appropriate neural cells, and
state-dependent release.

At the mouse calyx of Held, presynaptic action-potential waveform changed the magnitude and timing
of calcium current and transmitter release. This provides a defined central-synapse example of a
multivariable output transformation; it does not make spike duration a universal sensory or novelty
code. [Chao and Yang (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-41120-5)

SAN adds the hypothesis that these transformations should be followed into a receiver-relative
spatiotemporal pattern, and that a declared multivariable model may predict sensory distinctions
better than firing rate alone. That hypothesis is testable; it is not yet a demonstrated universal
sensory code.

The [[synaptic-unreliability-mvr-2021|ionotropic information-channel]] continues this transformation
chain through presynaptic waveform, calcium, release probability, MVR, receptors, and dendritic
state.

`a0448z.md`, a multi-speaker discussion internally dated September 3, 2013, adds an important
boundary: a transmitter's name is not an experience. Effects depend on receptor subtype and
location, cellular state, network context, and downstream consequence. It also recovers ATP's
extracellular transmitter or cotransmitter role. That role is distinct from intracellular ATP's
metabolic-energy role. A drug that activates a receptor does not thereby become an endogenous
neurotransmitter.

## From sensory transformation to a reconstruction test

The receptor-to-array chain does not by itself establish a sensory quality, a complete object, or a
read-write brain-computer interface. It defines measurable stages that an interface would need to
separate: the stimulus or body condition, peripheral and central response, the participant's
discrimination and action, and any controlled intervention. The first causal target is a bounded,
within-person feature or task relation; a full multimodal reconstruction is a later and much harder
test.
