## Reading and writing require calibration

The source distinguishes:

- extracellular spike sorting from complete neuron reconstruction;
- spikes from local field potentials and intracellular currents;
- decoding from meaning;
- stimulation from a learned percept;
- one person's encoding from another person's encoding.

A practical interface therefore needs individualized calibration, user-device co-adaptation,
bounded perturbation, measured readback, and explicit safety constraints. Electrode drift, axons
of passage, pathway specificity, changing neuromodulatory state, and current population context
all affect the result.

The recording also preserves an early multimodal instrument atlas: electrical impedance
tomography, Furaxa microwave imaging, laser Doppler holography, Openwater-style optical methods,
fNIRS, ultrasound, optoacoustic stimulation, invasive arrays, peripheral interfaces, and sensory
substitution. These are candidate routes with different spatial, temporal, depth, calibration,
and safety limits.

See [[bci-instrument-atlas]],
[[neo-mind-cycle-neurofeedback-bci-genealogy]],
[[multimodal-neural-state-reconstruction-perturbation]], and
[[conscious-state-perturbation-tomography]].

## Dendrites and the distributed cellular architecture

The discussion explicitly rejects a point neuron as a complete biological model. Dendritic
branches can support local nonlinear events; morphology and channel distribution constrain what
each branch detects; a somatic spike does not expose every local computation.

This gives a 2021 ancestor of SAN's cellular-agent architecture:

```text
structured dendritic input
-> branch-level transformation
-> state-dependent output
-> receiver-specific population consequence
-> recurrent comparison and action
```

See [[receptive-inceptive-dendritic-projection]], [[neural-tuning]], and
[[electrochemical-to-phase-graph-transformation]].
