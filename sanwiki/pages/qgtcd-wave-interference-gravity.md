Within [[qgtcd]], wave interference is the micro-mechanism that modulates local time density — and thereby gravitational strength.

**Constructive interference** between quantum wave-phases raises local time density (ρₜ), pulling particle random walks inward and producing stronger apparent gravitational attraction. **Destructive interference** lowers effective time density, yielding repulsive or "anti-gravity" effects — reinterpreting phenomena like like-charge repulsion and below-threshold virtual-particle states as failed constructive wave binding.

## Key claims

- Mass "folds" additional time frames locally; waves around mass constructively interfere, sustaining that higher ρₜ.
- Electron–electron repulsion is reread as destructive wave-phase mismatch: the waves cannot constructively combine, so net time density is suppressed and trajectories diverge.
- Quantum decoherence at high temperature arises from random destructive hits disrupting phase alignment, consistent with the general mechanism.
- In cosmic voids, lower time density (no constructive interference from mass) means expansion appears to accelerate — a potential explanation for dark energy without an extra field.
