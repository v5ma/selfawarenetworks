In SAN, **states** are short-term changes in [[synaptic-frequency]] — rapid phasic shifts in
the oscillatory configuration of a cell, array, or cortical column. **Stages** are long-term
structural changes driven by LTP (Long-Term Potentiation) and LTD (Long-Term Depression) that
gradually alter synaptic threshold configurations and life perspective.

The distinction maps onto a two-layer analogy:
- **Hardware layer** — synaptic growth configuration (slow, structural, stages).
- **Software layer** — synaptic frequency patterns (fast, dynamic, states).

## States

A state is a short-term information configuration of an oscillating system — neuron,
[[neural-array]], edge community, or [[cortical-column]] — described by variations in
frequency and magnitude (duration and amplitude). Each of these units is capable of
non-linear spatial computation: where, when, and how the unit is activated influences the
character of its response. The same principle scales from individual neurons to whole-brain
synchrony.

## Stages

A stage emerges after sustained LTP/LTD accumulation crosses a threshold. The resulting
synaptic growth or decay permanently alters how the network interprets incoming patterns,
producing what subjectively feels like a major life-perspective shift. Stages cannot be
induced by short-term stimulation alone — they require sustained time and/or intensity.

## Application to Artificial Minds

The states/stages framework maps directly onto the design of [[sentient-self-aware-neural-network]]
systems. A GPU or TPU simulation needs both:
- Fast matrix operations for phase-state updates (states — human-scale timescales).
- A slower synaptic-weight update layer for structural learning (stages — developmental timescales).

Neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Stanford's Neurogrid) may close the energy/speed gap vs.
GPUs for the fast-state layer while preserving the slow-stage layer in weight matrices.
