## Position

The core SAN move at [[undecidability-does-not-kill-simulation]] is to draw a sharp distinction between *execution* and *decision*: a physical or algorithmic process can run — instantiating local update rules step by step — even when *global* properties of the resulting trajectory are formally undecidable. Gödel- and Tarski-style limits constrain what formal systems can prove about themselves, not what physical dynamics can generate. Consequently the simulation hypothesis, [[oscillating-spacetime-theory|oscillating-spacetime-theory]] cosmologies, and SAN's own [[consciousness-as-high-bandwidth-choice-mechanism|computational-choice]] framing remain unaffected by Gödelian incompleteness. The "oracle" device the challenged paper proposes is an external truth-predicate generator that abandons operational physics for formal semantics; without empirical evidence that quantum measurement supplies decision power over undecidable languages, that device is metaphor rather than mechanism.

The argument situates this rebuttal inside SAN's wider physics program. The same execution-vs-decision distinction underwrites [[oscillating-spacetime-theory]], the [[quantum-gradient-time-crystal-dilation|QGTCD]] family ([[qgtcd-overview]], [[qgtcd-metric-tensor-extension]], [[qgtcd-wave-interference-gravity]], [[qgtcd-2015-crystal-time-dilation]]), the [[super-dark-time|SDT]] and [[super-information-theory|SIT]] frameworks ([[sit-history-corpus-2025]], [[sit-cfs-conceptual-convergence]], [[coherence-conservation-law]]), and the [[quantum-super-time-position|Quantum SuperTimePosition]] ([[supertimeposition]], [[quantum-supertimeposition]]) treatments — all of which model the world as a *generative*, locally-updating substrate rather than a globally-decidable formal system. On the cognition side it links to [[consciousness-as-prediction-determinism]], [[oscillatory-computational-agency]], [[brain-metal-robot-thesis]], [[humans-as-metal-robots]], and the broader [[substrate-independence-neural-computation|substrate-independence]] thesis: brains and simulators alike are operational, not deductive, systems.
