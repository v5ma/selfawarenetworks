# Memory Persistence

Memory can influence behavior long after many individual molecules have turned over. The scientific problem is therefore not how one permanent molecule stores a memory, but how biological organization maintains a usable past-dependent state while its components change.

## Multiple persistence mechanisms

No single mechanism is known to explain every durable memory. Relevant processes include:

- maintenance and remodeling of selected synapses;
- recurring molecular interactions and local protein turnover;
- altered intrinsic excitability;
- stable and changing dendritic structure;
- recurrent reactivation and systems consolidation;
- distributed ensemble connectivity;
- retrieval-dependent updating and reconsolidation.

KIBRA–PKMζ provides strong evidence for active maintenance of potentiated synapses and memory in specific hippocampal paradigms. It should be treated as one experimentally supported mechanism, not the universal answer to memory persistence. Likewise, a stable synaptic state is neither identical to remembered meaning nor sufficient by itself for conscious recollection.

Engram experiments show that manipulating learning-activated populations can alter or elicit behavior associated with a memory. These experiments identify causally important components of a trace; they do not demonstrate a stored movie or a complete memory localized in those cells.

## Persistence with change

A persistent memory need not require an invariant set of active neurons at every observation. Stable behavior can coexist with representational drift and systems-level reorganization. Persistence is therefore best tested at several levels: molecular state, synaptic and structural organization, ensemble participation, circuit interaction, behavior, and subjective report where appropriate.

Retrieval can also modify a trace. Reactivated memories may enter conditions in which they become sensitive to interference and require reconsolidation. Persistence is active and revisable rather than simple playback from fixed storage.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats memory persistence as preservation of **conditional response structure** across changing material. Earlier activity changes what cells and circuits are ready to receive, integrate, and transmit. A partial cue can then recruit a distributed process that regenerates a usable state.

The SAN extension proposes that tonic population activity supplies ongoing receiver context while phasic departures provide differentiated updates. This is an operational hypothesis, not an established consequence of KIBRA–PKMζ, engram tagging, or oscillatory correlation. Evidence would require perturbation and rescue linking the proposed phase organization to pathway-specific transmission, regenerated content, and behavior.
