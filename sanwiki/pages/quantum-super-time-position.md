A deterministic reinterpretation of quantum mechanics proposed by [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]], asserting that apparent quantum randomness is an artifact of **temporal undersampling** — our observational frame is too slow to resolve the rapid deterministic phase cycles of particles, so outcomes appear probabilistic.

## Core claim

Particles are not intrinsically random. Each particle runs a high-frequency deterministic phase cycle, evolving faster than any measurement device can track. When we measure a particle, we sample its phase cycle at a coarse interval, producing outcomes that *look* random but actually reflect a fixed phase relationship between the particle's clock and our observational frame.

In this view, [[napot]]-style oscillation is not confined to neural arrays — it is a fundamental feature of matter itself, operating at sub-measurement timescales.

## The gear analogy

The time-scale mismatch maps cleanly to interlocking gears:

- **Small gear** — the particle's internal phase cycle, spinning far faster than measurement resolution.
- **Large gear** — our observational time frame, which meshes with the small gear only at discrete measurement events.

At each mesh point the particle's phase is "sampled." Probability theory is a practical tool for guessing a particle's phase orientation relative to our larger gear, not evidence of fundamental indeterminism.

## Entanglement as phase-lock

Quantum [[entanglement-phase-lock]] is reframed as **synchronized phase attractors**: two entangled particles share a common phase cycle offset, so measuring particle A reveals A's phase alignment and — because A and B are phase-locked — deterministically constrains B's outcome whenever B is eventually measured.

This eliminates the need for faster-than-light signaling ("spooky action"). [[bell-inequality|Bell inequality]] violations arise because we are undersampling a coherent deterministic system; aggregated snapshots still reproduce the expected quantum correlations.

## Relation to Einstein's intuition

The framework rehabilitates Einstein's "God does not play dice" objection. Dice-like randomness emerges not from nature but from the mismatch between our measurement granularity and the particle's rapid internal clock — exactly the "deeper regularities" Einstein believed must exist.
