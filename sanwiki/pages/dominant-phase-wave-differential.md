**Dominant Phase-Wave Differential** (DPWD) is SAN terminology for a candidate neural pattern that has greater causal influence than competing patterns during a declared interval. It is not an established electrophysiological category and should not mean **the rhythm that becomes conscious**.

## Operationalize dominance

Dominance might refer to one or more measurable properties:

- stronger influence on a receiving population;
- broader or more selective spatial recruitment;
- longer persistence;
- greater resistance to distractors;
- improved decoding at a biological receiver;
- access to working memory or report;
- stronger bias on autonomic or motor action; or
- successful control of subsequent sampling.

These definitions are not equivalent. A pattern can dominate motor selection without being a percept, dominate a local circuit without becoming globally available, or be highly decodable without being used by the organism.

## Receiver-relative differential

A DPWD claim must inherit the full [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] contract: declared reference, source, route, receiver, measured variable, consequence, and artifact controls. Phase, power, rate, burst duration, persistence, and connectivity should remain separate variables until data justify combining them.

## SAN interpretation

The useful SAN question is how competing partial states become differently consequential across a distributed observer-action system. Attention, prediction, learned value, body state, inhibition, recurrence, and action cost can all shape selection. NAPOT may propose how selected relations are redistributed, but selection does not by itself establish tomography or phenomenal identity.

## Tests

Define dominance before analyzing data. Compare the phase-aware metric with rate, power, recurrent-state, attention, and decision models on held-out trials. Perturb the predicted receiver or route, measure the selective loss, and test rescue. Report separately whether the result concerns representation, access, metacognition, action, or phenomenal interpretation.
