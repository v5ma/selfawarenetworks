# a0347z: Superconducting Artificial-Synapse Source Intake

`a0347z` preserves a third-party 2018 science-news article by Fiona McMillan about
Schneider and colleagues' magnetic Josephson-junction device. Micah Blumberg's SAN
repository captured the article as a research input; it is not a Micah-authored
biological mechanism, an original SAN claim, or evidence that an artificial brain or
conscious machine was built.

This reader page maps the imported article to the primary paper without republishing
the copied article body.

## Recovered source operation

The useful engineering operation is:

```text
training pulses plus a controlled magnetic condition
-> change Mn-nanocluster magnetic order
-> change the Josephson junction's critical current
-> give the device a tunable internal state
-> let a surrounding superconducting circuit map that state to spike weighting
```

The sign of the circuit's effective synaptic weight should not be inferred from
"ordered means stronger" or "lower threshold means stronger" alone. The primary paper
measured how magnetic order changed critical current; the complete circuit determines
how that device state weights an input.

## What was demonstrated

Schneider and colleagues fabricated Josephson junctions with superconducting niobium
electrodes and a nominally amorphous silicon barrier containing manganese
nanoclusters. Device measurements at approximately 4 K showed that changing the
nanoclusters' magnetic order changed the junction's critical current. The paper
treated that trainable internal state as an artificial synapse because it could be
used to alter the relation between incoming and outgoing spikes.

The work demonstrated a tunable cryogenic device and characterized its scaling. It
also used circuit models to examine possible neuromorphic use. It did not demonstrate
a brain-scale network, autonomous learning in an embodied agent, biological synaptic
anatomy, or consciousness.

## Functional analogy, not biological equivalence

**Substrate.** A biological chemical synapse consists of living pre- and postsynaptic
cells, vesicles, transmitters, receptors, membrane conductances, and structural
machinery. The device consists of niobium electrodes and a silicon barrier containing
Mn nanoclusters.

**Modifiable state.** Biological synapses and circuits can change through many
presynaptic, postsynaptic, dendritic, intrinsic, inhibitory, and structural processes.
The engineered variable is magnetic order and the resulting superconducting critical
current.

**Signal.** A biological synapse uses neurotransmitter release and receptor-dependent
postsynaptic effects within a circuit. The device uses superconducting
single-flux-quantum voltage pulses within a cryogenic circuit.

**Operating setting.** Biological synapses operate in warm, aqueous, metabolically
maintained tissue. The Josephson device requires cryogenic superconducting hardware
near 4 K.

**Valid overlap.** In both cases, prior input can change an internal state that alters
how later events influence a receiver. The physical mechanisms and system meanings
remain different.

Calling the device an **artificial synapse** therefore identifies a bounded
input-weighting and memory function. It does not make the device anatomically,
biochemically, or developmentally equivalent to a biological synapse.

## Corrections and measurement boundaries

### Energy arithmetic

The imported article states that a device spike below 1 attojoule is about 10,000
times *higher* than a biological event near 10 femtojoules. That direction is
arithmetically reversed:

```text
10 femtojoules = 10,000 attojoules
```

On those quoted per-event numbers, a sub-attojoule device spike is more than 10,000
times **lower**, not higher. This does not prove that a complete superconducting
computer is more efficient than a brain. The device-event estimate excludes important
system boundaries such as refrigeration, field generation, control, communication,
training, and the fact that the compared biological and engineered events are not
identical operations.

### Speed

The paper and NIST release describe very fast individual Josephson-junction events
and potential gigahertz operation. That is not a demonstration of a large trained
network operating at 100 GHz. Device response, circuit simulation, and system
throughput must remain separate result levels.

### Biological plasticity

The popular explanation compresses biological learning into frequent firing lowering
one neuronal threshold. Real synaptic and circuit plasticity can alter release
probability, receptor number or conductance, dendritic integration, intrinsic
excitability, inhibition, structural connectivity, and gene expression. The hardware
analogy is useful only at the narrower level of a trainable connection weight.

## Relation to Artificial Neurology

The source belongs beside [[artificial-neurology|Artificial Neurology]] as an
engineering comparator. It shows that event-based hardware can combine:

1. a trainable internal state;
2. thresholded spiking;
3. state-dependent transfer; and
4. local memory in a device used as a connection.

SAN's stronger program asks how such components might participate in recurrent
world-and-body modeling, action selection, development, agency, and possible
consciousness. This device does not answer those questions. A useful engineering test
would compare trained arrays on defined tasks while measuring learning performance,
robustness, total system energy including cooling and control, and advantages over
strong non-neuromorphic baselines.

## Evidence levels

| Level | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Established in the 2018 device study | Magnetic configuration changed critical current in cryogenic Josephson junctions; the device supplied a tunable state and spiking behavior. |
| Engineering interpretation | A circuit can map that state to a trainable connection weight, motivating the term artificial synapse. |
| Open system question | Whether large arrays remain efficient, controllable, reliable, and useful once refrigeration and system overhead are included. |
| Not established by this source | Biological equivalence, a complete artificial brain, SAN/NAPOT, agency, perception, or machine consciousness. |
