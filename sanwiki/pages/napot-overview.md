# NAPOT Overview

**Neural Array Projection Oscillation Tomography (NAPOT)** is SAN's proposal that distributed neural systems repeatedly gather partial signals, transform them through learned and current biological state, redistribute the result, and update perception and action without a separate inner viewer.

NAPOT is a hypothesis, not an established name for a known anatomical pathway. The strongest version must be tested at declared cellular, circuit, and distributed-network scales.

## The operation in one cycle

1. **Receive:** dendrites, neurons, and populations receive incomplete sensory, bodily, memory, and recurrent signals.
2. **Transform:** receptor state, dendritic integration, inhibition, neuromodulation, recent history, and network dynamics change their consequences.
3. **Project:** axons and population pathways influence selected downstream receivers.
4. **Integrate:** recurrent populations combine overlapping partial information into an action-relevant working state.
5. **Update:** that state changes further sampling, memory, prediction, communication, and movement.

The next cycle begins from the changed state. No single input, neuron, column, or brain area contains the complete percept.

## Three scales that must remain separate

### Micro

- **Candidate SAN motif:** One neuron gathers many synaptic inputs, integrates them, and projects an axonal update.
- **Scientific boundary:** This is a receive-integrate-project motif, not literal tomography by one neuron.

### Meso

- **Candidate SAN motif:** A local circuit or cortical population combines neuron-level updates and redistributes a population state.
- **Scientific boundary:** Local recurrent computation is established; a universal cortical-column algorithm is not.

### Macro

- **Candidate SAN motif:** Distributed cortical, thalamic, association, memory, and motor systems combine partial state and update many systems.
- **Scientific boundary:** Distributed recurrent processing is established; a complete NAPOT reconstruction mechanism remains hypothetical.

See [[san-scale-ladder-and-claim-boundaries|SAN Scale Ladder and Claim Boundaries]] for the full claim ladder.

## Memory completion inside the cycle

During recall, a local learned state need not contain or transmit a complete scene. A partial cue can change selected receivers; direct projections, recurrent excitation, interneuron-mediated inhibition, and present context can then recruit a larger distributed pattern. [[oscillating-group-completion|Oscillating-Group Completion and Re-expression]] describes the 2022 SAN route, while [[telescoping-network-neighborhood-reinstatement|Telescoping Network-Neighborhood Reinstatement]] separates its 2012 structural-transfer ancestor, 2022 local-to-network mechanism, and later geometric analogy.

This is a candidate NAPOT memory operation, not an established universal retrieval circuit. Its phase- and PWD-specific terms earn explanatory status only if they predict or control reinstatement beyond cue identity, firing rate, learned connectivity, inhibition, and ordinary recurrent sequence models.

## Why call it tomography?

In medical tomography, many partial measurements constrain a reconstruction that no single measurement contains. NAPOT uses that as a computational analogy: multiple time-varying neural projections may jointly constrain a working body-world model.

The Encyclopedia calls that candidate distributed, action-ready working state the
[[san-tomogram|SAN Tomogram]]. NAPOT names the proposed operation that repeatedly revises it; the
Tomogram is not a second object located behind the operation.

The analogy does **not** imply that the brain performs a CT scan, stores an optical hologram, reconstructs Euclidean 3D space in one location, or gives a complete image to an inner observer. See [[napot-tomography-not-holography|NAPOT: Tomography, Not Holography]].

## Tonic context, phasic difference, and PWD

SAN uses **tonic** and **phasic** as receiver-relative roles:

- tonic activity supplies a maintained comparison context;
- phasic activity supplies a consequential departure from that context;
- a [[phase-wave-differentials|Phase-Wave Differential]] is the proposed typed, receiver-relative update carried by that departure.

These words do not permanently assign one function to one frequency band. A traveling wave is not automatically a semantic message, and synchrony alone does not establish binding or consciousness. See [[phase-language-firewall|Phase-Language Firewall]] and [[tonic-phasic-canvas|Tonic and Phasic Roles: Canvas and Ink]].

## Cellular transmission is multivariate

The historical NAPOT material links potassium conductance, action-potential duration, calcium entry, and vesicle release. The medically bounded sequence is:

1. membrane and dendritic state influence action-potential initiation and waveform;
2. a spike propagates along the axon;
3. terminal waveform and voltage-gated calcium dynamics influence release probability;
4. transmitter, receptor state, postsynaptic voltage, and dendritic location determine the receiver's consequence.

This supports variable, state-dependent transmission. It does not establish a universal vesicle-count alphabet or prove that one spike waveform encodes a complete spatial object. See [[action-potential-magnitude-via-duration|Action-Potential Magnitude via Duration]], [[multi-vesicular-release|Multivesicular Release]], and [[synapse|Synapse]].

## Established components and the SAN extension

Established neuroscience supports nonlinear dendritic integration, recurrent population computation, structured population dynamics, traveling cortical waves, and state-dependent synaptic transmission. SAN adds the stronger proposal that these components form a repeated projection-and-reconstruction architecture whose distributed state is used by the system itself.

Component evidence includes:

- [Polsky, Mel, and Schiller (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1253) on branch-local nonlinear integration;
- [Mante et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12742) on context-dependent recurrent population computation;
- [Churchland et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11129) on structured neural population dynamics during movement; and
- [Davis et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2802-y) on traveling cortical waves and perceptual sensitivity.

None of these studies demonstrates NAPOT, PWD content, or the complete SAN account of perception.
