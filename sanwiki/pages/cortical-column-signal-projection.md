## Core hypothesis

Sensory input arrives through thalamocortical pathways and is "columnized" — narrowed into column-local traffic as it rises through layers 1–4. At layers 5 and 6, apical and pyramidal cells link across columns. These inter-columnar connections do not merely synchronize timing; they transmit actual sensory and motor information — "virtual portraits" — between cortical areas.

The analogy: each cortical column is like a projector node in a fiber-optic network. It bottles a spatial-temporal pattern of reality, then re-projects it to distant cortical areas. The receiving area must listen to what is being projected in order to coordinate its response — this coordination requirement explains why [[oscillatory-synchrony]] is non-optional for brain function.

## Layered traffic model

- **Layers 1–4**: primarily columnar (bottom-up thalamocortical, feedforward sensory)
- **Layers 5–6**: apical/pyramidal cells bridge columns laterally; callosal bridge carries inter-areal traffic
- **Inter-areal traffic**: modulates sensorimotor activity; carries both sensory and motor pattern representations

## Connection to tomography

The projection metaphor directly anticipates [[slice-projection-theorem]] applied to neural imaging. Just as CT scanning reconstructs 3D volumes from 2D projections at multiple angles, the brain may reconstruct a unified percept by integrating projections from many cortical columns. This connects to the author's broader interest in [[tomography]] across modalities (EEG, fNIRS, electrical impedance tomography).

## Each neuron as a pattern system

A second key claim: each neuron is its own pattern-recognition and pattern-creation system. Neurons collectively create patterns with both temporal and spatial distinctions. The brain "virtualizes" reality so that it can flow across neural networks — each region recognizing and coordinating based on the flowing signal.
