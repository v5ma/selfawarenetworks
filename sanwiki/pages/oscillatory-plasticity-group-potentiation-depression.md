# Oscillatory Plasticity and Group Potentiation/Depression

## The idea in plain language

Imagine a group of cells participating in a larger rhythm. A smaller cluster can begin to move out
of step with the larger group while becoming more tightly coordinated with itself.

SAN gave names to both sides of that change:

- **GSTD:** the cluster becomes less connected in practice to the larger rhythm.
- **GSTP:** the cluster becomes more connected in practice to its own members.

The two changes can happen together. That is what makes the proposal more specific than saying
that synchrony is good and desynchrony is bad.

## Why it matters

Such a transition could temporarily select an assembly, separate one route from competing routes,
or prepare a memory pattern for replay. If repeated activity later changes synapses, the temporary
group state could also bias longer-term learning.

## Evidence boundary

Neural timing, oscillations, inhibition, LTP, LTD, and spike-timing-dependent plasticity are real.
The paired GSTP/GSTD operator is a SAN hypothesis. It must be measured at a declared scale and must
predict a downstream effect before it can be treated as more than a useful model.
