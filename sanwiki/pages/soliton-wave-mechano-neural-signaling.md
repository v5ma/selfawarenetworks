Neurons are not purely electrochemical signal devices — they also undergo *mechanical* deformation during action potentials. During an [[action-potential]], the cell body and dendrites physically expand and contract (APsyn: action-potential-synchronous mechanical oscillation). Research on how physical forces control brain development suggests that mechano interactions are as fundamental as electrical ones.

## Soliton wave hypothesis

A soliton is a [[gh-a0206z|self]]-reinforcing solitary wave that maintains its shape while propagating at constant velocity. In the context of neural signaling, the soliton hypothesis proposes that the mechanical compression wave traveling along the axon membrane during an [[action-potential|action potential]] is a soliton — not merely a side-effect of ion channel opening, but a co-primary signal carrier.

This has radical implications for neural coding: if mechanical soliton waves propagate along axons and dendrites, then the timing, amplitude, and interference patterns of those waves encode information in addition to (or instead of) purely electrical spike patterns. The acoustic / vibration dimension of neural signaling would be invisible to standard electrode recordings, which only measure voltage.

## Multi-modal dendritic expansion

Dendritic expansion during action potentials suggests that the mechanical state of a neuron's arbor is a multimodal input channel. External mechanical forces (touch, pressure, sound-conducted vibration) could modulate firing criteria directly via membrane stretch, providing a pathway for [[multimodal-integration]] that does not route through conventional synaptic receptors.

## Connection to SAN

In the SAN framework, this supports the view that [[action-potential]] amplitude carries information beyond a binary spike. Mechanical expansion/contraction during an AP may scale with [[multi-vesicle-release-spatial-encoding]] amplitude, creating a coupled electromechanical channel. The soliton's spatial [[gh-a0206z|self]]-consistency also makes it a candidate for carrying [[spatial-information]] along an axon without dispersion.
