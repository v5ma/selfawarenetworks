# Body Model as Control Surface

**Body Model as Control Surface** is a SAN bridge concept for the motor side of neural rendering. It says that the brain does not only render an external scene; it also maintains an action-ready model of the body as the surface through which perception, prediction, agency, and movement control are coordinated.

This page is a bridge, not a new empirical result. It connects established sensorimotor physiology to the proposed SAN/NAPOT interpretation without collapsing those categories.

## Core Idea

A useful SAN account of embodiment needs one explicit loop:

1. A goal and current body estimate shape a motor command.
2. The motor command has predicted sensory consequences through efference copy or corollary discharge.
3. Movement changes incoming visual, proprioceptive, tactile, vestibular, and interoceptive evidence.
4. Distributed comparator systems detect where prediction and evidence agree or mismatch.
5. The body model updates pose, agency, ownership, self-location, and action policy.
6. The updated body model constrains the next action.

In SAN language, the body model is therefore not just a passive body map. It is a **control surface**: the changing embodied state through which the system selects, checks, corrects, and experiences action.

## Established Physiology

The following claims are conventional enough to use as anchors:

- Voluntary movement depends on distributed cortical, brainstem, spinal, basal-ganglia, cerebellar, thalamic, and peripheral circuits.
- Proprioceptive, tactile, vestibular, visual, and interoceptive signals constrain body state.
- Efference copy and corollary discharge support prediction of sensory consequences during action.
- Forward-model and comparator processes help explain why self-generated sensory changes can be treated differently from unexpected changes.
- Body ownership, agency, pose, and self-location can be shifted by multisensory matching, mismatch, delay, and experimental perturbation.
- Somatotopic maps constrain body-part variables, but they are not a single body screen or a complete self-center.

These points support a medically careful body-model frame. They do not by themselves establish SAN, NAPOT, GCS, phase-wave differentials, consciousness, or a literal internal display.

### Recovered virtual-touch route

`a0450z.md` preserves a 2012-2013 collection of research leads, including the study later published
as Shokur et al., "Expanding the primate body schema in sensorimotor cortex by virtual touches of an
avatar" ([PNAS, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1308459110)). In that brain-machine-interface
experiment, nonhuman primates controlled a virtual arm and received artificial tactile information.
The result supplies a bounded empirical bridge for SAN: sensorimotor contingencies involving an
artificial effector can alter the cortical representation used for embodied control.

The paper is the evidence. `a0450z.md` is a curated source-intake record showing that the result
entered Micah's research program; its copied news excerpts are not independent confirmation and do
not establish NAPOT or machine consciousness.

## SAN/NAPOT Interpretation

SAN can treat the body model as a recurring many-to-one-to-many update:

- many partial streams contribute body/world state;
- receiving populations integrate the partial state;
- an updated working representation becomes available to perception, memory, affect, language, and action systems;
- downstream systems update their local state and participate in the next cycle.

This is where the page connects to [[napot-overview|NAPOT]] and [[static-map-vs-active-render]]. A static map is the stored anatomical or learned constraint; an active body render is the current population state built from those constraints plus live evidence.

The body model can be described with variables such as:

| Variable | What it means | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Pose | Where the body and limbs are estimated to be | Not one cortical pixel map |
| Ownership | Whether a signal or object is treated as "mine" | Not proof of consciousness by itself |
| Agency | Whether an event is treated as caused by one's own action | Not a free-will proof |
| Self-location | Where the embodied viewpoint is estimated to be | A distributed body-relative estimate, not one observer location |
| Action state | What the body is doing or preparing to do | Not reducible to M1 alone |
| Error or mismatch | Difference between predicted and observed consequences | Not automatically a phase-wave differential |

## GCS Relation

[[gamma-consideration-sandwich-diagram|GCS]] supplies a proposed timing rule for when predicted action consequences and incoming evidence become mutually effective in a declared cortical target.

The medically careful version is:

- **Established:** motor commands, efference copy, sensory feedback, and comparator processes are real components of sensorimotor control.
- **Proposed by SAN:** phase-structured interactions help decide which signals update the embodied render.
- **Still provisional:** the detailed GCS layer/frequency assignment, including any specific layer-2/3, layer-4, or layer-5 role, must be target-specific and experimentally tested.
- **Retired boundary:** PV interneurons are not an ascending proprioceptive afferent channel. They may regulate local timing, but peripheral proprioception reaches cortex through established sensory pathways.

This distinction matters because the strongest public version of SAN should not ask the reader to accept a universal laminar circuit. It should ask whether a specified circuit, task, and time window show an added body-state update signal beyond simpler explanations.

## Distributed Observer and Embodied Control

The body model is part of the observer operation, not an image delivered to some other system. Sparse, distributed cells receive body- and world-related changes, transform them relative to ongoing tonic state, re-express them through cellular output, and use the returning consequences to update perception and action. In aggregate, that closed field-cell and sensorimotor process is the SAN observer.

Within this account, the tonic action field is the current embodied comparison canvas and phasic synaptic or cellular changes are the differences registered against it. Each effective change alters participating neurons and their shared electrical and electromagnetic activity, then influences later sampling, consideration, autonomic response, or movement. Established physiology supports the underlying currents, fields, recurrence, and sensorimotor loops; their interpretation as a constitutive observer is the SAN hypothesis.

The safer language is:

- "working body model"
- "embodied latent state"
- "sensorimotor update"
- "population-level body-state representation"
- "action-ready control surface"

Riskier language should be bounded when used:

- "screen"
- "television"
- "mental ink"
- "camera"
- "speaker"
- "viewer"

Those metaphors are useful only as a coordinated receive-transform-project motif: dendrites play capture-like roles, cellular outputs play transmission-like roles, tonic activity supplies the comparison canvas, and phasic events supply the changing ink. The public SAN Wiki should translate each metaphor into population activity, synaptic integration, recurrent signaling, field change, and behaviorally measurable update.

## Claim Boundary

This page supports a bounded SAN claim:

> The body model can be treated as an active control surface that integrates predicted action consequences with visual, proprioceptive, tactile, vestibular, and interoceptive evidence. SAN/NAPOT proposes that phase-sensitive recurrent population dynamics participate in that update, but this remains a testable interpretation rather than an established medical fact.
