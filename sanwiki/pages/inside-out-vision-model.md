# Inside-Out Vision Model

> **Scientific boundary:** Inside-out vision is a SAN interpretive model of active, recurrent visual construction. It is not a claim that the eyes emit an image, that cortical columns are literal lenses, or that the brain contains a screen and viewer. Established retinal, thalamic, cortical, subcortical, oculomotor, and body-state pathways remain the biological substrate.

The useful inside-out claim is that perception is not a passive copy of an outside scene. Light changes the retina, and those changes are repeatedly transformed across neural and bodily systems. The resulting percept is an internally maintained, action-linked working model constrained by sensory evidence.

## What enters the nervous system

The brain never receives a photograph. Photoreceptors alter retinal circuits; retinal ganglion cells send spike trains through the optic nerve; and those signals are transformed in the lateral geniculate nucleus, superior colliculus, primary visual cortex, and many interacting extrastriate and association systems. Eye position, eye movements, attention, memory, expectation, and action also change the state against which new retinal evidence is interpreted.

This makes vision constructive without making it unconstrained. The model is corrected by receptor input, movement consequences, occlusion, binocular disparity, motion, and other sources of evidence.

## What "inside-out" means

The phrase names a reversal of explanatory emphasis:

1. An outside event changes receptors.
2. Receptor activity is transformed into neural signals.
3. Local circuits register features and context.
4. Recurrent and feedback pathways alter those local responses.
5. Distributed visual, memory, spatial, body, and action systems maintain a revisable working model.
6. Eye and body movements sample the world again, producing the next update.

The resulting activity is "inside" because it is physically realized by the organism. It remains world-constrained because action and sensing continually test it.

## The eye, camera, and display metaphors

SAN's older camera/microphone and screen/speaker language is best read as a role analogy:

- receptors and dendritic compartments receive consequential differences;
- cells and local populations transform those differences;
- axons and synapses re-express effects to other receivers;
- recurrent networks use the new effects to alter what happens next.

A neuron is not literally a camera or pixel, and an apical dendrite is not a television. The analogy becomes informative only after each role is translated into an identified biological compartment, signal, receiver, and timescale.

Likewise, the older "fractal eyeball" image is conceptual genealogy, not anatomy. The cortex is not a retina, the midbrain is not a focal point, and cortical columns are not lenses. The defensible remainder is that partial, differently situated signals can be combined across scales into an action-relevant internal model.

## No inner viewer

SAN does not relocate perception into a hidden agent. The network itself performs the receiving, transforming, comparing, and acting.

In SAN language, ongoing tonic activity supplies the current comparison context or canvas. Phasic cellular and synaptic changes supply changing ink. Those terms are relational: the relevant baseline and change depend on the receiving cell or circuit, behavioral state, timescale, and measurement. There is no requirement that every neuron share one universal rhythm.

The observer-action is the distributed aggregate of participating cells, fields, recurrent pathways, body state, and motor consequences. A recognizably decoded image can demonstrate recoverable information, but it does not reveal a literal internal picture or a viewer watching it.

## Scale map

- **Micro:** photoreceptors, retinal circuits, synapses, dendrites, and neurons transform local input.
- **Meso:** recurrent populations in retina, thalamus, cortex, and superior colliculus integrate context and control sampling.
- **Macro:** visual, spatial, memory, oculomotor, vestibular, proprioceptive, and action systems maintain a body-centered working model.

A similar receive-transform-re-express motif may appear at each scale, but that resemblance is not proof that one identical operator or code is repeated everywhere.

## What would strengthen the SAN interpretation

A useful SAN model should identify, for a named task:

- the sensory variable and receptor population;
- the receiving circuit and its ongoing state;
- the phasic change being measured;
- the recurrent or feedback path that changes interpretation;
- the downstream receiver that uses the update;
- the action or report that should change under causal perturbation.

Evidence that feedback or phase carries information in one circuit supports a bounded mechanism. It does not by itself prove a whole-brain tomographic render or a constitutive field-cell observer.

## Failure conditions

The strong inside-out interpretation would be weakened if its predicted recurrent or receiver-relative variables could be removed without affecting the relevant perceptual discrimination, state update, or action. It would also fail as an explanation if it could not distinguish its predictions from an ordinary feedforward-plus-decision model.

## Primary evidence anchors

- Zipser, Lamme, and Schiller found contextual modulation in macaque V1, including effects compatible with feedback or recurrent influence: [Contextual modulation in primary visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.16-22-07376.1996).
- Muckli and colleagues measured feedback-related information in nonstimulated regions of human early visual cortex: [Contextual feedback to superficial layers of V1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2015.08.057).
- Bastos and colleagues reported frequency-specific feedforward and feedback influences in primate visual cortex: [Visual areas exert feedforward and feedback influences through distinct frequency channels](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.018).
- Sommer and Wurtz causally studied a thalamic corollary-discharge pathway relevant to visual stability across eye movements: [A pathway in primate brain for internal monitoring of movements](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1069590).
