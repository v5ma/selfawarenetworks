# Dendritic Morphology and Memory

Dendritic morphology influences what inputs a neuron can receive, how local inputs interact, and how signals reach the soma. Experience-dependent structural plasticity can therefore contribute to learning and memory by changing the neuron's future response space. A dendritic tree is not, by itself, a complete memory or a literal picture of remembered content.

## Established biology

Many excitatory synapses terminate on dendritic spines. Activity can alter spine size, receptor content, cytoskeletal organization, stability, formation, and elimination. In hippocampal CA1 preparations, stimulation of an individual spine can produce input-specific enlargement and increased AMPA-receptor-mediated current through mechanisms involving NMDA receptors, calcium, CaMKII, and actin remodeling.

Learning-related spine formation and stabilization have also been observed in vivo. These findings support structural plasticity as one component of a distributed memory trace. They do not establish a one-spine/one-memory correspondence, a universal relationship between spine size and memory strength, or a static “tree-shape engram.” Spine turnover and persistence vary by brain region, cell type, developmental stage, and task.

Changes to larger dendritic branches can alter compartmental integration, but ordinary learning is not known to rewrite whole dendritic branching topology every time a memory forms. Most direct evidence concerns synapses, spines, local dendritic excitability, and circuit connectivity.

## SAN interpretation

SAN treats morphology as **changed readiness**. Past activity can modify which future combinations are consequential for a dendritic compartment and how strongly the neuron affects downstream cells. The remembered event remains distributed across cells, synapses, pathways, body state, and recurrent dynamics.

A source-faithful SAN hypothesis is therefore:

> Structural plasticity changes the receiver and transmitter conditions under which later activity is recognized and propagated; live network activity regenerates a memory rather than reading a complete scene from a dendritic tree.

Microtubules participate in dendritic structure, transport, and remodeling. A specific microtubule oscillation or polarity code for remembered content remains a separate hypothesis and is not established by spine-plasticity findings.

## Testable bridge

To support the SAN extension, an experiment would need to relate a measured structural change to later compartment-specific integration, population phase organization, receiver-relative transmission, and behavior. Perturbing the structural change should alter those downstream relations, and restoring it or an equivalent circuit state should rescue them.
