EEG-based biofeedback in VR measures mental states — not individual thoughts — and uses that signal to modulate the virtual environment in real time, forming a primitive but functional [[brain-computer-interface]].

## What EEG Can and Cannot Do

Current EEG is analogous to a microphone placed outside a football stadium: it detects aggregate neural activity but cannot resolve individual plays. It cannot enable volitional mind control. What it can reliably measure: gross arousal level, sleep stages, and the shift between active thought (beta waves, 12–38 Hz) and meditative states (slower waves, 3–12 Hz).

This limitation defines the design space: EEG is best suited for [[mental-state-assessment]] rather than command-and-control interfaces.

## Biofeedback Loop in Practice

Hardware such as the Muse system embeds 8 sensors into a VR headset via forehead and ear contact points. The signal drives environmental parameters — stormy audio during active thought, calm beach audio during meditation — creating a closed-loop feedback system that trains the user's own [[self-modeling]] capacity.

Artist Android Jones extended this pattern in *Microdose VR*: meditative state unlocks visual elements. He found heartbeat data even more reliable than EEG as a biofeedback channel.

## Privacy Implication

Biofeedback data can reveal intimate mental states before the user consciously recognizes them. This positions EEG/VR systems at the intersection of [[information-theory]] and personal sovereignty — a concern that prefigures the broader [[neural-lace-ar-vr-no-glasses]] vision.
