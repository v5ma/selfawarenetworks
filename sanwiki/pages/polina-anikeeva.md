Associate Professor of Materials Science and Engineering at MIT; pioneer in biology-driven neural interface hardware, magnetic nanoparticle brain stimulation, and the argument for generalist-brain + specialist-AI collaboration. Cited in [[gh-resnote04|Micah]] Blumberg]]'s [[neural-lace-journal]].

## Key contributions

**Nanotransducers** — magnetic nanoparticles (1/20,000th the size of a coffee ground) that can be injected into brain tissue, receive external magnetic signals, and convert them into ionic triggers. Enables wireless, minimally invasive neural stimulation without surgical implants.

**Ion channel control as the target** — Anikeeva frames neural interfacing as controlling ion concentrations inside and outside neurons, which governs membrane voltages and therefore firing. This maps naturally to the SAN view of [[action-potential]] and [[dendritic-computation]] as the fundamental computational events.

**"Biology-driven design"** vs. machine-inspired electronics — her lab's central design principle: electronics in the body must be soft, flexible, and bio-compatible. The interface must meet the brain's geometry, not the other way around.

## Generalist brain + specialist AI

Anikeeva's most SAN-relevant argument: AI develops far faster than neural hardware because software is democratic and ubiquitous. The practical path to augmentation is not uploading the brain but *connecting* a specialist AI module to the generalist human brain via a high-bandwidth interface. The human provides creativity, judgment, and contextual integration; the AI provides domain speed and scale.

This framing resonates with SAN's model of [[self-aware-network]] as an integration architecture — conscious experience as the binding of many specialist processes into a unified generalist observer.
