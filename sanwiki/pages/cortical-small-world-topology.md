The timing traces and entification panel illustrate that proposal; they are not recordings or proof that connectivity alone produces consciousness.

In graph theory, **small-world** describes high clustering together with short characteristic path lengths relative to appropriate comparison networks. Watts and Strogatz's [1998 model](https://doi.org/10.1038/30918) showed how this combination can influence propagation and synchronizability. Applying it to cortex requires specifying what the nodes and edges represent: individual cells, local populations, cortical areas, anatomical projections, and statistical functional connections are not interchangeable graphs.

Anatomical tracer studies in mouse and macaque cortex show strong relationships between connection distance, weight, and cortical geometry ([Horvat et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002512)). The figure's few long arrows illustrate routes beyond local neighborhoods, not a measured percentage of inter-area connectivity or a proof of globally optimal information density.

## Structural features

- Local and longer-range axons provide distinct paths for synaptic influence; their strength, delay, and target identity matter as well as their presence.
- The illustrated cylinders group cortical circuitry for explanation. They do not assign a universal neuron count, sharp physical wall, or identical algorithm to every cortical column.
- The blue cell bodies in the cylinders are **cortical neurons**. Thalamic relay cell bodies are in the thalamus; their axons reach cortical targets. A thalamic-input label at layer 4 denotes an input destination, not a thalamic soma located in cortex. Pathway-specific thalamocortical experiments resolve this distinction ([Cruikshank et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2826223/)).
- Synapses transform signals through their molecular and physiological state; an anatomical edge is not a fixed-weight electrical wire.

**Figure scope:** This is a generic granular-neocortex schematic, not a connectome or a universal laminar wiring diagram. The layer labels are landmarks, not exclusive input/output compartments. Blue denotes a schematic cortical cell of unspecified subtype, not a fourth neuronal class; the color key is not an exhaustive taxonomy. The aligned traces show one possible coordinated timing pattern, not whole-brain synchrony. The potentiated synapse is one possible efficacy-change illustration, not a rule that all LTP has the same pre- and postsynaptic expression.

## Relevance to SAN

SAN uses local interaction and larger-scale coupling as part of the proposed architecture of [[napot]] and [[entification]]. In the historical [[gh-b0103ywhisper|Oscillating Volumes transcription]], cells and oscillating groups are considered as coupled sensor-transmitter systems: local events affect other groups, activity is redistributed, and coordinated patterns can be expressed at larger scales. This is the source context for the network interpretation, rather than a claim that one diagram establishes the full mechanism.

Within that proposal, [[tonic-oscillation|tonic activity]] supplies an ongoing context and [[phasic-tonic-relationship|phasic departures]] can carry local distinctions into broader interaction. Excitation, inhibition, timing, and receiver state all belong in that account. Small-world statistics alone do not select one oscillation frequency, impose one global clock, or determine what a receiver makes of the activity.

The memory extension asks how changed synaptic efficacy and structure alter later network responses and [[attractor]] dynamics. That is a substantive connection to [[camkii-microtubule-ltp]] and [[synaptic-molecular-complexity-ltp]], but topology by itself does not show that one synaptic change captures an attractor or fixes the duration of memory. The proposed connection must be evaluated in a specified circuit with a measured plasticity mechanism and receiver consequence.
