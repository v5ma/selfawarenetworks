# Action Potential

An **action potential** is a regenerative membrane-voltage event produced by voltage-dependent ion
conductances. In a typical cortical pyramidal neuron, synaptic and dendritic state influence spike
initiation near the axon initial segment (AIS). The event then propagates through the axon and can
also backpropagate into the soma and dendrites.

> **Scientific boundary:** A spike is a real cellular output event, but it is not a universal semantic quantum, a fixed vesicle-count symbol, or consciousness by itself. At a chemical synapse, terminal voltage is transformed into local calcium entry, probabilistic vesicle fusion, transmitter action, and a receiver-specific postsynaptic consequence.

## The established cellular sequence

1. Synaptic currents, active dendritic conductances, inhibition, and somatic state alter membrane
   voltage across a neuron.
2. In cortical pyramidal cells, the AIS normally initiates the axonal action potential. Its
   specialized sodium-channel organization supports rapid, precise initiation
   ([Kole et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn2040)).
3. The spike usually propagates reliably through the axonal arbor under ordinary conditions, but
   reliability and waveform can depend on branch geometry, channel state, recent activity, and
   preparation. Propagation failure is possible; it is not the default explanation for every
   synaptic difference.
4. At a chemical terminal, the arriving waveform opens voltage-gated calcium channels.
5. Local calcium, active-zone organization, vesicle-pool state, and release machinery determine
   probabilistic vesicle fusion.
6. Released transmitter acts on receptors of a particular postsynaptic target.
7. The receiver combines that response with its other inputs and ongoing cellular and network state.

The spike therefore does not cross a chemical synapse intact. What reaches the next cell is a
transformed consequence, not a copy of the presynaptic voltage trace.

## All-or-none is real but incomplete as a communication description

"All-or-none" describes regenerative spike initiation under defined conditions. It does not imply
that every neuron, compartment, spike in a train, or terminal has an identical waveform. Width,
peak, afterpotential, conduction reliability, and terminal calcium can vary with cell type, channel
state, geometry, recent activity, temperature, and modulation.

This qualification is not only theoretical:

- In cortical neurons, modest changes in presynaptic somatic voltage altered axonal spike amplitude
  and duration and changed the average postsynaptic response through a calcium-dependent mechanism.
  Shu et al. described this as mixed analogue and triggered transmission
  ([Shu et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04720)).
- Somatic state and Kv1 channels can control spike repolarization in cortical axon collaterals and
  boutons ([Foust et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2752-11.2011)).
- Presynaptic excitability and waveform can be locally plastic, and activity-dependent broadening
  can facilitate transmitter release in bounded preparations
  ([Hoppa et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.09.038);
  [Cho et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2000790117)).

These results support a real **analogue-to-digital-to-analogue bridge**:

**somatodendritic state -> AIS event -> axonal and terminal waveform -> calcium and release ->
receiver-specific response**

They do not establish a universal action-potential-duration code. In some cells or conditions,
channel interactions can stabilize the relevant terminal consequence, so a waveform change at one
site need not produce a larger synaptic output everywhere.

## One axon can produce heterogeneous consequences

An action potential may invade many branches, but the terminals are not interchangeable copies.
In young rat layer 2/3 pyramidal cells, single spikes propagated reliably through the imaged arbor
while calcium transients varied by as much as tenfold among boutons on the same collateral
([Koester and Sakmann, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7793.2000.00625.x)).
Active-zone size, calcium-channel organization, vesicle availability, modulation, recent activity,
and target-cell identity can all contribute to local differences
([Holderith et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3137);
[Eltes et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2024-16.2017)).

This supplies the medically accurate operation behind part of SAN's older "print function"
language: one regenerative event can be transformed into a spatially distributed pattern of
conditional consequences. The evidence does **not** show that a neuron freely assigns an arbitrary
semantic symbol or exact vesicle number to every bouton on every spike.

## Bursts and dendritic interaction

In some pyramidal neurons, distal apical input and backpropagating spikes can recruit dendritic
calcium electrogenesis and change somatic output from isolated spikes to bursts
([Larkum et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/18686)). In a studied layer 5 visual-cortical
pyramidal-cell circuit, basal and apical drive jointly affected output frequency
([Shai et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004090)).

The direction must be stated correctly. An AIS-generated spike can backpropagate into dendrites;
dendritic depolarization can then influence subsequent axonal output. Apical and basal dendrites
are both receptive and integrative. This interaction is cell-type-, circuit-, and state-dependent,
not a universal basal-sensory plus apical-prediction gate.

## Physical consequences are not automatically separate codes

Action potentials have electrical and ionic dynamics with measurable magnetic, mechanical,
metabolic, and thermal consequences. Magnetic detection of a single-neuron action potential has
been demonstrated under specialized close-range conditions
([Barry et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1601513113)), and electromechanical coupling has
been modeled and measured as a physical accompaniment
([El Hady and Machta, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7697)).

Those findings establish physical correlates. They do not by themselves show that the brain
independently decodes each correlate as a semantic communication channel.

The recovered SAN operator is:

**learned and current somatodendritic state -> AIS event -> conditional axonal-arbor transformation
-> target-specific chemical consequences -> receiver-relative population update**

This is more specific than saying "neurons fire" and more defensible than saying "one neuron prints
a picture." It preserves the proposal that a neuron can contribute a structured one-to-many update
without turning an apical dendrite into an output terminal or treating stochastic synapses as a
deterministic display.

## What SAN adds as an open hypothesis

Established physiology already supports mixed analogue-triggered transmission, waveform-dependent
terminal effects, bouton heterogeneity, and receiver-specific consequences. [[self-aware-networks|SAN]] adds the joined
systems proposition that:

1. learned cellular structure and ongoing state can make the spatial output pattern reproducible;
2. receivers interpret each consequence relative to their own tonic state;
3. coincident phasic differences can alter local recruitment, inhibition, or effective routing; and
4. many such cellular transformations can contribute to a distributed NAPOT observer-action cycle.

One action potential does not itself perform tomography or observation. The proposed rendering
operation belongs to interacting populations across micro, meso, and macro scales.

## Discriminating tests

The stronger SAN interpretation should be tested incrementally:

1. **Waveform test:** after controlling spike count and timing, does somatodendritic state predict
   terminal waveform, calcium, or release better than a stereotyped-spike model?
2. **Arbor test:** after controlling anatomy, bouton size, active-zone composition, vesicle-pool
   state, and modulation, are learned spatial output patterns reproducible across identified boutons?
3. **Receiver test:** do those patterns predict receiver-state changes beyond transmitter identity,
   connectivity, rate, common input, and recent history?
4. **Population test:** does a declared receiver-relative phase-wave-differential model add
   predictive or causal value beyond ordinary timing, rate, synchrony, and network-state models?
5. **Behavior test:** does selective perturbation of the identified output pattern alter the
   predicted perceptual or motor consequence while preserving total spike count?

The hypothesis would be narrowed if known terminal heterogeneity, stochastic release, connectivity,
and common modulation fully explain the incremental result. It would gain support if learned,
reproducible output patterns and receiver-relative consequences survive those controls.

> **SAN boundary:** The axonal arbor may implement a richer conditional output transformation than a binary-spike abstraction. It is not established that one neuron prints a stored picture, performs tomography, or assigns an independently programmable neurotransmitter quantity to every bouton on each spike.
