Consciousness in the SAN framework is distributed across a **fractal hierarchy of semi-autonomous modules** — each module is itself a network capable of local processing, and modules nest inside larger modules without any single control center. The octopus is the prototype: its arms each contain substantial independent neural processing while still participating in a whole-organism intelligence.

This is distinct from both centralized models (one conscious locus) and mere modularity (fixed-function specialized regions). SAN's fractal modules are recursively [[gh-a0206z|self]]-similar: the architecture that makes a whole brain conscious is repeated inside each cortical column, and inside each neuron's dendritic tree.

## Key claims

- **Every brain is its own module**: sub-networks within the brain have the same structural properties as the full network — local sense, local process, local broadcast.
- **Octopus evidence**: octopus arms navigate obstacles and manipulate objects semi-autonomously; removing the central brain does not eliminate all behavior. This is the clearest biological existence proof of fractal modular intelligence.
- **[[brainwave-entrainment|Brainwave entrainment]] across persons**: when two individuals in different brain-frequency states (alpha vs. delta) interact, their oscillations tend to converge — the dominant [[attractor]] "wins" or they meet at an intermediate frequency. This is inter-brain fractal synchrony, the same entrainment mechanism operating between modules inside one brain, now operating between separate brains.
- **Proposed experiment**: six participants pre-entrained to different brainwave states; measure whether conversation produces delta, alpha, theta, or beta convergence — and whether the strongest initial entrainment state dominates.
