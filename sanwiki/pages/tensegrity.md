## Post-SAN

The mainstream literature most directly adjacent to this concept is the cellular-tensegrity tradition descended from Donald Ingber's 1990s-and-onward work on the cytoskeleton as a tensegrity structure, the broader soft-matter and biotensegrity traditions, and the Buckminster-Fuller architectural tensegrity that supplies the term. The SAN reading converges with cellular-tensegrity on the empirical claim that cells are tensegrity-balanced structures whose mechanical state matters for their function, and converges with biotensegrity on the multi-scale generalization to organisms; it adds the substrate-level commitment that the same balance-of-tension-and-compression principle is what makes oscillatory communication across scales — mechanical, acoustic, chemical, and rippling-wave — possible, and that it supplies the precondition for the [[soliton-wave-mechano-neural-signaling]] reading and the [[oscillating-cell-environmental-encoding]] reading.
