# Coincidence as a Bit

**Coincidence as a bit** is SAN's proposal that a consequential neural information event can be
defined relative to a receiver: selected inputs overlap within that receiver's present spatial,
temporal, and physiological criteria, and the conjunction changes what the receiver does next.
The proposal does not claim priority over biological coincidence detection, that every synapse is
a detector, that one spike always equals one bit, or that every local coincidence becomes a
brain-wide coherent state.

## What "bit" means here

For a declared receiver `R`, state `s_R`, input set `X`, and observation window `W`, a model can
define a binary criterion readout:

`b_R(W) = 1` when `C_R(s_R, X in W)` is met; otherwise `b_R(W) = 0`. [1]

This is a researcher's operational partition of two outcomes: criterion met or not met. It is not
a claim that the neuron contains a digital transistor or that the complete neural event is binary.
A two-outcome variable has one bit of entropy only when its alternatives are equally probable.
The biological event can also vary in location, timing, waveform, burst structure, release,
plasticity, and downstream consequence.

Before calling an event a candidate neural bit, name:

1. the receiving compartment, cell, or circuit;
2. the candidate inputs and their spatial arrangement;
3. the effective time window and present receiver state;
4. the criterion and alternative outcomes;
5. the immediate electrical, biochemical, or firing consequence; and
6. the downstream system that can use the distinction.

## Source recovery: the 2017 proposition

The phrase developed through a donor-and-extension sequence rather than appearing as a claim that
Micah discovered coincidence detection.

- **Peter Tse's 2013 precursor:** *The Neural Basis of Free Will* organized rapid synaptic
  plasticity and physically implemented neural criteria into the theory of
  [[criterial-causation|criterial causation]]. Coincidence-sensitive receptors, dendrites, and
  neurons already belonged to older neuroscience.
- **April 2017:** In [[gh-nlp1|the first Neural Lace monologue]], Micah explicitly credited Tse,
  called coincidence a candidate basis of a neural bit, and connected patterns of such events to a
  proposed differential write-in: compare the ongoing pattern with a target pattern and transmit
  the difference rather than overwrite the whole state.
- **April 22, 2017:** [[gh-nlp3|Neural Lace Podcast 3]] clarified that the relevant event concerns
  multiple inputs received within a modifiable time span and a network of coincidence patterns.
  The discussion also rejected reducing the whole event to one action potential because dendrites
  and networks contribute additional state.
- **November 2, 2017:** [[gh-nlpnervegear|The NerveGear Show]] connected hierarchies of coincidence
  patterns to learned world models and moment-to-moment conceptual sequences.
- **May 11, 2019:** In [[gh-nlps2e4|the fNIRS discussion with Jonathan Toomim]], the speakers
  separated spatial proximity on a dendrite from proximity in the outside world, discussed
  NMDA-dependent plasticity and lateral inhibition as component mechanisms, and cautioned that
  coincidence learning is neither causality by itself nor the whole brain architecture.

The first monologue's phrase "the cell firing twice within a few seconds" is therefore not a stable
physiological definition. The same 2017 source sequence supplies the clearer intended operation:
selected inputs overlap within a receiver-dependent window, the receiver crosses a criterion, and
networks retain and use patterns of those consequential events.

## Established cellular mechanisms

Neurons, rather than synapses, generate action potentials. A presynaptic terminal releases
transmitter; receptors and conductances change the postsynaptic cell; dendrites integrate those
changes; and spikes are commonly initiated near the axon initial segment. A coincidence can alter
a local dendritic event, somatic output, synaptic plasticity, or circuit state without producing all
of those consequences.

NMDA receptors are one important coincidence-sensitive mechanism at glutamatergic synapses.
Opening requires glutamate, a co-agonist, and enough local depolarization to relieve voltage-
dependent magnesium block. NMDA receptors are not one universal neural gate. Coincidence
sensitivity also depends on AMPA conductance, voltage-gated channels, calcium stores, metabotropic
signaling, inhibition, morphology, electrotonic distance, recent activity, and cell class.

Primary results show this diversity:

- [Larkum, Zhu, and Sakmann (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1038/18686) demonstrated nonlinear coupling
  between separated input zones of layer-5 pyramidal neurons.
- [Bi and Poo (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-24-10464.1998) showed that persistent
  synaptic change in cultured hippocampal neurons depended on relative pre- and postsynaptic timing,
  synaptic strength, and postsynaptic cell type.
- [Xu et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5220-05.2006) showed that prior LTP induction
  could alter temporal and spatial integration in CA1 pyramidal dendrites, directly supporting the
  idea that a receiver's effective coincidence rule can change with history.
- [Fino et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2010.188466) found distinct coincidence
  mechanisms for different forms of corticostriatal spike-timing-dependent plasticity.
- [Shai et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004090) combined recordings and
  detailed modeling in mouse V1 layer-5 pyramidal cells to study basal-plus-apical coincidence,
  dendritic calcium electrogenesis, and burst output.
- [Lafourcade et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.01.025) found input- and
  compartment-specific AMPA-to-NMDA organization in mouse retrosplenial layer-5b pyramidal neurons,
  opposing a single generic decoding rule for all inputs to one cell.

These studies support state-, compartment-, cell-, and circuit-dependent coincidence sensitivity.
They do not establish one universal neural information unit.

## The 2022 SAN extension

The 2022 sources do more than rename a local detector:

- [[gh-a0142z|a0142z]], publicly Git-fixed beginning June 8, described patterns as
  tempo-spatial, phasic, coincident differential potentials. It also argued that coherence can
  support coupling while structured separation can preserve distinctions.
- [[gh-a0215z|a0215z]], also fixed beginning June 8, described cycles of neural coincidence
  patterns that support prediction and coordinate movement.
- [[gh-a0007z|a0007z]], developed from August 30 through September 18, used evidence from specific
  layer-5 pyramidal cells to explore a richer cellular criterion: basal and apical input can jointly
  alter burst output, while morphology, inhibition, calcium conductance, and learned state tune the
  result.
- [[gh-a0296zwhisker|a0296z]], developed through November 29, linked fast sensory arrival in
  multiple layers of rat barrel cortex to the hypothesis that populations of multilevel
  coincidence operations contribute partial spatial-temporal constraints to NAPOT reconstruction.

The medically accurate recovery of `a0007z` does not call the apical dendrite an axonal output
terminal. Distal apical dendrites receive and integrate synaptic input; the axon and its terminals
carry neuronal output. The recoverable SAN operation is **evidence/context interaction -> changed
cellular output -> learned downstream consequence**, tested in declared cell classes and pathways.

Zhu and Zhu found short-latency whisker responses in layer 1 neurons, layer-5 apical tufts, and
somata in rat barrel cortex, supporting rapid multilevel sensory integration
([2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4805-03.2004)). That result does not prove that identical
thalamic messages are copied to every layer, that every cortical column uses the same algorithm, or
that coincidence alone performs tomography. Those are separate SAN hypotheses requiring circuit-
specific measurements and perturbations.

## Tonic context, phasic consequence, and PWD

The receiver's ongoing state supplies the [[tonic-phasic-canvas|tonic context]] that determines
which overlap matters. A criterion-crossing event is a phasic consequence relative to that context.
It becomes a candidate [[phase-wave-differentials|phase-wave differential]] only when the source,
reference, route, typed change, receiver, and downstream consequence are declared.

Coincident input can produce a spike, burst, local dendritic event, plasticity change, inhibition,
phase advance or delay, structured dispersion, or no measurable output. Phase locking is not the
default result. Likewise, lateral inhibition can support competition or sparsity in named circuits,
but every accepted coincidence does not pass through a winner-take-all stage.

## Scale ladder and bridge burden

- **Micro:** selected inputs interact within a spine neighborhood, dendritic branch, soma, axon
  initial segment, terminal, or other declared cellular compartment.
- **Meso:** multiple cellular events jointly alter a local circuit, oscillating group, column,
  nucleus, or downstream population.
- **Macro:** distributed activity changes a body-world model, report, choice, or action through
  declared sensory, recurrent, and motor pathways.

The abstract receive-test-update operation can recur across scales, but the receiver and denominator
must change at every step. One dendritic coincidence is not itself a traveling wave, a reconstructed
scene, a conscious percept, or a motor command.

## Testable SAN claim

The distinctive test is not whether coincidence detection exists. It is whether a model containing
the receiver's learned criterion, current state, spatial input arrangement, timing, and typed
consequence predicts later routing, reconstruction, or action better than spike count, rate, timing,
or phase alone.

For the stronger NAPOT bridge, experiments must show that:

1. different partial input configurations produce distinguishable receiver-relative criterion
   events;
2. those events alter named downstream populations;
3. their joint pattern improves held-out reconstruction or behavior;
4. selective disruption removes the predicted information or action consequence; and
5. mechanism-matched restoration rescues it.

This would test a network of consequential coincidences without installing a hidden viewer. The
participating receivers, recurrent pathways, body feedback, and actions are the observer-action
system.
