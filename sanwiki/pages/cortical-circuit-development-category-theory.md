**Cortical circuit development** is shaped by evolutionary mechanisms, and some cortical cell types serve multiple functions across developmental stages (Cossart & Garel, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2022). SAN theory extends this observation: if cell types are multi-functional across time, then [[dendritic-computation]] capabilities are not fixed at birth but are sculpted by developmental history.

## Category theory for cellular communication

The source proposes that category theory — the mathematics of structure-preserving maps — could formalize how cells communicate. Cells execute "learned sequences of functions" in response to environmental signals, analogous to function composition in category theory. Each signaling pathway is a morphism; the cellular response network is a category.

This framing connects to SAN's interest in [[fractal-pattern]] structure: if cellular communication follows categorical laws, then the same structural patterns that appear at the synaptic scale (signal → transform → output) recur at the cellular network scale and beyond.

## Chemical signaling as Turing-complete

The source proposes that chemical communication between cells may be Turing-complete — capable of arbitrary computation given sufficient complexity. If true, the computational substrate of the brain is not confined to electrical spikes alone; the chemical milieu participates in information processing. This aligns with SAN's rejection of spike-only models (cf. [[multivesicular-release-synaptic-unreliability]]).
