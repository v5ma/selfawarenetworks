# Clinical Bci Applications

**Clinical BCI Applications** is the [[selfawarenetworks-legacy-wiki|SAN legacy wiki]]'s slot for the medical-deployment surface of [[bci|brain–computer interfaces]] — the concrete clinical use-cases (motor restoration after spinal-cord injury, communication for ALS or locked-in syndrome, deep-brain stimulation for Parkinson's, epilepsy seizure prediction, phantom-limb prosthesis control, depression and OCD neuromodulation) that distinguish a *clinical* BCI from a research demo or a consumer wearable. The page sits inside the broader [[brain-interface-bci-map|BCI map]] cluster alongside [[bci-history]], [[bci-delivery-mechanisms]], [[bci-ethics]], and [[bci-industry-landscape]].

The page exists because the SAN wiki references the clinical-applications slot whenever it discusses interventions that run end-to-end through a regulated medical-device pathway rather than a hobby or a wearable EEG. Definitionally, a clinical BCI application is one in which (a) the device sits inside an [[fda-regulation|FDA]]-cleared or analogous regulatory regime, (b) it is prescribed and supervised by clinicians, and (c) its outcome metric is a medical end-point (function restored, symptom reduced, seizure averted) rather than a behavioural-research metric. This delimits the slot from companion pages: [[BrainGate]] is a clinical implant lineage that fits here; [[Neuralink]] partly does (subject to its current trials); EEG headbands marketed for meditation generally do not.

In SAN's framing, the clinical-applications page is the deployment endpoint for the molecular-mechanism work in [[bridging-molecular-mechanisms]], the recording-modality work in [[fnirs-brain-imaging]] / [[eeg-bci-limitations]], and the policy work in [[bci-ethics]] and [[cognitive-privacy-xr-bci|cognitive-privacy]]. It also ties into the [[neural-lace-podcast-archive|Neural Lace Podcast]] threads on motor-restoration interviews and into [[augmented-reality-bci]] / [[webxr-bci-integration]] as the *non-clinical* counterparts that share hardware lineage but not regulatory burden.
