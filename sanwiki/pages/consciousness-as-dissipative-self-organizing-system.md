## The Dissipative Systems Argument

A convection cell, a whirlpool, a flame: these are not objects but *processes*. They persist as long as energy flows through them; remove the energy gradient and the pattern dissolves. Consciousness, under this view, has the same character. Neural activity continuously dissipates electrochemical gradients; the subjective pattern we call awareness is the stable vortex that emerges from this throughput.

This connects to [[wave-dissipation-universality]] - [[gh-resnote04|Micah]]'s reformulation of entropy as a computational process - and to [[napot]] where oscillating arrays generate a rendered reality from phase differentials.

## The Encoding-Decoding Constraint

A counterpart claim from the same discussion: information alone is insufficient for consciousness. A rock encodes information in its atomic structure, yet is not conscious. What is missing is a *decoder* - a mechanism that extracts meaning from the encoded signal and uses it to modify future behavior. Consciousness requires both encoding and decoding phases operating in a closed loop.

In SAN terms: [[phase-wave-differential]] patterns are the encoding; the downstream neural array that reads those patterns and updates its own gain state is the decoder. The loop between encoder and decoder is the minimal unit of experience.

## Relation to Competing Theories

- **Panpsychism**: the view that all matter has some form of consciousness. The dissipative-systems framing partially supports this - any system with energy throughput forms some gradient - but the encoding-decoding constraint excludes passive structures (rocks, gas clouds) that lack recursive [[gh-a0206z|self]]-modification.
- **Global Workspace Theory**: posits a broadcast mechanism; compatible with SAN's oscillatory architecture.
- **[[integrated-information-theory|Integrated Information Theory (IIT)]]**: see [[integrated-information-theory]]. The dissipative framing overlaps in emphasizing integration but differs in grounding consciousness in *process* rather than static Phi measure.
