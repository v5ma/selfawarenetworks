# Augmented Reality

**Augmented Reality** (AR) is a perceptual computing paradigm that overlays digitally generated content — graphics, data annotations, interactive objects — onto a user's live view of the physical world, in contrast to full virtual reality which replaces it entirely. Within the [[self-aware-networks]] research program it functions both as an active engineering domain (WebXR applications, EEG-driven overlays) and as a theoretical horizon, pointing toward the long-term goal where [[neural-lace]] technology renders display hardware obsolete by delivering AR experience through direct [[brain-computer-interface]] stimulation rather than optical projection.

## AR as AI Interface

A central insight repeated across multiple SAN sources is that AR glasses represent the "most natural way to sort of merge our minds with [[artificial-intelligence|Artificial Intelligence]] in a natural way" ([[gh-a0304z|a0304z]]--md, line 5). The reasoning is structural: an AR overlay can present AI-generated analysis, predictions, and annotations directly within the user's visual field at the moment of relevance, without requiring context switching to a separate screen. The [[brain-as-rendering-engine|brain as rendering engine]] model implies that the brain is already continuously constructing a rendered world-model from sensory input; AR merely introduces additional synthetic primitives into that pipeline before the rendering stage, so that the [[consciousness-multimodal-rendering-loop|consciousness multimodal rendering loop]] integrates them as if they were natural percepts. An AI guidance system operating through AR could function like "a [[gh-a0206z|self]]-driving car, slash AI slash augmented reality guidance system" ([[gh-b0130y|b0130y]]--md, line 35), navigating the user through complex environments by overlaying directional cues onto live space rather than presenting abstract maps. This framing connects AR directly to [[attention-schema-theory]]: the AR system effectively acts as an external [[attention-schema|attention schema]], highlighting salient objects and relationships that the user's own [[four-dimensional-phase-tracking|four-dimensional phase-tracking]] perceptual system might otherwise miss.
