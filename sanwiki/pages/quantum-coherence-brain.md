# Quantum Coherence in the Brain

The phrase **quantum coherence in the brain** can refer to several very different claims. This page is a boundary map, not evidence that the brain performs quantum computation or that quantum coherence produces consciousness.

## Five levels that must remain separate

1. **Quantum substrate:** all molecular structure ultimately obeys quantum mechanics. This is true but does not show that a neuron exploits a coherent quantum state for computation.
2. **Quantum-biological effect:** a molecular process may depend on tunneling, spin, excitation transfer, or another quantum description.
3. **Prepared-system coherence or collective photophysics:** purified proteins or assembled microtubules may display optical or electronic behavior under laboratory excitation.
4. **Functionally exploited neural quantum state:** living neural machinery prepares, preserves, controls, and reads a quantum state to perform a neural operation.
5. **Consciousness mechanism:** that neural operation is necessary or constitutive for a declared conscious content or capacity.

Evidence for one level cannot silently establish the next.

## Current microtubule evidence

Kalra and colleagues measured ultraviolet-excited electronic energy migration over about 6.6 nanometers in prepared microtubules and found that isoflurane and etomidate reduced the measured diffusion ([ACS Central Science, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.2c01114)). Babcock and colleagues reported collective ultraviolet-emission behavior in large tryptophan networks assembled in biological architectures ([Journal of Physical Chemistry B, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c07936)).

These are relevant physical measurements at levels 2 or 3, depending on the state definition. They do not demonstrate level 4 in intact neurons or level 5 in conscious organisms.

Rodent studies show that microtubule-modulating drugs can alter isoflurane righting-reflex endpoints ([Khan et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024); [Li et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-025-02956-9); [Huang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2026.110834)). Those results support a microtubule contribution to anesthetic response. They do not identify that contribution as quantum, and righting reflex is not a direct measurement of conscious content.

## Coherence vocabulary

A publication should always specify which coherence it measured:

- coherence of a quantum state;
- optical coherence or collective emission;
- excitation-energy migration;
- phase locking between neural signals;
- statistical EEG or MEG coherence;
- synchronized spiking;
- persistence of a cognitive representation.

Using one word for all of these can create a false bridge from protein photophysics to consciousness.

## Relation to Orch OR and SAN

[[orch-or|Orch OR]] proposes biologically orchestrated quantum processes in neuronal microtubules that terminate through objective reduction and produce conscious moments. That is a conjunction of physical, biological, computational, and consciousness claims.

SAN does not need that conjunction. Its current mechanism can be stated as distributed cellular and network dynamics: tonic activity supplies receiver-relative context, phasic events alter that context, recurrent routes redistribute the update, and body/action consequences constrain the next cycle.

A verified quantum effect could contribute to a cell's readiness or transformation without becoming the observer. The system-level observer would still be the distributed relation among cells, fields, memory, body state, and action.

## Decisive experiment shape

A useful study would prespecify:

1. the quantum state and measurement;
2. where and how intact neurons generate it;
3. its lifetime and biological receiver;
4. a selective intervention that changes the state without broadly damaging microtubules;
5. the downstream cellular and network computation;
6. the conscious or behavioral endpoint;
7. classical cytoskeletal and circuit controls.

Without this chain, "quantum coherence in the brain" remains an umbrella phrase rather than a demonstrated mechanism.
