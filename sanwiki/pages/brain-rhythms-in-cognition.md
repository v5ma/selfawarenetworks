# Brain Rhythms in Cognition: Coupling, Differentiation, and State-Dependent Computation

![Brain rhythms across cellular, local-circuit, distributed-network, and cognitive scales](/v5ma.github.io/wiki/assets/generated/san/brain-rhythms-in-cognition/brain-rhythms-across-scale-medically-corrected-20260713-v2.png)

> a proven molecule-to-consciousness chain. It separates cellular contributors, population
> measurements, traveling activity, body-state controls, and the open SAN/NAPOT reconstruction

This page asks a narrower question than [[brain-rhythms|Brain Rhythms]]: **when does rhythmic
structure help explain a cognitive operation?** Cognition here includes perception, attention,
working memory, rule selection, recall, decision, and action. No one frequency, brain region,
cell class, or synchrony statistic implements all of them.

## Core correction: cognition is not maximal synchrony

Rhythmic coordination can make communication more effective when activity from a source reaches a
declared receiver during a consequential excitability window. But synchronization alone cannot
explain which signals remain distinct, which alternatives are suppressed, which route is selected,
or what changed for the receiver.

The source-faithful SAN proposition is therefore bidirectional:

```text
coherence, phase locking, or coincident arrival
-> candidate coupling, summation, recruitment, or route stabilization

structured phase separation, desynchronization, inhibition, or splay
-> candidate distinction, boundary formation, suppression, or rerouting
```

Both sides can be useful. A desynchronized state is not automatically cognitive failure, and a
coherent state is not automatically binding, correctness, or consciousness. The relevant source,
receiver, pathway, timing measure, task, and consequence must be stated.

## What established neuroscience supports

### Phase-sensitive communication in bounded pathways

The communication-through-coherence hypothesis proposes that a favorable phase relation can make a
presynaptic population more effective at a postsynaptic population. In macaque visual cortex,
Bosman et al. found attention-related selective gamma synchronization between V1 and V4
([Neuron, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3457649/)). Saalmann et al. found that the
pulvinar changed synchronization among connected visual cortical areas with attentional allocation
([Science, 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22879517/)).

These studies support pathway- and task-specific timing effects. They do not establish that all
cortical communication uses one phase code or that zero-lag synchrony merges distributed activity
into one cognitive subject. Fries's synthesis describes one influential mechanism family, not a
universal law ([Neuron, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26447583/)).

### Task rules and working memory can appear in transient assemblies

Buschman et al. reported rule-specific oscillatory ensembles in macaque prefrontal cortex during
task switching ([Neuron, 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23177967/)). Lundqvist et al. found
that beta and gamma activity during working memory often occurred as brief, variable bursts rather
than continuous clocks ([Neuron, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26996084/)).

The medically defensible conclusion is that transient timing structure can help identify or
coordinate task-relevant population states in particular preparations. A broad band label by itself
does not specify the represented rule, memory item, or computation.

### Reduced synchrony can improve active sensory processing

In behaving mice, active whisking reduced nearby membrane-potential correlation and desynchronized
local field potential and EEG while sparse, cell-specific excitatory events drove action potentials
with a higher signal-to-noise ratio
([Poulet and Petersen, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18633351/)). This result is important for
SAN because it shows why "more synchrony" cannot be the default success criterion. A lower shared
correlation can accompany more differentiated sensory processing.

### Rhythmic organization can move through space

Human intracranial recordings during a memory task revealed theta- and alpha-frequency traveling
waves across contiguous cortical regions. Their propagation was related to task events and more
consistent during better performance
([Zhang et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29887341/)). Traveling waves preserve spatial
phase gradients; they should not be reduced to simultaneous firing everywhere.

### The field still contains open controversies

A 2025 multi-author position paper organizes current questions around cyclic excitability,
inhibitory gating, phase-amplitude relations, sampling, traveling waves, brain-body interactions,
perception, attention, memory, and communication. It explicitly presents unresolved questions
rather than one accepted rhythm theory
([Keitel et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15639)).

## The bounded SAN cognition operator

The strongest recoverable form is:

```text
ongoing receiver, circuit, and body context
-> transient differentiated excitation, inhibition, burst, or waveform event
-> phase- and pathway-sensitive influence at named receivers
-> coupling among selected routes plus structured separation around alternatives
-> recurrent update across cellular, local-circuit, and distributed-network scales
-> perception, memory, decision, action, or learning consequence
-> updated context for the next cycle
```

This operator does not require all participating cells to share one phase. A cognitive state may
contain several locally coordinated assemblies with stable nonzero lags, traveling gradients,
intermittent bursts, inhibited alternatives, and relatively desynchronized sensory events.

## Five cognitive domains

| Domain | Established measurement target | Bounded SAN interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Perception and active sensing | Sensory responses, membrane potential, spikes, LFP/ECoG, behavior | Phasic differences are evaluated against a changing receiver and body context rather than a fixed global baseline. |
| Attention and routing | Pathway-specific coupling, directed influence, pulvinar-cortical interaction, response gain | Coherence can increase influence along selected routes while inhibition and dispersion reduce competing influence. |
| Working memory and rule use | Transient beta/gamma bursts, spiking, decoding, task performance | Temporarily recurrent timing states may coordinate retrieval and use without requiring one sustained oscillation or one PFC store. |
| Recall and sequence organization | Reactivation, hippocampal-cortical interaction, traveling waves, behavior | Spatially propagated timing may help reinstate distributed states; NAPOT reconstruction remains a separate hypothesis. |
| Choice and action | Premotor and motor activity, effective drive, movement onset, sensory return | Coincident activity can contribute to threshold crossing in named motor pathways, while inhibitory timing helps select among alternatives. |

The last row connects to [[electrical-valence-receiver-relative-drive|Electrical Valence]]. It does
not mean that scalp EEG power becomes the energy of muscle contraction. Motor neurons and muscle
fibers are activated through anatomical pathways, neuromuscular transmission, and membrane
depolarization.

## A bounded mathematical vocabulary

For a source population `i` and receiver `j`, define a receiver-relative phase difference:

```text
Delta_phi_ij(t) = wrap(phi_i(t) - phi_j(t))                  [1]
```

A candidate phase-sensitive receiver model is:

```text
u_j(t) = sum_i w_ij x_i(t) g_j(Delta_phi_ij, state_j)
         - I_j(t)                                           [2]
```

`g_j` is not universal. It represents the empirically estimated effect of timing on receiver `j`;
`I_j` represents inhibitory and other subtractive influences. The model must be compared with
firing-rate, power, common-input, and connectivity alternatives.

A cognitive population state needs more than one synchrony value:

```text
C(t) = [T(t), P(t), K(t), D(t), W(t), B(t)]                 [3]
```

where `T` is ongoing context, `P` transient phasic events, `K` coupling structure, `D` structured
dispersion or inhibition, `W` spatial propagation, and `B` body and behavioral state. This is an
analysis vocabulary, not a claim that the brain literally stores one six-slot vector.

Plasticity is a separate, conditional update:

```text
Delta_w_ij = H(pre_i, post_j, Delta_t, phase, calcium,
               neuromodulation, location, history)          [4]
```

Coherence alone does not determine whether a synapse undergoes LTP, LTD, no durable change, or a
different form of plasticity.

## Discriminating experiments

1. **Coupling versus common input.** Perturb one anatomical source while matching power and firing
   rate. Test a preregistered receiver and behavioral consequence.
2. **Coherence versus differentiation.** Match global synchrony while changing whether phase
   relations form coherent subassemblies, organized splay, stable lag, or random dispersion.
3. **Burst versus continuous-rhythm models.** Compare held-out prediction from event-based burst
   models, sustained oscillation models, and aperiodic-plus-transient models.
4. **Active sensing.** Record membrane voltage, spikes, LFP, movement, and sensory input during
   quiet and active sampling. Test whether differentiated events predict perception better than
   mean synchrony.
5. **Spatial propagation.** Compare local phase, traveling-wave geometry, and ordinary connectivity
   when predicting memory, attention, or action.
6. **Plasticity consequence.** Impose defined timing relations and measure later synaptic efficacy,
   excitability, assembly recruitment, and behavior. Do not infer LTP or LTD from phase alone.
7. **SAN increment.** Test whether receiver-relative PWD, organized dispersion, recurrence, and
   body state improve prediction beyond rate, power, phase, connectivity, and standard prediction-
   error models.
8. **Observer boundary.** Keep discrimination, report, action, learning, wakefulness, and subjective
   experience as separate outcomes. Rhythmic coordination is not by itself proof of a viewer or
   conscious subject.

## Claim ladder

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Neural rhythms, bursts, phase relations, traveling waves, and state-dependent desynchronization are measurable. | Established in many named preparations. |
| Timing relations can alter communication, attention, working memory, sensory gain, and action in bounded circuits. | Supported; mechanism and generality remain preparation-dependent. |
| Reduced synchrony or structured phase separation can accompany useful sensory and cognitive processing. | Supported in bounded cases; the relevant statistic must be specified. |
| Every frequency band has one fixed cognitive function. | Not supported. |
| Synchronization universally binds cortical columns into one cognitive coalition. | Not supported. |
| COT, NAPOT, and NDCA form a proven serial biological readout stack. | Not established; these are distinct SAN hypotheses and formalisms. |
| Receiver-relative coupling plus structured differentiation is a better account of SAN than synchrony alone. | Source-supported SAN interpretation. |
| The complete tonic-phasic, coherence-dispersion architecture constructs phenomenal content. | Open SAN hypothesis requiring causal and phenomenological tests. |

Three earlier assets were reviewed for this route:

- The June 29 phase-synchronization plate is rejected because it says synchronized rhythms
  implement cognition, depicts out-of-phase activity as generically suppressed, and presents COT,
  NAPOT, and NDCA as a serial readout stack.
- The July 11 first across-scale plate is rejected because it depicts a deterministic channel-to-
  cognition chain, a fixed cortical intrinsic-frequency gradient, and phase alignment as directly
  opening or closing a universal release pathway.

  modalities and scales, labels circuit and source dependence, distinguishes statistical effective
