# Category Theory Neuroscience

As a definitional consequence of the title, the term denotes the use of category-theoretic tools — objects, morphisms, functors, and natural transformations — to formalize relationships between brain structures and the computations they perform, rather than relying on ad-hoc network diagrams or purely statistical models. Within SAN this framing sits naturally beside [[biological-oscillating-tomography]] and [[neural-array-projection-oscillation-tomography]], because the same mathematics that handles morphisms between projection and reconstruction in tomographic imaging is exactly what category theory is designed to make precise.

In its definitional reading, **Category Theory Neuroscience** treats [[cortical-columns]], [[micro-columns]], [[neural-arrays]], and [[apical-dendrites]] as objects in a category, and treats the [[oscillatory-tomography]] phase relations between them as morphisms. The functorial step then maps from the category of brain states to a category of perceptual states, formalising what SAN normally describes informally as "phase variances" rendering [[qualia]].
