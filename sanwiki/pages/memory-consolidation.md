# Memory Consolidation

Memory consolidation is not one transfer event. It is a family of processes through which learning becomes more persistent, changes its dependence on particular circuits, and can be updated when it is reactivated.

> **Figure note:** The earlier "oscillatory absorption" plate has been withdrawn from use. A replacement must distinguish synaptic, circuit, and systems consolidation and must show the SAN extension as a hypothesis rather than depict a memory being absorbed into cortical columns.

## Four processes that should not be collapsed

| Process | Biologically bounded description |
|---|---|
| Synaptic and cellular consolidation | Activity-dependent molecular, receptor, excitability, gene-expression, and structural changes stabilize parts of a learned response over minutes to hours and beyond. [[ltp-ltd|LTP and LTD]] are important experimental models, not the only possible memory substrate. |
| Circuit and ensemble reorganization | Reactivation, competition, inhibition, plasticity, and changing excitability alter which neurons participate in a learned ensemble and how strongly they influence one another. |
| Systems consolidation or transformation | The contribution of hippocampal, cortical, amygdalar, striatal, cerebellar, and other systems changes with memory type, time, rehearsal, sleep, context, and task. This is not always a one-way handoff from hippocampus to cortex. |
| Reconsolidation and updating | Retrieval can make some memories modifiable again. Depending on boundary conditions, new information may strengthen, weaken, or transform the later memory. |

The enduring substrate is an [[engram]]: learning-induced physical and functional change distributed across cells and circuits. Retrieval is a new, state-dependent neural event constrained by that substrate, as explained in [[memory-as-regenerable-pattern|Memory as Regenerable Pattern]]. Consolidation can stabilize parts of that substrate while also reorganizing what later reconstruction will recruit.

## What established evidence supports

Several findings make oscillatory timing and distributed reactivation relevant without proving the complete SAN interpretation:

- Selectively suppressing post-training hippocampal sharp-wave ripples impaired a hippocampus-dependent spatial-memory task in rats ([Girardeau et al., 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.2384)). Interrupting awake ripples also impaired spatial learning in rats ([Jadhav et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22555434/)). These experiments support causal roles for some ripple events in some memory tasks; they do not show that every memory begins with a ripple.
- Human intracranial recordings show temporal nesting among slow oscillations, sleep spindles, and hippocampal ripples ([Staresina et al., 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.4119)). Later human recordings linked this sequence to increased firing, local assembly correlations, and medial-temporal communication ([Staresina et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01381-w)).
- In rats, experimentally coupling a detected hippocampal ripple to cortical stimulation improved later recall relative to delayed or random stimulation ([Maingret et al., 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.4304)). Timing therefore matters, but no single phase relation is a universal consolidation rule.
- Mouse engram studies show changing circuit contributions over time and widespread, functionally connected engram ensembles ([Kitamura et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28386011/); [Roy et al., 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29384-4)). A memory is not adequately represented as one phase-aligned cortical-column array.
- Retrieval can reopen some memories to protein-synthesis-dependent restabilization, establishing that durable memory is not necessarily fixed after initial consolidation ([Nader et al., 2000](https://www.nature.com/articles/35021052)).

These results support plasticity, state-dependent reactivation, temporally organized communication, distributed engram participation, and updating. They do not establish exact replay of a sensory movie, universal hippocampus-to-cortex transfer, literal absorption into cortex, or consciousness as a consequence of consolidation.

## The source-faithful SAN proposal

The SAN record contains an earlier and more specific proposal than the current page previously reported.

- **June 10, 2022:** [NAPOT 2](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/dfaedb9f8235d88af8af678ff4c421a6e03a35a4/a0007z.md#L629) distinguished relatively persistent routes shaped by synaptic growth and atrophy from faster-changing active subsets shaped by current electrical state. This supplies the persistent-readiness versus transient-recruitment distinction.
- **August 10, 2022:** [SAN source notes](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/75b194fb97d2da09fb8bdd4130be98b910a72adb/a0271z.md#L15) proposed that salient phasic activity perturbs ongoing tonic activity and can contribute to longer-term synaptic change.
- **September 11, 2022:** [Sharp-wave-ripple notes](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/217e306b6c2c2ddd5be3c07bd603bd17ff80bc66/a0309z.md#L22) proposed that a local high-phasic event can recruit a distributed sequence, allowing a partial learned state to scale into a multimodal reconstruction that can affect prediction and action. The source also connected sequential activation, inhibition, and downstream routing.
- **September 6, 2024:** [02san](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/64f018d2d93b7de6718058c4724c43b40e5dd4b7/02san.md#L154) introduced the phrase "oscillatory absorption" in an AI-assisted discussion. Micah immediately questioned whether the AI's edited understanding made sense. The phrase is therefore a later candidate interpretation, not the origin of the 2022 proposal and not accepted neuroscience.
- **October 5, 2024:** [04san](https://github.com/v5ma/selfawarenetworks/blob/fe415a93a7e5bfecf416c137c6f5196238d60a1e/04san.md#L501) developed "oscillatory pattern completion" and offline error-correction language. Those passages are SAN hypotheses, not direct findings of the oddball experiment being discussed there.

The recoverable operation is:

**learning episode -> persistent cellular and circuit readiness -> state-gated reactivation -> recurrent completion, competition, and inhibition -> distributed re-expression -> plastic update -> changed later recall, prediction, or action**

This chain preserves the innovative part of the sources without claiming that a memory is a literal waveform copied from one location to another.

## What "oscillatory absorption" can mean medically

Used carefully, *absorption* can name a change in future network behavior rather than a substance or trace moving into cortical columns. A transient event is "absorbed" when its consequences are incorporated into altered synaptic efficacy, cellular excitability, ensemble membership, or effective routing, so that later inputs produce a different response.

In SAN terms:

1. A current [[brain-state|brain and body state]] provides tonic receiver context.
2. Reactivation supplies phasic departures from that context, potentially including [[sharp-wave-ripple|sharp-wave-ripple]] sequences.
3. Recurrent excitation, inhibition, neuromodulation, and coincidence determine which routes are recruited and which are suppressed.
4. Plasticity changes future receiver readiness.
5. Later cues can evoke a related but not necessarily identical distributed pattern.

This is not a claim that maximal synchrony is always beneficial. Coordinated coupling can recruit an assembly, while structured phase dispersion, inhibition, and competition can separate alternatives and prevent indiscriminate activation. Consolidation may depend on the organization of both participation and exclusion.

## The open PWD extension

The stronger SAN hypothesis is that typed, receiver-relative [[phase-wave-differentials|Phase-Wave Differential]] variables help determine which reactivated events alter later routing. That hypothesis extends ordinary firing-rate, ensemble, and ripple-power descriptions by asking whether phase, timing, waveform, duration, transmitted quantity, variability, and receiver state jointly predict plastic consequences.

A valid test should compare nested models:

- **Model 0:** behavior, prior learning, arousal, firing rate, ensemble identity, and current state.
- **Model 1:** established event variables, including ripple occurrence, slow-oscillation phase, spindle timing, and coupling.
- **Model 2:** typed receiver-relative PWD variables added without removing the established predictors.

Model 2 earns a role only if it improves out-of-sample prediction and matched causal intervention. It should be rejected for this mechanism if PWD variables add no reproducible information beyond Models 0 and 1.

## Predictions and falsifiers

- Matched reactivation events should have different plastic or behavioral consequences when receiver state differs.
- Successful consolidation should depend on task- and circuit-specific timing relationships, not one universal frequency band or global phase-alignment target.
- Perturbing a relevant ripple, spindle, slow oscillation, engram ensemble, or neuromodulatory state should change only the memory functions that depend on that event and circuit.
- Distributed reactivation should sometimes outperform matched single-site reactivation, while indiscriminate synchrony should not.
- A stronger SAN claim requires PWD variables to predict later ensemble recruitment, synaptic change, or behavior beyond standard event timing, rate, power, and state measures.
- Evidence for consolidation or retrieval does not by itself establish conscious access or a conscious inner display.
